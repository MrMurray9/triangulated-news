Calgary council votes 13-2 to start building the legal tools for a pause on giant AI data centres

Calgary city council voted 13-2 on Tuesday for a motion from Ward 2 Coun. Jennifer Wyness that tells city staff to draft Land Use Bylaw changes by March 2027. Those changes would let the city temporarily freeze land-use applications for large-scale data centres. Wyness said provincial law allows a moratorium, but Calgary’s bylaw has no way to impose one yet (Calgary Herald, LiveWire Calgary).

A second part, passed unanimously, asks staff to define small, medium and large data centres and recommend where each belongs. A study of water, electricity and noise impacts is due in late 2027. Only one large data centre application is before the city now, from Stantec Consulting for a site in Redstone in the far northeast. Mayor Jeromy Farkas said any centres that come must face strict rules on noise, water and power. Ward 14 Coun. Landon Johnston, one of the two councillors who voted no, worried the city would turn away big business (Calgary Herald, LiveWire Calgary, CTV News Calgary).

Why it matters: Data centres use huge amounts of power and water, and Calgary wants its rules in place before more of them apply.

Sources (4)

  • CTV News Calgary: City seeks pause on data centres

Community Note

The vote doesn’t impose a moratorium. It starts bylaw changes that council would still have to approve, probably next spring.

Alberta’s current guidance for large data centres (75 megawatts or more) favours ones that generate their own power.

Edmonton fans still owed refunds for a rained-out Matt Rife show are left waiting as festival company Trixstar goes bankrupt

Trixstar Productions, the company behind the Great Outdoors Comedy Festival, has filed for bankruptcy. A storm cancelled its July 18 show at Kinsmen Park, headlined by Matt Rife, and ticket holders say they’re still waiting for refunds more than three months later (CityNews Edmonton, Culture Alberta, Taproot Edmonton).

Founder Mike Anderson said in a statement that the company couldn’t find “a financially sustainable path forward” and that he is “deeply sorry for the uncertainty and hardship this creates.” Insolvency trustee MNP Ltd. says it will contact known creditors. Vendors were hit too: a Moncton food-truck owner told CityNews she’s still owed $20,000 for working the Halifax festival in August.

Why it matters: Thousands of Edmonton ticket holders could get little or nothing back unless they file a claim with the trustee or ask their card company for a refund.

P.E.I. fisherman and carpenter Nicholas Herring wins the $100,000 Giller Prize for Your Breath in Charcoal

Nicholas Herring, who lives in Murray Harbour, P.E.I., and works as a fisherman and carpenter, won the 2026 Giller Prize on Monday night for his second novel, Your Breath in Charcoal. It follows Knickle, a young Islander who drifts through life, on a road trip from Prince Edward Island to Toronto. The jury called it “a riotously funny working-class odyssey filled with love, loss, and profound hope” (Giller Prize, The Canadian Press, The Globe and Mail).

For the first time, the winner was picked by a jury of nine independent booksellers from across Canada. The other four finalists, Tara Gereaux, Michelle Good, Nazanine Hozar and Patmeena Sabit, get $10,000 each. Herring’s first novel, Some Hellish, won the Atwood Gibson Writers’ Trust Fiction Prize (Giller Prize, The Globe and Mail, St. Jerome’s University).

Why it matters: The Giller is Canada’s best-known fiction prize, and a win usually brings a big jump in sales for the book and its small East Coast publisher.

A gas tanker sinks off Bulgaria, the second ship lost there in two days, and all 19 crew are rescued from a life raft

The Liberian-flagged gas carrier Gas Husky, carrying about 3,400 tonnes of liquefied petroleum gas from Alexandria, Egypt, to Midia, Romania, sank Tuesday about 50 nautical miles east of the Bulgarian town of Byala, inside Bulgaria’s exclusive economic zone. A Bulgarian Air Force jet spotted a life raft and a distress flare, and a Cougar helicopter flew all 19 Filipino crew members to Varna. Two had minor injuries (BTA, AP, Euronews).

The ship’s Greek manager, Stealth Maritime, said the vessel was “struck” but didn’t say by what. Stepping off the helicopter, the captain said, “We believe four drones hit us.” Bulgaria hasn’t officially confirmed a cause. On Monday the cargo ship Betul 8 sank nearby, and its 12 crew were rescued the same way (Maritime.bg, BTA, AP, Naftemporiki).

Why it matters: Attacks on shipping are now sinking vessels in the waters of a NATO member, which raises the risk and insurance costs for everything moving across the Black Sea.

Nvidia’s sales more than double to US$115.8 billion in its first report since Jensen Huang’s exit, and the stock rises after hours

Nvidia reported third-quarter revenue of US$115.8 billion after markets closed Tuesday, up 103% from a year earlier and well above the roughly US$109 billion analysts expected. Profit was US$65.3 billion, and sales of data-centre chips rose 110% to US$107.6 billion. Nvidia expects about US$130 billion this quarter, which has an extra week, and assumes no data-centre chip sales to China (Nvidia, CNBC, Axios).

It was the first report under interim CEO Colette Kress, who is also still the finance chief. She said sales of the new Vera Rubin systems are “off to an explosive start.” Shares rose 2% to 3% after hours. They had fallen 24% so far this month, and the Dow dropped about 850 points during the day as investors waited (CNBC, Axios, Yahoo Finance).

Why it matters: Nvidia makes most of the chips behind the AI boom, so its results move the whole market, including many Canadians’ retirement savings.

Science corner: a dome-shaped camera sensor packs 367,500 pixels and sees in full colour across 160 degrees

A team at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST), led by Fan Zhiyong, has built a camera that works like an eyeball. Its light sensors sit on a curved, dome-shaped “retina” instead of a flat chip. A new 3D laser technique let the team pack 1,905 pixels per inch onto that curve, 367,500 in all. Their first version, from 2020, had just 100 (Photonics Spectra, HKUST, Nature Materials).

Stacked blue, green and red layers produce full-colour images across a field of view of more than 160 degrees, without the extra lenses a flat sensor needs to fix distortion. The sensor can also detect movement on its own, sending only what changed rather than whole frames. In the team’s tests that cut the data needed by more than 99.95% and recognized motions with 98.6% accuracy. Fan says the next goal is more than a million pixels (Photonics Spectra, Nature Materials).

Why it matters: Small, wide-angle cameras that need very little data could help robots and self-driving cars see more while using less power.

Sources (3)
  • Nature Materials: An aberration-corrected bionic eye for full-colour vision and event-driven motion detection
  • HKUST: HKUST develops high-resolution bionic eye
  • Photonics Spectra: Research team boosts bionic eye pixel density
Community Note

This is a lab prototype for machines. It isn’t an implant that restores human sight.

The paper appeared in Nature Materials, and HKUST announced the work this week. The accuracy figures come from the team’s own test conditions.

Sources: Nature Materials; HKUST; Photonics Spectra

GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol arrive in ChatGPT, with Astra’s developer price cut 20% and better slides, spreadsheets and science work

OpenAI released two new models on Tuesday: GPT-6.2 Astra, its top model for demanding professional work, and GPT-6.2 Sol, a cheaper one for everyday tasks. Both are available to Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT and the Codex coding tool, and to developers through OpenAI’s API and Amazon Bedrock. Astra’s API price drops 20%, to US$8 per million input tokens and US$40 per million output tokens; Sol stays at US$2 and US$10 (OpenAI, The Decoder, AWS).

OpenAI says Astra makes slides, spreadsheets and documents that “read less like AI,” follows templates more closely, and is better at coding websites, games and 3D designs (The Verge). The independent tester Artificial Analysis ranks it second overall, just behind Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.2. It also found Astra’s coding scores unchanged and a high rate of guessing on very hard factual questions (The Decoder).

Why it matters: Cheaper top-tier models lower the cost of the AI tools that businesses and apps build on.
