A 40-unit home for veterans who have nowhere else to go opens in Edmonton’s Athlone neighbourhood, two days before Remembrance Day

Veterans’ House Canada cut the ribbon on Monday at its new Edmonton home, just north of the CN tracks in Athlone. The building has 40 permanent, affordable studio units for Canadian Armed Forces veterans who are homeless or at risk of it, plus on-site help with jobs, addiction and mental health. It also has a fitness room, a library, a garden, a meditation park and a park for service dogs and pets. Nine former service members have already moved in, and residents share a community meal every Wednesday (Edmonton Journal, CTV News Edmonton, Veterans’ House Canada).

It is the charity’s second home in the country, after Ottawa, which opened in 2021 and is always full, and three more are planned. The City of Edmonton sold the land for $1 and added a $2.5-million grant. The province put in $3.4 million and Ottawa $4.5 million, and a $6-million fundraising campaign covered the rest. Executive director Alan Mulawyshyn, a retired brigadier-general, said the family of a veteran is more than five times as likely to be homeless as the average Canadian (Edmonton Journal).

Why it matters: It’s a concrete, local answer to veteran homelessness in Edmonton, and eligible veterans can already ask about a unit.

Canada

Another “very robust” Pacific storm hits B.C., with up to 250 mm of rain forecast for Vancouver Island after weekend storms that left two people dead

Environment Canada has orange rainfall warnings out for west and central Vancouver Island, calling for 150 to 200 mm of rain, and possibly more than 250 mm in places, from Tuesday morning through Thursday morning. Howe Sound and the Sea to Sky corridor could get 120 to 160 mm. The B.C. River Forecast Centre has a flood watch for much of the south coast, Vancouver Island and the north Fraser Valley, and warns that rivers will rise quickly, with mild temperatures melting some high-elevation snow (Environment Canada, B.C. River Forecast Centre, Today in BC).

It follows a run of storms since Friday. About 415,000 BC Hydro customers lost power at some point, and about 13,300 were still in the dark late Monday. The storms are linked to two deaths in Metro Vancouver: a 78-year-old woman struck by a car in heavy rain on Friday, and a driver killed when a tree fell on two vehicles in Surrey on Saturday. A canoeist has been missing off northern Vancouver Island since Friday, and the remote village of Zeballos ordered 22 properties evacuated over landslide risk (The Globe and Mail). Another system is due Friday.

Why it matters: The same weather pattern, which forecasters call an omega block, is pushing warm Pacific air over the Rockies and could keep Alberta 7 to 10 degrees above normal for the next 10 days.

Canada reimposes sanctions on 11 Iranian banks, insurers and a tanker firm, a decade after lifting them under the nuclear deal

Foreign Affairs Minister Anita Anand announced on Tuesday that Canada is putting sanctions back on 11 Iranian entities it says contribute to Tehran’s nuclear and ballistic-missile programs or help finance violence abroad. Canadians are now barred from financial dealings with them. The list includes banks, insurance providers and a tanker company (Global Affairs Canada, The Canadian Press via The Globe and Mail).

Canada had lifted these sanctions in 2016 in recognition of Iran’s progress under the 2015 nuclear deal. Global Affairs says the move lines up with G7 efforts to get Iran to co-operate with the International Atomic Energy Agency. Iran stopped co-operating with the agency’s inspectors after U.S. and Israeli strikes on its nuclear sites, and UN sanctions were later restored (Global Affairs Canada, The Jerusalem Post).

Why it matters: It ties Canada more tightly to the G7 pressure campaign on Iran’s nuclear program and cuts off any remaining Canadian business with the named firms.

Sources (3)
  • Global Affairs Canada: Canada reimposes sanctions against 11 Iranian entities
  • The Canadian Press via The Globe and Mail: Ottawa to reimpose sanctions on Iran after lifting them a decade prior
  • The Jerusalem Post: Canada reimposes sanctions on 11 entities over Iranian non-compliance
Community Note

Canada cut diplomatic ties with Iran in 2012. These measures restore economic sanctions under the Special Economic Measures (Iran) Regulations. They aren’t a new break in relations.

Ottawa hasn’t published how much Canadian trade, if any, the 11 entities still had.

Sources: Global Affairs Canada; The Canadian Press; The Jerusalem Post

Portugal closes schools nationwide and Web Summit cancels its second day as Storm Charles brings a risk of 160 km/h gusts to Lisbon

Portugal’s government ordered schools and non-essential public services across the mainland closed on Wednesday and banned access to the coastline, riverbanks and flood-prone areas as Storm Charles moves in. Lisbon is shutting its tunnels, parks, gardens and cultural venues from midnight. The weather service IPMA forecast gusts of up to 120 km/h, and forecasters warned that a rare “sting jet” could push gusts above 160 km/h, especially around Greater Lisbon (Euronews, The Next Web, Diário de Notícias).

Web Summit, one of the world’s biggest tech conferences with about 70,000 attendees, called off its second day on Wednesday after the government formally advised it not to go ahead, citing tidal flooding, transit disruption and wind damage to outdoor structures. Organizers hope to hold the final day on Thursday, Nov. 12 (Business Insider, Euronews, Diário de Notícias, Lusa via Notícias ao Minuto).

Why it matters: A whole country shutting its schools for one storm shows how seriously forecasters are taking it, and thousands of travellers and conference-goers are stuck mid-trip.

Pope Leo draws an estimated 2.5 million people to Buenos Aires, his biggest Mass yet, after visiting a disability home hit by Milei’s cuts

The Vatican says about 2.5 million people attended Pope Leo XIV’s first Mass in Argentina on Monday at the Monument of the Spaniards in Buenos Aires. That is the largest crowd of his papacy, at the same site where John Paul II said the last papal Mass in Argentina nearly 40 years ago. The government declared a national holiday so people could attend, and President Javier Milei sat in the VIP section (The Associated Press via NBC News, El País, Buenos Aires Times).

Before the Mass, Leo visited the Cottolengo Don Orione, a home for more than 400 people with disabilities that has been hit by Milei’s austerity cuts. He told residents their lives were “a blessing, not a burden.” “A nation’s progress is also measured by how it welcomes all people with disabilities and particular needs,” he said, days before Argentina’s Senate takes up disability funding that Milei opposes. Milei’s cabinet chief denied it was a rebuke. On Tuesday Leo moved on to Córdoba, again drawing huge crowds (AP via NBC News, ABC News Australia, Euronews).

Why it matters: The first American pope is using his visit to his predecessor’s home country to press a message on welfare and the economy, and it is landing in the middle of Argentina’s fight over budget cuts.

U.S. prices rose 0.4% in October, a bit hotter than forecast, and traders now see a December Fed rate hike as a sure thing

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics said consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, down from 0.6% in September but slightly above what economists expected, the second hotter-than-forecast month in a row. Core prices, which leave out food and energy, rose 0.3%. The bureau didn’t publish a year-over-year figure because of data-collection problems from October 2025. Futures markets now price a 100% chance that the Federal Reserve raises its key rate by at least a quarter point in early December, and some traders are betting on a half point. Fed Governor Christopher Waller said tightening is needed “sooner, rather than later” (Investopedia, The Associated Press, Yahoo Finance).

Stocks gave back part of Monday’s big rally. The S&P 500 fell 0.4%, the Dow 0.3% and the Nasdaq 0.7%. The 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.03% before settling near 4.98%, and Nvidia fell another 5.5%. Oil kept sliding, with U.S. crude near US$86.50 a barrel, its lowest since late August (Investopedia, AP).

Why it matters: Higher U.S. rates and a 10-year yield near 5% tend to push up Canadian fixed mortgage rates and the U.S. dollar, and cheaper oil means less money flowing into Alberta.

The Claude maker sets its IPO at $70 to $78 a share, which would value it at up to about US$1.4 trillion when it lists as “ANT”

In an updated filing on Tuesday, Anthropic said it will offer 825 million Class A shares at US$70 to US$78 each, raising US$57.75 billion to US$64.35 billion. At the top of the range it would be worth about US$1.404 trillion on a fully diluted basis. That is up from US$965 billion in a May funding round, but below the US$2 trillion it was reportedly aiming for a few months ago. Separately, AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC agreed to buy US$26 billion of non-voting shares at the IPO price, with Google and Nvidia putting in US$10 billion each (TechCrunch, Investopedia, Reuters via Investing.com).

Trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker “ANT” is expected around Nov. 19, and up to 20% of the shares could go to retail investors. Founders keep a special share class with 50.1% of the votes, so public buyers will have limited say (TechCrunch, CNBC).

Why it matters: It would be one of the biggest IPOs ever, and its reception is being watched as a test of whether investors still want to fund the AI boom after a rough week for tech stocks.

Science corner

Science corner: a six-chip gallium oxide switch handles a megawatt in bursts, more than 100 times what this class of chip had managed before

Engineers at the University of Hong Kong, working with Virginia Tech and Nanjing University, built a power module from six gallium oxide chips that switched 1,000 volts and 1,000 amps a thousand times a second. That is a peak of 1 megawatt, the first time a multi-chip module made from an “ultrawide-bandgap” semiconductor has reached that level. The work is published in Nature Communications (University of Hong Kong, Interesting Engineering, Nature Communications).

Gallium oxide can handle very strong electric fields, but it moves heat away poorly. The team’s fix was to design the chip and its package together. They moved the hottest part of each chip closer to the cooling path and added an insulating layer to spread out the electric field. A single device carried 234 amps at 1,000 volts and kept working above 250°C (Interesting Engineering, HKU).

Why it matters: Smaller, tougher high-power switches could shrink equipment that needs short, intense bursts of electricity, such as particle accelerators, fusion experiments, medical machines and grid protection.

Sources (3)
  • The University of Hong Kong: World’s first megawatt ultra-wide bandgap semiconductor module
  • Interesting Engineering: World’s first megawatt power module uses gallium oxide
  • Nature Communications: A megawatt ultra-wide bandgap semiconductor module for pulsed power electronics
Community Note

“1 megawatt” is peak pulsed power in short bursts, not continuous output. The module isn’t a power plant, and it’s a lab demonstration, not a product.

The “more than 100 times” comparison is the researchers’ own claim about earlier ultrawide-bandgap demonstrations.

Sources: University of Hong Kong; Interesting Engineering; Nature Communications

AI news and advances

Google opens Gemini 4 Carbon, which it calls its strongest model yet, to paying developers and Ultra subscribers, and previews agent “swarms”

Google began rolling out Gemini 4 Carbon to the public on Tuesday, two weeks after giving it to a small group of cybersecurity defenders. It is available through the paid Gemini API in AI Studio and to business customers, and to Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search and the Antigravity coding platform. Google says Carbon is built for long, multi-step coding, debugging and open-ended scientific research, while last month’s Argon model stays the better pick for most everyday work (Google, The Verge, 9to5Google).

Introductory pricing is US$2 per million input tokens and US$10 per million output tokens until Nov. 30, after which prices double. The Verge says that is still well below rival frontier models per token. Google is also previewing a “swarm” feature in Antigravity that turns one prompt into a co-ordinated team of AI agents. There’s no word yet on access for free or lower-tier users (The Verge, Google).

Why it matters: It puts Google back at the top tier of AI models on price and power, and agent teams are the next big push for AI that does whole jobs rather than single answers.

Sources (3)
  • Google: Gemini 4 Carbon is now available
  • The Verge: Google releases Gemini 4 Carbon with agent ‘swarm’ feature
  • 9to5Google: Gemini 4 Carbon rolling out to Gemini app for AI Ultra
Community Note

“Strongest model” is Google’s claim. Benchmarks are chosen by the company, and The Verge notes Google’s own figures still put Carbon behind at least one rival on some tests.

A lower price per token doesn’t always mean a lower bill, because models can use very different numbers of tokens for the same task.
