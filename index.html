Alberta

Alberta moves to end DEI hiring at public bodies and to punish sharing sexual deepfakes, on the sitting’s first day

Justice Minister Mickey Amery tabled Bill 33, the Justice Statutes Amendment Act, 2026 (No. 2), on Tuesday afternoon as the legislature returned. It would stop provincial bodies, including agencies, Crown corporations, post-secondaries, school boards and municipalities, from using diversity, equity and inclusion policies in hiring and promotion, and would ban mandatory DEI training. Voluntary training stays allowed, and any mandatory training must be neutral and job-related (Edmonton Journal, CTV News, Global News).

“These amendments will support neutral hiring and training practices while ensuring people are hired and promoted based on their skills, experience and ability to do the job well,” Amery said. The bill also renames the 2017 intimate-images law the Intimate Content Protection Act, adds AI-made or altered content, and lets victims sue over threats to share. Starting in spring 2027, distributing such content could bring a fine of up to $10,000, up to two years in jail, or both (Edmonton Journal, CTV News).

Why it matters: It changes hiring rules at school boards, universities and city halls across the province, and gives victims of AI-made sexual images a clearer way to fight back.

Sources: Edmonton Journal; CTV News; Global News.

Edmonton council votes 9–3 to push for more power from the province, as Mayor Knack meets the premier

Council passed Coun. Aaron Paquette’s motion to lobby for greater municipal autonomy through the Federation of Canadian Municipalities, Big City Mayors and Alberta Municipalities. Supporters pointed to provincial moves on infill rules and photo radar as overreach. Couns. Thu Parmar, Mike Elliott and Karen Principe voted against, warning it could hurt the city’s relationship with the province (Edmonton Journal, CTV News, Taproot Edmonton).

While council voted, Mayor Andrew Knack was meeting Smith and cabinet ministers, a meeting he framed as “rebuilding trust.” The talks covered zoning, homelessness, safety and affordability (Edmonton Journal, CTV News).

Why it matters: Who decides on things like infill housing and photo radar in Edmonton, city hall or the legislature, shapes what residents see on their streets.

Canada

Ottawa tables Bill C-41 to allow advance requests for medical assistance in dying and to keep mental illness alone excluded

Justice Minister Sean Fraser introduced the MAID Safeguards Act on Tuesday. It would let adults diagnosed with a serious, incurable illness expected to take away their capacity, such as Alzheimer’s, sign an advance request so MAID could be provided later, once all eligibility rules and safeguards are met (Department of Justice Canada, Parliament of Canada, The Canadian Press).

The bill would also cancel the planned expansion of MAID to people whose only condition is a mental illness. Advance requests would apply only in provinces that pass their own laws to store and govern them. “This wasn’t a conclusion that we reached out of thin air,” Fraser said of the mental-illness exclusion, pointing to a joint committee of MPs and senators (Department of Justice Canada, The Canadian Press).

Why it matters: It answers two of the hardest questions in Canada’s assisted-dying law, and it must pass both the Commons and the Senate before the current deadline for the mental-illness expansion.

  • Parliament of Canada — Bill C-41, first reading

Community Note

Even with a valid advance request, the person would have to be suffering intolerably when MAID is provided. Alberta and other provinces would each decide whether to offer advance requests at all, so access could differ by province.

Sources: Department of Justice Canada; Parliament of Canada; The Canadian Press.

Canada and the UAE sign a free trade deal negotiated in a record 47 days, cutting tariffs on more than 99 per cent of Canadian exports there

Trade ministers Maninder Sidhu and Thani Al Zeyoudi signed the Canada–UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement on Tuesday, during the first-ever visit to Canada by Abu Dhabi’s Crown Prince, Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan. Ottawa says it is the fastest free trade agreement either country has negotiated (Prime Minister’s Office, The Globe and Mail, Al-Monitor).

Once in force, expected in 2027, it would reduce or remove tariffs on canola, pulses, machinery, aerospace and other exports. The two countries also signed a defence co-operation agreement that sets up a joint military committee (Prime Minister’s Office, Daily Sabah, Al-Monitor).

Why it matters: It is part of Carney’s push to sell more to countries other than the U.S., and Prairie crops like canola and pulses are among the winners.

  • Al-Monitor — Canada, UAE sign trade pact as Ottawa seeks to diversify beyond US
  • Daily Sabah — Canada, UAE sign major trade deal amid tensions with US
Community Note

The 99 per cent figure refers to the share of current Canadian export lines, not dollar value. Trade with the UAE is still small next to trade with the U.S., and the deal must be ratified before it takes effect.

Sources: Prime Minister’s Office; The Globe and Mail; Al-Monitor.

Egypt’s parliament lets el-Sisi send military help to Saudi Arabia after Houthi attacks

Egypt’s House of Representatives voted by the required two-thirds majority to authorize President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi to give Saudi Arabia military support if Riyadh asks for it, following Houthi attacks on the kingdom (AP, The National, Euronews).

The vote did not set the type, size or timing of any help. Officials described it as defensive, to protect Saudi territory and borders rather than to attack Yemen, and reports point to air-defence systems and aircraft rather than ground troops (AP, The National, Al Jazeera).

Why it matters: The Arab world’s most populous country stepping in would widen the regional conflict around Yemen and the Red Sea, where shipping and oil prices are already on edge.

Exit polls: Netanyahu’s bloc falls to about 50 seats and neither side reaches a majority in Israel’s election

What changed: This morning we reported record midday turnout. Polls have now closed, and exit polls show Netanyahu’s camp short of a majority, though his rivals are short too.

All three main TV exit polls (Kan 11, Channel 12 and Channel 13) gave Gadi Eisenkot’s opposition bloc 52 seats, Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc 50 and Arab-led parties 18, out of 120. A government needs 61. Likud is projected as the largest single party with 24 to 25 seats, ahead of Eisenkot’s Yashar at 21 to 23 (Reuters, AP, The Guardian).

AP called it “a sharp rebuke” to Netanyahu, whose bloc held 68 seats in the outgoing Knesset. The easiest path for the opposition would mean Eisenkot partnering with Arab-led parties, which could split his own camp, so weeks of coalition talks or even another election are possible (AP, The Guardian).

Why it matters: Who governs Israel next will shape the Gaza war’s aftermath, relations with the U.S. and regional diplomacy, and the answer may take weeks.

Business

Microsoft offers voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 workers, more than a quarter of its staff, including in Canada

Microsoft announced a “voluntary exit offer” for employees at level 70 and below on participating teams in the U.S., Canada, India and the U.K. About 60,000 people are eligible. Terms are similar to standard severance, with financial support and career-planning help (Microsoft, CNBC, GeekWire).

Eligible staff must decide in early December, and those who accept are expected to leave by Dec. 31. CNBC and NBC News report the move is part of a push for efficiency as the company pours money into AI infrastructure (CNBC, NBC News, TechCrunch).

Why it matters: It is one of the largest workforce offers ever by a tech company, and it signals how AI spending is reshaping jobs even at highly profitable firms.

Boeing stock drops about 9 per cent as it pushes its 737 MAX production goal to late 2027

Boeing lost US$116 million, or 26 cents a share, in the third quarter. Its adjusted loss of 34 cents was wider than the roughly 21 cents analysts expected, though revenue rose 4 per cent to US$24.19 billion, helped by a 15 per cent jump in defence sales (CNBC, Investing.com, AFP).

CEO Kelly Ortberg said 737 MAX output won’t reach 52 jets a month until the second half of 2027, citing slower wing production and the need to keep the factory stable first. The shares fell about 9 per cent on Tuesday (CNBC, AFP, Investing.com).

Why it matters: Airlines, including Canadian carriers that fly the 737 MAX, are waiting on these deliveries, and Boeing’s pace affects fares and fleet plans for years.

Science corner

Flat aluminum sheets, folded like origami, become rigid blocks that screw together into a room-sized shell with no welding

Researchers at the Center for Bits and Atoms in Cambridge, Mass., designed origami-style units, inspired by artist Ron Resch’s folded tessellations, that are cut from flat sheets by a CNC machine, folded by hand and screwed together. Each block is locked rigid, and together they form a load-bearing shell with no interior columns (MIT News, International Journal of Solids and Structures).

The team at MIT reports that simulations show the structures are about 3.5 times stiffer than some other origami methods while using roughly two-thirds the material. A team with no construction experience built a 23-foot folded-aluminum sculpture, Lexicon, now hanging in the Boston Public Library, in a matter of days (MIT News, Boston Public Library, Axios Boston).

Why it matters: Shelters that ship flat and go up with screwdrivers could help after floods or earthquakes, and the same idea could make lighter parts for aircraft and buildings.

Sources (4)

  • Boston Public Library — Lexicon installation
  • Axios Boston — With BPL’s new installation, art meets science
  • Boston.gov — Creativity powers innovation in new installation at Boston Public Library
Community Note

The stiffness figures come from simulations in the team’s own paper. The method hasn’t yet been tested in a real, occupied building, which the researchers say is their next step.

Sources: MIT News; Boston Public Library; Axios Boston.

AI news and advances

Google DeepMind’s Genie 4 builds playable 3D worlds with sound and lets you change them mid-scene by typing

Genie 4 is a “world model”: it generates an interactive, realistic scene you can move through in real time. New this version is native audio, including music, background sound and dialogue, plus the ability to steer a scene with text prompts, such as changing the weather or camera angle or adding people and objects (Google DeepMind, TechCrunch, The Verge).

Sessions stay coherent for about two minutes, double Genie 3’s limit, and the model uses Gemini to plan and keep scenes consistent. It is a research preview for trusted testers first, with Google AI Ultra subscribers and API partners to follow (TechCrunch, Ars Technica, Google DeepMind).

Why it matters: World models are how AI labs plan to train robots and game agents safely in simulation, and they hint at a new kind of interactive entertainment.
