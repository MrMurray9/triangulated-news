Elections Alberta flags more than 3,000 mail-in referendum ballots with errors

Elections Alberta says more than 3,000 returned special-ballot packages have errors that make them invalid as submitted. Most involve a missing or incorrectly completed declaration form, while others are missing a copy of ID or have ID problems. The agency says it is contacting affected voters, who can fix the problem by email.

Elections Alberta has verified nearly 221,000 completed mail-in ballots, about one-third of the more than 652,000 packages sent out. It has hired more than 41,000 election workers and wants more than 60,000.

Why it matters: Mail-in voters whose packages are missing a declaration or ID must get the corrected documents in by 5 p.m. Oct. 16. If they don’t, the special ballot is rejected and they have to vote in person during advance voting (Oct. 13–17) or on Oct. 19.

What changed since yesterday: The agency’s count of verified mail-in ballots rose to nearly 221,000, up from more than 131,000 on Sept. 29, and it disclosed the 3,000-plus packages with errors.

Sources (4)
  • The Globe and Mail
  • CP24 / The Canadian Press
  • Elections Alberta: special ballot process
  • Elections Alberta: Oct. 1 update
Community Note

An invalid package is not automatically a lost vote. Elections Alberta’s published process says electors missing a declaration or ID copy are contacted and can correct it before the deadline. If they don’t, they can still vote in person. The 3,000 figure is small compared with the nearly 221,000 ballots verified so far. Canada Post recommends mailing completed packages by Oct. 9.

Sources: Globe and Mail; Canadian Press; Elections Alberta.

Alberta doctors ratify three-year master agreement with the province

Members of the Alberta Medical Association voted to ratify a proposed three-year master agreement with the provincial government. In a letter to members, AMA president Dr. Heidi Fell said 22 per cent of eligible members voted and 76 per cent of those voted in favour. She said it is not the agreement the AMA first sought and came after mediation, but that it gives doctors three years of stability and some meaningful gains.

In a joint statement, Hospital and Surgical Health Services Minister Adriana LaGrange and Finance Minister Jason Nixon called the deal fair and sustainable. The government and the AMA said they will share details once the agreement is finalized.

Why it matters: The agreement sets how Alberta pays its physicians for the next three years, but its terms have not been made public yet.

Sources (3)
  • Global News / The Canadian Press
  • Government of Alberta statement
  • CTV News Edmonton
Community Note

Turnout was low. With 22 per cent voting and 76 per cent of them in favour, roughly 17 per cent of all eligible members voted yes. The terms, including any fee changes, have not been released, so claims about specific raises or cuts cannot be verified yet.

Sources: Canadian Press via Global News; Government of Alberta; CTV News.

Canada

Carney pledges to work with Quebec’s incoming PQ premier, calling it “a crucial time”

Prime Minister Mark Carney said Tuesday that he spoke with Quebec premier-designate Paul St-Pierre Plamondon, congratulated him, and discussed working together. He named housing, infrastructure, electricity capacity, and support for workers hurt by U.S. tariffs as shared priorities. “This is a crucial time for our country, it’s a crucial time for Quebec,” Carney said, adding that it was “not a time to wait for something a few years down the road.”

Asked about a possible referendum, Carney said he too was working toward sovereignty, “but it’s Canada’s sovereignty.” St-Pierre Plamondon has promised to pull Quebec out of the Alto high-speed rail project. Industry Minister Mélanie Joly said she believes Alto and the Churchill Falls energy development will still go ahead.

Why it matters: A Parti Québécois government could slow federal priorities that run through Quebec, such as Alto rail and hydro expansion, while the PQ has promised a sovereignty referendum only after 2029.

What changed since yesterday: Carney made his first direct contact with the premier-designate and gave Ottawa’s first public pledge of cooperation after Monday’s PQ minority win.

Sources (3)
  • The Globe and Mail / The Canadian Press
  • National Post
  • AFP
Community Note

Carney did not directly answer whether he is worried about a referendum. The PQ won a minority of 59 of 127 seats, five short of a majority, with about 28 per cent of the popular vote according to AFP. The PQ has said any referendum would come during its mandate but only after Donald Trump leaves office in 2029.

Sources: Canadian Press via Globe and Mail; National Post; AFP.

Foreign influence registry receives about 30 filings, far below federal estimates

Foreign Influence Transparency Commissioner Anton Boegman told a House of Commons committee Tuesday that his office received about 30 submissions by the deadline for arrangements that existed before the law took effect on Aug. 4. Only two entries were on the public registry as of Tuesday, and the rest are being validated before they are published.

Public Safety Canada has estimated that about 2,422 people or organizations could be required to register. Boegman said the registry is brand new and he expects the numbers to rise as awareness grows.

Why it matters: The registry is a new federal tool meant to show who works on behalf of foreign governments or principals to influence Canadian political processes, and opposition MPs questioned its early effectiveness.

Drones hit two ships off Bulgaria; one sinks and its crew is missing

Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev said sea and aerial drones struck two commercial ships at about 3 a.m. local time Tuesday, roughly 70 nautical miles (130 km) off the coast in Bulgaria’s exclusive economic zone. The Togo-flagged Alfa Watan sank, and authorities launched a search for its crew. The Palau-flagged Able caught fire. All 18 of its crew were evacuated, and two seriously injured sailors were flown to hospital in Varna.

Radev called the attack “a blatant violation of international law and maritime law.” Bulgarian outlet Fakti.bg reported early Oct. 7 that no trace of the Alfa Watan’s crew had been found.

Why it matters: The strike in an EU and NATO member’s exclusive economic zone widens the Black Sea shipping risk tied to the Russia-Ukraine war. Radev said it is raising insurance costs and making shipping extremely difficult.

France’s student protests have their biggest day yet after a teen loses a hand in Lens

About 250,000 people joined student-led protests across France on Tuesday, according to the interior ministry, while organisers put the number at 450,000. Physics Nobel goes to Francis Halzen for the South Pole “ghost particle” observatory

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics to Francis Halzen of the University of Wisconsin–Madison “for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin.” IceCube is a cubic kilometre of Antarctic ice fitted with light sensors. Halzen proposed the idea in 1988, and the detector was finished in 2011.

Neutrinos pass through Earth almost without interacting. Because they travel without changing direction, they can carry information from the most violent places in the universe. The prize is worth 12 million Swedish kronor.

Why it matters: University of Alberta physicists are part of the IceCube collaboration and played roles in its 2018 identification of the first likely source of high-energy cosmic neutrinos.

Sources (4)
  • NobelPrize.org press release
  • Reuters (via ThePrint)
  • UW–Madison News
  • University of Alberta Physics (2018)
Community Note

The prize goes to one person, but IceCube is a large international collaboration. Halzen is its principal investigator. Reuters describes Halzen, 82, as Belgian-American, and the Nobel committee lists him as born in Tienen, Belgium, in 1944.

Sources: NobelPrize.org; Reuters via ThePrint; UW–Madison; University of Alberta.

McDonald’s faces a U.S. class action over its AI pricing tool

A proposed class action filed Friday in federal court in Illinois alleges that McDonald’s AI-assisted pricing tool shares nonpublic data, such as store-level sales, among franchisees who may compete with each other. The suit says this amounts to coordinated pricing in violation of antitrust law. Lead plaintiff Michael Thomas alleges violations of the Sherman Act and Illinois laws.

McDonald’s has said it makes the tool available to U.S. franchisees to help them set prices at their own restaurants.

Why it matters: The case adds to growing legal scrutiny of algorithmic pricing tools that use shared data to recommend prices.

Sources (4)
  • AP (via The Washington Post)
  • Reuters
  • ABA Journal
  • McDonald’s statement on pricing
Community Note

These are allegations, and no court has ruled. The claim that average menu prices rose about 40 per cent in five years comes from the plaintiffs, as reported by Law360 via the ABA Journal. McDonald’s says the AI makes recommendations and franchisees set their own prices.

Sources: AP via Washington Post; Reuters; ABA Journal; McDonald’s.

A Canadian rocket startup’s first satellite is working in orbit

Ontario-based NordSpace says its Terra Nova pathfinder satellite is operating successfully after launching Oct. 1 on SpaceX’s Transporter-18 rideshare mission from California. The company says this makes it the first Canadian launch company to operate its own hardware in space. Terra Nova has completed its early-orbit phase and is entering commissioning as engineers power up its subsystems.

NordSpace says the small satellite was designed, built, and tested in-house in nine months. It carries an electric thruster and AI-powered cameras, and in the coming weeks the company plans to test whether it can help monitor wildfires and the Arctic.

Why it matters: Wildfire and Arctic monitoring from Canadian-built spacecraft are directly relevant to Alberta and the North, and the mission is a step toward Canada launching its own payloads.

Sources (3)
  • CTV News
  • SpaceQ
  • NordSpace release (EIN Presswire)
Community Note

This is a small test satellite in early commissioning, not an operational wildfire-detection service. Its monitoring capabilities are still to be tested. NordSpace’s own rockets have not yet flown, and Terra Nova rode a U.S. SpaceX rocket.

Sources: CTV News; SpaceQ; NordSpace.
