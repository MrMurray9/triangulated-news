Police say an online drug shop run out of Edmonton homes mailed about 8,700 packages across Canada — five people face 133 charges

The Edmonton Police Service announced charges Monday against five Edmonton residents linked to “BlueBuddhaCo,” an online business that advertised fast, “secure” nationwide delivery of cocaine, MDMA, LSD, GHB and methamphetamine. Police say it shipped about 8,700 packages of drugs across the country and took in roughly $1.98 million in e-transfers, moving the money through bank accounts and cryptocurrency platforms (Edmonton Police Service, CTV News Edmonton).

The drug and gang unit investigated from March 2024 to January 2025, with help from the RCMP and Ottawa police. Search warrants that January found storage and shipping sites in central Edmonton and the Hollick-Kenyon and Schonsee neighbourhoods, along with packaging gear, scales and mailing supplies; police put the street value of the drugs seized at about $224,000. The five were charged in February 2026 with offences including organized crime, trafficking and laundering the proceeds of crime (Edmonton Police Service, Edmonton Journal, CityNews Edmonton).

Why it matters: Police say mail-order drug sites like this are hard to spot, and they’re asking anyone who sees such a website or ad to screenshot it and report it to the EPS non-emergency line, 780-423-4567.

Grass-fire rules were already on the books at Wainwright — and Canada’s top soldier promises to make public an inquiry that could take a year

An internal 2024 “range standing orders” document for CFB Wainwright, obtained by The Globe and Mail and confirmed by the Army as still in effect, gives a whole section to grass fires. The Forces’ fire marshal is leading the search for a cause, and military police are investigating whether anyone did wrong. Alberta RCMP say their “initial involvement is winding down” (Edmonton Journal).

Why it matters: A public report is the main way soldiers’ families, and the soldiers who still train at Wainwright, will find out whether safety rules failed.

Science corner: Cornell builds a 3-millimetre chip that blows tiny bubbles on command — then uses sound to make them pump and steer liquid

Tiny bubbles are useful for moving liquid around in microscopic devices, but they’re slippery to catch and hold in place. Physicist Itai Cohen’s lab at Cornell skipped trapping them altogether. Their chip has a grid of electrodes, each six microns across, that split water to grow a hydrogen bubble right where it’s wanted; the longer the voltage runs, the bigger the bubble, anywhere from 10 to 100 microns (Cornell Chronicle, Science Advances).

Ultrasound then makes bubbles of the right size vibrate, and the vibration pushes the surrounding fluid. Lining bubbles up in a row, a diagonal or an L-shape steers the flow in different directions, and a higher voltage fires off a stream of bubbles like a jet. If a bubble misbehaves, the team simply grows it until it floats away and makes a fresh one. “With this bubble array, we can control the bubble size, numbers and position in real time, so we can program the flow direction,” said first author Zhiyuan Zhang. The study was published Oct. 14 (Cornell Chronicle, TechXplore, Interesting Engineering).

Why it matters: Moving liquid at tiny scales with no moving parts could lead to cheaper, reprogrammable lab-on-a-chip tests and, eventually, microscopic robots that move on their own.

Washington orders DeepSeek ripped out of federal civilian computers — including any outside tools built on the Chinese company’s AI models

A Homeland Security order, issued July 24 and made public this week in the Federal Register, requires civilian agencies such as the Treasury, State and Justice departments to remove DeepSeek products from their systems, along with third-party products and services built on its models, and bars them from buying any more. Why it matters: It’s the widest U.S. government ban yet on a Chinese AI model, and it reaches software vendors who quietly built their products on DeepSeek, not just the DeepSeek app itself.

CERN teams up with Paris-based Mistral to build “Flare,” one AI model trained on billions of simulated collisions inside the Large Hadron Collider

Europe’s particle-physics lab and the Paris-based AI company have signed a statement of intent for a long-term partnership. The goal is what CERN calls the first “foundation model” for collider physics: one pretrained AI, named Flare according to Reuters, that’s trained on billions of simulated proton-proton collisions and can handle many jobs at once, including reconstructing collisions, identifying particles, flagging unexpected signals and testing designs for future detectors (CERN, Reuters).

Today that work runs through a chain of specialised algorithms, each built for one step. CERN already stores about one exabyte (a million terabytes) of LHC data, and the number of collisions will rise tenfold when the upgraded High-Luminosity LHC starts around 2030. CERN’s chief information officer, Enrica Porcari, said the aim is AI “that can serve the whole physics community and be shared” (CERN, The Next Web).

Why it matters: The world’s biggest physics lab is betting one general AI model can replace dozens of hand-built tools, and it chose a European AI company over the U.S. giants to build it.
