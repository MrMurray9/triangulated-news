Alberta

Most calls to Alberta women’s shelters don’t end in an admission, and a new report says many women can’t afford to leave

The Alberta Council of Women’s Shelters says 82% of calls asking for a shelter bed in 2025–26 didn’t lead to an admission. The number of people staying in its more than 50 member shelters has dropped since 2023–24. The council says that doesn’t mean there is less abuse. Rent, deposits and the cost of setting up a new home are keeping women in unsafe homes, and some are choosing that over the risk of homelessness (Global News, Edmonton Journal, ACWS report).

Women who do get in are staying longer. The average emergency-shelter stay is up about 50% since 2019, which leaves fewer beds for new arrivals. Over the same period, the report says, housing costs rose 31%, food 36% and gas 35%, and Alberta food-bank visits went up 134%. The report says 72% of survivors in 2024–25 described physical violence getting worse over the past year. The council is asking for priority housing, help with rent and damage deposits, and less red tape around benefits such as the Escaping Abuse Benefit (Global News, CTV News Edmonton).

Why it matters: Fewer shelter admissions could easily look like good news. The council says it means more women are stuck at home with an abuser, and it wants faster housing and money help for those who leave.

Sources (4)

  • Edmonton Journal: Alberta women’s shelters report rising abuse and declining admission rates
  • CTV News Edmonton: ‘Leave or eat’: Rising costs may hinder those fleeing domestic abuse, advocates say
  • ACWS: The Cost of Leaving, 2024–26 Shelter Impact Report (PDF)
Community Note

The figures come from the council’s own report on its member shelters. An unanswered call isn’t always a refusal, since some callers are referred elsewhere or decide not to come. The report covers 2024 to 2026, and 11,332 adults and 6,277 children were admitted in that time. Help is available 24 hours a day at Alberta’s Family Violence Info Line, 310-1818.

Sources: Global News; Edmonton Journal; CTV News Edmonton; ACWS report

Tenants at Old Strathcona’s Kennedy Towers push back on rent hikes of up to 60% from the building’s new owner

About 75 tenants at Kennedy Towers, an apartment building in Edmonton’s Old Strathcona, are protesting rent increases of up to 60%. The building was sold earlier this year to B.C.-based Platinum Properties Group, and some rents would jump from about $900 to more than $1,300 a month. Residents called it “economic eviction” and say renovations have mostly been to the office and individual suites (CityNews Edmonton, Global News).

Tenants have proposed a flat 5% increase for every unit and say they aren’t planning a rent strike. Ward papastew Coun. Michael Janz said Alberta’s lack of rent control makes cases like this possible. He is pushing for a city landlord registry that would show renters who owns a building, its maintenance record and any complaints. CityNews said Platinum Properties hadn’t responded to its request for comment (CityNews Edmonton, HighOnCity).

Why it matters: Alberta has no limit on how much rent can rise once a lease ends, so a sale like this can price out long-time renters. Edmonton council may now be asked to look at a landlord registry.

Sources (3)
  • CityNews Edmonton: Edmonton tenants protest proposed 60% rent hike
  • Global News: New owners of Kennedy Towers give rent increases upwards of 60 per cent (video)
  • HighOnCity: Kennedy Towers tenants protest 60% rent hike
Community Note

“Up to 60%” is the highest increase tenants reported. Not every unit faces that much. The owner hasn’t commented publicly. In Alberta, a landlord can raise rent once every 12 months with three months’ written notice for a periodic tenancy, and there is no cap on the amount.

Sources: CityNews Edmonton; Global News; HighOnCity

Canada is set to close a free-trade deal with the Philippines at next week’s ASEAN summit in Manila

Canada and the Philippines are expected to announce that they’ve finished negotiating a free-trade agreement when Prime Minister Mark Carney attends the Association of Southeast Asian Nations summit in Manila, a source told The Globe and Mail. The Prime Minister’s Office says Carney will also “work to finalize” a separate deal with the whole ASEAN bloc. Ottawa expects the Philippines deal to triple two-way trade by 2035 (The Globe and Mail, Prime Minister’s Office).

International Trade Minister Maninder Sidhu’s office says both deals were more than 90% done by September and negotiators are “in the final stretch.” Canada’s ambassador in Manila told the Daily Tribune that the deal is on track. It would be the Philippines’ first bilateral free-trade agreement with a North American country. The Philippines hosts the summit from Nov. 15 to 17. Carney’s nine-day trip starts in Dublin, and he goes on to the Francophonie summit in Cambodia and APEC in Shenzhen afterward (The Globe and Mail, Daily Tribune, BusinessWorld, Reuters, iPolitics).

Why it matters: These would be Canada’s first trade deals with Southeast Asia as a whole, part of Ottawa’s push to sell more outside the United States while U.S. tariffs stay in place.

A magnitude 5.7 quake jolts central Japan overnight, injuring 22 people and shattering shop windows

A magnitude 5.7 earthquake struck at about 1:04 a.m. Sunday local time, about 41 km under western Shizuoka Prefecture, the Japan Meteorological Agency said. Shaking reached “upper 5” on Japan’s seven-level intensity scale in Chita, Aichi Prefecture, and “lower 5” in Hamamatsu, Seto, Ena and a village in Nagano. No tsunami was triggered (Jiji Press via Nippon.com, NHK World, AP via Al Jazeera).

Twenty-two people were hurt, 19 in Aichi and three in Shizuoka. Some fell on stairs while rushing outside, and a woman in her 50s in Hekinan broke her ankle. TV footage showed shattered store windows and a bookstore with books piled on the floor. Tokaido Shinkansen bullet trains ran late after safety checks. The agency warned of possible strong aftershocks over the next week, especially the next two to three days, but said it saw no unusual change along the Nankai Trough (Jiji Press, AP, NHK World).

Why it matters: The quake hit near Japan’s car-making heartland around Toyota City and Nagoya. Because the Nankai Trough is nearby, officials watch every quake there for signs of a much larger one.

Why it matters: More than a year into the ceasefire, most of Gaza’s population is still living in tents, and winter rain is now putting lives at risk.

Herzog formally hands Gadi Eisenkot the mandate, and he now has 28 days to form Israel’s next government

What changed: On Nov. 6 we reported that the Joint List’s backing put Eisenkot close to a Knesset majority. Since then, Herzog has formally given him the mandate, with 67 recommendations to Netanyahu’s 46, and his 28-day clock has started.

President Isaac Herzog gave Yashar party leader Gadi Eisenkot the mandate to form a government at a ceremony in Jerusalem on Sunday. Herzog said 67 incoming Knesset members recommended Eisenkot and 46 recommended Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Eisenkot has 28 days, and Herzog can add up to 14 more (The Times of Israel, Jerusalem Post, Anadolu).

Why it matters: This is the first time in years that someone other than Netanyahu has the first chance to form a government, which could change Israel’s approach to Gaza, Iran and its own courts.

Science corner: a skin-like gel electrode that reads brain signals all day without the usual sticky-cap hassle

Most brain-computer interfaces that don’t need surgery read EEG signals through electrodes on the scalp. Hard ones press uncomfortably, and soft gels dry out or shift when you move, which blurs the signal. A team in China reports in npj Flexible Electronics that it built a hydrogel electrode that changes from soft to firm along its length, like the layers of human skin. It is gentle against the scalp and sturdy where it connects to the electronics (npj Flexible Electronics, South China Morning Post).

The team made the gradient by curing the gel across a temperature gradient. That also left tiny pores of different sizes that release electrolyte slowly, so the gel doesn’t dry out. The electrodes kept a clean signal for more than eight hours while the wearer moved around and more than 14 hours at rest. In a brain-controlled typing test, accuracy stayed above 94% for the whole session (npj Flexible Electronics, Scienmag/Bioengineer.org, EEG Signal).

Why it matters: EEG caps that stay comfortable and accurate for a full day could make home brain monitoring and hands-free typing practical for people with paralysis or ALS, without brain surgery.

Sources (4)
  • npj Flexible Electronics: Skin-mimetic adaptive gradient electrode for ambulatory noninvasive brain-computer interfaces
  • South China Morning Post: China’s comfortable brain-reading device achieves 94% mind-typing accuracy in day-long use
  • Bioengineer.org (Scienmag): Skin-inspired gradient hydrogel electrode keeps EEG signals sharp for over 14 hours
  • EEG Signal: Gradient EEG electrodes exceed 94% mind-typing accuracy
Community Note

“Mind-typing” here means a visual-evoked typing task: the user looks at flickering targets and the system reads the brain’s response. It isn’t reading thoughts. The results come from lab tests on a small number of volunteers, and the electrode isn’t a medical product yet.

Sources: npj Flexible Electronics; South China Morning Post; Bioengineer.org; EEG Signal

AI news and advances

Nvidia opens its chip-linking tech to a Korean AI chip for the first time, pairing Rebellions’ R200 with its GPUs at SK Telecom

Nvidia, Korean AI-chip startup Rebellions and SK Telecom signed a deal at Nvidia’s AI Day in Seoul on Monday. Rebellions will connect its next-generation Rebel R200 neural processing unit to Nvidia’s NVLink Fusion, the high-speed link Nvidia now licenses to other chipmakers, and build the system on Nvidia’s standard MGX server racks. SK Telecom will install and test the combined system in its AI data centre (Nvidia, Korea IT Times, Seoul Economic Daily).

The idea is to split AI answers into two jobs. Nvidia GPUs handle “prefill,” the heavy first pass that reads your prompt, and the R200s handle “decode,” the word-by-word reply, which depends more on memory speed than raw power. The companies say the split could cut power use and cost per answer. It is Nvidia’s first formal deal of this kind with a Korean chip company, after Marvell and MediaTek. Rebellions expects systems to be ready in 2028. Nvidia also said it plans to invest in Korean startups Upstage, Motif Technologies and RealWorld (Seoul Economic Daily, Asia Business Daily, SK Telecom).

Why it matters: Running AI, not training it, is becoming the main cost for data centres, and this lets countries such as Korea use their own chips without leaving Nvidia’s ecosystem.

Sources (5)
  • NVIDIA Newsroom: NVIDIA, Rebellions and SK Telecom announce collaboration to advance sovereign AI infrastructure
  • Korea IT Times: SKT, NVIDIA and Rebellions join forces to build Korea’s sovereign AI infrastructure
  • Seoul Economic Daily: Rebellions joins Nvidia ecosystem to target global AI inference market
  • The Asia Business Daily: NVIDIA, SK Telecom and Rebellions to build Korean-style sovereign AI infrastructure
  • SK Telecom Newsroom (Korean)
Community Note

This is a memorandum of understanding, not a purchase order. The R200 systems are about two years away, and the efficiency gains are the companies’ own goals that haven’t been tested yet. Analysts note that moving data between GPUs and NPUs can add delay that eats into the savings. Nvidia’s startup investments haven’t closed yet.

Sources: NVIDIA; Korea IT Times; Seoul Economic Daily; Asia Business Daily

FA trapped Florida mom posts on Facebook from her kitchen counter, and two teen brothers paddle over to rescue her, her son and three dogs

Seen on: Facebook — Grant’s own videos from her counter, shared by dozens of people, as reported by The Washington Post.

As record rain flooded Palm Bay, Fla., Brittany Grant and her 11-year-old son climbed onto their kitchen counter while water rose almost to bed height. When 911 said crews were too busy to come right away, she posted videos on Facebook: “We’re OK right now… I don’t know what’s going to happen.” Neighbour Stephanie Fanguy saw the posts and sent her sons (The Washington Post via The Spokesman-Review, WKMG News 6 ClickOrlando).

Lucas Marshall, 17, paddled over on an inflatable paddleboard, and his brother Jonah, 15, followed in a borrowed kayak. They ferried the boy, then Azzy, Delilah the Great Dane and Diamond about 100 yards to dry ground, and then brought Grant out. Lucas stayed out checking on other neighbours. “My faith in humanity has been restored,” Grant said (The Washington Post, Fox News, WKMG).

Why it matters: In a disaster, a neighbour seeing a Facebook post and two teenagers with a paddleboard got help there faster than the overloaded emergency system could.
