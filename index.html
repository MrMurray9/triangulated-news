Alberta

Edmonton has three months to bring back its old house-size and parking rules under a provincial order — and builders mid-permit don’t know where they stand

An order in council published Thursday directs Edmonton to change its small-scale residential (RS) zone within three months so that density, height and parking match the bylaw in force until the end of 2023, then write a new zoning bylaw the minister approves within 18 months. Calgary must restore its low-density districts within three months and finish a new land-use bylaw by Sept. 1, 2027 (Edmonton Journal, Medicine Hat News, CTV News).

Municipal Affairs Minister Dan Williams calls it a “pause and reset.” Mayor Andrew Knack said Friday the order “will create real harm,” costing construction jobs and limiting affordable housing, and that he will meet Premier Danielle Smith later this month. A local investment manager told the Journal at least 15 of his clients have spent hundreds of thousands of dollars on permits and drawings that may now be wasted. Council takes up the order Tuesday.

Why it matters: The rules decide what can be built on an ordinary Edmonton lot — row houses and fourplexes versus single homes — which affects rents, neighbourhood change and trades jobs.

Sources (3)
  • Edmonton Journal — Investors in limbo as mayor cautions infill edict will cost jobs
  • Medicine Hat News — Alberta’s orders in council: municipal land use directed
  • CTV News — Province provides Edmonton, Calgary with infill regulation
Community Note

The province first said in early October it would force the change; what is new is the published order with its deadlines. How projects already in the permit pipeline will be treated is still unclear. The province says the reset answers residents’ concerns about infill on their streets; the city says the 2024 bylaw was meant to add housing near transit.

Sources: Edmonton Journal; Medicine Hat News; CTV News.

World

Greece passes a law keeping children under 15 off social media from Jan. 1

Greek MPs passed the bill Thursday with votes from the governing New Democracy party alone; SYRIZA, the Communists and other opposition parties voted against and PASOK abstained. Platforms have until Jan. 1, 2027, to block under-15 accounts, using age checks such as the government’s “Kids Wallet” app, which Digital Minister Dimitris Papastergiou says reveals only whether a user is over or under 15 (DW, AFP, Anadolu).

“We owe our children their childhood,” Papastergiou said. Enforcement falls on the platforms, not on children or parents, and runs through the EU’s Digital Services Act, which allows fines of up to 6 per cent of global turnover.

Why it matters: Greece joins Australia and a growing list of countries setting age limits — and age-check tools built for kids could end up checking adults too.

Sources (3)
  • DW — Greece passes social media ban for kids under 15
  • AFP — New Greece law bans social media for under-15s
  • Anadolu Agency — Morning briefing, Oct. 24, 2026
Community Note

DW’s report draws partly on AFP and Reuters, so it is not fully independent of AFP. How well age checks work is untested: children elsewhere have got around similar bans. Canada’s own proposal (Bill C-34, under 16) is a separate bill still before Parliament.

Sources: DW; AFP; Anadolu Agency.

Storm Austen leaves one man dead and about 120,000 French homes still without power, mostly in Normandy and Brittany

The season’s first autumn storm hit France’s west coast overnight Thursday into Friday. Grid operator Enedis says it had restored power to 264,000 customers by Saturday morning; 146,000 were still out at 7 a.m. local time and about 120,000 were still out at noon local time, with crews working through the weekend (Ouest-France, Le Parisien, franceinfo).

A man died in the Manche department, franceinfo reported, and three people had minor injuries in the Nord. Fallen trees stopped regional trains in Hauts-de-France on Friday. The storm is moving on toward Belgium, the Netherlands and Germany.

Why it matters: Early-season storms are hitting a Europe already short on energy, and the outages show how exposed overhead power lines still are.

SpaceX is reported to be negotiating for 80,000 U.S. cell sites to become a fourth national phone carrier — and AT&T and T-Mobile shares fall 5%

Bloomberg reported that SpaceX is in talks with tower owner Crown Castle for access to at least 80,000 cell sites, which it would combine with its direct-to-phone Starlink satellites. That is roughly half of the about 158,500 towers in the U.S., Reuters noted (The Verge, Reuters via Investing.com, Yahoo Finance/Bloomberg).

Asked about satellite companies on a call this week, Crown Castle CEO Chris Hillabrant said, “You can assume that we’re talking to all parties.” Crown Castle shares rose about 10 per cent Friday, while AT&T and T-Mobile fell more than 5 per cent and Verizon about 1 per cent.

Why it matters: A fourth big carrier with satellite backup could push U.S. phone prices down — and Starlink already sells service in Canada, where a similar move would shake up Rogers, Bell and Telus.

Science corner

A chip that runs on superconducting circuits keeps pace with a million fusion-plasma readings a second, watching for a meltdown-style collapse

Tokamaks — the doughnut-shaped machines meant to make fusion power — can suffer “disruptions,” when the super-hot plasma suddenly collapses and can damage the machine. Brookhaven National Laboratory, start-up Great Sky and Princeton’s plasma lab built a brain-inspired (neuromorphic) AI chip from superconducting circuits to spot the warning signs (Brookhaven National Laboratory, Scientific American, HPCwire).

They replayed real data from the DIII-D tokamak in San Diego at full speed, one million samples per second, and the chip kept up while flagging anomalies and predicting disruptions. Next comes a version with a neural network 10 times larger, due at Brookhaven early next year and later at DIII-D itself.

Why it matters: If fusion is ever to make electricity, plants need split-second protection against plasma crashes; very fast, low-power chips like this could also run AI at remote sites far from data centres.

Sources (3)
  • Brookhaven National Laboratory — Superconducting chips analyze fusion plasmas
  • Scientific American — New superconducting chip aims to help tame fusion plasma
  • HPCwire — Superconducting neuromorphic chip passes fusion test
Community Note

The chip has not been connected to a running reactor, the results are not yet peer-reviewed, and the team describes it as an early demonstration. Superconducting chips need deep cooling, which adds cost.

Sources: Brookhaven National Laboratory; Scientific American; HPCwire.

AI news and advances

China’s Moonshot releases Kimi K3.1 Pro, an open model it says is nearly as good at coding as the top U.S. systems — after stripping out hacking material first

Kimi K3.1 Pro is available now in Moonshot’s apps and API, with free downloadable weights promised by Nov. 4. Built on the nearly 3-trillion-parameter Kimi K3, it handles up to a million tokens of text at once, and a “Highspeed” version runs two to three times faster (The Next Web, Simon Willison, WebProNews).

Moonshot says it scored 65.7 per cent on the Terminal-Bench 4.0 coding test, just behind Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5 at 66.4. Because open weights can’t be recalled once downloaded, it removed cyberattack training data: on its own test of exploiting real software flaws, the released model solved 81 per cent of tasks versus 90.5 per cent for an untrimmed version.

Why it matters: Free models this close to the best paid ones put pressure on prices — and the hacking cut is a rare case of an open-model lab publicly limiting what it releases.

Sources (3)
  • The Next Web — Moonshot releases Kimi K3.1 Pro and says it cut its hacking skills
  • Simon Willison — Introducing Kimi K3.1 Pro
  • WebProNews — Moonshot removes cyber training data ahead of open-weight release
Community Note

All scores are Moonshot’s own and have not been independently checked. 81 per cent on exploit tasks is still far more capable than its previous model (33 per cent). In July the White House accused Moonshot of using banned Nvidia chips.

Sources: The Next Web; Simon Willison; WebProNews.

OCanadian cliff diver Molly Carlson climbs a floating iceberg off Newfoundland and backflips about 15 metres into the North Atlantic

Seen on: Other — Red Bull’s YouTube video, as reported by The Weather Channel and The Canadian Press
