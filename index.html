Oil money turns Alberta’s forecast $9.4-billion deficit into an $8.5-billion surplus, and the fuel-tax break is extended to March

Finance Minister Jason Nixon released the second-quarter fiscal update Thursday. The province now expects an $8.5-billion surplus this year, about $18 billion better than the deficit in February’s budget and up from the $2-billion surplus it forecast in August (Edmonton Journal; The Globe and Mail; Government of Alberta).

The main reason is oil. Prices jumped after the U.S.–Iran war began, and resource revenue is now expected to reach a record of about $28.6 billion. Every US$1 change in the price of West Texas Intermediate oil moves the budget by about $680 million (The Globe and Mail).

The 13-cent-a-litre provincial fuel tax stays suspended until the end of March 2027. Nixon ruled out another $100 rebate, saying the money will go to one-time costs, roads, schools, health care and savings (Edmonton Journal; MyLethbridgeNow).

Why it matters: A surplus this size shapes the next budget and the 2027 election. It also depends on oil prices that could fall as quickly as they rose.

Sources (4)

  • The Globe and Mail: Alberta projects $8.5-billion surplus as global turmoil drives up oil prices
  • MyLethbridgeNow: Nixon cautions against complacency as Alberta projects $8.5-billion surplus
  • Government of Alberta: 2026–27 Second Quarter Fiscal Update and Economic Statement
Community Note

The surplus is a forecast, not a final result. The province’s numbers assume WTI averages about US$74 a barrel for the rest of the fiscal year (Edmonton Journal).

Spending is also up about $0.6 billion from the budget, partly for referendum costs, wildfires and floods.

Sources: Edmonton Journal; The Globe and Mail; Government of Alberta

Nenshi says the NDP will argue in court that Alberta’s new riding map breaks the Charter right to vote, with papers filed in the coming days

What changed since the last edition: This morning’s edition reported that the map bill passed its final debate and the NDP planned to sue. At a news conference Thursday, Naheed Nenshi laid out the case: a Charter challenge under Section 3 aimed at both how the map was drawn and what it produces. The government said it will defend the law.

Nenshi said the challenge rests on two points: the unusual process, in which a government-led committee redrew the lines after rejecting the independent commission’s map, and a result he says doesn’t give voters effective representation (Canada’s National Observer; Edmonton Journal).

“Danielle Smith and the UCP have left us no choice but to go to court,” Nenshi said. He wouldn’t say what remedy the party will ask for, and said the court filing will be made public in the coming days (CityNews; MyLethbridgeNow).

Justice Minister Mickey Amery said effective representation is “not just population growth or favouring major urban centres” and added, “We’ll defend our legislation” (Canada’s National Observer).

Why it matters: The next election is set for Oct. 18, 2027. If a court rules against the map with little time left, it could have to decide which boundaries are used.

Sources (4)
  • Canada’s National Observer: Alberta NDP launching legal challenge against new electoral map
  • Edmonton Journal: Alberta NDP to challenge province’s new electoral maps in court
  • CityNews Calgary: ‘Left us no choice’: Alberta NDP says it will launch legal challenge
  • MyLethbridgeNow: Nenshi says Alberta’s new electoral map violates Charter rights
Community Note

Nothing has been filed yet. Bill 40 was still awaiting royal assent Thursday morning, and the map takes effect when the next election is called.

Courts have accepted some differences in riding sizes before. Canada’s Supreme Court said in 1991 that the Charter protects “effective representation,” not exact equality of voters.

Hackers took names and email addresses from Toronto-based 1Password after breaking into an employee’s GitHub account, but not passwords

1Password said it began investigating on Nov. 19 after spotting signs that an employee’s corporate GitHub account had been taken over. The attacker used it to push malicious code to backend servers and download some customer account records (TechCrunch; BleepingComputer).

The stolen data includes names, sign-in email addresses, when accounts were created and last used, and tokens from emailed invitation and account-recovery links. The company says vaults, passwords and secret keys were “at no point” available to the attacker (1Password; The Verge).

1Password says only a small number of users were affected and have been notified. It has made its email verification codes longer, 10 letters and numbers instead of six digits, and is not asking customers to change their passwords (BleepingComputer).

Why it matters: Millions of people and more than 200,000 businesses trust 1Password with their passwords. Even without vault access, stolen names and emails make convincing phishing emails easier.

Community Note

1Password hasn’t said how many customers were affected. The attacker also took internal source code, which the company says it has secured.

If you use 1Password, be wary of emails asking you to “recover” or “verify” your account, and sign in through the app instead of an email link.

Doug Ford and Unifor team up to ask Ottawa to waive tailpipe-emission penalties for carmakers that build in Canada

In a rare joint statement Thursday, Ontario Premier Doug Ford and Unifor president Lana Payne asked the federal government to exempt Canadian automakers from penalties under its passenger-vehicle greenhouse gas rules (The Globe and Mail; Government of Ontario).

Companies that miss fleet targets can buy credits from other carmakers. Ford and Payne say that forces Canadian plants to pay companies that don’t build here, calling it an “own goal” that helps President Trump’s aim of putting “every single Canadian autoworker out of a job” (The Canadian Press via Toronto Sun).

They want any exemption tied to a promise to keep production in Canada, including reversing the sale of Stellantis’s Brampton Assembly Plant to armoured-vehicle maker Roshel (The Globe and Mail).

Why it matters: A union and a Conservative premier asking for the same thing adds pressure on Ottawa, as U.S. tariffs and plant closures squeeze Ontario’s auto jobs.

Net migration to the U.K. drops to 77,000, its lowest level since 1997 outside the pandemic

The Office for National Statistics estimates net migration was 77,000 in the year to June 2026, down from 204,000 a year earlier. About 738,000 people arrived and 661,000 left (ONS; The Guardian).

The ONS says the drop came mainly from fewer people arriving from outside the EU for work, study and asylum, after tighter visa rules for workers, students and their families (ONS; ITV News).

It is the lowest figure since 1997 apart from the Covid years, when travel was restricted, a target several governments promised but didn’t reach (ITV News; Reuters).

Why it matters: Immigration is one of Britain’s hottest political issues. Lower numbers please many voters, but economists warn they can mean fewer workers and less tax revenue.

Sources (4)
  • Office for National Statistics: Long-term international migration, provisional, year ending June 2026
  • The Guardian: Net migration to UK falls to lowest level since 1997 excluding pandemic
  • ITV News: UK net migration falls to 77,000
  • Reuters world news summary (via Devdiscourse): UK net migration falls to almost three-decade low
Community Note

These are provisional estimates that the ONS often revises, sometimes by tens of thousands.

Net migration is arrivals minus departures. Emigration stayed high, which is part of why the net figure fell so far.

Sources: ONS; The Guardian; ITV News; Reuters

Marlboro maker Altria says it may make an all-cash bid for Imperial Brands, and Imperial’s shares jump as much as 17%

U.S. tobacco company Altria said Thursday it is considering a possible offer for Imperial Brands, the British maker of Winston, Davidoff and Gauloises cigarettes and Rizla papers. It said any offer would likely be all cash (Altria; Alliance News via Morningstar).

Altria is also talking with an unnamed third party about buying part of Imperial’s business. Bloomberg reported Japan Tobacco is also weighing a bid (CNBC).

Imperial’s shares rose as much as 17.6% in London, valuing it at about £26.8 billion. Under U.K. takeover rules, Altria has until 5 p.m. London time on Dec. 24 to make a firm offer or walk away (CNBC; Altria).

Why it matters: A deal would be one of the biggest tobacco mergers in years, as cigarette sales fall and companies look for size and new products.

Science corner: Delft physicists finally control a qubit made from Majorana particles, after 15 years of trying

Quantum computers are fragile: their bits, called qubits, lose information at the slightest disturbance. One long-sought fix is to store information in pairs of exotic “Majorana” states that sit apart from each other, so local noise can’t easily scramble them.

Researchers at QuTech in the Netherlands built tiny chains of quantum dots that host four such states and, for the first time, made the qubit they form flip back and forth on command between its two states. They published the results in Nature on Nov. 25 (QuTech; Nature).

The two states oscillated at frequencies that matched within about 5%, a sign the Majorana states were well isolated, as theory predicts (Nature; XenoSpectrum).

Why it matters: Majorana qubits are one of the big bets for building quantum computers that don’t need huge amounts of error correction. This is the first working step from detecting them to computing with them.

Sources (4)
  • Nature: Coherent parity oscillations in coupled minimal Kitaev chains
  • QuTech: QuTech demonstrates a qubit based on Majorana quasiparticles
  • XenoSpectrum: QuTech demonstrates a qubit built from Majorana modes
  • Qubitwire: QuTech demonstrates coherent control of a Majorana qubit
Community Note

These are “poor man’s” Majoranas in very short chains, so they don’t yet have the built-in protection that makes Majoranas attractive. The qubit lost its state within tens of nanoseconds.

Majorana claims have a rocky history, including retracted papers. This result is peer-reviewed, and the team says longer chains are the next test.

Sources: Nature; QuTech; XenoSpectrum; Qubitwire

New York outlaws toys with built-in AI chatbots for five years, just as holiday shopping begins

Gov. Kathy Hochul signed a law banning the manufacture, distribution and sale of children’s toys with built-in AI chatbots, such as talking dolls, stuffed animals and robots, for five years (Governor of New York; NBC News).

The ban starts 90 days after signing, on Feb. 22, 2027. The state attorney general can seek penalties of up to $15,000 a day for each violation (Governor of New York; CBS6 Albany).

New York is the second state to ban the toys, after California. Supporters cited reports of AI toys having inappropriate conversations with children and encouraging kids to get emotionally attached (NBC News; The Toy Association).

Why it matters: AI toys are a fast-growing holiday gift. A ban in a state as large as New York pushes toymakers to rethink products sold across North America.

Sources (4)
  • Governor of New York: Governor Hochul signs five-year ban on AI chatbot toys
  • NBC News: New York governor Kathy Hochul signs AI toy ban into law
  • CBS6 Albany: Gov. Hochul signs five-year ban on AI chatbot toys in New York
  • The Toy Foundation: New York governor signs AI chatbot toy bill into law
Community Note

The law was signed Tuesday, Nov. 24. AI toys already on shelves can still be sold in New York until the ban starts in February.

The ban covers toys with embedded chatbots, not ordinary electronic toys that play recorded phrases.

Sources: Governor of New York; NBC News; CBS6 Albany; The Toy Foundation

OFans are picking favourites among SeaWorld’s jump-roping toy soldiers, and the joke “lore” videos were made by one of the soldiers

Seen on: Instagram Reels and TikTok, where @wejumprope_memes’ top video passed 1.2 million views (WIRED; Know Your Meme).

For two weeks, short videos about the masked toy-soldier jump-rope troupe at SeaWorld Orlando have filled people’s feeds. An AI narrator reads silly stats for each soldier, like Holidave, who can “jump rope while sitting down,” Jackery Jingleson, Big Chestnut and Sugarsnookie, as if they were pro athletes (WIRED).

The page behind them is run by Devin Meek, who plays Holidave. He told WIRED he made the first video on Nov. 13 to make his coworkers laugh: “I really just made these videos for my friends in the jump-roping community.”

The made-up lore is based on real skills. Jackery Jingleson once held the U.S. record for fastest jump roper, and the performers who play Big Chestnut and Sugarsnookie really are a couple. “The people are real. The athleticism is real,” Meek said (WIRED).

Why it matters: It’s a funny, harmless trend, and it rewards real athletes in a niche sport who usually perform in costume with no fame at all.

Sources (2)

  • Know Your Meme: Jump Rope Toy Soldiers
Community Note

This light item rests on two outlets, WIRED, which interviewed Meek, and Know Your Meme. The narration is AI-made and the stats are jokes, but the troupe and performers are real.

Meek says it isn’t a SeaWorld ad campaign, and SeaWorld didn’t comment to WIRED.

Sources: WIRED; Know Your Meme
