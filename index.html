Alberta

No leadership review before the next election, Smith’s party says, as would-be board members must pledge to respect the stay-in-Canada vote

The governing party’s president, Rob Smith, told members in an email that at least 65 of its 87 constituency associations have declared support for Premier Danielle Smith, so there will be “NO UCP leadership review before our next general election.” Under party rules, 22 associations would have to ask for one. The Bonnyville-Cold Lake-St. Paul association voted for a review on Monday, the fourth to do so (The Globe and Mail, The Canadian Press).

At the same time, anyone running for the provincial board at the annual meeting, which opens Nov. 27 in Calgary, must now sign a form saying they “recognize, respect, and will abide by” the result of Question 10 in the referendum, in which Albertans chose to stay in Canada. The premier said the party is “not an independence party,” adding: “There’s lots of range for people to be able to advocate on policy, but just not that.” Two separatist-leaning board hopefuls told reporters they will sign (The Canadian Press, The Globe and Mail).

Why it matters: It settles, for now, whether the premier faces an internal vote before the next election, and it draws a firm line against separatist organizers inside the party that governs Alberta.

The 65-association count comes from the party itself, and members who want a review dispute the process. The pledge covers board candidates and party officials; it is a party rule, not a law, and does not stop ordinary members from holding separatist views.

Canada

B.C. NDP picks an interim leader this week after Eby quits over the snap-election loss

David Eby resigned as NDP leader on Saturday after the B.C. Conservatives won a 63-seat supermajority in the snap election he called. The NDP lost 19 seats. The outgoing 47-member caucus meets Wednesday and the new, smaller 28-member caucus meets Thursday to choose an interim leader, who will lead the Official Opposition (CTV News, Victoria News, Rob Shaw in North Shore News).

Names in circulation include Bowinn Ma, Adrian Dix and Mike Farnworth, along with Josie Osborne, Niki Sharma and Ravi Parmar. Party rules bar the interim leader from running for the permanent job, and there is open anger inside the party over the decision to go to the polls early (CTV News, Victoria News, North Shore News).

Why it matters: B.C. now has a Conservative supermajority next door to Alberta, and the shape of the opposition there will affect pipeline, resource and trade fights that matter to Albertans.

World

Two high-speed trains collide in central Spain, killing at least 30 people

A Renfe AVE train travelling from Madrid to Seville hit a stopped Iryo high-speed train near Urda, in Toledo province, at about 8:54 a.m. local time on Wednesday. More than 500 people were on board the two trains. Castilla-La Mancha’s regional president, Emiliano García-Page, said at least 30 people were killed and about 50 were seriously hurt, with roughly 80 injured in all (AP, Cadena SER, The Guardian, Reuters).

Rescuers were still working through the most damaged carriages, and officials warned the toll could rise. Spanish media report investigators are looking at the signalling system. High-speed service between Madrid, Ciudad Real and Andalusia is suspended. It is Spain’s second deadly high-speed crash this year, after the Adamuz derailment in January (AP, Cadena SER, Euronews).

Why it matters: It is one of Europe’s worst rail disasters in years, and a second major crash in one year will put Spain’s high-speed safety record, long seen as one of the world’s best, under heavy scrutiny.

With most ballots counted, Netanyahu’s bloc rises to 54 seats but is still short of a majority

What changed: Last evening’s exit polls had Netanyahu’s camp at about 50 seats, behind the opposition. The real count now has his bloc ahead at 54, but still seven short of the 61 needed.

With about 84.5 per cent of ballot boxes counted, partial results from Israel’s Central Elections Committee give Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc 54 of 120 seats, Gadi Eisenkot’s bloc 49 and the Arab-led Joint List and Ra’am 17. A government needs 61. Turnout was 77.7 per cent, up from 70.6 per cent in 2022 (Times of Israel, Ynet, WION).

About 600,000 double-envelope ballots, mostly from soldiers, are counted last and may not be finished until Friday. Netanyahu says he will form a right-wing government; Eisenkot’s camp can only reach 61 with Arab-party support, which some of his partners have ruled out (Times of Israel, Ynet).

Bank of Canada expected to raise its key rate to 2.5 per cent this morning, its first hike in three years

Most economists in a Reuters poll, 19 of 34, expect the central bank to lift its overnight rate by a quarter point to 2.50 per cent when it announces at 7:45 a.m. MDT. It would be the first increase since July 2023, as an oil-driven jump in prices pushes inflation up (Reuters, The Globe and Mail, The Canadian Press).

Markets were pricing roughly an 80 per cent chance of a hike, and a slim majority of economists in the poll expect another by the end of March. The U.S. Federal Reserve announces its own decision this afternoon (Reuters, The Globe and Mail).

Why it matters: A hike would raise costs for variable-rate mortgages and lines of credit in Alberta, even as higher oil prices support the provincial economy.

Science corner

Engineers flip a material’s magnetism with a shaped laser beam alone, no magnet needed

A team at the University of California San Diego focused an ultrafast laser into a tiny, specially shaped spot and used it to reverse the magnetic direction in a thin stack of platinum and cobalt layers. No outside magnetic field was needed, and the trick worked regardless of the light’s polarization (UC San Diego, Optica, EurekAlert).

It worked in a nine-layer stack, beyond the roughly three-layer limit of earlier all-optical methods. The researchers estimate this kind of switching could be more than 1,000 times faster than using magnetic fields. The work is published in Nature Communications (UC San Diego, Optica, ScienceDaily).

Why it matters: Hard drives and some new memory chips store data as tiny magnetic directions; flipping them with light instead of magnets could make storage far faster and more efficient.

Sources (4)

  • Optica, Optics & Photonics News — Shaped light switches magnetic information
  • EurekAlert! (AAAS) — News release
  • ScienceDaily — Release
Community Note

This is a lab demonstration. The 1,000-times speed figure is the team’s own estimate, and fitting lasers onto everyday chips is a major hurdle still ahead.

Sources: UC San Diego; Optica; EurekAlert!.

AI news and advances

Apple adds an AI assistant to Keynote, Pages and Numbers and lets outside AI agents edit your documents

Apple announced a beta assistant inside its office apps, built on Apple’s own models and OpenAI’s, that can draft text, build slides and work with spreadsheet data. It also added support for the Model Context Protocol, an open standard that lets third-party AI agents read and edit documents on Macs running macOS 27 or later (Apple, The Verge, 9to5Mac).

Keynote gains a new open file format, and the update also brings generative fill to Pixelmator Pro and an assistant to Logic Pro. It arrives in November as a free update for Apple Creator Studio subscribers, in U.S. English first (Apple, iThinkDiff, The Verge).

Why it matters: Opening Apple’s apps to outside AI agents is a big shift for a company known for closed software, and it brings AI help into tools many schools and families already use.

FA Newfoundland town grabs buckets, hoses and the mayor’s excavator to push two stranded whales back to sea

Seen on: Facebook, in photos and video posted by resident Matt Lamkin, credited by VOCM.

When two pilot whales beached themselves at Point May, on Newfoundland’s Burin Peninsula, on Sunday, about 20 residents spent hours keeping them wet with buckets and hoses. Mayor Darren Harris brought his excavator to dig a channel, and a fishing boat helped guide the animals out (CTV News, VOCM, NTV).

The male swam away and appeared healthy. Sadly, the female washed ashore again and died. Residents said they were proud the community came together to give both a chance (CTV News, NTV, VOCM).

Why it matters: It is a reminder of how small towns pull together, and a true story behind photos and video shared widely on Facebook this week.
