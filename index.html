Alberta

UCP MLA Shane Getson quits the legislature effective immediately, hours before the fall sitting opens

Shane Getson, the United Conservative MLA for Lac Ste. Anne–Parkland since 2019, resigned his seat on Monday, citing personal and family reasons. He said he could no longer give the job the time and attention it needs. The legislature reconvenes Tuesday afternoon (Edmonton Journal, CityNews/Canadian Press, Global News).

Getson, a former caucus whip, was known as one of the government’s more outspoken backbenchers. Because the vacancy falls in the assembly’s final year, a byelection is not required; the next provincial election is set for Oct. 18, 2027. The sitting is expected to cover ticket-sale protections, auto insurance changes and rules for AI data centres (Edmonton Journal, Western Standard).

Why it matters: His constituents west of Edmonton may go nearly a year without an MLA, and the sitting opens with the government one seat lighter, though still with a clear majority.

Coun. Anne Stevenson steps off Edmonton’s police commission to focus on prisoners released into Chinatown

Ward O-day’min councillor Anne Stevenson resigned from the Edmonton Police Commission effective Oct. 21, after five years as one of council’s two members. The commission announced it Friday (Edmonton Police Commission, Edmonton Journal).

She told Postmedia she wants to spend her time on how provincial jails release people, after video showed corrections staff dropping inmates in Chinatown parking lots. “I think it is underpinning the crises that we’re continuing to see in the downtown, in Chinatown,” she said. Council has not yet named her replacement (Edmonton Journal, CTV News, CityNews).

Why it matters: Prisoner drop-offs are a big part of the downtown safety debate, and a councillor now plans to make them her main focus, while council has a seat to fill on the body that oversees city police.

Canada

Ottawa and Washington are working on a deal to ease U.S. steel and auto tariffs before next week’s midterms, sources say

Two U.S. and four Canadian sources told Reuters the countries are negotiating relief from Section 232 tariffs, including duties of up to 50 per cent on Canadian steel and 25 per cent on autos. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc have been in regular contact (Reuters).

Global News’ sources say talks are “moving quickly” at the political level, though some doubt a deal can close before the Nov. 3 vote. Prime Minister Mark Carney said there had been “a slight intensification,” adding: “To the extent any progress has been made, it’s because the US is coming closer to our position” (Global News, AP).

Why it matters: Steel and auto tariffs hit Canadian factories directly, and Trump has said both rise to 50 per cent on Jan. 1. Any relief would matter for jobs in Ontario and beyond.

Sources (3)
  • Reuters (via Investing.com) — US, Canada working on deal to ease steel, auto tariffs, sources say
  • Global News — Canada-U.S. trade talks ‘moving quickly’ ahead of midterms
  • AP — Carney says Trump moving closer to Canada’s trade demands
Community Note

No deal exists yet. The reporting relies on unnamed sources, and U.S. sources said Washington still wants more concessions. Talks broke down once before, in August. Asked how he knows Trump would stick to a new deal, Carney said simply: “I don’t.”

Sources: Reuters; Global News; AP.

A tornado rips apart workers’ shelters at a construction site in Antalya, Turkey, killing four

The tornado struck a building site in the Fevzi Çakmak neighbourhood of Antalya’s Kepez district around 11 p.m. Monday, tearing apart two containers where five workers were staying and toppling a tower crane. Two died at the scene and two in hospital; the fifth is in good condition (Daily Sabah/Anadolu, AP).

Governor Hulusi Şahin called it “a very unusual natural phenomenon,” saying no other tornado was reported in the city and nearby buildings were not damaged. A yellow warning for heavy rain was in effect. Officials said three of the dead were Syrian nationals and one was Turkish, and prosecutors have opened an investigation (Daily Sabah, AP, Washington Post).

Guns fall silent in occupied Oleshky as the Melania Trump–brokered truce takes effect and holds

What changed: Our Oct. 23 edition reported the deal. The ceasefire is now in force, both sides say it is being respected, and the aid mission has started.

A local ceasefire began at 3 a.m. Kyiv time on Tuesday in Oleshky, a Russian-occupied town in Ukraine’s Kherson region, so aid can get in, roads can be demined and civilians can leave. Hours later it appeared to be holding, with both sides still fighting elsewhere (NBC News, Interfax-Ukraine).

“An important humanitarian mission has begun in Oleshky,” wrote Kyrylo Budanov, head of the Ukrainian president’s office. Ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets is on site to coordinate. The UN says no food has reached the town since May; about 2,000 people remain. A Melania Trump adviser said the operation should take five to seven days (NBC News, Interfax-Ukraine, RBC-Ukraine).

Israeli turnout hits its highest midday level in decades as voters pick a new Knesset

By noon Tuesday, 31.5 per cent of Israel’s roughly 7.3 million eligible voters had cast ballots, about 2.3 million people, up from 28.4 per cent at the same point in 2022 (Times of Israel, Ynet, Kan).

Science corner

Living heart cells power their own electronics: UMass Amherst’s “biohybrid mesh” turns each heartbeat into electricity

Engineers at UMass Amherst built an ultraflexible polymer mesh threaded with thin ribbons of PZT, a piezoelectric ceramic that makes a small voltage when it bends. They grew human heart-muscle cells into the mesh, and every time the tissue contracted, the ribbons flexed and produced current (UMass Amherst, Science Advances).

Because the harvesters are spread through the tissue instead of sitting in one spot, the team, led by Jun Yao, reports power density about ten times higher than centralized designs, and the thin films can be stacked for more output (UMass Amherst, New Atlas).

Why it matters: Pacemakers and implanted sensors still rely on batteries that must be replaced surgically. Tissue that powers its own electronics could one day remove that step.

Sources (3)
  • UMass Amherst — The Body Electric: researchers integrate electronics with human cells
  • Science Advances — A biohybrid mesh harvester for distributed energy harvesting in living tissues
  • New Atlas — Bio-hybrid harvester generates electricity from human cells
Community Note

So far this has only worked in lab-grown heart tissue, not in animals or people. PZT contains lead, so any implant would need to be fully sealed and proven safe first, and that is likely years away.

Sources: UMass Amherst; Science Advances; New Atlas.

AI news and advances

Privacy group noyb files five complaints accusing Scale AI of spying on the gig workers who train AI models

The Vienna-based group noyb filed GDPR complaints Tuesday in Austria, France, the Netherlands and Italy against Scale AI’s Outlier platform, which pays freelancers to write and rate answers used to train AI chatbots. It says up to 19 trackers log workers’ keystrokes and mouse movements every few seconds, and that workers must repeatedly pass face-video and ID checks (noyb, Euronews/AP, Reuters).

Researchers from the DiPLab project found the data could be rebuilt into something like a video of a work session. “Why should LinkedIn or Facebook know which tasks you’re working on?” said noyb lawyer Lisa Steinfeld Leiser. Four workers who asked for their data got no reply. Meta owns 49 per cent of Scale, whose clients have included OpenAI and Google (Euronews, Reuters).

Why it matters: Hundreds of thousands of people do this hidden work behind chatbots. The case tests whether Europe’s privacy law protects them from constant monitoring.

Sources (3)
  • noyb — AI training platform Outlier illegally surveils European platform workers
  • Euronews (with AP) — Scale AI accused of spying on AI trainers
  • Reuters (via CNA) — Privacy group files complaints against Scale AI over alleged worker surveillance
Community Note

These are allegations from an advocacy group, not findings; national data regulators will decide. Scale AI did not immediately respond to requests for comment. noyb is asking for fines.

Sources: noyb; Euronews/AP; Reuters.
