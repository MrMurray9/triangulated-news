Alberta

Yes leads on eight of the nine other ballot questions — about 55 per cent want more provincial say over immigration, while the constitutional questions split almost down the middle

Counting moved on Tuesday from the separation question to the nine questions Premier Danielle Smith’s government added to the ballot. With 84 per cent of polls reporting at noon, “yes” was ahead on all but one, the Globe and Mail reports. About 55 per cent backed Alberta taking more control of immigration, and about 57 per cent supported limiting some services for newcomers or charging them fees. On the constitutional questions — such as provinces naming superior-court judges or abolishing the Senate — support ranged from 49 to 53 per cent (Globe and Mail, Elections Alberta, CityNews Calgary).

On Question 10 itself, the unofficial tally stood at 1,418,912 votes to remain in Canada and 461,503 for starting the process toward a separation vote. The picture was much closer in parts of the countryside: Central Peace–Notley (5,275 to 5,068) and Rimbey–Rocky Mountain House–Sundre (12,459 to 12,098) narrowly backed the separation-process option, while Smith’s own Brooks–Medicine Hat riding chose remain, 13,842 to 8,073 (Calgary Herald).

“There really aren’t any legal consequences flowing directly from the outcome of these nine questions,” University of Calgary law professor Shaun Fluker told the Globe. Smith has said she’ll use the results to push for talks with Ottawa and the other provinces. She addresses Albertans at 6:45 p.m. tonight.

Why it matters: The nine questions are what Smith plans to take to Ottawa, and several are close enough that the final count matters.

What changed since the last edition: This morning’s edition had only the Question 10 result, with about 74 per cent of locations counted. New this evening: the first counts on Questions 1 to 9, updated Question 10 vote totals, and riding results showing two rural ridings narrowly backing the separation-process option.

A yes vote on Questions 1 to 9 doesn’t change any law by itself; most of them ask the government to seek changes that would need Ottawa or other provinces to agree.

Canada

The federal budget lands Nov. 5 — and on the same day, the budget watchdog says deficits will stay above $63 billion a year into the next decade

Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne announced the date during question period on Tuesday. “This plan will unlock investment, create high-paying jobs across our nation, and strengthen our economy and our country for generations to come,” he said. It will be the second budget under Prime Minister Mark Carney, who said last month his government is on track to balance its operating budget by 2027-28 (The Canadian Press, The Globe and Mail).

Hours earlier, the Parliamentary Budget Officer released an outlook that’s less rosy. It projects a $63.3-billion deficit this fiscal year, peaking at $70.2 billion in 2027-28 and staying above $63 billion through 2030-31. It also trims its economic growth forecast because of the new U.S. tariffs and Canada’s counter-measures, and says Ottawa will miss its goal of a falling deficit-to-GDP ratio (PBO, BNN Bloomberg, CP24). Defence spending is set to climb from about $75 billion a year to $95.7 billion by 2030-31, the PBO says.

In Calgary, Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre said “all that matters is you… whether you’re saving money, whether your job is saved” (CP24).

Why it matters: The budget will show how Ottawa plans to pay for higher defence spending and tariff relief — and whether it still expects to balance day-to-day spending on schedule.

World

Eastern Libya’s parliament reverses itself and ratifies Turkey’s 2019 sea-boundary pact — Greece says it breaks the law of the sea, and the EU stands with Athens and Cyprus

The Tobruk-based House of Representatives, aligned with military commander Khalifa Haftar, voted unanimously on Tuesday to approve the 2019 memorandum that carved out maritime zones between Turkey and Libya in the eastern Mediterranean. It also repealed its own 2020 decisions rejecting the deal and cutting political ties with Ankara (AP, Al Jazeera/Reuters, Libyan News Agency).

Greece’s foreign ministry said the memorandum violates the international law of the sea and infringes the sovereign rights of other states, and that Athens will take “all appropriate measures” to protect its rights. European Council President António Costa and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen offered “solidarity” to Greece and Cyprus (Hellenic Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Cyprus Mail). The AP reports the vote revives the dispute with Egypt, too. “Some countries may be upset by this agreement, but what matters to us is our national interest,” eastern lawmaker Tarek al-Jeroushi said (Al Jazeera/Reuters).

The vote caps a turnaround: Turkish drones and advisers helped stop Haftar’s forces at the gates of Tripoli in 2019–20, but Ankara has spent the past year building military ties with his camp, including a July deal on military facilities (Al Jazeera/Reuters).

Why it matters: The deal overlaps waters Greece claims off Crete, so it reopens a fight involving two NATO members, Egypt and the EU.

Business

SpaceX books its first profit as a public company — US$4.4 billion — as renting computing power to AI labs overtakes Starlink as its biggest business

SpaceX’s third-quarter revenue jumped 161 per cent to US$14.6 billion, well above the US$12.7 billion analysts expected, and it earned US$4.4 billion after losing US$1.1 billion a year earlier, the company reported after the bell Tuesday (SpaceX filing, Reuters, Wall Street Journal).

The surprise was its AI unit: revenue there rose more than ninefold to US$7.6 billion, mostly from renting out data-centre capacity to developers such as Anthropic, Google and Reflection AI rather than from its own models. That unit also swallowed US$16.4 billion of the company’s US$19.6 billion in capital spending. Starlink’s side of the business grew 69 per cent to US$5.2 billion, with 13.6 million consumer subscribers, but average revenue per subscriber slipped to US$69 a month as cheaper plans roll out abroad. The company also flew its first paying Starship mission, carrying 26 larger Starlink satellites (SpaceX filing, Reuters, StockTitan).

“We are in an inflection moment for SpaceX across launch, connectivity and superintelligence,” Elon Musk told investors. Shares rose about 2.5 per cent in after-hours trading (Reuters).

Why it matters: SpaceX is now valued on its AI-cloud business as much as its rockets — and that business needs enormous ongoing spending to keep growing.

Science corner

Science corner: Stanford engineers send data with no radio signal at all — a tag flips how its antenna reflects the faint electrical “static” all around it

Every conductor hums with thermal noise: electrons jiggling enough to make a faint background hiss that radio engineers normally try to filter out. A team led by Zerina Kapetanovic has turned that hiss into a carrier. Their transmitter generates nothing; it just switches its antenna between an open circuit (a one) and a short circuit (a zero). That changes how much noise bounces back to a nearby receiver, which reads the reflection as “hotter” or “colder” and decodes the bits (Stanford University, PNAS).

The puzzle was how this worked with everything at room temperature. The answer, published this month in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, is that the receiver’s amplifier acts like an “electron refrigerator,” making the noise it sends out look cold, so the tag’s reflections stand out. With a proper equation for the link in hand, the team boosted its data rate 100-fold. “We’ve built a system that others could very easily implement,” Kapetanovic said (Stanford via TechXplore, Mirage News).

Why it matters: A transmitter that needs up to 1,000 times less energy than ordinary radio could let tiny battery-free sensors report from farm fields, hospitals or observatories where radio interference is a problem.

Sources (3)
  • Stanford University (via TechXplore) — Background static can carry wireless data without an actively generated radio signal
  • PNAS — First-principles channel model and capacity-achieving transmission scheme in thermal noise backscatter links (Ecola et al., 2026)
  • Mirage News — Thermal noise used to send data without active signal
Community Note

Coverage so far is the peer-reviewed paper and Stanford’s own release, which other sites have republished; we couldn’t find separate reporting by a third independent outlet, so treat the energy-saving figure as the researchers’ claim. The tests ran in a shielded chamber over short distances and at low data rates. The receiver still needs power; only the tag is passive.

Sources: PNAS; Stanford University; TechXplore; Mirage News.

AI news and advances

OpenAI buys Orchestra Bio, a startup whose AI agents track drug trials and flag delays — folding it into its Rosalind life-sciences tools

OpenAI said Tuesday it has acquired Orchestra Bio, whose platform lets drug-company scientists hand off chores like monitoring studies, tracking timelines and spotting knock-on delays — say, a failed manufacturing batch that would push back a clinical trial — to AI agents. Orchestra’s team and technology will join Rosalind, OpenAI’s set of models and tools for biology and chemistry research. Terms weren’t disclosed (OpenAI, Axios, The Verge).

OpenAI notes that a new medicine typically takes 10 to 15 years to go from discovery to U.S. approval. The deal follows rival Anthropic’s push into science, including its purchase of AI biotech startup Coefficient Bio in April for roughly US$400 million, according to The Information, as reported by Axios. Venture firm 8VC, an Orchestra backer, confirmed the deal (8VC).

Why it matters: AI companies are racing to prove their models can speed up real-world science, and drug development — slow and expensive — is the biggest prize.

  • The Verge — OpenAI has acquired Orchestra Bio, a biotech startup
  • 8VC — Orchestra Bio is joining OpenAI

Orchestra’s software manages the logistics of drug programs; it doesn’t discover drugs itself. No price was disclosed, and claims that AI will shorten drug timelines are goals, not yet demonstrated results.

Nvidia releases free-to-download Nemotron 3.5 models — a 120-billion-parameter one for business agents and a “Bolt” model that runs on a 24 GB laptop

Nvidia’s new open-weight Nemotron 3.5 Super VL can read images and documents as well as text and is built for companies running AI agents on their own servers. Nvidia says that once it’s fine-tuned on a specific job — such as insurance claims, accounting or document processing — it beats closed frontier models from OpenAI and Anthropic on those tasks, and it lists Salesforce, CrowdStrike, Perplexity and EY among early users (Nvidia, TechCrunch, VentureBeat).

Two smaller “Bolt” models target PCs: a 74-billion-parameter model squeezed into 24 GB of memory for local coding and browsing agents on Nvidia’s new RTX Spark laptops, and a tiny 4 GB model for chat assistants due later this month. “Organizations can post-train Super VL on their own data, deploy it on their own infrastructure and own what they build,” Nvidia vice-president Kari Briski wrote (Nvidia, TechCrunch).

Why it matters: Running capable AI on your own hardware means sensitive data doesn’t have to leave the building — and it helps Nvidia sell chips as big AI labs build their own.

The models were announced Oct. 15 and are new to this digest. The benchmark wins are Nvidia’s own and apply only after task-specific training, not to general use. “Open-weight” means the trained model can be downloaded and run, not that all training data is published.
