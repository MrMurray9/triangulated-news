Alberta

Elections Alberta says it’s “ready to deliver” as advance referendum voting opens Tuesday; more than 300,000 mail-in ballots are already back

Advance polls for the Oct. 19 referendum open Tuesday at 310 stations and run through Saturday, Oct. 17, from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. Voters must use a station in their own electoral division. “We’ve completed scenario planning, and our teams are prepared to handle any issue that may emerge,” Elections Alberta spokesperson Maia Hanrahan told The Canadian Press. “We are ready to deliver.” As of Friday, the agency had received just over 300,000 completed mail-in ballots out of about 652,000 sent out, and had hired about 44,000 of the at least 60,000 workers it needs.

Elections Alberta is preparing for a record turnout of about 85 per cent of the province’s roughly three million voters. The 10 questions come on separate colour-coded ballots, and votes must be hand-counted within 48 hours. Question 10, on whether to stay in Canada or start the process toward a binding vote on separation, will be counted first, with results expected in the early hours of Oct. 20. Mail-in ballots must reach Elections Alberta by 5 p.m. on Oct. 16.

Why it matters: Advance polls give Albertans five extra days to vote in person, and Elections Alberta is urging people to use them to avoid long lines on Oct. 19.

What changed since the last edition: Last evening’s edition explained the advance-voting rules. Since then, Elections Alberta has reported how many mail-in ballots are back (just over 300,000) and how many workers it has hired (about 44,000).

Fish and Wildlife investigating two grizzly bears shot near Swan Hills and Fox Creek; one had all four paws cut off

Alberta Fish and Wildlife Enforcement Services says officers found a grizzly that had been shot and left beside a lease road running parallel to the Swan River, about 20 kilometres north of Swan Hills, on Sept. 30. The next day, another dead grizzly was reported in the Deep Valley Road area between Little Smoky and Fox Creek. Investigators found it had been shot, its carcass dragged off the main trail, and all four paws removed.

Officers are asking anyone who saw suspicious activity in the Deep Valley Road area between Sept. 25 and Oct. 3 to call the Report-A-Poacher line at 1-800-642-3800. Information leading to charges may be eligible for a cash reward, the agency said.

Why it matters: Grizzly bears are a threatened species in Alberta, and hunting them has been banned in the province since 2006.

Prime Minister Mark Carney spoke with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on Sunday. “The prime minister was clear: this agreement is in direct opposition to the efforts of Ukraine, Canada, and our allies to create peace and stability in Europe,” the Prime Minister’s Office said of the U.S. deal to buy millions of tonnes of Russian diesel. Houthis claim the deadly Riyadh airport attack as new strikes hit its Terminal 4 and Dammam’s airport; death toll rises to 13

In a televised speech Sunday, Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree claimed Saturday’s attack on King Khalid International Airport and said the group had also struck the airport in Dammam and two Saudi Aramco facilities, the Shedgum gas plant and the Tanajib oil site. Hours later, the Saudi-led coalition said an “unidentified projectile” hit Riyadh’s Terminal 4, causing a fire, and that air defences intercepted a cruise missile nearby. Saudi Arabia’s civil aviation authority reported an unspecified number of injuries, and later said King Fahd International Airport in Dammam had also been attacked.

India’s embassy said one of its citizens hurt Saturday had died, raising the toll from that attack to 13, Reuters and AFP reported. The Riyadh airport stayed closed, and Saudi authorities moved all Riyadh schools to remote learning through Thursday. The Houthis said Saudi Arabia carried out 124 airstrikes in Yemen over 24 hours and warned that “worse is yet to come.” The U.S. Federal Aviation Administration told U.S. airlines to use caution in Saudi airspace.

Why it matters: Canada’s embassy has told Canadians to stay away from the Riyadh airport, and attacks on Aramco sites put oil supply at risk, which affects the prices that drive Alberta’s royalties.

What changed since the last edition: This morning’s edition reported 12 dead and 309 hurt, with no one claiming responsibility. Since then, the Houthis have claimed the attack, the toll has risen to 13, and new strikes have hit Riyadh’s Terminal 4 and Dammam’s airport.

Category 4 Hurricane Simon skirts Mexico’s Pacific coast near Manzanillo and Cabo Corrientes; hundreds evacuated in Jalisco and Colima

At 3 p.m. MDT Sunday, Simon’s eye was about 170 km west-northwest of Manzanillo and 90 km south of Cabo Corrientes, with maximum sustained winds near 215 km/h, the U.S. National Hurricane Center said. It was passing near the coast of west-central Mexico and is expected to move a little farther offshore Monday before turning back toward the coast midweek. A hurricane warning is in effect from Punta San Telmo to El Roblito and for the Islas Marías.

Mexico’s civil protection agency said 321 people had been evacuated in Jalisco, including 80 taken from Yelapa by ferry, and 143 people were in shelters in Colima. More than 22,000 federal personnel were deployed, non-essential activities were suspended in 24 municipalities, and preventive measures were put in place at Puerto Vallarta’s airport. Colima officials reported flooding, damaged waterfront businesses and roads blocked by fallen trees.

Why it matters: Puerto Vallarta and Manzanillo are popular with Canadian travellers, and anyone booked there should check with their airline or tour operator.

Science corner

Singapore researchers build a robot that spins down from a drone like a maple seed, then sails away on the wind

Engineers at the Singapore University of Technology and Design built ALBATROSS, short for Airborne Lander with Buoyant AuToROtating Sailing Sensor. Its rigid wingsails autorotate like a maple seed when it’s dropped, slowing its fall. After it lands, its weighted keel rights it and the same wings work as sails. In field trials, it was released from 150 metres, landed on a reservoir, righted itself, then sailed between GPS waypoints for about three hours at up to nearly 1 km/h while logging temperature, humidity and wind direction.

The team estimates a similar platform without autorotation would hit the water with about 18.5 times more impact energy. It uses only three actuators and three navigation sensors. The study appeared in Science Robotics.

Why it matters: Cheap, wind-powered sensor platforms that can be dropped from the air could make it easier to monitor remote waters, such as for climate research or search and rescue.

Sources (4)
  • Singapore University of Technology and Design (via EurekAlert!)
  • heise online
  • The Engineer
  • Tech Edition
Community Note

This is a proof of concept tested only in sheltered reservoir water. The researchers say future versions need to handle stronger winds, currents and waves before going to sea.

Sources: SUTD; heise online.

AI news and advances

Odyssey opens a free public preview of Odyssey-3, an AI “world model” that generates explorable 3D scenes in real time

California-based Odyssey has launched a public research preview of Odyssey-3. Users type a prompt and can move through the generated world in first- or third-person view while the model predicts how the scene responds to their actions. The free browser demo runs the lighter Odyssey-3 Flash version. Developers can apply for API access, and no downloadable model weights have been announced, The Decoder and Quantslant reported.

Odyssey says the base model has 14 billion parameters and that it has used it to control robot arms, a simulated drone and video-game characters. The company, founded in 2023 by Oliver Cameron and Jeff Hawke, raised US$310 million in June from investors including Amazon and AMD Ventures, The Decoder reported.

Why it matters: World models like this one, Google DeepMind’s Genie 3 and World Labs’ technology are seen as a path to training robots and self-driving systems in simulation instead of the real world.
