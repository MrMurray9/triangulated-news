A judge empties Edmonton’s Stadium Plaza apartments, a three-year-old building with no heat after thieves stripped its copper pipes

Tenants of Stadium Plaza, a 78-unit, six-storey apartment block on 86 Street near Commonwealth Stadium, were moving out on Friday after Court of King’s Bench Justice Michael Lema released them from their leases and ordered them out by noon on Nov. 20. Lema called it a “state-of-chaos apartment building” (Global News, Edmonton Journal).

The building went into receivership in August after the owner stopped paying the mortgage in January. The receiver had collected only about $13,000 of an estimated $90,000 in rent since then. Lema listed broken elevators, damaged suites, squatters, gang and drug activity, and thefts of copper pipe that knocked out the heating. Repairs are estimated at about $461,000 and would take until at least mid-February, and the receiver said they couldn’t be done with people living there (Global News, Edmonton Journal, CTV News Edmonton).

Residents told Global News they had been assaulted and had units broken into. Some said they had nowhere to go and no damage deposit to get back. The judge also questioned why the building had never been declared unfit under the Public Health Act.

Why it matters: It shows how fast a nearly new rental building can fall apart, and leaves dozens of low-income Edmontonians looking for housing as winter sets in.

Canada

Regina shuts down its entire bus system from Monday morning, saying a union overtime ban leaves it unable to run safely

All regular Regina Transit routes are out of service as of Monday, Nov. 23, the City of Regina says, citing “an operational safety directive.” Paratransit keeps running (City of Regina, 980 CJME, SaskToday).

The shutdown follows notice from Amalgamated Transit Union Local 588 that its members would stop working overtime from Sunday at 6:15 p.m. An internal city memo shared online says the city can’t keep the minimum staffing, maintenance and oversight needed to run buses without overtime, and suspends service from 3 a.m. Monday. CJME reports the letter also says a lockout of the union’s members takes effect Monday morning (SaskToday, 980 CJME).

The city says it wants a “fair” contract and that, with colder weather, it needed service to be reliable “so that no one is left outside waiting for a ride.”

Why it matters: Thousands of riders in Saskatchewan’s capital, including students and shift workers, lose their buses with no end date as winter arrives.

Sources (3)

  • SaskToday: No transit service as of Monday morning, as labour dispute looms
Community Note

The city’s public notice doesn’t mention the labour dispute. The link to the overtime ban comes from a memo shared on social media and the city’s statements to reporters.

The city says it won’t give more details while bargaining continues.

Becky Druhan, who left Tim Houston’s cabinet last year, becomes the first woman to lead Nova Scotia’s Liberals

Former Progressive Conservative education and justice minister Becky Druhan won the Nova Scotia Liberal leadership Saturday at a convention in Halifax. She beat Cape Breton municipal councillor Amanda Mombourquette with 1,915 of 2,869 ballots, or 68% of points under the party’s weighted system (Global News, CTV News Atlantic, 98.9 XFM).

Druhan was dropped from Premier Tim Houston’s cabinet last fall, sat as an Independent, then joined the Liberals in May. That gave the party a third seat in the legislature, after it fell from 14 seats to two in 2024 and lost official Opposition status to the NDP. “I know Tim Houston, and he doesn’t intimidate me,” she told delegates (Global News).

Why it matters: Nova Scotia’s third party now has a permanent leader for the first time since its 2024 collapse, and she’s a former Houston minister.

Part of an apartment block in Dymer, north of Kyiv, collapses in an overnight attack, killing at least five, as a boy is pulled out alive after about 14 hours

A Russian Shahed drone hit a five-storey apartment building in Dymer, about 45 kilometres north of Kyiv, early on Sunday, collapsing part of it. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy first said 14 people were missing and nine had been evacuated. Through the day, rescuers recovered bodies and officials raised the death toll to at least five, with several more people still feared under the rubble (RFE/RL, Ukrainska Pravda, UNN).

In the evening, rescuers pulled a seven-year-old boy out alive after about 14 hours under the debris, Kyiv regional officials said. More than 130 rescuers worked the site in temperatures just above freezing, with repeated air-raid alerts slowing the search. The Kyiv region declared Monday, Nov. 23, a day of mourning (RFE/RL, UNN, Euronews).

In the same overnight attack, a drone hit a supermarket in Vyshneve, killing two, and strikes killed people in Dnipro and Slovyansk (RFE/RL).

Why it matters: It is one of the deadliest single strikes on a residential building near Kyiv this autumn, as Russia keeps hitting homes and power plants before winter.

Rescuers carry 24 body bags out of the burned candy factory near Jakarta, and police say families must give DNA to identify the dead

What changed since the last edition: This morning’s edition reported three dead and 36 missing. By Sunday evening, local time, rescuers had removed 24 body bags, and police began collecting DNA from families to identify the victims.

Search teams had removed 24 body bags from the burned PT Fonusa Agung Mulia candy factory in Cikande, Serang regency, by 5 p.m. Sunday local time, Serang’s regent said. That was up from seven earlier in the day. Officials still confirm only three deaths, because remains have to be identified before the toll is updated (Jakarta Globe, AP, The Nightly).

Police identification teams said the remains were too badly burned to recognize and asked relatives to give DNA samples. Of 59 workers believed to be inside, 16 had gone home and one was in hospital; the rest were still being traced. Hotspots kept burning, and the search was to resume Monday (Jakarta Globe, AP).

Why it matters: The final toll could make this one of Indonesia’s worst factory fires in years, and raises questions about safety at the industrial estate.

About 2,400 Bombardier workers in Montreal and Dorval reject the company’s “final” offer by 97.5% and vote to allow a strike

Members of the International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers Local 712 at Bombardier rejected the company’s final contract offer by 97.5% on Sunday, then voted 97.8% for a strike mandate at the Palais des congrès in Montreal, the union said (The Canadian Press, Montreal Gazette).

Their contract expires Dec. 4, so a strike would be possible after that. The union says it wants to get back to the table quickly, after 21 bargaining sessions since May, but “will have no choice but to use pressure” if there’s no deal by then. Bombardier called its offer “excellent in every respect” and said it would assess its options while keeping operations running (The Canadian Press, Montreal Gazette, Le Quotidien).

Why it matters: Bombardier is one of Canada’s biggest aerospace employers, and a strike at its Montreal-area plants could slow its business-jet work.

Science corner

Science corner: MIT’s dry, rubbery skin layer lets a wearable ultrasound patch image the body for nine days with no gooey gel

Ultrasound scans usually need a cold, wet gel to carry sound waves from the probe into the body. Engineers at MIT and the Singapore-MIT Alliance for Research and Technology have made a dry, stretchy “bottlebrush” elastomer that does the same job, and they report it this week in the journal Science (MIT News, Science).

Gel dries out within a day or two, which has held back ultrasound patches meant to be worn for days. The new material is soft like skin and has no liquid to lose. In lab tests on a synthetic skin-and-tissue model, it kept images clear for about nine days. In short human tests, a key-fob-sized patch on a volunteer’s neck kept tracking blood flow through a 30-minute bike ride up steep hills and a 10-minute treadmill run (MIT News, Inside Precision Medicine).

“It’s very much like skin,” says MIT researcher Shu Wang. The team, led by mechanical engineering professor Xuanhe Zhao, is aiming at continuous monitoring at home, including for older people.

Why it matters: Gel-free patches could let doctors watch the heart, blood vessels or muscles for days at a time without a technician reapplying gel.

Sources (4)
  • MIT News: Ultrasound imaging without the gel
  • Science: Interpenetrating bottlebrush elastomer for low-loss soft ultrasonic interfaces
  • Inside Precision Medicine: Gel-free material could enable continuous ultrasound monitoring
  • Yahoo Tech: Wearable ultrasound patches may outlast gel
Community Note

The nine-day result was on a model of skin and tissue, not a person. The human tests lasted 10 and 30 minutes.

It’s a research prototype, not an approved medical device.

Sources: MIT News; Science; Inside Precision Medicine; Yahoo Tech

South Korea’s education minister resigns after last-minute rules against AI cheating threw the national university exam into confusion

Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin stepped down Sunday, and President Lee Jae Myung accepted his resignation within about an hour. Choi said changes made just before the Suneung, the college entrance exam held Thursday, caused “inconvenience” to test takers (Yonhap, The Korea Times, The Guardian).

Fears of AI-assisted cheating grew after the top scorer on this year’s tax accountant exam admitted using a tiny camera, a phone and generative AI. The ministry then changed the rules repeatedly in the last days. Students had to cover the buttons on their clothes with stickers to block hidden cameras, many items they had been allowed to bring were banned the day before, and three emergency alerts were sent to phones nationwide during the test to catch anyone who hadn’t handed theirs in. The ministry says 111 suspected cheating cases with electronic devices were found (Yonhap, The Korea Times).

Calls for Choi to quit grew after he told lawmakers that, “perhaps because of my age,” he couldn’t be sure AI cheating would be caught. Choi is 72.

Why it matters: It’s one of the first times a cabinet minister has lost their job over how a government handled AI cheating, and schools everywhere face the same problem.

Signal starts blocking screenshots on phones to keep Siri, Gemini and other AI assistants from reading your chats

The encrypted messaging app Signal is now restricting screenshots on iOS 27 and on Android versions where it can detect an AI assistant trying to read the screen. The aim is to stop features like Apple’s Siri and Google’s Gemini from capturing private messages while they do tasks for users (The Verge, BleepingComputer, Reuters).

Signal president Meredith Whittaker wrote that AI agents built into operating systems are “undermining Signal’s ability to independently guarantee privacy” and called them “an existential threat” to privacy inside apps. She said the fix is “incomplete,” there is no protection on macOS yet, and Apple, Google and Microsoft should give developers clear ways to opt out of AI access. Users can switch screenshots back on, but Signal warns that may expose their chats to AI agents (The Verge, BleepingComputer).

Why it matters: As phone makers build AI that “sees” your screen, apps that promise privacy are starting to push back.

Sources (4)

  • BleepingComputer: Signal to block Siri, Gemini AI from capturing chats on iOS, Android
  • Reuters (via Investing.com): Signal restricts screenshots to block AI agents from reading messages
  • Signal Support: Screen security and blocking Siri AI
Community Note

On iOS, the setting also stops you from taking your own screenshots of Signal chats. It’s on by default, and you can turn it off in Signal’s privacy settings.

Signal took a similar step in 2025 to block Microsoft’s Recall feature on Windows.

OAn empty electric car in China tries to park itself 868 times in one night, and the garage camera catches it all

Seen on: Chinese social media, then widely reshared on X and Instagram, per Times Now and NDTV.

Security-camera video from an underground garage in China’s Anhui province shows an electric car with nobody inside pulling into a spot, adjusting, reversing and trying again, over and over, at about 3 a.m. A guard went to check after hearing the noise.
