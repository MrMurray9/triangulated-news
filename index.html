Alberta

Three Calgary police officers are charged with manslaughter in the 2024 death of Jon Wells, a Blood Tribe man pinned face down in a hotel lobby

Alberta’s police watchdog, ASIRT, said Monday that constables Roy Braun, Filip Poskovic and Vincent Washington were arrested and charged with manslaughter and assault causing bodily harm. They were released on undertakings and the case returns to the Alberta Court of Justice in Calgary on Jan. 6, 2027 (Calgary Herald, APTN News, CityNews).

Wells, 42, a rodeo steer wrestler from the Kainai (Blood Tribe) Nation, died on Sept. 17, 2024, at the Carriage House Hotel on Macleod Trail after staff asked police to remove him from the lobby. ASIRT’s earlier summary of body-camera video says he raised his hands, said he would leave and told the officer “I don’t want to die” before he was grabbed, tackled, punched, Tasered and restrained face down in a spit mask until he stopped responding (APTN News).

“It should be a heavier charge,” his mother, Edith Wells, told APTN. The Calgary Police Service called the charges “extremely serious” and said the three officers are relieved of duty with pay.

Why it matters: It’s rare for Alberta officers to face manslaughter charges, and the case will test how police use force on people in distress.

  • Police Review Commission: Calgary Police Service officers charged in connection with man’s death
Community Note

The charges haven’t been proven in court. Lawyers for all three officers told the Herald their clients deny the allegations and will fight them at trial.

The body-camera video hasn’t been released; the account above comes from ASIRT’s September 2024 summary. ASIRT says its investigation is finished and no more charges are planned. The Globe and Mail notes no fatality inquiry has been called in the case.

Calgary opens a week-long budget hearing on a proposed 15% property tax hike, and one councillor asks staff to show what a 0% budget would cut

More than 260 Calgarians signed up to speak as city council’s public hearing on the 2027–30 budget began Monday. The plan calls for a 15% property tax increase in 2027, then 10%, 7.7% and 6% in the following years (Calgary Herald, Global News, City of Calgary).

Combined with a similar jump in water rates and higher waste fees, the city says a typical home assessed at $688,000 would pay about $53 more a month next year. The plan includes nearly $6 billion in operating spending for 2027 and a $21-billion, four-year capital program.

Ward 1 Coun. Kim Tyers plans to ask administration for a version with no tax increase. Mayor Jeromy Farkas and Ward 7 Coun. Myke Atkinson called that irresponsible; Farkas said 4% to 5% is the minimum to keep services from slipping. Councillors have drafted nearly 70 amendments so far.

Why it matters: Calgary’s first-year hike would be about three times Edmonton’s proposed 4.9%, and both councils are debating their budgets this week.

Sources (3)
  • Calgary Herald: Hundreds signed up to speak at city hall budget public hearing
  • Global News: Calgary budget talks begin as hundreds sign up for public hearing
  • City of Calgary: The City releases proposed 2027–2030 Budget
Community Note

The 15% figure covers only the city’s share. The Herald reports that once the frozen provincial education tax and lower overall assessments are factored in, the city expects the total property tax bill to rise about 6.9% in 2027. Individual bills will vary.

Nothing is final until council votes after the hearings and department questioning, expected to run into next week.

Sources: Calgary Herald; Global News; City of Calgary

Canada

The Pentagon publishes a “fact sheet” saying only 41.9% of Canada’s air force fleet was ready to fly and that Ottawa has no plan to reach NATO’s 5% target

The U.S. Defense Department posted the document online Monday after Axios first reported it on Sunday. It says just 41.9% of the Royal Canadian Air Force fleet was serviceable in 2025–26, 53.9% of the navy’s fleet, and that the army’s readiness rate was 48% (Axios, The Globe and Mail, AP).

It accuses Ottawa of putting “parochial economic and commercial interests” ahead of military needs, pointing to the review of the 88-jet F-35 order, and says Canada “has yet to articulate a resourced path” to spending 5% of GDP on defence by 2035. “Canada must get serious and focused,” it says.

The sheet follows a memo from Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth that requires U.S. military personnel to get approval before attending events with Canadian counterparts. Sen. Mike Rounds told the Halifax International Security Forum it doesn’t change day-to-day co-operation (The Globe and Mail).

Why it matters: It’s an unusually public rebuke from Canada’s closest ally, and it lands while Ottawa decides whether to buy the rest of its F-35s or more European aircraft.

Sources (3)
  • Axios: Scoop: The Pentagon’s case for Canada to “get serious” on defense
  • The Globe and Mail: Pentagon claims Ottawa has no plan to meet defence spending target
  • AP: Pentagon sharply rebukes Canada over defense spending and the F-35 fighter jet review
Community Note

Canada’s own Defence Department has reported the same equipment shortages, but it found 61% of its units ready for operations, a higher figure than the Pentagon’s, Axios notes.

Canada reached NATO’s old 2% target this year. The November budget puts core defence spending at 2.2% this fiscal year and 2.6% for the following four, without a detailed path to 5% (The Globe and Mail). Defence Minister David McGuinty’s office didn’t comment Monday night.

Sources: Axios; The Globe and Mail; AP

Ottawa lists Masar Badil as a terrorist group, days before it planned to host a North American conference in Toronto

Public Safety Canada added Masar Badil, also called the Palestinian Alternative Revolutionary Path Movement, to the Criminal Code list of terrorist entities on Monday as an alias of Samidoun, which Canada listed in 2024. The government says the two share leaders, fundraising platforms and events (Public Safety Canada, Global News, National Post).

The group had promoted a conference in Toronto on Nov. 28 and 29 whose materials celebrated the Oct. 7, 2023, Hamas attack on Israel, according to the Simon Wiesenthal Centre (Global News). Ottawa says Masar Badil advances the interests of Hamas and the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine.

Listing makes it a crime to knowingly deal with the group’s property, and banks must freeze its assets. Public Safety Minister Gary Anandasangaree said it gives police tools to “disrupt their financing” and “dismantle their networks.”

Why it matters: It effectively makes it a crime to fund or organize the Toronto event, and it comes as Jewish groups report rising antisemitic attacks in Canada.

Sources (3)

  • Global News: ‘Resistance’ group hosting Toronto conference added to Canada’s terrorist list
  • National Post: Feds list Masar Badil as terrorist entity before conference
Community Note

A terrorist listing is an administrative step by cabinet. It doesn’t charge anyone with a crime, and groups can ask for a review of their listing.

The U.S. had already sanctioned Masar Badil as a front for the PFLP. CIJA, B’nai Brith Canada and Wiesenthal Canada welcomed Monday’s move. It isn’t yet clear whether the Toronto conference will go ahead in any form.

Hundreds of German schools get the same emailed bomb threat on Monday morning, and police say none of them was real

More than 400 schools in Baden-Württemberg and more than 250 in Saxony-Anhalt received word-for-word identical threats, with others hit in Thuringia, Lower Saxony and Hamburg, police said. Some schools evacuated as a precaution, but police said there was no concrete danger and advised schools to carry on as normal (DW, Euronews, Tagesschau).

Police in Thuringia said they had no information yet on who sent the emails. Danish police also reported a series of similar threats against schools on Monday and said they weren’t considered credible (DW).

It follows emailed hoaxes that hit more than 300 Italian schools earlier this month, signed by a fictitious “Mikhail Andropov,” the same name used on threats to media outlets in Ireland and Bulgaria (Euronews).

Why it matters: The waves of hoax threats across Europe are disrupting schools and tying up police, and investigators haven’t said who is behind them.

Iran’s Guard says three oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz, even with U.S. jets escorting them

In a statement carried by Iranian state media late Monday, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps navy said three “violating” tankers hit naval mines, one near the southern coast and two in the eastern part of the strait, causing powerful explosions and fires. It said U.S. air cover and escort “failed to prevent” the blasts (CNBC, The Jerusalem Post, Iran International, Reuters).

The Guard warned shipowners that a U.S. escort is no substitute for following its traffic orders, and said ships using “unauthorized routes” would be treated as working with “aggressor forces.” Iran says only a northern lane it approves is safe.

The U.S. added a third aircraft carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, to the region last week as it enforces a blockade of Iranian ports (CNBC).

Why it matters: Any new damage in the strait can push oil and fuel prices higher, and attacks there are running at their highest level since the war began.

Business

Sanofi agrees to buy lung-drug maker Insmed for US$41 billion in cash, the drug industry’s biggest deal in three years

The French drugmaker will pay US$175 a share, 17.5% more than Insmed’s last close and 46% above its price before Bloomberg first reported talks on Nov. 19. It’s Sanofi’s biggest deal in about two decades and is expected to close in the first half of 2027 (Reuters, CNBC, Sanofi).

Insmed sells two respiratory drugs: Brinsupri, for the chronic lung disease bronchiectasis, and Arikayce, for a hard-to-treat lung infection. Sanofi CEO Belén Garijo, who took over in May, says the portfolio could top US$10 billion in combined peak sales.

Insmed shares rose about 14% in premarket trading and Sanofi gained about 6% in Paris (CNBC). Sanofi will fund the deal with debt and cash and expects more than US$500 million a year in cost savings (Reuters).

Why it matters: Sanofi’s top seller, the eczema and asthma drug Dupixent, loses patent protection early next decade, and this deal is its biggest bet on replacing that income.

Sources (3)
  • Reuters (via Investing.com): Sanofi’s $41 billion Insmed bet charts life beyond Dupixent
  • CNBC: Insmed shares rally 14% after Sanofi agrees to buy it in pharma’s biggest deal in three years
  • Sanofi: Sanofi to acquire Insmed in strategic step that strengthens long-term future
Community Note

The deal still needs regulatory and Insmed shareholder approval.

Analysts were split on how much it fixes. Jefferies wrote that it “does not fix Sanofi’s R&D/pipeline questions, but it buys time.” Sanofi’s former CEO, Paul Hudson, joined Insmed’s board in October (Reuters).

Sources: Reuters; CNBC; Sanofi

Singapore will go from four phone carriers to three as StarHub agrees to merge with M1 in an all-share deal

StarHub will issue about 651.7 million new shares to M1’s owners, giving them 27.4% of the combined company. Keppel, M1’s main owner, will end up with about 23% and a board seat. The companies put M1’s enterprise value at about S$1.4 billion and expect to close in the first half of 2027 (The Straits Times, CNA, Keppel).

StarHub also exercised an option to buy the Singapore business of Circles.Life, a budget carrier that runs on M1’s network. The combined group would have about 4.1 million customers, leaving it, Singtel and Simba as the country’s operators.

“Four operators competing for the same mature market will no longer deliver the infrastructure Singapore needs next, and consolidation is the answer,” said outgoing StarHub CEO Nikhil Eapen.

Why it matters: Fewer carriers can mean less price competition, the same worry Canadians heard during Rogers’ takeover of Shaw, so Singapore’s regulator will be watching prices closely.

Sources (3)

  • CNA: StarHub set to merge with M1 by mid-2027; will acquire Circles.Life in separate deal
  • Keppel: Keppel advances monetisation of non-core business through telco consolidation between M1 and StarHub
Community Note

The merger needs approval from Singapore’s Infocomm Media Development Authority and votes by StarHub and Keppel shareholders, expected early in 2027. Customers keep their current plans, and the two carriers keep competing until the deal closes.

An earlier plan to sell M1 to Simba fell apart in May after the regulator suspended its review. StarHub says it has no plans to cut its roughly 2,700 staff, but expects about S$110 million a year in cost savings by 2030, largely from fewer stores, network sites and IT systems (The Straits Times).

Sources: The Straits Times; CNA; Keppel

Science corner

Science corner: Penn’s new artificial muscle has a built-in copper coil that heats it and senses how far it has moved, so a robot finger can tap a piano key softly or hard

Engineers at the University of Pennsylvania wound an ultrathin copper coil inside a fibre made of liquid crystal elastomer, a rubbery material that contracts when heated, reinforced with graphene. They call it CoilLCE. The coil does two jobs: running current through it warms the fibre so it contracts, and its electrical resistance shows how much the fibre has moved (Penn Engineering, Popular Science, Interesting Engineering).

Most heat-driven artificial muscles drift as they cool. Here a feedback loop reads the coil’s resistance and keeps adjusting the heat, so the muscle can hold a set position, such as 30% contraction for 30 minutes, without a separate sensor. Penn says it recovers from temperature disturbances in about five seconds.

To show it off, the team built a 3D-printed hand whose fingers pressed piano keys with programmed force and timing, and a pair of robot eyeballs moved by four pairs of opposing muscles that could shift and hold their gaze. The work is published in Advanced Functional Materials.

Why it matters: Soft muscles that sense their own position could give humanoid robots, prosthetic hands and medical devices finer, gentler movements than bulky motors allow.

Sources (4)
  • Penn Engineering: Artificial Muscles Give Robots Precise Control
  • Popular Science: Scientists created artificial, life-like muscles for humanoid robots
  • Interesting Engineering: Robots play piano, mimic human eye gaze with artificial muscles
  • Advanced Functional Materials: Multistage, Self-Sensing Artificial Muscles With Coordination From Liquid Crystal Elastomer-Graphene Composite Fibers
Community Note

This is a lab demonstration. The muscles are moved by heat, so they’re slower and use more energy than electric motors, and the researchers haven’t shown them working for long in a real product.

Next, the team wants to link dozens of these muscles together, for example to make lifelike facial expressions.

Sources: Penn Engineering; Popular Science; Interesting Engineering; Advanced Functional Materials

AI news and advances

Meta brings Muse, its AI agent that can shop, book travel and send email for you, to the U.K., and regulators there say they’re watching

Meta launched Muse in the U.K. on Tuesday for users 18 and older, as an app on iOS and Android, on the web and inside WhatsApp. Users can connect it to email, calendars and shopping apps and give it jobs such as chasing a refund, renewing car insurance or buying Christmas gifts. It’s free, with optional paid plans (Meta, The Guardian).

Meta says Muse must ask permission before buying anything or sending a message, can’t see passwords or payment details, and a separate “security agent” checks its actions against the permissions you’ve granted. It signed deals with Marks & Spencer, Currys, Sainsbury’s and easyJet so Muse can use their sites more easily (The Guardian).

Britain’s Competition and Markets Authority and Information Commissioner’s Office both issued statements on the launch, saying consumer protection and data protection law apply to AI agents and that they’re monitoring the rollout (CMA, ICO).

Why it matters: AI agents that act on your accounts are moving from demos to mass-market apps, and the U.K. is the first test of how regulators will police them.

Sources (4)
  • Meta: Introducing Muse in the UK: A Personal AI Agent You Control
  • The Guardian: Meta launches Muse in UK – the AI agent that can do your Christmas shopping
  • Competition and Markets Authority: CMA statement on UK launch of Meta Muse
  • Information Commissioner’s Office: Statement on UK launch of Meta Muse
Community Note

Muse has had glitches. The Guardian, citing Reuters, reports Meta staff testing it saw it send an unwanted reminder about a Signal message and share a message with random bank balances. Meta says most of those problems came from a bug that has been fixed.

Muse isn’t available in Canada. Meta says it doesn’t share Muse conversations with advertisers.

Sources: Meta; The Guardian; CMA; ICO
