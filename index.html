Science corner: Johns Hopkins builds pencil-tip magnetic “millirobots” that could carry antibiotics straight to an infected heart

Johns Hopkins engineers report wireless magnetic millirobots — as wide as a pencil tip and about as long as a grain of rice — designed to deliver drugs to infected tissue inside the heart. The robots work in pairs: a needle module latches onto tissue at the infection site as a docking station, then a drug module travels to it, docks magnetically and slowly releases antibiotics. An external electromagnetic coil system, similar in principle to an MRI, steers them; they carry no batteries or motors.

In tests on realistic heart phantoms and pig heart tissue, the robots navigated autonomously, even against simulated blood flow. The team, led by medical roboticist Axel Krieger, published the work in Advanced Materials Technologies and next plans tests in phantoms that beat and contract like a real heart.

Why it matters: Endocarditis affects about 50,000 people a year in the U.S., and today’s options are weeks of IV antibiotics or risky open-heart surgery; targeted delivery could one day cut treatment to hours.

Sources (4)

  • Johns Hopkins Mechanical Engineering — Krieger Lab creates tiny robots that speed medicine to the heart
  • Bioengineer.org — Pencil-tip robots ride the bloodstream to deliver drugs to a beating heart
Community Note

Sigma started as online “lone-wolf” male-status talk, but in school it is mostly ironic: “sigma” can mean “excellent,” and what the sigma? is a silly stand-in for “what the heck?” — often said to confuse adults. “NPC” — calling someone robotic or on autopilot

NPC (non-player character) is gaming talk for a computer-controlled background character. As slang, “acting like an NPC” means doing things on autopilot or without original thought. Example: “I was such an NPC this morning — walked into the wrong class.” Keep it self-directed or friendly; aimed at others it can be an insult. Recency: Merriam-Webster entry updated May 2026.
