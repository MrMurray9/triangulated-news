Alberta

Calgary goes back under Stage 4 water limits today for about four weeks while crews hook up its new feeder main

City-wide Stage 4 water restrictions took effect in Calgary on Wednesday, Nov. 4, as crews begin connecting the replacement Bearspaw South Feeder Main to the rest of the water system. The City says the limits should last about four weeks, and the new pipe is targeted to be in service by the end of the year (City of Calgary, Calgary Herald, CityNews).

Outdoor water use is banned, including flooding backyard and community rinks, snowmaking and outdoor cleaning; only watering edible plants is allowed. Residents are also asked to cut indoor use by taking shorter showers, flushing less and running dishwashers and washing machines only with full loads (City of Calgary, Calgary Herald, Global News).

Why it matters: It’s the last big step in replacing the pipe that burst in 2024, and it means outdoor rinks in Calgary will have to wait. Edmonton’s water system is separate and isn’t affected.

RCMP arrest a 17-year-old Albertan after months of online contact with ISIS-linked accounts and seek a terrorism peace bond

The RCMP’s Integrated National Security Enforcement Team says it began investigating in February 2026 after finding online correspondence between a user and accounts affiliated with the Islamic State group. Officers arrested a 17-year-old Alberta youth on Oct. 26. The teen was released on conditions, and the case first came before Fort McMurray Provincial Court on Tuesday, Nov. 3 (RCMP, Edmonton Journal, Global News).

Police have not laid a criminal charge. Instead they started proceedings for a terrorism peace bond under section 810.011 of the Criminal Code, a preventive court order that a judge can impose when there are reasonable grounds to fear someone may commit a terrorism offence. Conditions can include limits on internet use and travel. The Edmonton Journal reports the youth is a girl; the Youth Criminal Justice Act bars naming her, and police say they won’t comment further (RCMP, Edmonton Journal, Culture Alberta).

Why it matters: It’s a reminder that online radicalization reaches Alberta teens, and an Edmonton-based extremism prevention group says the bigger new worry is youth drawn into nihilistic online networks.

Sources (4)

  • Edmonton Journal: Alberta teen facing terrorism proceedings for alleged ISIS communication
  • Global News: Alberta teen accused of terrorism offences connected to ISIS
  • Culture Alberta: Alberta teen, 17, arrested over alleged online ISIS contact
Community Note

Some headlines describe this as terrorism “charges.” According to the RCMP’s own release, it is a peace bond application, which is not a criminal charge and does not lead to a criminal record on its own. Breaking a peace bond’s conditions is a crime. Nothing has been proven in court, and police have not said where the teen lives or whether a judge has granted the bond.

Sources: RCMP news release; Edmonton Journal; Global News; Culture Alberta

Canada

Carney says Washington is now “asking less and offering more” in trade talks, and some of Canada’s red lines are off the table

Speaking at the Commonwealth leaders’ meeting in Antigua on Tuesday, Prime Minister Mark Carney said trade negotiations with the United States have become “more intense” since they collapsed in August. “They’re asking less and they’re offering more, but we’re not quite there,” he said (The Globe and Mail, Bloomberg, Global News).

Carney said U.S. demands that had sunk the summer talks, including objections to rules that make French-language content easy to find on streaming services and limits on Canada’s right to sign trade deals with other countries, are now off the table. He said big issues remain, with autos expected to be among the hardest, and no deal has been finalized (The Globe and Mail, Bloomberg, Global News).

Why it matters: Tariffs on Canadian steel, aluminum and autos hit Alberta manufacturers and shippers. Any deal would come as Democrats prepare to take over Congress in January, though the tariffs were imposed under the president’s own trade powers.

Democrats win both the House and Senate in the U.S. midterms, flipping Senate seats as far afield as Kansas and Texas

Democrats will control both chambers of the U.S. Congress from January after Tuesday’s midterm elections, according to projections by AP and NBC News. They flipped at least seven Senate seats, in Iowa, Kansas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina, South Carolina and Texas, where James Talarico became the first Democrat to win a Senate race since 1988. The Guardian’s tally early Wednesday had Democrats at 54 Senate seats (including two independents) with two races uncalled, and at 228 House seats with about 70 races still to call; NBC projects Democrats will end with at least 258 House seats (AP, NBC News, The Guardian, Euronews).

Democrats also flipped governorships in Texas, Georgia, Nevada, Ohio and Iowa,. NBC’s exit poll put President Donald Trump’s disapproval at 62%. Trump congratulated Democrats and said rising gas prices tied to the Iran war hurt his party, while House Speaker Mike Johnson promised Republicans would win the House back in 2028 (NBC News, Euronews, AP).

Why it matters: A Democratic Congress can block Trump’s legislation, run investigations and shape spending from January. That matters for Canada on trade, energy and Ukraine, though Trump keeps control of tariffs and foreign policy.

Spain’s transport minister resigns over the Urda train crash, which has now killed 69, and quits the Nov. 29 election race

Óscar Puente announced at the end of a long parliamentary hearing on Tuesday that he is stepping down as transport minister and withdrawing his name from the Socialist lists for Spain’s early general election on Nov. 29. He had ruled out resigning several times since the Oct. 28 collision near Urda, about 130 km south of Madrid, in which a Renfe train ran into the back of a stopped Iryo train after a control centre let it proceed while an automatic safety system was down (The Guardian/AFP, Euronews, Politico Europe, Al Jazeera).

Hours earlier Puente had announced safety changes: new control-centre alert software and an AI oversight tool, a second operator to confirm decisions whenever the safety system is down, and €30 million in aid for victims’ families and the injured. The train drivers’ union Semaf has called strikes at Renfe for Nov. 10 and 12 (The Guardian/AFP, Euronews, Politico Europe).

Why it matters: It’s Europe’s deadliest rail disaster in years, on a network used by millions, and it now hangs over Spain’s Nov. 29 election. An earlier crash in Andalusia in January killed 46.

Israel’s election committee certifies last week’s results after finding no fraud, rejecting Netanyahu’s “stolen election” claims

What changed since the last edition: Last week Likud called the vote “stolen.” On Wednesday the Central Elections Committee certified the results after an audit found no fraud, and President Herzog has begun talks on who should try to form a government.

Israel’s Central Elections Committee certified the results of the Oct. 27 vote on Wednesday after reviewing 9,069 polling stations and examining every complaint. It found no irregularities that compromised the election or changed its outcome. The committee’s chair, Supreme Court Justice Noam Sohlberg, cast the deciding vote after Likud and Shas representatives abstained, and said those casting doubt on the results pose “a real danger” to Israel’s democracy (Al Jazeera/AP, AFP, Ynetnews).

The results leave Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc with 53 seats, Gadi Eisenkot’s bloc with 48 and Arab-led parties with a record 19, so no side has the 61 needed to govern. President Isaac Herzog received the results Wednesday and starts two days of consultations with party leaders. National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir still insists the vote was “stolen,” and a Likud member quit the committee in protest. Opposition figure Yair Lapid said the Democrats’ win in the U.S. Congress rules out a unity government led by Netanyahu (Al Jazeera/AP, AFP, Ynetnews).

Why it matters: Certification puts an official stamp on a deadlocked result, and Herzog’s pick of who gets the first chance at a coalition decides whether Israel heads toward a new government or another election early next year.

AMD posts a record US$13.4-billion quarter as data-centre chip sales nearly double, and its shares jump about 8% after hours

AMD said Tuesday that third-quarter revenue rose 45% from a year earlier to a record US$13.4 billion, above the top of its own forecast. Data-centre sales, led by its Instinct MI450 AI chips and EPYC server processors, rose 97% to US$8.5 billion, almost two-thirds of the total. Why it matters: AMD is Nvidia’s main rival in AI chips, and its results are a read on whether the AI data-centre spending boom is still growing. It is.

Euro-area inflation hits 4%, a three-year high, as energy prices jump almost 21% and a December ECB hike looks likely

Annual inflation in the 21-country euro area rose to 4.0% in October from 3.8% in September, according to Eurostat’s flash estimate on Wednesday, the highest since September 2023. Energy prices were up 20.8% from a year earlier, while services inflation was 3.3% and food, alcohol and tobacco 1.6% (Eurostat, CNBC, Euronews).

Lithuania had the highest rate at 6.1% and Malta the lowest at 2.3%, but every member was above the European Central Bank’s 2% target. Markets priced in more than a 72% chance that the ECB, which held its key rate at 2.5% in October after a September hike, will raise rates again at its December meeting (CNBC, Euronews, Investing.com).

Why it matters: Europe is paying for the Iran war through its energy bills. Higher European rates and a stronger euro feed into oil prices and the loonie, which matter for Alberta.

Science corner

Science corner: a fingernail-sized light chip that pulls a jumble of laser light apart into clean, separate beams

Engineers at Stanford and the Institute of Science and Technology Austria (ISTA) have built a silicon photonic chip, about the size of a fingernail, that takes in “partially coherent” light (a blend of light waves that only partly march in step) and sorts it into separate, completely independent beams. Physicists described how this could work in the 1980s, but the team says this is the first device to actually do it. The work is published in Nature Photonics (Stanford via Phys.org, ISTA, Nature Photonics).

Inside the chip, a network of tiny tunable light paths works out the hidden “coherence modes” in the incoming light by itself and sends each one to its own output. In tests, the team mixed two infrared lasers, then three, and the chip sorted them out each time; checks showed the output beams had no correlation left. The researchers say the method could let microscopes tell apart overlapping glowing particles, and they are working with NASA on whether it could separate light from stars and planets that blur together in a telescope (Phys.org, ISTA, Interesting Engineering).

Why it matters: Ordinary cameras squash all of that light information into one flat picture. A chip that separates it keeps details like depth and the number of sources, so sharper scientific imaging could one day fit on a chip.

Sources (4)
  • Nature Photonics: Automatic on-chip separation of partially coherent light into its mutually incoherent components
  • Phys.org (Stanford University): Fingernail-sized chip untangles mixed light into clean beams
  • ISTA: Splitting light on a chip
  • Interesting Engineering: Photonic chip splits mixed light into 3 modes for sharper imaging
Community Note

This is a lab demonstration using lasers sent into the chip, not a finished imaging product. The tests separated up to three modes; real-world light from microscopes or telescopes is messier, and the NASA work is still exploratory. The Phys.org article was written by Stanford, one of the institutions behind the research.

Sources: Nature Photonics; Stanford via Phys.org; ISTA; Interesting Engineering

AI news and advances

Tencent releases Hy4, an open AI model it trained without hacking know-how to make it harder to turn into a cyberweapon

China’s Tencent released Hy4 on Wednesday, the newest model in its Hunyuan family, with image understanding, long-document handling, computer use and “agent” abilities for multi-step tasks. Tencent says it ranks among the top tier of open-source models and is available through its products and APIs now, with downloadable weights and safety documentation promised in the coming days (Reuters, Nikkei Asia, South China Morning Post).

Tencent says it filtered exploit development, reverse engineering and penetration-testing material out of the training data so the model knows defensive security but is less able to help with attacks. It follows rising worry about AI-assisted hacking, and Moonshot AI limiting its own newest model’s offensive cyber skills last month (Reuters, Bloomberg, South China Morning Post).

Why it matters: Open models can be downloaded and modified by anyone, so leaving dangerous skills out at training time is one of the few safety levers that still works after release. It’s a sign that Chinese labs are under the same pressure as U.S. ones.

Sources (5)
  • Reuters (via StreetInsider): Tencent rolls out AI model trained with limited cybersecurity data amid hacking fears
  • Nikkei Asia: Tencent says its Hy4 joins ‘top-tier’ of open-source AI models
  • South China Morning Post: Tencent unveils Hy4 as open-weight AI race heats up
  • Bloomberg: Tencent touts new AI model it claims outperforms Z.AI, Moonshot
  • Tencent: Tencent launches Hy4
Community Note

The “top tier” ranking and the claim that it beats rival Chinese models come from Tencent’s own benchmarks, which have not been independently checked. Researchers have shown that filtered capabilities can sometimes be added back by fine-tuning open weights, so data filtering lowers the risk but doesn’t remove it.

OA deaf Indianapolis dad “talks” to his 2-day-old daughter in sign language at 3 a.m., and she can’t take her eyes off his hands

Seen on: The Haney family’s social media account (TODAY.com doesn’t name the platform), where several videos have hundreds of thousands to millions of likes.

In the video, Robert Haney, who lost his hearing to meningitis at age 3, signs to newborn Matilda, or Tilly, in American Sign Language in the middle of the night: “You are a beautiful girl. Your daddy is deaf. You’re hearing ... I can’t wait to teach you sign language. Mommy’s tired. I’m tired. But you’re worth it.” Tilly stares at his hands the whole time (TODAY.com, Fox 5 New York, Crescer).

Robert and his wife Maggie, who is hearing and earned a college degree in ASL interpreting, are raising Tilly, Robby, 9, and Mylah, 4, with ASL as the main language at home. Each child has a name sign; Tilly’s is a “T” blended with the sign for “I love you,” with a little wiggle. The couple say they post to show deaf family life and encourage people who know some sign language to use it (TODAY.com, Crescer, Parents.fr).

Why it matters: It’s a sweet, simple glimpse of deaf culture, and a nudge to anyone who knows a little ASL: use it, because it usually makes someone’s day.
