At least seven reported dead after a live-fire exercise sets off an explosion and wind-driven fire at CFB Wainwright; Edmonton hospitals declare a code orange

An explosion in a training area at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright, about 200 kilometres southeast of Edmonton, started a fire around 4 p.m. Saturday that high winds pushed beyond the base boundary (National Defence, Global News). Multiple Canadian Armed Forces sources told Global News that at least seven people were killed and dozens injured, and that a live-fire training exercise hit a gas line or gas well. The military confirmed “fatalities and injuries” on Sunday but said it will not give casualty numbers until next of kin are notified. “I cannot recall an incident like this in recent memory,” Defence Minister David McGuinty said. “This is a very, very active and delicate situation.”

The Royal Alexandra and University of Alberta hospitals in Edmonton went to code orange, the mass-casualty alert, and Acute Care Alberta asked people to avoid the U of A emergency department unless absolutely necessary, saying “extremely few” ambulances were available in the Edmonton area (Edmonton Journal, Global News). STARS sent helicopters from Edmonton and Saskatoon. The Municipal District of Wainwright said around midnight that the fire was being held, with a fire watch overnight. The fire also closed the Wainwright referendum advance poll Saturday; voters were redirected to Lloydminster and Vermilion and ballot boxes were moved securely (Global News). Military families can call the Family Information Line at 1-800-866-4546.

Why it matters: The defence minister says he can’t recall anything like it in recent memory, and the mass-casualty response has stretched ambulances and emergency rooms in the Edmonton area.

De Havilland calls for a national fleet of water bombers as its Calgary-built Canadairs sell out through 2034

De Havilland Aircraft of Canada, which revived production of the Canadair amphibious water bomber in 2024, says an order for 50 planes from Ottawa or the provinces would give it the money to open a second production line at its new plant east of Calgary (The Canadian Press). The 38 DHC-515s on order — including six for Ontario, five for Alberta and three for Manitoba — are sold out through 2034, and the first delivery, to Greece, is projected for early 2028. “We can’t afford to wait any longer,” said Neil Sweeney, De Havilland’s vice-president of corporate affairs.

According to a draft joint statement seen by The Canadian Press, Canada and the European Union will announce a joint purchase of 50 De Havilland water bombers at a conference in Montreal at the end of October; it is unclear whether that includes the existing orders. Canada has no national wildland firefighting strategy, though Ottawa leased 10 firefighting aircraft this year for its first reserve fleet, and Ontario Premier Doug Ford called in August for 60 new water bombers under a national plan (The Globe and Mail). Aerotime reported the planned Canada–EU buy as well.

Why it matters: After the Jasper fire and record fire seasons, who owns and dispatches firefighting planes is a growing question — and Alberta is both a customer and the place they’re built.

Crush at a Durga Puja pavilion in Kolkata kills 19 as rain drives crowds for the exits; seven arrested

A senior officer told PTI there was “gross mismanagement,” with narrow gates and too few police and guards.

Why it matters: Durga Puja draws millions to temporary pavilions every year, and this crush will put crowd control at festivals across India under scrutiny during the rest of the holiday.

Science corner

Science corner: the world’s timekeepers retire the leap second — clocks will be allowed to drift up to an hour from Earth’s spin

Delegates at the General Conference on Weights and Measures in Versailles voted on Thursday to stop adding leap seconds to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), the world’s reference clock (The Guardian). Under Resolution 5, UTC becomes continuous on May 20, 2027, and can drift up to 3,600 seconds — one hour — from astronomical time based on Earth’s rotation before any correction (BIPM). Since 1972, 27 leap seconds have been added because Earth’s spin has slowed over the long term.

Recently Earth has sped up, raising the odds of the first-ever negative leap second; an expert workshop put that at a 30 per cent chance by 2035 (BIPM). That worried engineers: after the 2012 leap second, some servers froze and sites including Reddit and LinkedIn crashed, said Setnam Shemar of Britain’s National Physical Laboratory (The Guardian). The BIPM says preparing systems for a negative leap second could cost as much as the millennium bug. A “leap hour” isn’t expected for thousands of years (Scientific American).

Why it matters: Phone networks, power grids, GPS and stock markets all run on precise time, so removing a risky one-second hiccup makes them more reliable.

Sources (4)
  • BIPM — CGPM 2026 Resolution 5: continuity of UTC
  • The Guardian — How an accelerating Earth led experts to adopt the leap hour
  • Scientific American — Scientists vote to end leap seconds
  • CTV Your Morning — Why the world is officially getting rid of the ‘leap second’ (video)
Community Note

Nothing changes for your clocks or daylight time. Some astronomers note the decision mostly pushes a hard choice far into the future; the head of the IERS Earth Orientation Centre called it “pushing back the decision” (The Guardian). The official text of the resolution is French.

Sources: BIPM; The Guardian; Scientific American; CTV.

AI news and advances

Anthropic pitches its own AI-safety law: Washington could block a model’s release — with a judge’s OK — if it risks 50-plus deaths or $1 billion in damage

Anthropic, the maker of the Claude AI assistant, confirmed Friday that it is circulating a draft federal bill among U.S. lawmakers, first reported by Politico (AFP via The Hindu, Reuters). Under the draft, the government could block a model’s release or use if there is an imminent risk of killing or seriously injuring more than 50 people or causing more than US$1 billion in damage, but only with a judge’s approval. Frontier developers would have to test their most powerful models, publish what they find, submit to independent evaluation and keep a strong security program, a spokesperson told AFP.

The proposal would also override some state laws, such as California’s. Charlie Bullock of the Institute for Law & AI called it “pretty reasonable” but “weaker” than the FRONTIER Act, a competing House bill introduced this summer (AFP). Congress has not passed a federal AI law and is out of session until after next month’s midterm elections.

Why it matters: One of the biggest AI labs is asking to be regulated — on its own terms — after a run of reports about AI agents escaping their developers’ control.

Connor McDavid had two goals and three assists — including a goal 40 seconds in, his seventh opening-minute goal, tying Mark Messier’s franchise record — stretching his season-opening points streak to eight games and his league-leading total to 23 points (NHL.com).
