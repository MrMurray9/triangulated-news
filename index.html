Alberta

The Elks play Montreal for the Grey Cup in Calgary tonight, their first shot at the trophy since 2015

Kickoff for the 113th Grey Cup is 5 p.m. Edmonton time at McMahon Stadium, on TSN. Edmonton (13-5) and Montreal (14-4) had the CFL’s two best records this season. The Elks beat the BC Lions 28-16 in the Western Final, and the Alouettes beat Winnipeg 33-23 in the East. The teams split their regular-season games, with the Elks winning 32-29 in overtime at Commonwealth Stadium in June and Montreal winning 48-30 at home in August (Edmonton Elks, TSN).

The game is full of reunions. Elks quarterback Cody Fajardo and receiver Austin Mack won the 2023 Grey Cup together with Montreal, which traded Fajardo to Edmonton after the 2024 season to start Davis Alexander, now the league’s Most Outstanding Player. Alouettes coach Jason Maas played quarterback for Edmonton and coached the team from 2016 to 2019, while Elks coach Mark Kilam spent nearly two decades with the Calgary Stampeders. It’s the 12th Edmonton-Montreal Grey Cup, the most common matchup in the game’s history; Edmonton has won eight of the previous 11 (CFL.ca, Edmonton Elks, TSN).

Why it matters: After going from last in the West Division last year to first this year, Edmonton is one win from its 15th Grey Cup, and TSN notes that playing in Calgary gives the Elks a bit of a home-field edge.

A fifth soldier, Pte. William Dal Bianco, dies of injuries from last month’s CFB Wainwright fire, with 24 others still in hospital

What changed since the last edition: We last covered Wainwright on Nov. 11, when about 300 members of the soldiers’ battalion marched on Remembrance Day. Since then, a fifth soldier has died of his injuries.

The Canadian Armed Forces said Private William Douglas Dal Bianco of the 3rd Battalion, Princess Patricia’s Canadian Light Infantry, died on Nov. 13 in an Ontario hospital, where he’d been treated since the Oct. 17 fire. Of the 38 soldiers injured, 24 are still in hospital and 14 have been released (National Defence, Global News, AP).

The fire started during live-fire training, and strong winds pushed it across the unit’s administrative area before it jumped the base fence. Sgt. Morgan Wong of Edmonton and privates Shaun Farrell, Aidan Goulding and Hayden Lykkemark died earlier. “Private Dal Bianco and his fellow soldiers were training to defend Canada,” Prime Minister Mark Carney said. “That is service. Now, we serve them.” (Global News, National Defence)

Why it matters: 3 PPCLI is based at CFB Edmonton, so this loss is felt directly by military families across the city.

Canada

Carney pledges up to $560 million to a Brazil-led fund that pays countries to keep tropical forests standing

Prime Minister Mark Carney announced the four-year contribution on Sunday after meeting Brazil’s President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva at the ASEAN summit in Manila. The Tropical Forest Forever Facility, co-led by Brazil and Norway and launched at last year’s COP30 in Belém, wants to raise US$35 billion from governments to draw in up to US$140 billion of private money. Investment returns would pay more than 70 countries for forest they can prove they’ve protected, with at least 20% going to Indigenous Peoples and local communities (Prime Minister’s Office, Bloomberg, The Jerusalem Post).

Lula said he invited Carney to Brazil in the first three months of next year, with a visit to Canada to follow. Carney then flew to Phnom Penh for the Francophonie summit, where he’s set to meet Quebec Premier Paul St-Pierre Plamondon after the province elected a sovereigntist government; Canada hosts the next summit in the Ottawa area in 2028 (Bloomberg, The Canadian Press via Winnipeg Free Press).

Why it matters: It’s a large chunk of Canada’s climate-aid budget, and it marks a warmer relationship with Brazil as Ottawa looks for trade partners beyond the U.S.

World

U.S. negotiators ask whether Ukraine would leave Donbas in exchange for NATO membership, Zelensky says, the first time the idea has come up

“The Americans raised the question: ‘What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?’ This was the first time this issue had been raised,” Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky told reporters, in remarks his office released Saturday. Donbas is the eastern region of Donetsk and Luhansk, part of which Russia has never managed to capture (Kyiv Independent, CNBC with Reuters).

Zelensky said Ukraine won’t give up territory, but if the U.S. and other NATO allies confirm a full package of security guarantees, a strong, funded army and money to rebuild, he would put the question to Ukrainians, and any deal would not recognize occupied land as Russian. He said U.S. officials told him American troops wouldn’t be deployed in Ukraine under the guarantees. Russia has long called Ukrainian NATO membership a red line, and at talks this week returned to its pre-invasion demands that NATO pull back (Kyiv Independent, CNBC, The Washington Post).

Why it matters: Swapping land for NATO protection would be the biggest shift yet in efforts to end the war, and Canada, as a NATO member, would be part of any promise to defend Ukraine.

Iran says three oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz, one of them a supertanker

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards navy said two tankers that had switched off their radar and tracking systems exploded and caught fire after striking a mine while trying to slip through the south of the strait on an unauthorized route; it said one was a supertanker. Iran’s regular navy said a third tanker hit a mine and stopped in a “prohibited zone” east of the strait. Neither named the ships or said where they were from (Reuters via CNA, AFP via Arab News, Anadolu Agency).

Since its war with the U.S. began in February, Iran has demanded that ships get its permission to cross the strait and has attacked those that don’t. The U.S. has blockaded Iranian ports and escorts tankers through the waterway, and says it has cleared the main shipping lanes of mines. On Friday, the Guards released video of smoke rising from what they said was a gas carrier that had hit a mine (CNA, Arab News).

Why it matters: About a fifth of the world’s oil normally moves through Hormuz, so every attack there feeds into fuel prices, including at Canadian pumps.

Business

Tesla starts taking orders for a personal Cybercab at US$29,990, but it only works with a US$299-a-month subscription and inside Robotaxi zones

Tesla invited “select customers” on Friday to order the two-seat, steering-wheel-free car for personal use, with a US$1,000 non-refundable deposit and an estimated December delivery in Austin, Texas. The US$299 monthly “Autonomy Subscription” is mandatory: without it the car can’t drive, since there are no manual controls. It will only operate inside Tesla’s Robotaxi service areas, which are in Texas and Florida (Electrek, The Verge, TechCrunch).

Over five years the fee adds about US$17,900, for a total near US$47,600 before insurance and charging, The Verge calculated. Owners can’t use it to earn money on ride apps for now. Elon Musk replied on X that the car will roll out nationwide “as we receive regulatory approval” and called the fee “too high.” U.S. safety regulators are still reviewing how Tesla certified a car without a steering wheel or pedals (TechCrunch, The Verge, Electrek).

Why it matters: It’s the first time Tesla has tried to sell a fully driverless car to the public, and how it goes will shape the robotaxi bet that much of Tesla’s stock price rests on.

More than 100,000 people, including Elon Musk, sign a petition to bring back Jensen Huang, as Nvidia’s board reportedly sounds out Arm’s CEO

What changed since the last edition: On Nov. 12 we reported Huang’s resignation and Colette Kress stepping in. Since then, a campaign to reinstate him has gathered six figures of signatures, and the first possible successor has been named in reports.

The “Stand with Jensen” petition says Huang was pushed out “without meaningful due process” and asks the board to bring him back while an independent investigation runs. By Saturday afternoon its website listed more than 115,000 signatures, including about 6,000 people who say they’re current Nvidia employees; Musk, Replit’s Amjad Masad, Epic Games’ Tim Sweeney and Y Combinator’s Garry Tan signed on. Separately, the Financial Times reported that the board has approached Arm Holdings CEO Rene Haas, who worked at Nvidia for about seven years, as a possible successor. The board said it is still choosing a search firm, hasn’t formally engaged any candidates, and called talk of names “premature.” Two big investors, Altimeter’s Brad Gerstner and the managing partner of Atreides Management, urged the board not to rush, even if that means Huang returns (The Economic Times with ANI, Business Insider).

Why it matters: Nvidia makes most of the chips behind the AI boom and is one of the biggest holdings in many Canadian retirement and index funds, so who leads it matters well beyond Silicon Valley.

Science corner

Science corner: SpaceX catches a Starship spacecraft with giant tower arms as it returns from orbit, the first time a spaceship has been caught this way

Rockets usually throw away their upper stage, the part that reaches orbit. On Saturday, after 22 hours and 14 laps of Earth, SpaceX’s Starship spacecraft descended through cloud over Starbase, Texas, flipped upright and settled between two mechanical “chopstick” arms on its launch tower, nicknamed Mechazilla. SpaceX says its heat shield and other systems appear to have worked well (Al Jazeera with AP and Reuters, CNBC, The Verge).

The plan was to catch both stages. The Super Heavy booster’s engines shut off too early on its way back Friday, so it automatically steered out over the Gulf of Mexico and blew itself up, and engineers are still looking for the cause. SpaceX has caught boosters three times before. The ship released 26 new Starlink satellites, and NASA, which is relying on Starship to land astronauts on the Moon, called the catch “almost magic” (Al Jazeera, CNBC).

Why it matters: A rocket that can land and fly again, like an airliner, could make launches far cheaper, from internet satellites to Moon missions.

Sources (3)
  • Al Jazeera (AP, Reuters): SpaceX’s Starship captured by launchpad arms in dramatic first
  • CNBC: SpaceX catches upper stage of Starship rocket in historic first
  • The Verge: Watch SpaceX successfully execute the first “chopstick” catch of Starship
Community Note

It was a test flight, and it fell short of SpaceX’s goal of catching both stages.

Elon Musk runs SpaceX and Tesla; SpaceX trades under SPCX, which appears in our Markets section.

Sources: Al Jazeera (AP, Reuters); CNBC; The Verge

AI news and advances

LangChain, whose free tools are used to build AI agents, raises US$300 million and is now valued at US$3 billion

Alphabet’s CapitalG fund led the round, joined by IVP, Sapphire Ventures, Singapore’s GIC and DFJ Growth, the Wall Street Journal first reported. The San Francisco startup’s open-source software helps developers hook an AI model up to tools and instructions, link several agents into one workflow, and add steps where a human must approve sensitive actions (SiliconANGLE, Reuters, LangChain).

The tools are free; LangChain makes money from LangSmith, paid software for testing, tracking and fixing agents once they’re running, used by firms such as Autodesk and Cisco. The company had said its open-source tools were downloaded about 90 million times a month, and it reportedly plans to nearly double its staff to 850 by the end of 2027 (SiliconANGLE, LangChain).

Why it matters: Many of the AI helpers now showing up in workplace software are built on tools like these, and investors are betting the plumbing behind agents will be as valuable as the AI models themselves.

FA teenage roper and her horse finally lasso a sheep that outran an entire Ohio town for 45 days

Seen on: Facebook, where Whispering Acres Farm Animal Sanctuary and volunteers ran the search and shared the capture, as reported by the New York Post and News 5 Cleveland.

An American Blackbelly sheep had dodged volunteers, sheriff’s deputies and even drones around Wadsworth, Ohio, for more than six weeks. On Wednesday, Reagan Crumpler rode out on her 18-year-old horse, Catalena. After hours of near-misses, she told her horse, “Come on, Cat. We got this,” and they got in front of the sheep and roped it (New York Post, UPI, News 5 Cleveland).

The sheep is now at Whispering Acres Farm Animal Sanctuary, which had led a Facebook campaign to rescue it, and has been named Gerri Reagan after the girl who caught her. If no owner turns up, she’ll stay there for good. “We actually can breathe because she is safe,” volunteer Rebecca Roxberry told News 5 Cleveland (New York Post, News 5 Cleveland).

Why it matters: A whole town, plus a kid and her horse, teaming up to save one stubborn sheep is the kind of good news that spreads for a reason.
