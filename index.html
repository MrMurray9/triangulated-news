Alberta

Tenants of an Edmonton apartment block with no heat must be out by noon today, after thieves stripped copper from its heating system

A Court of King’s Bench judge has ended the leases at Stadium Plaza, a six-storey, 78-unit building near Commonwealth Stadium that was only finished in 2024 and is now run by a court-appointed receiver. Justice Michael Lema called the property a “state of chaos,” citing the broken heating system, squatters, gang and drug activity, and other damage (Edmonton Journal, CTV News Edmonton).

Thieves cut out copper tubing from the heating system. Repairs are expected to cost about $461,000 and take months, so remaining tenants would likely be without heat until about February. Thirty-six units are still occupied, and the judge gave residents a week to leave, with extensions possible on request (Edmonton Journal, Court of King’s Bench decision).

Why it matters: With overnight lows well below freezing, the court decided it wasn’t safe for people to stay. It also shows how fast a brand-new building can fail when it goes into receivership.

Reports give the address as 11224 or 11228 86 St. NW. Tenants who can’t leave by the deadline can ask for more time, and at least one has been given until Nov. 23.

Canada

The Liberals’ 2026 budget passes the House of Commons 162 to 151, with every opposition MP voting against it

MPs approved the budget motion on Thursday evening. All 162 votes in favour came from Liberals; the Conservatives, Bloc Québécois, NDP and Green MPs voted no. Because it was a confidence vote, its passage means there will be no early election over the budget (The Canadian Press via Global News, La Presse, House of Commons vote record).

The budget, tabled Nov. 5 by Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne, adds billions in new borrowing and tax measures meant to speed up building of infrastructure and other big projects. The vote approves the plan in general; the actual changes still have to pass in one or more budget implementation bills, which can be amended in committee (The Canadian Press, La Presse).

Why it matters: This removes the risk of a snap election this fall, but none of its individual measures are law yet.

The U.S. sanctions six Toronto-area men it calls leaders of the ’Ndrangheta Mafia, along with a chain of Ontario funeral homes

The U.S. Treasury named Angelo Figliomeni as head of the Italian crime group’s Toronto faction, along with Cosimo Figliomeni, Francesco Commisso, Rocco Remo Commisso, Vincenzo DeMaria and Luigi Vescio Sr. It says the faction is powerful enough to dictate “events in Italy from across the ocean” and launders money through Canadian and U.S. banks (U.S. Treasury, The Canadian Press, National Post).

Also sanctioned: Vescio funeral homes in Woodbridge, Toronto and Maple, and a Toronto limousine company. Treasury says the group helps Mexican cartels move drugs, and it sanctioned Australia’s Comancheros motorcycle gang the same day (The Canadian Press, National Post).

Why it matters: Sanctions freeze any U.S. assets and bar Americans and U.S. banks from doing business with those named, which can cut them off from much of the financial system, including in Canada.

Russia fires more than 100 missiles and 400 drones at Ukraine overnight, killing at least eight and damaging three bridges in Kyiv

Waves of missiles hit the capital from about 1:25 a.m. local time, Ukrainian officials said. Kyiv’s mayor told people not to drive after the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi, Southern and Darnytskyi bridges were damaged, and parts of the city lost power, heat and water. Why it matters: It’s one of the largest attacks of the war, aimed at the power and transport Ukrainians depend on going into winter, and it came just after the three-way Trump-Xi-Putin “peace” declaration.

Britain and the EU agree a post-Brexit “reset” letting 100,000 young people a year from each side live and work in the other for up to three years

At a summit in Brussels on Friday, Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s government said talks had concluded on three agreements: a Youth Experience Scheme, shared food-safety rules that should ease farm and food trade, and linking the two sides’ carbon-emissions markets (Al Jazeera, Euronews, The Guardian).

The youth scheme lets young Britons and Europeans live, work or study in each other’s countries for up to three years and is due to start by January 2030 at the latest. The two sides couldn’t agree on what EU students should pay at British universities and gave themselves six months to settle it (Euronews, The Guardian).

Why it matters: It’s the biggest step back toward Europe since Brexit a decade ago, though Burnham says rejoining the EU is only one possible outcome and far from imminent.

Fiona, the Cincinnati Zoo hippo the internet watched grow up after her premature birth, dies at 9 after swallowing a visitor’s knit hat

The zoo says the hat got into her enclosure about Nov. 8 after a child tossed it in the air; it hit a ceiling fan and flew through a one-foot gap above the 12-foot viewing window. Fiona seemed fine at first, then stopped eating (Cincinnati Zoo, Wired, Cincinnati Enquirer).

Vets ruled out surgery because hippos react unpredictably to anaesthesia. Instead they tried fluids, enemas, a nine-foot scope borrowed from the Columbus Zoo and even a vibration plate from a horse farm. She died Thursday (AP, Wired, Cincinnati Zoo).

Why it matters: Fiona drew millions of online fans after surviving a birth six weeks early in 2017; her death is a reminder of why zoos ask visitors never to throw anything near animals.

Science corner

Science corner: chips smaller than a grain of rice sit inside test tubes and radio out a reaction’s temperature every half-second

Chemists usually learn how a reaction went only at the end, by pulling a sample and running it through a separate instrument. A Stanford and Cornell team instead dropped a half-millimetre silicon chip into each tube. Light powers the chip, and it flashes back temperature readings with a tiny onboard laser, a system the authors call “reaction telemetry” (Stanford Report, Science).

Running 96 tubes at once for an hour produced about 700,000 data points, compared with 96 from the usual end-point checks. In tests, the heat traces showed when a “poisoned” catalyst made a reaction stall and flagged reactions running dangerously hot before scaling up (Stanford Report, Chemistry World).

Why it matters: Faster, cheaper live data from every tube could speed up the search for new drugs and materials, and feed the self-driving labs that use AI to choose the next experiment.

Sources (3)
  • Stanford Report: Wireless sensors track chemistry as it happens
  • Science: Telemetric calorimetry for wireless continuous reaction profiling
  • Chemistry World: Sand grain-sized wireless chip can monitor reactions to provide data for self-driving labs
Community Note

For now the chips measure only heat. The team says future versions could sense acidity, conductivity or light.

Several co-authors founded OWiC Technologies, which makes the chips, and hold patents on them, as the study discloses.

Sources: Stanford Report; Science; Chemistry World

AI news and advances

Google’s Gemini app for Mac can now run your other apps for you, filling in forms and sorting files in a background window while you keep working

The new “computer use” feature lets Gemini carry out multi-step tasks across local files, desktop apps and websites, such as filling in forms, renaming a batch of files or building a slideshow from a dashboard. You choose which apps it may touch and can watch a live preview, then pause, stop or approve its actions (TechCrunch, The Verge).

It’s rolling out now in English in the U.S. and requires a paid Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra plan. TechCrunch notes Google is late here; OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta already offer similar agents. Gemini pauses for sign-ins, payments and other sensitive steps (TechCrunch, Cult of Mac).

Why it matters: AI that operates a computer on its own can save hours of clicking, but it also gets wide access to your files and accounts, which is why Apple is tightening Mac permissions for such agents.

Sources (3)
  • TechCrunch: Google gives Gemini the ability to use your Mac for you
  • The Verge: Gemini on Mac can now use your computer
  • Cult of Mac: Google Gemini can now control your Mac while you keep working
Community Note

It isn’t available in Canada yet, and Google hasn’t given a date for other countries or for Windows.

The Verge reports Apple is previewing new limits on “full disk access” in the macOS 27.3 beta because of risks from AI agents that can control a computer.

Sources: TechCrunch; The Verge; Cult of Mac
