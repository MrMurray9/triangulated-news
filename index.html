Alberta

Alberta’s chief electoral officer tells MLAs to shelve the new riding map or push back the October 2027 election

In a letter on Friday to Speaker Ric McIver and all 87 MLAs, Chief Electoral Officer Gordon McClure said Elections Alberta cannot properly roll out new electoral boundaries in time for the fixed Oct. 18, 2027, vote. He asked MLAs either to run that election on the current map, or to adopt the new map and extend the election date “where legally authorized and necessary” (Edmonton Journal, The Globe and Mail, The Canadian Press via CTV News Edmonton).

McClure wrote that the agency usually needs 12 to 18 months to get ready for a general election after boundaries change, and that a flurry of votes over the past year has stretched it thin. The letter, made public by the Alberta NDP, comes before a minority report on the boundaries expected in the legislature Monday, while the opposition accuses the government of gerrymandering (Edmonton Journal, The Globe and Mail, CTV News Edmonton).

Why it matters: The map decides which Edmonton neighbourhoods vote together and how many seats the city gets, and McClure’s warning now puts the date of the next provincial election in question too.

Sources (3)
  • Edmonton Journal — Chief electoral officer: province should delay electoral boundary changes or October 2027 election
  • The Globe and Mail — Elections Alberta urges MLAs to hold off on approving new electoral map
  • CTV News Edmonton (The Canadian Press) — Elections Alberta recommends 2027 vote be delayed as gerrymandering accusations fly
Community Note

McClure is the legislature’s independent elections officer, and he did not say which of the two options he prefers. Nothing has been decided yet: keeping the old map or changing Alberta’s fixed election date would both take action by MLAs.

Sources: Edmonton Journal; The Globe and Mail; CTV News Edmonton (The Canadian Press).

Canada

Canada’s biggest banks shut personal AI agents out of online banking, starting with RBC

Royal Bank of Canada says it is blocking third-party personal AI agents, such as Meta’s Muse, from logging in to online banking and its other secure digital services after a security review. Existing connections through those tools stop working; customers can still bank through RBC’s own app, website and phone lines (RBC, Bloomberg via Insurance Journal, The Globe and Mail).

BMO, TD and Scotiabank have followed with their own restrictions, CIBC says it does not support personal agents accessing accounts, and National Bank says it is still assessing. In an internal memo, RBC said agents “accessing banking systems can create unacceptable customer risk, including acting beyond customer expectations or creating liability exposure” (Bloomberg via Insurance Journal, The Globe and Mail).

Why it matters: If you have handed an AI assistant your banking login to pay bills or track spending, that set-up is about to stop working at most big Canadian banks.

Sources (4)
  • RBC — Understanding third-party AI agents and your RBC banking
  • Insurance Journal (Bloomberg) — Canada’s biggest banks move to block personal AI agents
  • The Globe and Mail — Banks restrict AI agents’ access to client data
  • The Globe and Mail — BMO blocks third-party AI agents after RBC move
Community Note

The banks have not called the block permanent; some say it applies while they work out safeguards for letting customers authorize AI tools. Several banks urged customers not to share their banking passwords with any AI agent in the meantime.

Sources: RBC; Bloomberg via Insurance Journal; The Globe and Mail.

Poland’s top court splits in two as a judge suspends its chief, and protesters face police for a second night

Judge Anna Korwin-Piotrowska on Friday ordered Constitutional Tribunal president Bogdan Święczkowski and his deputy suspended for a month, and said she was taking charge as the longest-serving judge. Święczkowski, who was appointed under the former Law and Justice (PiS) government, rejected the order and called it a “coup d’état” (Politico Europe, Euronews, NBC News).

President Karol Nawrocki, a PiS ally, turned back from a trip to Senegal and accused Prime Minister Donald Tusk’s government of trying to seize the court. Police detained several people after scuffles outside the tribunal, and protesters returned on Saturday. Tusk wrote that “restoring the rule of law is our duty” (AP, Politico Europe, Euronews, NBC News).

Why it matters: The tribunal can strike down laws, so whoever controls it shapes Poland’s long fight with the EU over independent courts, and a key NATO ally on Ukraine’s border is now in a constitutional standoff.

Washington clears a US$2.1-billion sale of 500 Patriot interceptors to Ukraine, paid for partly by Canada

The U.S. State Department approved a possible sale to Ukraine of 500 PAC-3 MSE Patriot interceptors, plus parts and support, worth an estimated US$2.1 billion. Ukraine will pay with money from Canada, Germany and Norway routed through a U.S. program called Jumpstart, plus European Union loan funds. Lockheed Martin is the main contractor (AP, NBC News, The Guardian).

Volodymyr Zelenskyy welcomed the decision but warned on X that deliveries could start “ONLY IN A FEW YEARS” because the missiles have not been built yet, and he asked allies to hand over existing stock now and replace it later. People briefed on the deal told Reuters the missiles may not arrive until the early 2030s (The Guardian, Reuters via Investing.com, NBC News).

Why it matters: Patriots are Ukraine’s best shield against the ballistic missiles that keep hitting its cities and power grid, and Canadian contributions are helping pay for them.

DoorDash agrees to buy Instacart for about US$17 billion, and Canada’s Competition Bureau will take a look

DoorDash will pay US$65 a share for Instacart, a 26 per cent premium to its Oct. 22 closing price and about US$17 billion in all, with at least 75 per cent in cash. The deal needs shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected to close in late 2027 or the first half of 2028 (Instacart and DoorDash, Reuters via Investing.com, Global News).

Canada’s Competition Bureau told Global News it is reviewing the merger, and the Canadian Anti-Monopoly Project has urged the bureau to block it. In the U.S., the deal is expected to draw scrutiny from the Federal Trade Commission as grocery prices stay high (Global News, Canadian Anti-Monopoly Project, Reuters via Investing.com).

Why it matters: Both apps deliver groceries in Edmonton; merging the two biggest players could mean less competition over the fees and markups shoppers pay.

After 13 years of trying, the Bank of Japan says it has finally hit its 2% inflation goal

The Bank of Japan held its key interest rate at 1.25 per cent on Friday in an 8–1 vote, with one board member pushing for 1.5 per cent, and said underlying inflation had generally reached its 2 per cent target for the first time since it set the goal in 2013 (CNBC, Nikkei Asia, Euronews).

Governor Kazuo Ueda cautioned that the target is not yet being met in a lasting way, but the bank signalled more rate hikes ahead, and analysts see a move as soon as December (Nikkei Asia, The Asahi Shimbun, Reuters via Investing.com).

Why it matters: Japan spent decades fighting falling prices; rising rates there can pull Japanese money home from abroad and move bond markets and the yen worldwide.

Science corner

Science corner: a Tokyo microscope films the chemistry inside living cells in 3D, about 400 times faster than before

University of Tokyo physicists built a dye-free microscope that pulses mid-infrared light into a living cell. Each kind of molecule soaks up that light at its own frequency and warms very slightly, and visible light shone in from 11 angles picks up those tiny changes to rebuild a 3D chemical map of the cell (University of Tokyo, Nature Communications, Phys.org).

Earlier versions managed fewer than 0.05 3D images a second. The new system, called MIP-ODT, uses a fast light-steering device to switch angles and captures 19.2 volumes a second, one every 52 milliseconds, fast enough to follow fat droplets as they move around inside a cell (University of Tokyo, Phys.org, arXiv).

Why it matters: Watching cell chemistry move without toxic dyes could help researchers study the clumps of protein inside cells that are linked to neurodegenerative diseases.

Sources (4)
  • Nature Communications — Video-rate mid-infrared photothermal optical diffraction tomography (Fukushima et al.)
  • University of Tokyo — Press release on high-speed 3D chemical imaging of live cells
  • Phys.org — High-speed microscopy captures 3D images of living cells
  • arXiv — Preprint of the MIP-ODT study
Community Note

This is a research instrument, not a hospital scanner. Phys.org’s article is based on the university’s release; the findings are in the peer-reviewed Nature Communications paper led by Masato Fukushima and Takuro Ideguchi, with an earlier preprint on arXiv.

Sources: Nature Communications; University of Tokyo; Phys.org; arXiv.

AI news and advances

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic agree to honour one shared ‘don’t train on my site’ signal

The four companies say they will respect a single token in a website’s robots.txt file that tells their crawlers not to use the site’s content for AI training, instead of making publishers block each company’s crawler one by one. The signal comes from a proposal at the Internet Engineering Task Force written by Anthropic’s Kevin Kelley (The Verge, Engadget, The Next Web, OpenAI).

Anthropic already honours it; OpenAI says it will in the coming months, and Google and Microsoft plan to support it next year. Microsoft will also offer a separate opt-out from AI answers and says Bing search rankings won’t be affected. Meta voiced support but did not commit (The Verge, The Next Web, Engadget).

Why it matters: Writers, newsrooms and small sites get one simple switch to keep their work out of AI training, at least from the companies that choose to obey it.

Sources (5)
  • The Verge — OpenAI, Google and Microsoft back a single AI scraping opt-out
  • Engadget — Google, OpenAI and Anthropic agree on AI crawling controls
  • The Next Web — AI training opt-out comes to robots.txt
  • OpenAI — Helping websites express AI training preferences
  • IETF — AI training preference signal (Internet-Draft)
Community Note

The signal is voluntary and still a draft, not an approved internet standard, and it only covers training. It does not stop a site from being crawled, and companies that haven’t signed on are not bound by it.

Sources: The Verge; Engadget; The Next Web; OpenAI; IETF.

OpenAI trims its fundraising goal by US$10 billion as the AI stock slide makes investors cautious

OpenAI is now seeking up to about US$20 billion in its latest funding round, down from about US$30 billion, after the Financial Times first reported the cut. The FT said the target valuation also fell, to US$1.25 trillion from US$1.4 trillion, as some investors trimmed their commitments before a Friday deadline (CNBC, Bloomberg, TechCrunch).

A consortium from the United Arab Emirates including state-backed fund MGX is expected to be the biggest backer, alongside Founders Fund, Coatue, Sequoia and Andreessen Horowitz. The cut comes as tech stocks wobble: the Nasdaq fell 2.9 per cent on Friday and is 7.8 per cent below its Oct. 6 record (CNBC, Bloomberg, TechCrunch).

Why it matters: Even the most valuable AI start-up is feeling investor nerves about the cost of the AI build-out, the same mood that hit Tesla and SpaceX shares on Friday.

OFirefighters in Borneo dance, jump and kiss the ground as rain finally falls after months of wildfire smoke

Seen on: Instagram, where clips of the celebration were shared widely, as reported by The Indian Express and CNA.

Video from Palangkaraya, the capital of Indonesia’s Central Kalimantan province on Borneo, shows exhausted volunteer firefighters leaping, dancing and bowing to the ground as heavy rain poured down this week. Months of forest and peat fires had choked the city in smoke, with its air-quality index reported to have peaked around 4,000 (The Indian Express, CNA, Channels Television).

“We were overjoyed,” volunteer firefighter Lukman El Hakim said, explaining that the crew bowed down in gratitude. Similar rain-celebration clips spread from neighbouring South Kalimantan. Fires have burned roughly 1.9 million hectares across Indonesia this season (The Indian Express, Channels Television, CNA).

Why it matters: After months of choking smoke, it’s a moment of pure relief, and one Albertans who have lived through wildfire seasons will recognize.

Sources (4)

  • CNA — Indonesian firefighters celebrate as rain falls in Central and South Kalimantan
  • Channels Television — Indonesians celebrate rain after months of smoke and suffering

Community Note

The rain is a break, not the end: fires in drained peatland can smoulder underground, and dry weather may return before the rainy season fully sets in. The 4,000 air-quality reading is a reported local peak, far beyond the scale’s usual “hazardous” range.

Sources: The Indian Express; CNA; Channels Television; Lumi News.
