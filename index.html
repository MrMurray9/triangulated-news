“United in grief”: Carney tells the Commons about 50 investigators are now at Wainwright, with the RCMP helping military police find what started the fire

Prime Minister Mark Carney opened Monday’s sitting of the House of Commons with a tribute to the four soldiers killed at CFB Wainwright — Sgt. Morgan Wong of the Loyal Edmonton Regiment and Ptes. Aidan Goulding, Shaun Farrell and Hayden Lykkemark of 3rd Battalion, Princess Patricia’s Canadian Light Infantry. “The original cause of the fire is not yet known,” he said. The 39 injured are being treated at the University of Alberta and Royal Alexandra hospitals in Edmonton, Foothills in Calgary and the regional hospital in Red Deer. Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre, whose riding includes the base, and the Bloc, NDP and Green leaders followed with their own tributes (Global News, The Canadian Press).

The Globe and Mail reports that one key question for investigators is why live-fire training went ahead on a day Environment Canada had forecast high winds. In Edmonton, Legacy Place Society says it has had more than 1,500 calls from people offering places to stay for the soldiers’ families (Global News).

Why it matters: The investigation will show whether these deaths could have been prevented, and most of the injured soldiers are in Edmonton hospitals.

What changed since the last edition: This morning’s edition had the soldiers’ names, the general’s account of the wind-driven fire and the rush of blood donors. A B.C. judge rules the ban on “faint hope” early-parole reviews for multiple murderers unconstitutional — delaying sentencing again in the Abbotsford De Jong killings

B.C. Supreme Court Justice Brenda Brown ruled Monday that the Criminal Code section barring people convicted of more than one murder from using the “faint hope” clause infringes the Charter’s protection against cruel and unusual punishment. The clause lets some offenders ask for a judicial review of their parole ineligibility after serving 15 years (The Canadian Press, The Globe and Mail).

The ruling delays sentencing for Abhijeet Singh, Gurkaran Singh and Khushveer Toor, found guilty in May of first-degree murder in the 2022 home-invasion killings of Arnold and Joanne De Jong. Lawyers return in November to argue whether the infringement can be justified under Section 1 of the Charter, and sentencing is now set for February 2027. Joanne De Jong’s sister, Helen Leusink, said the family is frustrated and disappointed by another delay (The Canadian Press, Abbotsford News).

Why it matters: The ruling could reopen a route to earlier parole reviews for people convicted of multiple murders, if it holds up through the next arguments and any appeal.

Science corner: Japan launches MMX, a robotic sampler headed for Mars’ moon Phobos — aiming to be the first spacecraft to bring home soil from a Martian moon

Japan’s Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) spacecraft lifted off on an H3 rocket from Tanegashima Space Center at 1:41 p.m. MDT Monday (4:41 a.m. Tuesday in Japan) and separated from the upper stage about 71 minutes later, on course for Mars (Space.com, AP). It should arrive in August 2027 and settle into a “quasi-satellite” orbit that keeps it close to the 22-kilometre-wide Phobos.

The technology: MMX carries a small wheeled rover, IDEFIX, built by the French and German space agencies, to drive on Phobos in late 2028 or early 2029. The main spacecraft will touch down twice, gathering soil with a coring sampler and a NASA-funded pneumatic sampler built by Honeybee Robotics that uses bursts of gas to push grains into a container. A capsule carrying at least 10 grams of Phobos soil is due to land in Australia in 2031 (Space.com, NASA, ESA, JAXA).

Why it matters: The samples could settle a decades-old debate over whether Mars’ moons are captured asteroids or debris from a giant impact on Mars, a clue to how the inner planets formed.

Sources (5)

  • AP — Japan launches spacecraft to explore Martian moons and bring home soil
  • NASA Science — NASA to help unlock mysteries of Mars’ moons in upcoming mission
  • ESA — Mars moon lander set to launch on 19 October

The launch worked; the hard parts — reaching Mars, landing on Phobos and getting samples home — are years away, and the dates are plans. Russia’s Phobos-Grunt tried a Phobos sample return in 2011 but never left Earth orbit. NASA will receive at least 15 per cent of the returned sample.

Canada’s biggest news publishers sue Anthropic in Ontario, saying Claude was built on their journalism without a licence

Postmedia, Torstar, The Globe and Mail and The Canadian Press have sued Anthropic in Ontario Superior Court, alleging the AI company copied their articles without consent to build and run its Claude models. The claim, filed Oct. 6 and first reported by Cartt.ca, says Anthropic breached the sites’ terms of service, got around paywalls and other technical protections, and keeps scraping their content to answer user prompts (Cartt.ca, Broadcast Dialogue, The Canadian Press via Global News).

The publishers want damages and an injunction, and statutory damages under the Copyright Act can reach $20,000 per work infringed. Anthropic hadn’t responded to requests for comment. The same companies, along with CBC/Radio-Canada, sued OpenAI in 2024; that case hasn’t been settled (Global News, Broadcast Dialogue).

Why it matters: The case could help decide whether AI companies must pay Canadian newsrooms, since Ottawa’s national AI strategy left the copyright question open.

Amazon releases TorchNeuron so ordinary PyTorch code can run on its Trainium AI chips with one setting change — its biggest swing yet at Nvidia’s software lock-in

Amazon Web Services released TorchNeuron, free open-source software that plugs its Trainium2 and Trainium3 chips directly into PyTorch, the most widely used framework for building AI models. AWS says developers can move existing PyTorch code to Trainium with a single configuration change instead of rewriting it, using standard features such as eager mode, torch.compile and distributed training (Reuters, Amazon, PyTorch).

Nvidia’s dominance rests partly on CUDA, the software most AI code is written for; making Trainium easy to use from PyTorch is meant to lower the cost of switching. TechCrunch called it Amazon’s biggest shot at Nvidia yet (TechCrunch, Moneycontrol).

Why it matters: More competition for AI chips could lower the cost of training and running the AI tools people use every day.
