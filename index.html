Alberta

An early-morning fire wipes out three businesses on Mayerthorpe’s Main Street, including the town hardware store

The fire broke out before dawn on Saturday in the town of about 1,400 people, 135 km northwest of Edmonton. It destroyed Mayerthorpe Hardware, a gym called The Gym 2.0 and the health-food store Thyme for Health & Herbs, sent heavy smoke over downtown and forced temporary water restrictions while crews fought it. RCMP say the cause is still under investigation (Edmonton Journal, Rural Alberta Report, Raven Radio).

Mayor AJ Malcolm said he was at the scene by 4 a.m. and called the damage “absolutely heartbreaking.” In a Facebook statement he asked residents to look out for one another and to shop at “our remaining local businesses,” adding that “recovery will take time” (Edmonton Journal, Big West Country).

Why it matters: In a small town, losing the hardware store and two other shops at once takes away jobs and everyday services that people would otherwise drive to Whitecourt or Edmonton for.

Edmonton seizes 98 cats from one downtown home, and the city’s animal shelter stops taking in healthy strays

City animal protection officers carried out a search warrant on Thursday and found the cats living in extremely cramped conditions. All 98 are getting veterinary exams and treatment, and the city says the people involved are expected to be charged under Alberta’s Animal Protection Act (CityNews Edmonton, Edmonton Journal, CTV News).

With the Animal Care and Control Centre at more than double its capacity, it has paused intake of healthy stray and lost animals until further notice; injured or distressed animals are still accepted. “An operation of this scale requires significant resources,” said John Wilson, the city’s director of animal care and park rangers (CityNews Edmonton, Edmonton Journal).

Why it matters: If you find a healthy stray in Edmonton right now, the city shelter can’t take it, so neighbours and rescues will be filling the gap for a while.

Canada

Carney and Quebec’s sovereigntist premier finally meet, in Cambodia, and agree on homelessness money and a 2027 trade summit

What changed since the last edition: This morning’s Canada story on Carney’s Asia trip noted he was due to meet Quebec’s premier in Phnom Penh. That meeting has now happened, and both sides came away with agreements.

Paul St-Pierre Plamondon had insisted his first meeting with the prime minister be in Quebec City, while Carney offered Montreal or Gatineau. Six weeks after the Parti Québécois won the provincial election, they settled for 30 minutes on the sidelines of the Francophonie summit in Phnom Penh. The premier called it “a very good first meeting,” and said the two exchanged personal phone numbers (Global News, The Globe and Mail, Le Devoir).

He said Carney agreed to let Quebec choose the projects for its share of Ottawa’s new homelessness money, $11.2 billion over four years from 2027 in the federal budget, and backed Quebec City hosting a 2027 meeting of Francophonie trade ministers and businesses. Carney didn’t speak to reporters afterwards. The Globe reports the premier still plans to pursue his own outreach to the U.S. (Global News, The Globe and Mail).

Why it matters: Quebec now has a sovereigntist government, so how well these two get along matters for national unity, and the homelessness deal is a template other provinces, including Alberta, could be offered.

Yemen’s government pulls its last troops back from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, and Houthi fighters close to within 60 km of Aden

The Saudi-backed government said Sunday it had moved its forces away from Dhubab and the area around the strait to defend Aden, its temporary capital, and the provinces around it. Hours later, four military and two government sources told AFP the Houthis had taken two districts of Lahj province and reached the Al-Kharaz camp, about 60 km from the city. Officials say crews have been building sandbag defences and digging tunnels around Aden (France 24 with AFP, NBC News, The National).

The pullback leaves the Iran-aligned Houthis in control of the coast overlooking the strait, the narrow southern entrance to the Red Sea. Yemen’s presidency called the move “a calculated military move.” The Houthis tried and failed to take Aden in 2015, and an Oct. 15 attack on the presidential palace there killed four people (The National, Arab News, France 24).

Why it matters: Ships heading to and from the Suez Canal pass through Bab el-Mandeb, so Houthi control of both its shores raises the risk to global shipping on top of the trouble in the Strait of Hormuz.

Hong Kong developer New World asks shareholders for US$3.2 billion, selling 11 new shares for every one at an 81% discount

New World Development, one of Hong Kong’s biggest property companies, announced the HK$24.9-billion rights issue on Sunday at HK$0.90 a share, 81.5% below its last trading price. Most of the money will repay 10% of HK$82 billion in bank loans, and banks will push back the rest of those loans by two years once the deal closes (The Standard, South China Morning Post, Dimsum Daily).

The Cheng family’s Chow Tai Fook Enterprises will buy its full share, and together with HSBC and DBS will buy whatever other shareholders don’t. If nobody else takes part, the family’s stake would jump from about 46% to about 73%. It’s the company’s fourth rights issue in two decades, and the earlier three were at discounts of 36 to 38%. Trading in the shares and bonds resumes Monday morning in Hong Kong (The Standard, SCMP).

Why it matters: It’s a sign of how deep Hong Kong’s property slump has become: small shareholders who don’t pay up will see their stake shrink by about three-quarters.

AI is now the top worry for the U.S. financial system, named by 87% of people in a Federal Reserve survey, up from 50% in the spring

The Fed’s twice-yearly Financial Stability Report, released Friday, polls market participants, economists and analysts. Respondents pointed to three AI dangers: sky-high stock valuations that could crash if AI profits disappoint, cyberattacks by state hackers or by powerful AI models themselves, and the complicated borrowing used to pay for data centres (Federal Reserve, CNBC, Chosun Biz).

The next-biggest worry, a sharp drop in risky assets, was named by 52%, followed by higher long-term interest rates at 48%. The Fed said the system is otherwise fairly stable, but noted hedge-fund borrowing is at its highest level since 2013 (CNBC, Federal Reserve, Infobae).

Why it matters: AI stocks now make up a big part of the index funds in many Canadian RRSPs and pensions, so a warning like this from the U.S. central bank matters to ordinary savers too.

Science corner

Science corner: rubber turbine blades that bend with the tide make as much power as rigid ones, with far less wear

Underwater turbines in fast tidal channels take a beating from turbulence and waves. The biggest ones use motors, sensors and bearings, called pitch control, to twist their blades out of strong currents, and that machinery is costly and breaks down often. Engineers at the University of Edinburgh and tire-maker Michelin built blades out of flexible rubber around a stiff steel spine. As the current gets stronger, the back edge of each blade bends and flattens, shedding the extra force on its own (University of Edinburgh, Nature, heise online).

A 1.26-metre model turbine was tested in Edinburgh’s FloWave tank, a 25-metre round pool that recreates the turbulence at real tidal sites. Combined with simulations of a full tidal cycle, the rubber blades produced the same energy as a turbine with pitch control while cutting the rapid load swings that cause fatigue damage by up to 29%. Simulations suggest it could also work on a full-size 20-metre turbine (University of Edinburgh, heise online).

Why it matters: Unlike wind and sun, tides can be predicted years ahead, and simpler, tougher turbines could make that steady power cheap enough to use widely, including in places like the Bay of Fundy.

Sources (3)
  • University of Edinburgh: Flexible blades could make tidal power cheaper
  • Nature: Flexible rubber blades for tidal turbines
  • heise online: Flexible rubber rotors could make tidal power cheaper
Community Note

This is still lab-scale research: the team says much more testing is needed before any commercial turbine uses it.

Michelin helped fund the work and has filed a patent on the design.

Sources: University of Edinburgh; Nature; heise online

AI news and advances

Runway, known for AI video, gives away its robot-control model, which learns how the world moves by watching video

Praxis-1 is now free to download and use, including commercially, under the Apache 2.0 licence, with the code to run and fine-tune it. Built on Runway’s video-generation model, it predicts what a scene should look like next and the motions a robot needs to get there. Early partners have used it to drive a legged humanoid, a factory arm and a two-armed warehouse packing station (The Robot Report, The Decoder, Runway).

Runway says the model learned one partner’s warehouse task, picking up wooden planks and putting them in a container, from half an hour of examples and got it right 15 times out of 15. On Nvidia’s RoboLab-120 test it succeeded 46.2% of the time, ranking fifth overall on the leaderboard and first among open models that control a robot end to end. It runs on hardware from data-centre chips down to a robot’s onboard computer (The Robot Report, Runway).

Why it matters: Free, capable robot “brains” let smaller companies build useful machines without training their own models from scratch, which could speed up robots in warehouses and factories.

XStephen King jokes on X that “the aliens have landed” and that’s why U.S. flights were grounded, and millions of people play along

Seen on: X, where King posted it on Nov. 13; IBTimes UK, Marca and la Repubblica reported it drew millions of views.

As flights across the U.S. sat on the ground Friday, the horror writer posted: “The aliens have landed. That’s the REAL reason for the ground stop. They don’t want to start a worldwide panic.” He added a line claiming the aliens are blind but have excellent hearing. Other users piled on with “the alien mothership is here” jokes (IBTimes UK, Marca).

The real cause was far less exciting: officials blamed a telecommunications failure in old air traffic control equipment, and the Transportation Secretary called for US$50 billion to modernize it. Italian newspaper la Repubblica was among the outlets abroad that ran the post (IBTimes UK, la Repubblica).

Why it matters: When thousands of flights were cancelled, a well-timed joke from the king of horror gave a lot of stranded travellers something to laugh about.
