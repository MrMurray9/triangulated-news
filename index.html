Alberta

About 150 people lose their homes after a fire RCMP say was set on purpose rips through a downtown Fort McMurray apartment building

Wood Buffalo RCMP were called to a fire at 111 Charles Ave. in downtown Fort McMurray at about 10:37 p.m. on Monday, Nov. 16. Firefighters were already on scene, and crews needed pumpers, tankers, heavy rescue and aerial ladders before the fire was declared out shortly after 3 a.m. Everyone got out, but several people were hurt and paramedics took one person to hospital (Regional Municipality of Wood Buffalo, CTV News Edmonton).

Investigators concluded the fire was set intentionally. A 40-year-old Fort McMurray woman was arrested without incident and remains in custody while charges are prepared; police haven’t named her. The municipality’s emergency social services put about 150 displaced tenants into temporary housing and told them to contact the building’s property manager (CFWE, WestNet News, CTV News Edmonton).

Why it matters: Losing 150 homes at once is a big hit in a city where rentals are already tight heading into winter.

Albertans stuck at the registry because of missing birth, marriage or citizenship papers get three ways to renew their licence starting Thursday

Starting Thursday, Nov. 19, Albertans who can’t produce the citizenship documents now needed to renew a driver’s licence can apply under one of three exemptions. ID cards follow on Dec. 7. The streams cover long-time residents who can’t get old birth or citizenship records, people waiting on Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada or another agency to issue a document, and rare cases of exceptional hardship, such as papers lost in a fire or flood (Global News, Edmonton Journal, CityNews Calgary).

Each exemption needs a signed declaration. A licence renewed this way can last up to five years, but it won’t carry the new citizenship marker unless proof of citizenship is shown. Since the three-in-one cards launched in July, many people, especially married women whose names no longer matched their birth certificates, had been turned away at registries (Global News, Red Deer Advocate, The Albertan).

Why it matters: It should get people who have driven legally for decades back on the road without hunting down papers they may never find.

Canada

Meta tells senators it could pull WhatsApp and its other encrypted apps out of Canada if the lawful-access bill passes unchanged

Rachel Curran, Meta’s head of public policy in Canada, told the Senate national security committee on Monday that the company would have to consider withdrawing encrypted services, including WhatsApp, if Bill C-22 passes as written. She told The Globe and Mail that Meta would refuse any order to weaken or break its encryption: “That’s a red line for us” (The Globe and Mail, TechRadar, Rolling Stone Canada).

The bill would let the public safety minister issue secret orders requiring tech companies to build in capabilities for police and CSIS, and make them keep some customer metadata for six months. Signal has already said it would leave Canada. Public Safety Minister Gary Anandasangaree’s office says the bill doesn’t compel “backdoors” and that an amendment rules out forced decryption (The Globe and Mail, National Post).

Why it matters: WhatsApp is how many Canadian families and small businesses message each other, so a pull-out would be felt widely.

  • National Post (video): Meta would consider pulling WhatsApp from Canada
Community Note

Meta hasn’t said it will leave. It said it would “consider” its options if served with an order it believes weakens encryption.

C-22 is before the Senate and isn’t law yet. Apple pulled an advanced encryption feature from Britain after a similar order there.

A rockslide knocks a CN train off the tracks west of Terrace, B.C., sending containers into the Skeena River and two crew members to hospital

A CN train derailed at about 1 p.m. Tuesday roughly 15 kilometres west of Terrace, B.C., on the main line to Prince Rupert. CN said a rockslide caused it. Two locomotives and five intermodal cars loaded with forest products left the track, and several containers ended up in the Skeena River (Terrace Standard, CFNR, CTV News).

Paramedics took two CN employees to hospital in serious but stable condition, and Terrace firefighters helped free one of them. CN said diesel was leaking from a locomotive’s fuel tank and it was working to contain it. Highway 16 was closed for a time and has reopened to alternating single-lane traffic (Terrace Standard, CFTK, CTV News).

Why it matters: The line carries much of the freight moving through the Port of Prince Rupert, and the river is important salmon habitat.

The U.S. is no longer measles-free after 26 years, as health officials rule the virus has spread nonstop for more than a year

An independent commission of the Pan American Health Organization ruled on Tuesday that measles is endemic again in the United States, which had held elimination status since 2000. The commission found the same strain had circulated continuously from January 2025 to January 2026. Mexico and Bolivia also lost their status (NBC News, PAHO, ABC News).

The U.S. has recorded more than 5,400 cases this year, the most since 1991, and 10 deaths, including at least four babies too young to be vaccinated. The CDC and its partners said they would push MMR vaccination and expand access to shots, and the U.S. is expected to send PAHO a corrective plan within weeks (NBC News, CDC, UNICEF).

Why it matters: Endemic measles next door raises the risk of new outbreaks in Canada, which is still working to win back its own status.

Israel’s new parliament picks Matan Kahana, an ally of Netanyahu rival Gadi Eisenkot, as Speaker in a 66-52 vote

Israel’s Knesset elected Matan Kahana of the Yashar party as Speaker late Tuesday with all 66 opposition-bloc votes, beating Likud’s Ofir Katz, who got 52. Kahana replaces Likud’s Amir Ohana, Speaker since 2022, and said he intends to be “the Speaker for everyone” (AP, i24NEWS, Ynet).

It’s the first big vote in the new Knesset since Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu lost his majority in this fall’s election. Eisenkot called it “a new chapter” for the Knesset. Likud called it “a new low,” and far-right leaders attacked the Arab lawmakers who backed Kahana (AFP, i24NEWS, Ynet).

Why it matters: The Speaker controls the Knesset’s agenda, so the vote shows who has the numbers as Eisenkot tries to form a government.

Target posts its best sales growth in five years, with store traffic up 5%, and raises its outlook for the year

Target said Wednesday that sales rose 7.1% in its third quarter, which ended Oct. 31, and comparable sales jumped 5.6%, well ahead of the roughly 3.8% analysts expected. More shoppers came through the doors, with traffic up 5%, and digital comparable sales climbed almost 10%. Revenue was US$27.07 billion and profit was US$1.21 billion, or US$2.65 a share (CNBC, Target, Yahoo Finance).

CEO Michael Fiddelke said shoppers are “responding to our changes.” Target now expects sales to grow about 6% this year, up from 5%, and raised its earnings forecast to US$10.65–$11.40 a share. Beauty was up 9% and food nearly 7%, and the chain cut prices on about 2,000 more items during the quarter (CNBC, U.S. News/Reuters, Target).

Why it matters: It’s a good early read on how U.S. shoppers are spending heading into the holiday season.

Science corner

Science corner: X-ray scans of a 200-million-year-old skull reveal Nyana, a new South African dinosaur that weighed 400 kg as a three-year-old

Researchers led by Atashni Moopen of the University of the Witwatersrand have named a new dinosaur, Nyana rooiwalensis, from a near-complete skull and some bones found in 2018 in South Africa’s Eastern Cape. It lived in the Late Triassic, more than 200 million years ago, and was an early relative of giant long-necked dinosaurs like Brachiosaurus (Royal Society Open Science, Phys.org, Sci.News).

The team used powerful X-ray scanning to build a 3D model of the fragile skull without damaging it, then compared it with relatives to place Nyana on the family tree. Thin slices of its leg bones showed it was at least three years old and still growing fast. The thigh bone suggests it already weighed about 400 kilograms, and adults may have reached several tonnes (Phys.org, Sci.News).

Why it matters: Skulls this old are rare in southern Africa, and this one shows early sauropod relatives grew big very quickly.

Sources (3)
  • Royal Society Open Science: Nyana rooiwalensis, South Africa’s earliest near-complete sauropodomorph skull
  • Phys.org: A newly discovered South African dinosaur was a heavyweight even as a youngster
  • Sci.News: South African fossil reveals new species of early sauropodomorph
Community Note

The 400 kg figure is an estimate from the thigh bone’s size, not a weighed fossil.

The researchers also see a link to similar dinosaurs in North America, which they say needs more study.

Sources: Royal Society Open Science; Phys.org; Sci.News

Microsoft unveils seven of its own AI models at Ignite, pitching them as close to rivals’ quality for a small part of the price

At its Ignite conference on Tuesday, Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman launched seven new MAI models: MAI Code 2 and Code 2 Flash for programming, MAI Cyber 2 Flash for security work, MAI Image 2.7 for photo editing, MAI Realtime 1 for live voice conversations, the Dragon Copilot Transcribe medical note-taker, and a fast decision model called Decision 1.1 (Microsoft AI, Thurrott, Times of India).

Microsoft says MAI Code 2 delivers 98% of the coding quality of OpenAI’s GPT-6.1 Sol at about 40% of the price, and that MAI Realtime 1 starts at one cent per audio minute. All seven run on Microsoft’s own Maia 200 chips, and a coming option will let companies run and customize the models on their own Azure setups (Thurrott, Microsoft AI, Microsoft).

Why it matters: Microsoft is building its own models instead of relying only on OpenAI, and price competition could make AI tools cheaper.

FA woman’s Facebook post reaches 6 million people and finds the university mentor in the photo she has kept in her wallet for 30 years

Seen on: Facebook: Delta Farrington’s post asking for help identifying her childhood mentor drew about 6 million views, ABC11 reported.

When Delta Farrington was 7, she was paired with a University of North Carolina student through UNC’s Big Buddy program in Chapel Hill. She kept a photo of the two of them in her wallet for decades but forgot her mentor’s name. Now 39, she shared the photo and her story on Facebook asking for help (ABC11, Hoodline).

A tip led her to email a possible match, and the reply came back: “Oh my gosh. Delta, it’s me.” Her mentor was Keli Rytter, now an accounting professor in Pennsylvania. On their first video call in about 30 years, Farrington held up the photo. “Kindness is free,” Rytter said. “You just never know when somebody’s going to take it with them for 30 years” (ABC11).

Why it matters: It’s a reminder that small kindnesses to kids can stick for a lifetime.
