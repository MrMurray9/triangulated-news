Alberta

Alberta tables a law that would let the transportation minister veto new bike lanes and order cities to rip out old ones at their own cost

Transportation Minister Devin Dreeshen introduced the Safe and Sensible Roads Act on Thursday. If passed, no new bike lane could take away space from cars unless he signs off, and the province could order existing lanes removed when they reduce road capacity. Cities would pay for any removal (Global News, Edmonton Journal, CityNews Edmonton).

Dreeshen said the province would use traffic studies and work with Edmonton and Calgary to pick out lanes that “don’t make sense,” ideally removing them during already planned road work. The same omnibus legislation sets safety standards for privately owned e-scooters, raises penalties for unsafe trucking companies and gives the province more say over city LRT projects. It would take effect next spring (Global News, Canada’s National Observer).

Why it matters: Edmonton and Calgary decide their own street layouts today; this moves the final say on bike lanes, including busy routes like Edmonton’s Hermitage Road, to the province.

Sources (4)
  • Global News: Alberta tables law giving itself power to remove municipal bike lanes
  • Edmonton Journal: Alberta bill would give the province control over bike lanes
  • CityNews Edmonton: Alberta proposes law for power to remove municipal bike lanes
  • Canada’s National Observer: Now Alberta wants to get rid of its bike lanes, too
Community Note

The legislation doesn’t yet say what criteria a lane must meet to survive or how cities can win an exemption; those details are left to regulations due in the spring.

The province’s online bike-lane portal, which drew more than 14,000 submissions, only offered options for complaints, not support. Ontario and Nova Scotia have passed similar laws.

Sources: Global News; Canada’s National Observer; Edmonton Journal

A mother and her partner are charged with manslaughter after a three-year-old girl died of carfentanil poisoning in central Edmonton

Edmonton police say Amiraa Fraynn was found dead in a suite near 91 Street and Jasper Avenue on May 23. Toxicology tests later showed carfentanil, an opioid far stronger than fentanyl, caused her death (Edmonton Police Service, Global News, Edmonton Journal).

Her mother, Anthea Fraynn, 30, was arrested Oct. 31, and William Okeymow, 29, who police say was her partner at the time, was arrested in Calgary on Nov. 6. Both face manslaughter, failing to provide the necessaries of life and criminal negligence causing death. Police say they aren’t looking for anyone else (Edmonton Police Service, CityNews Edmonton, Global News).

Why it matters: Carfentanil has become the main opioid in Edmonton’s overdose deaths, and this case shows it reaching young children at home.

Sources (4)
  • Edmonton Police Service: Police lay charges in death of toddler
  • Global News: Drug overdose death of girl, 3, leads to mother, partner charged in Edmonton
  • Edmonton Journal: Couple charged after 3-year-old toddler dies of carfentanil poisoning
  • CityNews Edmonton: Edmonton mother charged in her toddler’s drug poisoning death
Community Note

The charges haven’t been proven in court, and both accused are presumed innocent. Police said the medical examiner still hasn’t ruled on the manner of death.

Global News, citing provincial data, reports carfentanil was involved in 78% of Edmonton opioid deaths from January to May 2025, up from 17% the year before.

Sources: Edmonton Police Service; Global News

Canada

MPs on the major projects bill committee will sit through the weekend after Liberals voted down every attempt to bring back its 2030 sunset clause

The special committee studying Bill C-39, the Building Canada Strong Act, met from 9 a.m. to midnight on Thursday, and chair James Maloney told The Globe and Mail it will keep going through the weekend, which rarely happens on Parliament Hill (The Globe and Mail, House of Commons).

The 240-plus-page bill would make permanent cabinet’s power to fast-track projects it deems in the national interest, which last year’s law had set to expire in 2030. Conservative, Bloc and NDP amendments to keep an expiry date were rejected by the Liberal majority. Bloc MP Gabriel Ste-Marie called the 16-hour days “a tactic to wear down MPs” (The Globe and Mail, Environmental Defence coalition release).

Why it matters: The bill decides how quickly pipelines, mines and ports can be approved, including projects in Alberta, and the rules would no longer have an end date.

Sources (3)

  • Environmental Defence, Ecojustice, West Coast Environmental Law, UBCIC: Government MPs reject Indigenous rights, environmental safeguards in Bill C-39 review
Community Note

Conservatives support the bill’s main goal; it passed second reading 296-26 in October with their votes. Their objection is to removing the sunset and to the pace.

Environmental Defence and its partners are advocacy groups opposed to the bill. Business groups back its Canada Labour Code changes, which unions say limit the right to strike.

Denmark’s parliament votes 109-0 for the Greenland defence pact with the U.S., leaving only Trump’s notice to Congress before it takes effect

Danish MPs unanimously approved the deal signed in September by President Trump, Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen and Greenland Premier Jens-Frederik Nielsen. Greenland’s own parliament approved it earlier this month (The Guardian, AP, Danish Parliament).

The pact updates a 1951 defence agreement. It gives the U.S. underwater access around Greenland and permission to build a missile defence system, treats Greenland as an equal partner, and says Greenland would stay in NATO if it became independent. Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen said it “removes all uncertainty,” but that trust with the U.S. must be rebuilt (The Guardian, Euronews).

Why it matters: It closes, at least on paper, a standoff that saw Trump threaten to take the island and Denmark prepare for the worst, and it shapes NATO’s Arctic defences next door to Canada.

U.S. Ambassador Ken Howery welcomed the 109-0 vote. The Guardian reports Denmark had sent blood supplies and explosives to Greenland in January in case of a U.S. attack.

The U.S. and Venezuela agree to restart military cooperation, with joint training and intelligence sharing, as Hegseth visits Caracas

Secretary of War Pete Hegseth signed the agreement with Venezuela’s interim government on Wednesday, less than a year after U.S. forces captured Nicolás Maduro. He said it covers joint training, strategic cooperation, intelligence sharing and the supply of U.S. defence equipment (AP, EFE via Clarín, U.S. Department of War).

Hegseth, who the Pentagon says is the first U.S. defence chief to visit in more than a century, told drug cartels “your time is up.” Venezuela’s defence ministry said the cooperation will respect its sovereignty and constitution, which bans foreign military bases (U.S. Department of War, EFE via Clarín).

Why it matters: It reverses two decades of hostility that began when Hugo Chávez cut military ties in 2005, and makes Venezuela a U.S. partner in its campaign against cartels.

Walmart raises its yearly forecast for the third time as online sales jump 17%, but its shares slip on worries about diesel costs

Walmart earned an adjusted 65 cents a share on revenue of US$188.8 billion in the quarter ended Oct. 31, just ahead of analysts’ forecasts. It now expects sales to grow 4.9% to 5.2% this year. Global e-commerce sales rose 17%, and its U.S. advertising business grew 37% (CNBC, AP).

Why it matters: Walmart is a bellwether for how stretched shoppers are heading into the holidays, and its pricing affects grocery competition with its Canadian stores too.

Science corner

Science corner: MIT makes a dry, skin-like pad that replaces sticky ultrasound gel and keeps a wearable scanner imaging for nine days

Ultrasound needs gel because sound travels poorly through air and most solids. Wearable scanners that use water-based gel stop working once it dries out in a day or two. Xuanhe Zhao’s lab at MIT built a soft rubber instead, from “bottlebrush” molecules whose bristly side chains slide past each other with little friction, so sound passes through almost as easily as through liquid (MIT News, Science).

A second molecular network keeps the soft rubber from crumbling, making it stretchy like skin. In tests it gave clear, continuous images for about nine days, wicked away sweat on a treadmill, and tracked a volunteer’s neck blood flow through a hilly 30-minute bike ride. The study was published Thursday in Science (MIT News, Inside Precision Medicine).

Why it matters: Patches that can scan the heart, blood vessels or muscles for days could let doctors monitor patients at home instead of in a clinic.

Sources (3)
  • MIT News: Ultrasound imaging without the gel
  • Science: Interpenetrating bottlebrush elastomer for low-loss soft ultrasonic interfaces
  • Inside Precision Medicine: Gel-free material could enable continuous ultrasound monitoring
Community Note

This is a lab result. The nine-day run was on a synthetic “phantom” model, and the on-body tests lasted minutes, not days.

The team is still working on making the material easier to produce before it can be used in real devices.

Sources: MIT News; Science; Inside Precision Medicine

AI news and advances

Isomorphic Labs, the AI drug-design company spun out of DeepMind, raises US$5 billion to take its first medicines toward human trials

The Series C was co-led by Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital, with Andreessen Horowitz joining and existing backers including Alphabet’s GV and Thrive Capital putting in more. It comes six months after a US$2.1-billion round in May (TechCrunch, Fierce Biotech, Axios).

Isomorphic builds on AlphaFold, the protein-structure AI that earned Demis Hassabis and John Jumper a Nobel Prize. It says its engine has produced positive early results in cancer and immune diseases, and it has deals with Eli Lilly, Novartis and Johnson & Johnson. The money will go toward clinical trials and new kinds of medicines (TechCrunch, Isomorphic Labs).

Why it matters: It’s one of the biggest bets yet that AI can shorten the decade or more it usually takes to bring a new drug to patients.

No Isomorphic-designed drug has been tested in people yet; “positive preclinical results” means lab and animal studies.

The company didn’t disclose its valuation. Bloomberg had reported it was seeking about US$50 billion. The round was announced Nov. 17.
