Alberta

Flu shots open to every Albertan as influenza arrives early — book online, call 811 or try a pharmacy

Primary Care Alberta opened influenza immunization bookings to the general public on Monday, Oct. 19. Albertans can find the nearest clinic or participating pharmacy through the province’s booking site, or call Health Link at 811; where you go depends on your age and which vaccines you need (Edmonton Journal, Government of Alberta). The flu shot is free for everyone in Alberta aged six months and older (Government of Alberta).

The season has started early and hard. In the week of Sept. 27 to Oct. 3, the province recorded 21 flu hospitalizations, two of them in intensive care, and 194 lab-confirmed cases, the Edmonton Journal reports. Pharmacies were allowed to start before the public rollout, and infectious-disease specialist Dr. Lynora Saxinger and emergency physician Dr. Warren Thirsk both urged people not to wait (CTV News Edmonton).

Why it matters: Vaccines take about two weeks to work, so booking now gives your family protection before flu season peaks.

Polls finally close at 10 p.m. after late openings at 106 stations — Alberta’s hand count on separation runs into the small hours

Voting in Alberta’s 10-question referendum ran past 8 p.m. at 106 locations that had opened late or closed temporarily. Chief Electoral Officer Gordon McClure extended their hours so voters got the full advertised time, with closings ranging from 8:10 p.m. to 10 p.m. (Elections Alberta, Global News). The last to close was Westminster School in Edmonton’s Glenora neighbourhood, which closed for reconfiguration and didn’t reopen until around noon; some voters there waited about two hours (CityNews).

Under election rules, no results can be released until every location has closed, so unofficial results only start after 10 p.m. (Edmonton Journal). Every ballot is counted by hand because the province has banned vote tabulators. Elections Alberta estimates full results on Question 10, on separation, between 1 a.m. and 4 a.m. Tuesday, with Questions 1–9 counted later Tuesday and into Wednesday (Edmonton Journal). More than 1.3 million ballots, about 43 per cent of eligible voters, were cast before voting day through advance polls and mail-in special ballots, and Elections Alberta forecasts 80–85 per cent turnout (Edmonton Journal, National Post).

Why it matters: If you’re waiting up for the separation result, it’s likely to come before breakfast. Results on the other nine questions, including immigration and the Senate, will take until mid-week.

What changed since the last edition: The evening edition covered voting day while polls were open. New: 106 stations kept open late, the last closing at 10 p.m., which delayed the release of any results, plus Elections Alberta’s 1–4 a.m. window for the Question 10 count.

Canada

Canada’s two-year measles outbreak is officially over — after more than 6,400 infections in 10 provinces and the loss of elimination status

The Public Health Agency of Canada declared the multi-jurisdictional measles outbreak over on Monday. It began in New Brunswick in October 2024 and infected more than 6,400 people across 10 provinces. About a year in, the chain of transmission had lasted more than 12 months, which cost Canada its measles-elimination status (Public Health Agency of Canada, The Canadian Press).

Two infants in Ontario and Alberta who caught measles in the womb died after birth. Ontario declared its outbreak over last fall and Alberta’s ended in July; Manitoba’s last lab-confirmed case was in August. Chief Public Health Officer Dr. Joss Reimer warned that “measles continues to circulate globally, and we can expect to see further travel-related cases here at home” (The Canadian Press, Global News).

Why it matters: The outbreak is over, but measles still spreads fast wherever vaccination rates drop, so it’s worth checking your family’s shot records.

Sources (3)
  • Public Health Agency of Canada — Multi-jurisdictional measles outbreak is over (Oct. 19)
  • The Canadian Press (via The Globe and Mail) — Measles outbreak over after two years, PHAC says
  • Global News — Canada’s 2-year measles outbreak officially over: PHAC
Community Note

“Over” means no new outbreak-linked cases for a full monitoring period; it doesn’t mean Canada has its elimination status back yet, which requires sustained proof of no ongoing spread. Experts quoted by The Canadian Press link measles’ return to falling vaccination rates, misinformation and pandemic-era gaps in routine shots.

Sources: Public Health Agency of Canada; The Canadian Press; Global News.

Saskatchewan senator Pamela Wallin, 73, resigned effective Oct. 18, two years before the mandatory retirement age of 75, according to a notice from the Clerk of the Senate (Global News, The Canadian Press). “I am happy and healthy and want to do some other things,” the former broadcaster told 650 CKOM. “I’m a believer that you shouldn’t go to the Senate for life, and I probably stayed a little longer than I thought I would.”

Why it matters: Her departure opens another Saskatchewan Senate seat, just as Alberta voters weigh a referendum question on abolishing the Senate.

Kenyan doomsday preacher Paul Mackenzie is found guilty of murdering 190 children and one adult in the Shakahola starvation cult

A High Court judge in Mombasa found self-proclaimed pastor Paul Mackenzie and his co-accused guilty on Monday of 191 counts of murder. “I am satisfied that the prosecution has proved the case against all the 29 accused persons for the murder of 190 children and one adult,” Justice Diana Kavedza Mochache said (AFP, Daily Nation). Prosecutors said Mackenzie’s Good News International Church told followers to starve themselves and their children to death so they could reach heaven before the world ended (The Guardian).

Bodies began turning up in shallow graves in Shakahola forest, inland from Malindi, after his arrest in 2023, and more than 400 have now been found. Prosecutors described a “structured hierarchy of death” in which children were to die first. Mackenzie denied the charges. “I know that my people will not come back again, but at least something has happened,” said Francis Wanje, who lost eight relatives (AFP).

Why it matters: It’s one of the deadliest cult cases in modern history, and the verdict adds to calls in Kenya for tougher oversight of religious groups — Mackenzie had been arrested twice before and released.

A drone strikes twice at a Sudanese market-day transport hub, killing 80 villagers waiting for rides home

A drone hit a transport hub near the market in Al-Dibab, in Sudan’s West Kordofan state, on Sunday evening, killing 80 people, a medical source and a witness told AFP. Most were villagers waiting for trucks home after market day. The drone struck twice, 10 minutes apart, said Ahmed Abakar Wadi, who helped bury 50 bodies, including his brother (AFP, The Guardian).

Another 47 wounded were taken to nearby Al-Mujlad, where 30 died. The Rapid Support Forces’ self-declared administration, Tasis, blamed the army; all of West Kordofan is under RSF control. Al Jazeera reports more than 160 people killed in drone attacks across Sudan since Friday, including three children in Khartoum on Monday (Al Jazeera, AFP).

Why it matters: Drones are now a leading killer of civilians in Sudan’s war, which aid workers say has killed more than 200,000 people and caused the world’s largest hunger and displacement crisis.

Booking Air Canada by phone now costs $30 a passenger — $120 for a family of four

Since Oct. 15, every new Air Canada booking made through its contact centre carries a non-refundable $30 fee per passenger, plus tax. Booking on aircanada.com or the app stays free (National Post, Air Canada). Aeroplan reward bookings by phone have carried a similar fee since the mid-2000s (National Post).

The fee doesn’t apply if you call because a flight you already booked is delayed, cancelled or disrupted, and customers who need accessibility support are exempt (Travelweek, Milesopedia).

Why it matters: Families who book by phone pay more, so it’s worth booking online and saving calls for problems.

Science corner

Science corner: 10,000 fresh cracks show the moon has shrunk by about 45 metres — and it’s still contracting

Mapping images from NASA’s Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter, a team led by Smithsonian geologist Thomas Watters counted more than 10,000 young faults on the moon: lobate scarps, where the crust is shoved up into curved cliffs, and small wrinkle-like ridges on the dark lunar plains. Together they show the moon’s radius has shrunk by about 45 metres (148 feet) over the past 400 to 800 million years as its interior cools (Space.com, Smithsonian). The study appeared Oct. 14 in The Planetary Science Journal.

“The number of these young faults revealed in LROC images is much greater than I ever imagined,” Watters said, adding that new faults are expected to keep forming today. Because the moon is one solid plate, its faults are scattered everywhere rather than lined up along plate edges, and regions with thicker crust have fewer of them (Space.com, NDTV).

Why it matters: Fresh faults can still slip and cause moonquakes, which matters for where future lunar bases are built.

Sources (4)
  • Space.com — The moon is shrinking. 10,000 cracks on the lunar surface reveal by how much
  • Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum — New study reveals how much the moon is shrinking
  • NDTV — Shrinking moon has 10,000 new faults
  • The Planetary Science Journal — Thrust faulting and the late-stage contraction of the moon
Community Note

45 metres is tiny next to the moon’s radius of about 1,737 km, so you’ll never notice it from Earth. The 400- to 800-million-year span is an estimate based on how fresh the faults look.

Sources: Space.com; Smithsonian; NDTV; The Planetary Science Journal.

AI news and advances

One hacker’s “army” of AI agents broke into more than 100 company websites — including John Deere and SharkNinja — with little human help

Security firm Norforge says it found the attacks through exposed work on Cairn, an open-source AI platform that calls itself a “general-purpose problem-solving engine.” The most active attacker used it to send AI agents after more than 100 websites in the U.S., Canada, Australia and Japan, mostly on their own. The hacker set out preferred intrusion tactics in fewer than 100 Chinese characters, and successful breaches took about 23 hours each (The Verge, citing Reuters; Norforge).

John Deere told Reuters there was “no indication that sensitive information was accessed,” and SharkNinja said a stand-alone product-support site was hit and the threat contained. Cummins says it is investigating a possible attack on a site run by a vendor (The Verge, Tom’s Hardware). Norforge found more than 20 exposed Cairn instances it calls “likely malicious.” Cairn’s creator says it “was never a ‘hacking tool’” (The Verge).

Why it matters: Breaking into websites used to take skilled people working for a long time. Now one person can point AI agents at a list of targets and leave them to it, so even small companies need to tighten their defences.

Sources (4)

  • Tom’s Hardware — A Chinese hacker used AI agents to break into more than 100 company websites
  • NDTV — How a Chinese-speaking hacker used AI agents to breach over 100 companies
  • Norforge — Inside Cairn: open windows into massive AI-driven cyber operations
Community Note

Norforge didn’t name the victims; the company names come from Reuters’ reporting. “Chinese-speaking” describes the language of the instructions, not a confirmed nationality or government link. The companies say no sensitive data was taken, as far as they know.
