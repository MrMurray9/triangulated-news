Alberta

Alberta scraps parental income from student-aid math for 2026–27, and will reassess about 21,000 applications

Alberta Student Aid says that as of Nov. 3 it will no longer use an expected parental contribution when it decides who gets a provincial student loan for the 2026–27 loan year. About 21,000 applications will be reassessed automatically. Students who qualify for more funding will get a notice in their Student Aid account and do not need to reapply (Alberta Student Aid, CTV News Calgary, StrathmoreNow).

The change reverses a rule that took effect earlier this year and drew pushback from student unions at the University of Alberta and Mount Royal University. Spousal income and a doubled student contribution of $3,000 per loan year remain part of the assessment. Advocates call the parental-income U-turn a win but say the higher student contribution still squeezes learners who work part-time (CTV News Calgary, The Griff / Mount Royal, Alberta Student Aid).

Why it matters: Thousands of Alberta students who were dinged because their parents’ income looked high on paper may get more provincial loan room without filing a new form.

Mayor Knack calls Alberta’s new 91-seat map “textbook gerrymandering,” pointing to Edmonton-Nisku’s odd borders

What changed: Since Thursday morning’s coverage of UCP MLAs turning the 91-seat map into a bill, Edmonton’s mayor and at least one councillor have gone on the record calling the map gerrymandering and highlighting hybrid urban-rural seats.

Edmonton Mayor Andrew Knack told the Edmonton Journal that the provincial electoral map now moving through the legislature is “textbook gerrymandering,” after an independent commission’s maps were rejected and replaced. He singled out Edmonton-Nisku as looking like “the most complicated Tetris piece,” mixing neighbourhoods inside and outside the Anthony Henday with the airport. Ward papastew Coun. Michael Janz said elected officials shouldn’t set their own boundaries (Edmonton Journal, Postmedia).

Justice Minister Mickey Amery defended the map, saying Albertans deserve “meaningful and effective representation” and citing an advisory panel chaired by retired Court of Appeal justice Brian O’Ferrall that included government and Official Opposition appointees. Edmonton gains one seat under the plan. The next provincial general election is scheduled for Oct. 18, 2027 (Edmonton Journal, Government of Alberta).

Why it matters: City hall’s pushback keeps the riding-map fight in the news as the bill advances, and it frames how Edmontonians may see the 2027 vote.

Canada

Bank of Canada and Ottawa renew the 2% inflation target through 2031, keeping the 1–3% control range

In a joint statement dated Nov. 5, the Government of Canada and the Bank of Canada renewed the monetary-policy framework for another five years, ending Dec. 31, 2031. The inflation target stays at 2%, the midpoint of the 1% to 3% consumer-price-index control range. Policy will still treat misses above and below the target symmetrically, with flexibility to respond to shocks while aiming to bring inflation back to target over time (Bank of Canada, Yahoo Finance / Reuters, Globe and Mail morning briefing).

The statement says the past five years of supply shocks and geopolitical conflict tested the framework’s implementation, not its design, and that the Bank has strengthened how it analyzes supply-side shocks. The renewal was unveiled the same day as Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne’s budget (Bank of Canada, Reuters market wrap).

Why it matters: Mortgage holders, renters and businesses get another five years of the same clear inflation anchor, even as growth and trade remain uncertain.

Ottawa will process permanent residency for 200,000 temporary residents already in the country

Immigration reporting on the new levels plan says the federal government will open a one-time track to permanent residency for 200,000 people who are already in Canada on temporary status. That sits on top of the regular permanent-resident target of 380,000 a year for 2027–29. Two cohorts of 100,000 each get priority: a workers’ group drawn from pathways such as the Start-up Visa, home-care pilots, the Atlantic Immigration Program and the Agri-Food Pilot, and a humanitarian group covering remaining Ukraine family-reunification and Hong Kong pathway files plus spouses and children already here (The Globe and Mail, Reuters market wrap).

Temporary-resident intake targets stay at 370,000 a year over the same three years. Officials frame the measure as clearing a backlog rather than raising the headline permanent-resident cap (The Globe and Mail).

Why it matters: Employers and families stuck in temporary status get a clearer queue, separate from the usual annual immigration targets.

About 600,000 people march across France over living costs as unions back a day of strikes

France’s Interior Ministry put Thursday’s nationwide turnout at about 600,000 people (some tallies said about 575,000), while the CGT union claimed close to 1.6 million. Workers and students demanded higher wages, a stronger minimum wage and relief from living costs. Teachers, rail, hospital and postal staff joined strikes; education officials said about 16% of teachers were out (AP, AFP, DW, Al Jazeera, The Guardian).

Demonstrations were mostly peaceful, though police used tear gas in places and outlets reported hundreds of arrests and dozens of injured officers in Paris and Lyon. The government said it would hold a “social conference” with unions and employers on purchasing power (AP, France 24, DW).

Why it matters: A large Western European strike wave over wages is a live risk for markets and for Canada’s European trade partners heading into winter.

Sweden will bring back internal Schengen border checks for six months starting Nov. 12

The Swedish government said it will reintroduce controls at its internal Schengen borders — land, air and sea, including the land border with Denmark — from Nov. 12, 2026, through May 11, 2027. Police will decide where and how checks run. Ministers cited a serious threat to public order and internal security (Government Offices of Sweden, Krisinformation.se, Arab News).

Temporary internal border controls are allowed under Schengen rules when a member state notifies the European Commission. Sweden has used similar measures before; this round is a fresh six-month window (European Commission Schengen pages, Swedish government release).

Why it matters: Anyone flying or driving between Sweden and its Nordic neighbours should expect longer queues and ID checks through the spring.

Ottawa will give OSFI a secondary competition goal and aims to lift CDIC coverage to $250,000

The federal fiscal plan proposes amending the law that governs the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions so OSFI has a secondary objective to promote competition, alongside its stability mandate. It also sets a one-year clock for decisions on applications to create new federally regulated lenders, raises the widely held shareholding threshold from $12 billion to $25 billion in equity, and eases growth rules for smaller foreign subsidiaries and credit unions (The Globe and Mail, Investment Executive).

The same package would update the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation framework by raising the basic coverage limit to $250,000 from $100,000 and removing the cap on eligible deposits in registered and tax-free accounts. None of that is in force yet; CDIC’s current limit remains $100,000 per category until legislation and regulations catch up (The Globe and Mail, Finance Canada deposit-insurance review materials).

Why it matters: Higher deposit insurance and a clearer on-ramp for new lenders would change how Canadians shop for savings accounts — if Parliament and regulators follow through.

Science corner

Science corner: a battery-free toothbrush that makes its own electricity to zap plaque bacteria

Researchers describe a manual toothbrush whose polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) bristles generate a small electric charge when they bend during brushing. That charge helps create reactive oxygen species in saliva that damage bacterial membranes. In a three-month trial of 109 adults, the brush cut plaque, gum inflammation and bleeding more than a conventional brush (Science Advances, Nature briefing, New Scientist, Live Science).

The team reports no short-term toxicity to tooth surfaces or human cells in lab tests. Long-term safety, durability and cost are still open questions before any consumer product could ship (Nature, New Scientist).

Why it matters: A brush that needs no battery yet still delivers a mild antibacterial boost would be a neat everyday health gadget — if larger trials agree.

Google says Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash is generally available through the Gemini API and Google Cloud.
