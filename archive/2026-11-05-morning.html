Alberta

UCP MLAs vote to turn Alberta’s contested 91-seat riding map into a bill, as Nenshi vows a court fight

Premier Danielle Smith’s United Conservatives used their majority on Wednesday to pass a motion, 45 to 36, endorsing a proposed 91-seat provincial electoral map and urging the government to introduce a bill that would lock the new boundaries into law. Justice Minister Mickey Amery says he wants the legislation through by the end of the year, ahead of the scheduled Oct. 18, 2027, election (Edmonton Journal, The Globe and Mail, CTV News Edmonton, Global News).

The map was drawn by a five-member advisory panel with a UCP majority; the two NDP appointees filed a minority report. Hybrid rural-urban ridings would rise from 19 to 29, including changes in Lethbridge and Calgary’s west side that the Opposition says dilute city votes. NDP Leader Naheed Nenshi called the map “the most gerrymandered” in Canadian history and said his party will go to court if it becomes law. Smith called the gerrymandering charge “ridiculous,” arguing that under 2023 voting patterns the map would not hand the UCP a bigger majority. Elections Alberta has warned the timeline is tight (Edmonton Journal, The Globe and Mail, Global News).

Why it matters: The next provincial election is less than a year away, and the map will decide who Albertans vote for in every riding — including Edmonton’s.

Mayor Knack backs Edmonton’s draft budget with a 4.9% property-tax hike for 2027, saying about $20 a month more buys real services

Edmonton city council gave an early nod this week to a proposed 2027–2030 operating budget that would raise residential property taxes 4.9% next year, then 4.7%, 4.5% and 3.9% in the three years after. Mayor Andrew Knack said the increases are lower than the hikes of 2023–2026 and that roughly $20 a month more for a typical homeowner should fund better snow clearing, more police funding and five new fire halls (Global News, CTV News Edmonton, Taproot Edmonton).

A $500,000 home would pay about $340 a month in municipal property tax under the 2027 plan, or about $68 per $100,000 of assessed value. Coun. Karen Principe said council still needs to dig harder into core-service efficiencies. A public hearing is set for Nov. 23, with final approval targeted for Dec. 18; utility budgets land Nov. 12 (Global News, CTV News Edmonton).

Why it matters: Property tax is the city’s main revenue lever, and these numbers will show up on every Edmonton homeowner’s and renter’s bill next year.

Canada

Canadian astronaut Joshua Kutryk heads outside the ISS today for his second spacewalk, to finish the station’s solar-array upgrade

Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk and NASA astronaut Anil Menon are scheduled to leave the International Space Station today for a roughly seven-hour spacewalk to install the eighth and final rollout solar array in an upgrade campaign that began in 2021. NASA says the arrays can raise the station’s power capacity by up to about 30%. Coverage begins on NASA’s live channels mid-morning Edmonton time (Canadian Space Agency, NASA, Space.com).

The pair completed a similar 7-hour-and-11-minute spacewalk on Oct. 29. Kutryk now joins Chris Hadfield, Steve MacLean, Dave Williams and David Saint-Jacques as Canadians who have worked outside the Station. Separately on Wednesday, Industry Minister Mélanie Joly named former NASA chief economist Alexander MacDonald as the new CSA president, succeeding Lisa Campbell (Canadian Space Agency, Government of Canada, SpaceQ).

Why it matters: A Canadian is literally finishing a power upgrade that keeps the Station — and Canada’s labs on it — running for the rest of the decade.

Why it matters: This is the first full fiscal plan since Carney took office, and it sets spending and tax signals that hit Alberta paycheques, housing and energy.

More than a dozen European flights divert or land early after a fresh wave of bomb threats, the second scare this week

Airlines and airports across Europe scrambled on Thursday morning after bomb threats hit multiple commercial flights. Authorities said more than a dozen aircraft from cities including Copenhagen, Dublin, Helsinki, Madrid, Milan and Warsaw were targeted; several made emergency diversions while others landed at their destinations for searches. Inspections so far have found no explosives or suspicious devices (AP, The Guardian live blog).

The wave follows hoax threats earlier this week that already forced searches, diversions and at least one military escort. Among Thursday’s cases, an Aer Lingus Dublin–Rome flight diverted to Évreux in France, a Wizz Air Milan–Warsaw service went to Vienna, and a Ryanair London–Sofia flight diverted to Plovdiv, according to airline and airport statements compiled by AP and The Guardian (AP, The Guardian).

Why it matters: False bomb threats still ground planes, burn crew hours and rattle passengers — and copycats can shut corridors Canadians use to reach Europe.

Organigram agrees to buy Edmonton-based Aurora Cannabis for about $561-million, topping Curaleaf’s hostile bid

Aurora Cannabis, the Edmonton-rooted medical grower, has agreed to a friendly takeover by Organigram Global worth about $561-million. Organigram is offering $7.80 a share in cash, shares or a mix — roughly a 21% premium to Curaleaf Holdings’ latest hostile cash offer of US$5 a share. Aurora’s board unanimously supports the deal; a shareholder vote is expected in December, with closing targeted for January 2027 if courts and regulators agree (The Globe and Mail, Edmonton Journal, company release).

Organigram is partly funding the purchase with a $188-million private placement from British American Tobacco, its largest shareholder, and $210-million of loan facilities from ATB Financial. Both firms have shifted toward higher-margin medical and international markets after Canada’s recreational boom went bust; together they would run five EU-GMP plants (The Globe and Mail, Edmonton Journal).

Why it matters: Aurora was once a $14-billion legalization darling headquartered in Edmonton. A Canadian white knight ending a U.S. hostile bid is a marker of how far the sector has shrunk — and who still wants the export licences.

Brent slides back under US$100 as traders bet the U.S. midterm rout cools Iran-war escalation risk

Brent crude settled more than 8% lower on Wednesday — its weakest close since late August — and was changing hands near US$96 early Thursday after briefly dipping under US$100. West Texas Intermediate was around US$88–89. Traders linked the sell-off to hopes that Democrats’ sweep of Congress will push President Donald Trump toward diplomacy rather than fresh strikes on Iran, even as analysts warn a renewed escalation could shove Brent back into the US$110–125 range (CNBC, Anadolu Agency, Gulf News).

JPMorgan says the global market swung back into surplus in September and October as Gulf shipments recovered toward pre-war volumes, though commercial inventories in big consuming countries remain historically tight. Iran’s Guard also reported tanker fires in Hormuz on Thursday (see World), a reminder that the ceasefire talk and the shooting risk are running at the same time (CNBC, Anadolu Agency).

Why it matters: Cheaper crude eases gasoline pressure for drivers and cuts revenue assumptions for Alberta’s budget and oil sands producers — until the next Hormuz scare.

Science corner

Science corner: a Shanghai “self-driving” biology lab where AI agents and robots run DNA experiments from a single prompt

Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University describe AutoDNA, a molecular-biology lab built so that a team of AI agents can design an experiment, drive more than 20 kinds of instruments and finish thousands of steps with almost no human pipetting. After the prompt, people mainly refill reagents and fix hardware. The work is published in Nature (Nature news, Nature research article, Shanghai Jiao Tong University).

In demos the system detected a cancer-linked DNA mutation and encoded the sentence “Intelligence is the ability to adapt to change” into DNA letters, then read it back. Outside researchers say fully autonomous wet labs are still rare because biology workflows are fiddly; AutoDNA’s pitch is that the AI layer was designed with the robots from day one, not bolted on later (Nature, 36Kr, university project page).

Why it matters: If AI can run reliable DNA work overnight, drug screens and diagnostics get cheaper — but only after other labs reproduce the results outside Shanghai.

AI news and advances

OpenAI ships Images 2.6 with native GIFs, SVG export and a six-second “Flare” model for faster AI pictures

OpenAI released Images 2.6 on Wednesday across ChatGPT, Codex and its API. The company says generation is about 50% faster than the previous version, and developers get two API models: GPT-Image-2.6 Flare for quick drafts (as little as about six seconds at low quality) and Sunburst for higher-detail edits. New tricks include animated GIF output, SVG exports for sharp logos and icons, markup-guided edits and a “complexity” knob that controls how hard the model plans a scene (OpenAI, MarkTechPost).

OpenAI says users now make more than four billion images a week across ChatGPT and the API — over half a billion a day. Token prices for Flare and Sunburst match the prior generation’s image rates. Independent write-ups note the release still lacks public quality benchmarks and a simple per-image price card (OpenAI, MarkTechPost, The Agentic Weekly).

Why it matters: GIF and SVG support turns a chat box into a quick animation and icon shop, which matters for classrooms, small shops and newsrooms that can’t staff a motion designer.
