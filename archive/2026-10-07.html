Ottawa touts $8 billion in Alberta defence spending, including a $550-million Edmonton base for the new Husky tanker fleet

Defence Minister David McGuinty, standing with Premier Danielle Smith and Edmonton Riverbend MP Matt Jeneroux at CFB Edmonton on Wednesday, said Alberta is receiving about $8 billion in ongoing defence investment. It includes $550 million to set up the western operating base for the Royal Canadian Air Force’s CC-330 Husky refuelling and transport fleet, about $4 billion for a fighter-squadron facility and future fighter capability at Cold Lake, and $582 million to maintain Alberta’s four main defence sites.

National Defence says the spending will support roughly 29,000 to 38,000 Alberta jobs over the next five to 10 years. McGuinty said Confederation was “not an accident, it was a choice.” Smith called it a “generational investment” and said it was also meant to show “a new day in the relationship between Canada and Alberta.”

Why it matters: Edmonton gets a new air-force base and long-term defence work, and the announcement lands 12 days before Albertans vote on Oct. 19, including on whether to start a path toward a separation referendum.

Musk says Quebec, Alberta and Saskatchewan should separate; Moe replies “No” and Smith says her party backs staying in Canada

Elon Musk wrote on X on Wednesday morning, in reply to Quebec premier-designate Paul St-Pierre Plamondon’s comments on independence: “Good. Quebec should separate. They would be better off. Alberta and Saskatchewan should separate too.”

On the third anniversary of Oct. 7, Carney says there is “more work to do” to keep Jewish Canadians safe

Prime Minister Mark Carney marked three years since the Hamas attacks on Israel, in which nearly 1,200 people were killed and more than 200 taken hostage, including Canadians. In a statement he said Canada “unequivocally condemns Hamas,” which “must play no role in the future governance of a demilitarised Palestinian state,” and repeated support for a two-state solution.

Heading into caucus, Carney pointed to anti-hate legislation and $75 million through the Canada Community Security Fund for synagogues, schools and community centres, and cited last month’s attack outside the Sons of Jacob Synagogue in Belleville, Ont., on Yom Kippur. “There’s more work to do,” he said. The Centre for Israel and Jewish Affairs welcomed his remarks.

Why it matters: It is the government’s clearest statement of its anti-hate and community-security plans since the Belleville attack, which police called hate-motivated.

Russian missile flattens part of a Ukrainian apartment block in Pryluky; at least 20 dead, including five children

Russia launched a heavy overnight missile and drone attack that President Volodymyr Zelensky said involved about 70 missiles, many of them ballistic. In Pryluky, in the northern Chernihiv region, a missile struck a five-storey apartment building; Ukrainian officials and the Kyiv Independent put the toll there at at least 20 dead, five of them children, and about 55 wounded by Wednesday evening. Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi said 15 people, including one child, were pulled out alive.

Other deaths were reported in Kyiv, Oleksandria and Kremenchuk, and Ukraine began emergency power cuts in five regions. Russia’s defence ministry said it struck military-industrial sites, including where Ukrainian firm Fire Point builds its Flamingo cruise missiles. Zelensky said Ukrainian forces hit two Russian oil facilities and a training ground.

Why it matters: Strikes on energy infrastructure ahead of winter keep pressure on Ukraine’s power grid, which Canada and other allies are helping to defend and repair.

WHO presses Russia for answers after a Siberian plague-lab worker’s death; Moscow says there is no epidemic risk

A 28-year-old worker at the Irkutsk Anti-Plague Research Institute, identified by Russian authorities as Darya Shipilova, died Oct. 2 of pneumonia that officials still describe as of unknown origin. Russia’s health watchdog Rospotrebnadzor said Wednesday that no plague was found in her contacts, nearly 5,000 lab tests found no dangerous pathogens, and about 90 per cent of the roughly 200 people quarantined had been released.

The World Health Organization said it still does not know what caused her death. Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said WHO had asked Russia to clarify the cause, the pathogen that prompted the public-health measures, and media reports of a second employee with unexplained pneumonia. The Kremlin said Russia had properly informed WHO.

Why it matters: Unexplained deaths at labs that handle dangerous pathogens are a global concern, and WHO’s questions show the information so far is incomplete.

Isaias nears hurricane strength; landfall on the U.S. northern Gulf Coast expected late Friday or early Saturday

At 7 p.m. CDT (6 p.m. MDT) the National Hurricane Center put Tropical Storm Isaias about 775 km south-southwest of the mouth of the Mississippi River with sustained winds of 100 km/h, moving east-northeast at 13 km/h. Air Force Hurricane Hunters were investigating the storm.

The NHC expects Isaias to become a hurricane by Thursday morning, keep strengthening into Friday, pass north of Mexico’s Yucatán Peninsula and make landfall late Friday or early Saturday. It warns of storm surge of 1.5 to 2 metres (5 to 7 feet) from Ocean Springs, Miss., to Indian Pass, Fla., including Mobile Bay. A hurricane watch covers Bay St. Louis, Miss., to Indian Pass.

Why it matters: Canadians with Thanksgiving-weekend travel to Alabama, Mississippi or the Florida Panhandle should watch NHC and local advisories.

India’s central bank raises rates for the first time since 2023, citing oil-driven inflation

The Reserve Bank of India’s monetary policy committee unanimously raised its repo rate by a quarter point to 5.50 per cent on Wednesday, its first increase since February 2023, and shifted its stance from “neutral” to “calibrated tightening.” Governor Sanjay Malhotra pointed to the re-escalation of the West Asia conflict in September and volatile crude prices.

The RBI raised its growth forecast for the fiscal year to 7.1 per cent from 6.7 per cent and its inflation forecast to 5.2 per cent. Malhotra said the next move “can only be a rate hike or pause.”

Why it matters: It’s another major central bank turning toward hikes because of oil prices, alongside Fed minutes released Wednesday showing most U.S. officials expect another increase this year.

Slovenia’s Triglav glacier, tracked since 1946, is down to a few patches of ice that could vanish within days

Photos and video of the last ice below Mount Triglav, Slovenia’s highest peak and national symbol, have been shared widely. Researcher Miha Pavšek of the Slovenian Academy of Sciences and Arts, who has monitored it for 30 years, said “the very last remnants of ice will literally disappear within these coming days.” The glacier covered about 14 hectares in 1946 and has stopped functioning as a glacier, with no new ice forming.

Verdict: Confirmed. Reuters, Slovenia’s public broadcaster and the Slovenian government all report the near-disappearance.

Why it matters: Alpine glaciers feed rivers used for irrigation; Pavšek warned that losses in neighbouring Austria could reduce flows in Slovenia’s Drava and Mura basins.

Science corner

A 7-electronvolt semiconductor: Cornell-led team turns a would-be insulator into working transistors

Researchers led by Cornell University reported in Nature on Wednesday silicon-doped α-(AlxGa1−x)2O3 — an aluminum gallium oxide — with a bandgap above 7.0 electronvolts, wider than cubic boron nitride, the previous widest-bandgap semiconductor. Grown as thin films on sapphire by suboxide molecular-beam epitaxy, the material conducts more than 100 million times better at room temperature than any earlier material reported above 6 eV.

The team built a Schottky diode and a transistor it calls the AlphaFET from it. A wider bandgap lets a device block higher voltages in a thinner layer, which matters for power electronics such as chargers, electric-vehicle drives and grid converters.

Why it matters: More efficient power switching could cut energy lost as heat in everything from EV chargers to transmission equipment.

Sources (3)
  • Nature — research paper
  • Nature News & Views (Hironori Okumura, University of Tsukuba)
  • Scienmag
Community Note

These are lab-scale devices. The paper does not claim commercial products or a timeline, and real-world performance, reliability and cost still have to be shown.

Sources: Nature; Nature News & Views.

AI news and advances

OpenAI publishes 722 math papers from an unreleased model; mathematicians are impressed but urge caution

OpenAI posted 722 mathematics manuscripts, grouped into 372 families of results, to a public GitHub repository, all produced by an “internal frontier model” it has not released. They span about 20 subfields, including a result described as a quasi-Riemann hypothesis and work on matrix and integer multiplication. Many proofs come with formal versions in Lean, a language that lets a computer check them; OpenAI says some without one could contain errors.

University College London’s Francis Johnson, who worked on Wall’s D(2) problem for 25 years, told New Scientist he was “astonished.” Imperial College London’s Kevin Buzzard said only seven of 30 papers in his field looked impressive and only one was formally verified. An advisory panel hosted by the Institute for Advanced Study said its earlier guidance was not an endorsement.

Why it matters: AI is starting to produce research-level math at a scale human reviewers can’t check quickly, which raises questions about verification and who gets access to these models.

Sources (3)
  • New Scientist
  • The Next Web
  • SiliconANGLE
Community Note

Most results have not been peer reviewed. “Discoveries” is OpenAI’s framing; the quasi-Riemann result is not a proof of the Riemann hypothesis itself. The model is not public, so others can’t reproduce the process yet.

Sources: New Scientist; The Next Web; SiliconANGLE.

Microsoft launches Nvidia-chip Surface PCs built to run AI on the device, plus a Windows sandbox for AI agents

At an event in San Francisco, Microsoft opened preorders for the Surface Laptop Ultra, built on Nvidia’s Arm-based RTX Spark chip, starting at about US$2,600, and a Surface RTX Spark Dev Box workstation from about US$6,000. Both are pitched at running AI models locally rather than in the cloud.

Why it matters: Running AI on your own machine can keep personal files off the cloud, and sandboxing agents is a key safety step as they gain the ability to act on a PC.
