Cameras at 12 Edmonton and Calgary school crosswalks catch 72 near-misses between cars and pedestrians in just five school days

The Alberta Motor Association and the Canadian Automobile Association had traffic-data company Miovision film 30 school-route crossings in 11 Canadian cities and use AI to flag every time a vehicle came close to hitting someone. Across Canada that worked out to 660 near-misses, or about 4.5 a day, and one in every 84 people using the crosswalks had a close call (Edmonton Journal, Global News, CTV News Edmonton).

In Alberta, where six crossings in Edmonton and six in Calgary were watched, 79% of the close calls involved drivers going straight through rather than turning, and the worst hour was 3 to 4 p.m., when school lets out. The study doesn’t name the crossings. Brandon Kwong of the group Paths for People said he was “horrified to see that number,” and the City of Edmonton said the results support its work on zebra markings, stop signs and flashing beacons (Edmonton Journal, My Lethbridge Now).

Why it matters: Most of the near-misses happened at home time, when kids are walking out of school, which is exactly when drivers in school zones need to slow down most.

The Elks are Grey Cup champions: Cody Fajardo scores four touchdowns as Edmonton beats Montreal 37-30 in Calgary

What changed since the last edition: Sunday morning’s edition previewed the game. The Elks have now won it, 37-30, for their first Grey Cup since 2015.

Fajardo, named the game’s most valuable player, threw two touchdown passes to Joshua Cephus and ran for two more, going 16 of 22 for 199 yards. The lead changed hands six times before kicker Vincent Blanchard settled it with two late field goals, from 42 yards with 2:14 left and 43 yards with 1:05 left, after Edmonton’s defence stopped Montreal on downs (CFL.ca, Sportsnet, The Associated Press).

It is the club’s record 15th Grey Cup and its first in 11 years, one season after it finished last in the West Division. Montreal, which had the league’s best record at 14-4, lost the final for the second year running. The crowd at McMahon Stadium was 32,419, short of a sellout. Fajardo also won the trophy with Montreal in 2023 (CFL.ca, Sportsnet, The Associated Press).

Why it matters: It’s Edmonton’s first pro football title in more than a decade, and a big turnaround for a team that was at the bottom of the league a year ago.

Canada joins Australia, Italy and Britain in telling Israel to let 150,000 tents into Gaza now, as winter floods swamp camps

In a joint statement on Sunday, the four foreign ministers said the tents and other supplies are being held at the border, and urged Israel to “stop the delays now” and open the crossings. They said items Israel treats as having possible military uses, but that aid groups need, must also be let in “before it is too late” (Global Affairs Canada, SBS News, Anadolu Agency).

The statement follows the season’s first big storm. The UN reported five deaths and thousands of families losing their shelters, with floodwater up to 1.5 metres deep in places. Nearly two-thirds of Gaza’s people live in tents or temporary shelters. The European Union made a similar appeal on Saturday (SBS News, Anadolu Agency).

Why it matters: Canada is putting its name to one of the bluntest Western demands on Israel since the ceasefire, and the tents could decide whether families stay dry this winter.

Estonia will shut all its road crossings into Russia on Dec. 1 after a foiled arson attack on a drone maker that it blames on Russia’s spy agency

Police detained eight Latvian citizens early Saturday as they tried to break into Threod Systems, which builds long-range reconnaissance and attack drones, in Viimsi just outside Tallinn. Estonia’s security service says the group meant to set off an explosion and start a fire on the orders of Russia’s GRU military intelligence. It’s the second attempted arson at an Estonian defence company since August (ERR News, Reuters, Politico Europe).

The foreign ministry says the three road crossings, at Narva, Luhamaa and Koidula, will close to pedestrians and trucks from Dec. 1, giving Estonians in Russia two weeks to get home. Prime Minister Kristen Michal said Russia wants to “make us afraid” and weaken support for Ukraine. The government will decide this week whether the closure is temporary or open-ended (ERR News, AFP, Reuters).

Why it matters: Narva is one of the last land routes between Europe and Russia still open to travellers, so closing it is a big step in the standoff between NATO’s eastern members and Moscow.

Six months in, Africa’s health agency says Congo’s Ebola outbreak is still out of control, with more than 11,200 cases and 5,500 deaths

What changed since the last edition: Our last case-count update, on Oct. 31, reported the outbreak passing 5,000 deaths. At the six-month mark, Africa’s top health agency now says it is still not under control and could become the worst on record.

“If we don’t stop this outbreak now, it would be the worst Ebola outbreak that the world will have,” Africa CDC director-general Jean Kaseya told Reuters on Sunday. More than 80% of new cases are turning up in people who weren’t on any contact list, and most deaths are happening at home rather than in treatment centres. Cases have now been reported in East, Central and West Africa, the widest spread of any Ebola outbreak on the continent (Al Jazeera with Reuters, Africa CDC, The Hindu).

Africa CDC counts more than 80 attacks on health care in six months, and Kaseya said only US$1.3 billion of the US$2.9 billion donors pledged has actually been released. There’s no approved vaccine or treatment for the Bundibugyo strain, which kills about half the people it infects, though the WHO approved trials of two vaccine candidates this month (Al Jazeera, Xinhua, The Hindu).

Why it matters: Every month without control raises the chance of more cases travelling abroad, and the money gap shows how much of the promised help hasn’t arrived.

A ULA Vulcan rocket fails 10 minutes after launch and destroys 40 Amazon Leo internet satellites, the company’s first outright failure in 20 years

The rocket lifted off from Cape Canaveral at 10:40 a.m. Edmonton time on Sunday. United Launch Alliance, owned by Boeing and Lockheed Martin, said it suffered an “anomaly” during the upper-stage engine burn and failed to reach orbit. The cause isn’t known yet, and Vulcan is expected to be grounded during the investigation (Spaceflight Now, The Verge, Reuters).

It was the first of 38 Vulcan launches Amazon has bought for Leo, its rival to SpaceX’s Starlink, and would have brought the number of Leo satellites launched to 436 of the more than 3,300 it needs. Amazon’s other heavy rocket option, Blue Origin’s New Glenn, is also still grounded after an explosion in May. Amazon said “there is risk with any launch” and that it still trusts ULA (Spaceflight Now, The Verge).

Why it matters: Losing a full batch of satellites and grounding its main rocket sets Amazon further behind Starlink, which already sells satellite internet across rural Canada.

Hackers steal the names and addresses of 22 million people in France from a La Poste mailing company most of them had never heard of

Maileva, part of the French postal group, sends payslips, registered letters and invoices on behalf of more than 70,000 business users. It says first names, last names and postal addresses of 22 million people who received those documents were stolen in a mid-September attack; bank details were not. It has told France’s privacy regulator, the CNIL, and filed a criminal complaint (Security Affairs, franceinfo, Anadolu Agency).

Some letters were also read. Medical billing company Kiwee.care, which mails through Maileva, had payment reminders exposed that showed invoice numbers, treatment dates and amounts owed, affecting patients of a Paris-area hospital. Social housing providers and medical labs are also among the affected clients (Security Affairs, franceinfo, L’indépendance).

Why it matters: It’s a reminder that your data can leak through a supplier you’ve never dealt with. Canadian businesses use similar outsourced mailing and billing services.

Science corner

Science corner: Superb-seq, a new test that shows every place CRISPR cut a cell’s DNA, including the ones it wasn’t supposed to

CRISPR gene editing works like molecular scissors guided to a chosen stretch of DNA, but it sometimes cuts in the wrong places too. Existing tests can find where those stray cuts happen across a batch of cells, but not which cell got them or what they did. Salk Institute scientists built Superb-seq to do both. It tags each cut with a short DNA marker, then reads that tag along with the cell’s gene activity, one cell at a time (Salk Institute via Phys.org, Nature Methods, GEN).

Tested on about 34,000 cells from three cell lines, it found 43 cut sites in a batch of 10,000 leukemia cells, and 36 of them were off target. Some of those stray cuts changed how nearby genes behaved. Senior author Graham McVicker said the stray edits were “far more frequent than I was expecting.” The team has filed a patent and published the method in Nature Methods on Nov. 16 (Phys.org, GEN, Nature Methods).

Why it matters: Gene-editing treatments, like the approved one for sickle cell disease, depend on knowing a cut won’t harm other genes. A test like this could make future therapies safer to approve.

Sources (3)
  • Phys.org (Salk Institute): New tool tracks unintended CRISPR edits in individual cells
  • Nature Methods: Joint single-cell capture of Cas9 edits and transcriptomes
  • GEN: Single-cell method reveals on- and off-target effects of Cas9 edits
Community Note

The tests used lab-grown cell lines, not patients. How common stray cuts are in a real therapy depends on the guide design and delivery method.

The off-target count applies to the guides the team chose to test; well-designed clinical guides are screened to reduce this.

Sources: Salk Institute/Phys.org; Nature Methods; GEN

AI news and advances

China’s Meituan gives away the full weights of LongCat-2.5, a giant AI agent model trained without Nvidia chips, under a licence anyone can use

What changed since the last edition: On Nov. 2 we covered Meituan’s launch of LongCat-2.5. It has now released the model’s downloadable weights, and its setup guide shows it running on Huawei chips, under a free licence.

The food-delivery company released LongCat-2.5 under the MIT licence, which allows commercial use and changes. It has 1.6 trillion parameters but uses only about 48 billion for each word it writes, can read text, images and video, and is built for long, multi-step coding jobs. Meituan says it was trained entirely on Chinese-made AI chips (The Decoder, VentureBeat, TokenPost).

Its setup guide runs the model on 194 Huawei Ascend chips and offers no instructions for Nvidia hardware. On Meituan’s own tests it scored 88.4 on the Terminal Bench coding test, close to the 89.1 it lists for Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5, though it trails on harder tests (The Decoder, Hugging Face).

Why it matters: A top-tier free model tuned for Huawei chips makes it easier for companies around the world to build AI without buying Nvidia hardware, which U.S. export rules restrict in China.

Community Note

Meituan doesn’t say which Chinese chips it trained on; Huawei Ascend chips are named only for running the model.
