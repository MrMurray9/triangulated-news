Alberta

A year after being ordered back to work, Alberta teachers say classes are still packed and the relationship with the province is “frosty”

One year after the province used the Back to School Act, with the notwithstanding clause, to end the teachers’ strike, Alberta Teachers’ Association president Jason Schilling says classrooms have not improved enough. He says some high school teachers report classes of 37 or more students, and that the government’s $200-million grant, meant to hire about 1,400 teachers, was aimed mostly at elementary and junior high schools (LiveWire Calgary, CTV News Edmonton, Edmonton Journal).

Schilling called the relationship with the government “frosty” and asked it to restore teachers’ Charter rights. Education Minister Demetrios Nicolaides says the province has hired about 2,100 teachers and 1,000 educational assistants. The union’s court challenge to the law is still under way (CTV News Edmonton, LiveWire Calgary, Edmonton Journal).

Why it matters: Class size was the core issue in the strike, so whether it is improving says a lot about how the next round of bargaining, and the court fight over the notwithstanding clause, will go.

Canada

Quebec’s new premier is refused a speaking role at this week’s Canada-EU summit in Montreal, and turns down the dinner invitation

Premier Paul St-Pierre Plamondon asked to speak at the Canada-European Union summit, which runs Thursday and Friday in Montreal. Ottawa said no, and also declined Quebec’s request to host its own reception. Instead, the premier was invited to the leaders’ dinner and to greet EU leaders at the airport. He turned down both, accusing Ottawa on Facebook of offering Quebec little more than the chance to “serve the shrimp cocktails” (National Post / Montreal Gazette, Politico Europe, Le Journal de Québec).

Prime Minister Mark Carney said: “He was invited to the dinner; he refused,” adding that it was the premier’s decision “to leave the chair empty — again,” after he also skipped Monday’s first ministers’ meeting. Industry Minister Mélanie Joly said the summit is a federal event in a federal area of jurisdiction (National Post, Politico Europe, Le Journal de Québec).

Why it matters: It is the first public clash between Carney and Quebec’s new Parti Québécois government, which has promised a sovereignty referendum, and it comes as Canada tries to deepen trade ties with Europe, its second-largest trading partner.

World

Congo’s Ebola outbreak passes 10,000 cases, now the second-largest on record, as vaccine trials begin

The Democratic Republic of the Congo has recorded 10,141 cases of Ebola disease caused by the Bundibugyo virus, with nearly 5,000 deaths, as of Monday, according to health ministry data shared by the World Health Organization. It is the country’s 17th and largest Ebola outbreak, and second only to the 2014–2016 West Africa epidemic. It has spread to eight provinces (UN News, AP, ABC News Australia).

The WHO says more than 2,600 people have recovered and testing and treatment have expanded. There is no approved vaccine or treatment for this strain, so a trial called TUMAINI (Swahili for “hope”) has started in Bunia to test whether the Ervebo vaccine, made for a different strain, protects close contacts. Kenya has recorded nine linked cases, including two deaths (UN News, AP, ABC News Australia).

Why it matters: An outbreak this size, in a conflict zone and with cases now in neighbouring countries, raises the risk of wider spread and puts pressure on global health funding.

Toll climbs to 68 as Spain says a control-centre order sent one train into another that was stopped on the track

What changed: This morning the official toll was at least 30, and the cause was unknown. It is now 68, and the transport minister says a control-centre instruction to ignore a stop signal led to the crash.

Officials in Castilla-La Mancha say 68 people died in Wednesday’s crash near Urda, in Toledo province, with four bodies still trapped in the wreckage. Of the 544 people on the two trains, 107 went to hospital and 15 are in serious or critical condition. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez visited the site and declared three days of national mourning, Oct. 29 to 31 (The Guardian, AP, El Español).

Transport Minister Óscar Puente said the automatic train-protection system went down at about 8:30 a.m. because of a computer fault. The Madrid control centre then, “inexplicably,” told the Renfe train to carry on past a stop signal. It travelled about 25 km and hit the stopped Iryo train about 17 minutes later. Opposition leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo wants an independent inquiry. Canada says at least two Canadians were hurt (The Guardian, AP, The Canadian Press, La Moncloa).

The Fed holds U.S. rates at 3.75 to 4 per cent, but two officials vote to raise them

The U.S. Federal Reserve left its benchmark rate unchanged on Wednesday in a 10–2 vote. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed president Beth Hammack dissented because they wanted a quarter-point increase to fight inflation (Federal Reserve, CNBC, Reuters).

Chair Kevin Warsh also said that starting in 2027, one of the Fed’s eight meetings each year will be set aside for strategy, with no rate vote. Stocks moved little after the decision (CNBC, Reuters via Investing.com, Federal Reserve).

Why it matters: Dissents in favour of a hike show inflation worries are growing at the Fed, which affects the U.S. dollar, Canadian borrowing costs and markets here.

Sources (3)

The Fed sets its policy rate as a range, so 3.75 to 4 per cent is the target for overnight lending between banks. Two dissents is unusual but not rare; it does not mean a hike at the next meeting.

Alphabet shares drop about 9 per cent after hours as heavy AI spending pushes its cash flow negative

Google’s parent company reported third-quarter revenue of US$127.58 billion, up about 25 per cent but slightly below forecasts. Search revenue of US$63.35 billion also missed. Spending on data centres and chips reached about US$51 billion, leaving free cash flow at minus US$9.07 billion, deeper than last quarter (CNBC, Reuters via Investing.com, AP).

Science corner

Science corner: a snake-like soft robot can crawl through the small intestine to find and treat problems

Researchers at MIT and UT Southwestern have built a soft robotic scope that inches through the small intestine the way a snake or inchworm moves. Air-driven sections stretch and bend, while small balloons covered in tiny kirigami-cut needles grip the gut wall without damaging it. In tests in pigs it travelled about 2 metres (Science Robotics, MIT News, UT Southwestern).

The robot carries a camera, a sprayer for drugs and tools to take tissue samples or clip polyps. The work was led by Giovanni Traverso’s lab; lead author Binbin Ying was supported in part by Canada’s Banting and NSERC fellowships (MIT News, UT Southwestern, Science Robotics).

Why it matters: Most of the small intestine, about 6 metres long, is hard to reach with today’s scopes, so a tool like this could make diagnosing bleeding, Crohn’s disease and tumours easier and less invasive.

Sources (3)
  • Science Robotics — study paper
  • MIT News — Soft robot could help doctors diagnose and treat small intestine disorders
  • UT Southwestern — Robot could improve gastroenterologists’ diagnosis
Community Note

This is early research tested in animals, not people. Human trials and regulatory approval would come first, which usually takes years.

Sources: Science Robotics; MIT News; UT Southwestern.

AI news and advances

Gemini 4 Carbon tops a major AI leaderboard, but at first only vetted cyber defenders can use it

Google has unveiled Gemini 4 Carbon, its new top AI model. For now it is only available to approved security teams through a new Fairwind Program, because the company says its hacking and bug-finding skills are strong enough to be misused. It ranks first on the independent Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, ahead of the newest models from Anthropic and OpenAI (Google, The Verge, Ars Technica).

Google says the model can work on long tasks for days, makes about half as many unsupported claims as the previous version, and leads security tests such as SEC-bench Pro. Approved users pay US$2 per million input tokens and US$10 per million output tokens (Google, Reuters via Investing.com, The Verge).

Why it matters: It is a new pattern for top AI models: releasing them first to defenders, so they can patch software holes before attackers get the same tools.

FWanted: dog walkers, US$20 an hour. The catch: the dogs are robots, and you carry them home if they overheat

Seen on: A since-deleted job ad in the “Bay Area Jobs” group on Facebook, as reported by The New York Times and UPI.

A San Francisco start-up called Dimensional posted an ad in a Bay Area jobs group on Facebook looking for people to walk its robot dogs on city sidewalks for four to six hours at a time. Applicants had to be able to carry about 33 pounds, because a robot that overheated had to be carried back to the office, which is in the Dogpatch neighbourhood (UPI, The New York Times via Quartz, Digital Trends).

Founder Stash Pomichter said the walks collect data to train the company’s robot software, and that the experiment showed AI is “still very bad at controlling hardware” and “humans are still needed in the loop” (UPI, Digital Trends, The New York Times via Quartz).

Why it matters: It is a funny reminder that today’s robots still need a lot of human help, even for a walk around the block.

Confirmed

OSeven months after escaping, Samba the capybara turns up alive and well on a wildlife camera in England

Seen on: Wildlife-camera photos posted by Marwell Zoo on its own channels and widely shared, reported by ITV News and The Independent.

Samba escaped from her enclosure at Marwell Zoo in Hampshire on March 17 and has dodged thermal drones, sniffer dogs and camera traps ever since. New photos from the zoo’s wildlife cameras show she is healthy and living on private land near the River Itchen (ITV News, The Independent, UPI).

Staff have set up a feeding spot to get her used to visiting, as a first step to bringing her home safely. Her sister, Tango, was caught right away and now has a new companion, a male capybara named Ripple (ITV News, Marwell Zoo, The Independent).

Why it matters: A happy update for the many people who have followed the runaway rodent’s story, and a sign the zoo may soon bring her back.
