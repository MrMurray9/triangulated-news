Ottawa will give Express Entry applicants extra points for “high-wage” jobs — and drop the LMIA for those job offers — starting Nov. 3

Immigration Minister Lena Metlege Diab announced in Kitchener, Ont., on Friday that from Nov. 3, Express Entry candidates with eligible Canadian work experience or a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation will get extra Comprehensive Ranking System points. IRCC defines high-wage as an occupation whose median wage is at least 1.3 times the national median — about $57,000, The Globe and Mail reports. It is based on the occupation’s typical pay, not the candidate’s own salary.

IRCC says job offers in occupations at 2, 1.5 or 1.3 times the median earn 100, 70 or 50 points, and high-wage work experience earns 70, 40 or 20 extra points; candidates get the higher of the two, not both. Qualifying job offers will no longer need a Labour Market Impact Assessment, though IRCC says it will still check that offers are genuine. Scores in the pool will be recalculated automatically — “may take up to two weeks, during which invitation rounds will be paused.” IRCC’s list covers 186 occupations, CIC News reports, including engineers, electricians and health-care jobs.

Why it matters: It shifts permanent-residence selection toward higher-paid skilled workers — relevant to Alberta employers recruiting engineers, trades and health workers — and removes a costly step for employers.

Housing starts rose 3% in September, but actual building is down 11% from a year ago — and Edmonton is 14% behind 2025’s pace

Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation says the seasonally adjusted annual rate of housing starts was 248,896 units in September, up 3% from 242,223 in August, while the six-month trend was virtually flat at 250,411. Actual starts in centres of 10,000 or more fell 11% from a year earlier to 19,984 units, leaving January–September 4% behind 2025.

In Alberta, actual starts so far this year total 35,560, down 13% from 40,714. Metro Edmonton is at 14,202, down 14%, and Calgary at 17,612, down 16% — though Edmonton’s six-month trend rose 5% in September. “Canada still faces a long-term housing supply gap and unless housing construction levels increase, improvements in housing affordability since 2023 will be difficult to sustain,” said CMHC deputy chief economist Aled ab Iorwerth. TD economist Rishi Sondhi said he expects starts “trending lower over the next few quarters.”

Why it matters: Fewer starts now mean fewer homes to buy or rent in a couple of years — and Alberta’s two biggest cities are building less than they did last year.

Science corner: a chip-sized “tape measure for light” built from two lasers matches a tabletop frequency comb — a step toward portable atomic clocks

Researchers at NIST and the Joint Quantum Institute (a NIST–University of Maryland partnership), with the University of Auckland, UMBC, UC Santa Barbara and the U.S. Air Force Research Laboratory, reported in Nature a new kind of microchip optical frequency comb — a device whose evenly spaced colours act like tick marks on a ruler for light. Two lasers at frequencies an octave apart circulate in a tiny ring resonator and produce a “self-aligned parametrically driven cavity soliton,” a single clean set of comb teeth between them. Because the lasers bookend the comb, the edge teeth needed to pin down the comb’s zero-frequency offset are strong, so no power-hungry extra equipment is needed.

Using one chip, the team linked microwaves to optical light and back, turned a stable atomic clock reference supplied by AFRL into stable microwaves — measuring light oscillating several hundred trillion times a second to within about 100,000 oscillations per second of the atoms’ value — and made ultra-pure microwaves. To the levels they could check, the chip generally matched their tabletop comb. NIST says each wafer holds more than 400 chips, each with up to 300 ring resonators.

Why it matters: Portable optical clocks could provide navigation for drones, cars and submarines when GPS is jammed or unavailable, and help with mineral exploration.

XA predawn SpaceX launch lights up a glowing “space jellyfish” seen from Georgia to the Carolinas and beyond

Seen on: X — NASA’s Kennedy Space Center post (“Many of you are talking about the ‘space jellyfish’…”), plus photos shared widely along the U.S. East Coast

Tuesday’s Falcon 9 launch of NASA’s CRS-35 cargo mission lifted off from Cape Canaveral at 6:35 a.m. EDT (4:35 a.m. MDT) on Oct. 13. As the rocket climbed into thinner air, its exhaust plume spread far wider than the rocket and caught early-dawn sunlight, producing a glowing jellyfish shape. Because it flew north along the East Coast, people saw it from a farm in Colbert, Georgia, to Durham, North Carolina, where one viewer told Space.com: “At first, I thought it was a comet.”

NASA’s Kennedy Space Center explained on X: “A jellyfish happens near sunrise or sunset when the exhaust plume from the rocket becomes brightly illuminated.” The Dragon capsule carried about 6,300 pounds (2,860 kg) of science and supplies to the International Space Station.

Why it matters: A rare sky show for millions of early risers — and a neat lesson in how sunlight high above can light up a rocket plume while the ground is still dark.

“Touch grass” — log off and go do something in the real world

Touch grass means getting off your phone or game and taking part in normal, real-world life — often said as a playful (or not-so-playful) dig at someone who is too online. “Bussin’” — when something (usually food) is extremely good

Bussin’ (also spelled bussin) means extremely good or excellent, and especially delicious. Merriam-Webster labels it African American English slang, first known use 2002. Example: “The cafeteria pizza was actually bussin’ today.” Recency: current Merriam-Webster dictionary entry.

“Lowkey” (and “highkey”) — kinda, secretly … or totally

Lowkey used as an adverb means “kinda” or “somewhat,” or “secretly / in a subtle way”: “I’m lowkey nervous about the math test.” Its opposite, highkey, means “totally” or “extremely.” Merriam-Webster added this meaning of low-key in October 2021 and notes the word started out as a photography term in the 1890s.
