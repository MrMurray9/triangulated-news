Alberta

Edmonton City Centre sells for $57.3 million, a sixth of its 2019 price, and the buyer wants 1,500 homes in the old Bay store

Edmonton developer Westrich Pacific has closed its purchase of the downtown mall from Otera Capital (now part of La Caisse), in a sale approved through the Court of King’s Bench after the property went into receivership last year with more than $139 million in unpaid loans. Co-CEO David Sanche confirmed the $57.3-million price; the complex sold for more than $300 million in 2019 (Edmonton Journal).

The 1.4-million-square-foot site spans three blocks, including both halves of the mall, Centre Point Place, TD Tower and 102A Tower. Westrich’s plan converts the former Hudson’s Bay store into about 1,500 residential units and adds street-facing shops, a refreshed exterior, new anchor tenants and possibly a rooftop Nordic spa and pickleball courts (Edmonton Journal, CTV News Edmonton, Westrich Pacific).

Why it matters: City Centre is the biggest piece of downtown’s retail core, and turning a dead department store into homes is the city’s best shot at putting more people on those blocks after 6 p.m.

Sources (3)
  • Edmonton Journal: Five things to know about the $57.3M sale of Edmonton City Centre
  • CTV News Edmonton: You may soon be able to shop and live at this Edmonton mall
  • Westrich Pacific: Edmonton City Centre project page
Community Note

Sanche says the residential conversion is underway but the rest of the plan is still being designed and will take “many years.” No construction schedule or cost has been released.

The deal was first announced in August; this week’s news is that it has officially closed and the price is public.

Sources: Edmonton Journal; CTV News Edmonton; Westrich Pacific

Alberta’s teachers’ union says the new high school graduation rules came with no consultation and asks the province to “go back to the drawing board”

At a Thursday news conference, Alberta Teachers’ Association president Jason Schilling said the updated diploma requirements, announced Nov. 13, were made without talking to teachers, school leaders or post-secondary schools, and called a string of recent education moves “political distractions” (LiveWire Calgary, Alberta Teachers’ Association).

By 2030, Grade 11 and 12 students will need fewer core credits and will have more room for electives; phys-ed becomes mandatory through high school, and everyone must take a revised career and life management course. Education Minister Demetrios Nicolaides says his student advisory council and research backed the change, and Premier Danielle Smith says parents asked for financial literacy and physical health in the curriculum (LiveWire Calgary, Edmonton Journal).

Why it matters: Fewer required core courses could affect what Alberta students qualify for after graduation, and schools say they don’t yet know if they have the staff and gym space to deliver the new mix.

Sources (3)
  • LiveWire Calgary (Local Journalism Initiative): ‘Chaos and confusion’: Alberta teachers’ union rebukes education changes
  • Edmonton Journal: Alberta Education Minister defends high school graduation requirements
  • Alberta Teachers’ Association: ATA responds to changes to high school graduation requirements
Community Note

The ATA agrees the diploma requirements were due for an update; its complaint is mainly about consultation and the risk of literacy gaps for students who take the minimum core.

The Calgary Board of Education says it has not yet received details from the ministry on programming or resources. Nicolaides says more consultation will happen before the changes take effect.

Sources: LiveWire Calgary; Edmonton Journal; Alberta Teachers’ Association

Canada

An Ottawa startup signs 12 Canadian partners, including Pratt & Whitney Canada and Telesat, to build an AI “wingman” drone for the Arctic

Dominion Dynamics unveiled the consortium behind Scout, an uncrewed aircraft meant to fly alongside fighter jets, on Friday. Pratt & Whitney Canada would supply PW300-family engines, with Thales Canada, Top Aces, PV Labs, SRC Can, North Aircraft Industries and Altitude Aerospace among the others handling avionics, sensors, training and structures (The Canadian Press, The Globe and Mail, BetaKit).

Founder Eliot Pence called it “the most ambitious air platform since the Avro in Canada” and says Scout is designed for Arctic conditions and would work with either F-35s or Gripens. Separately this week, Ottawa gave the 18-month-old company a $17-million contract for a remote-controlled marine drone that hunts underwater mines (The Canadian Press, The Logic).

Why it matters: Ottawa is expected to shop for these autonomous “collaborative” aircraft as the CF-18s retire, and a mostly domestic bid gives the government a made-in-Canada option.

Ottawa will buy carbon-removal credits from 19 Canadian projects to cancel out emissions it can’t cut, like military jets and ships

Treasury Board President Shafqat Ali announced a national standing offer on Thursday that lets federal departments buy permanent carbon-dioxide-removal credits from 19 projects, from direct air capture and biochar to mineralization. Ottawa says it’s the first national government to run a competitive purchase like this (Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat, Ottawa Citizen, The Globe and Mail).

Alberta projects include Deep Sky Alpha, Vaulted Deep’s biomass storage pilot and Calgary-based Remova’s planned plant near Whitecourt, which says it won half the potential dollars in its category and aims to start in 2028. The offers can be used to March 2029, with an option to 2031 (The Deep Dive, Treasury Board).

Why it matters: Government buying gives young carbon-removal companies, several of them in Alberta, the kind of guaranteed-looking customer that helps them raise money and build.

Sources (4)
  • Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat: Government of Canada establishes national standing offers for Canadian carbon dioxide removal credits
  • Ottawa Citizen: Government looks to offset emissions by buying carbon credits
  • The Globe and Mail: Ottawa to purchase credits from carbon-removal startups to offset its emissions
  • The Deep Dive: Ottawa opens carbon removal buying program to 19 projects but purchases aren’t guaranteed
Community Note

A standing offer is not a contract: departments must place individual orders before any money changes hands. The underlying commitment is the 2024 pledge to buy at least $10 million in removal credits by 2030.

Removal credits pay to pull CO2 out of the air and store it; they are different from cheaper offsets that pay to avoid emissions.

Sources: Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat; Ottawa Citizen; The Globe and Mail; The Deep Dive

Two Russian missiles and a drone crash in southeast Poland near a European troop exercise, and Tusk calls it a deliberate provocation

Poland says a Kh-101 cruise missile came down at Nowy Lipowiec, about 50 km from the Ukrainian border, around 6:30 a.m. Friday local time, with a second suspected missile near Rudnik nad Sanem and a jet-powered drone near Mołodycz. It happened during one of Russia’s heaviest overnight attacks on Ukraine. No one was hurt and no damage was reported (AP, Reuters, Al Jazeera).

Prime Minister Donald Tusk said it was no coincidence the objects fell near Nowa Dęba, where 11 “coalition of the willing” countries are training for a possible post-war mission in Ukraine. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte condemned “grave violations” of Polish airspace, NATO jets were scrambled, and Rutte is due in Warsaw on Saturday (AP, Reuters, Al Jazeera).

Why it matters: This is the most serious Russian incursion into a NATO country in this war so far, and it lands just as Washington is trying to restart peace talks.

NATO’s Article 5 mutual-defence clause is not automatic; it needs every member to agree, and analysts quoted by Al Jazeera say the bar is a sustained attack. Poland can ask for Article 4 consultations, as it did after the September 2025 drone incursion.

Russia had not commented on the incident when the outlets above published.

Science corner: a “smart floss” checks blood sugar and gum inflammation from the fluid it picks up while you floss

Engineers at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, working with the University of Hong Kong’s dentistry faculty, coated a two-strand floss with pectin so that about 20 seconds of normal flossing pulls a tiny droplet of gingival crevicular fluid, the liquid that seeps from blood vessels at the gumline, between the threads. A pulley then reels the thread into the handle, where a sensor stack reads glucose, uric acid, pH and hydrogen peroxide in about three minutes and sends the results to a phone (Science Advances, HKUST).

In a 12-person pilot, the floss’s glucose readings tracked finger-prick blood glucose closely (a correlation of 0.89) and uric acid tracked blood levels reasonably well (0.78), while its inflammation readings lined up with a dental exam (Science Advances, Medical Xpress, The Young Dentist).

Why it matters: People with diabetes or gout could one day get a needle-free check during a habit they’re already supposed to do every day, and catch gum disease early too.

Sources (4)
  • Science Advances: A floss-based platform for native gingival crevicular fluid analysis enables noninvasive systemic and oral health monitoring
  • HKUST: HKUST pioneers Smart Floss for non-invasive systemic metabolic monitoring
  • Medical Xpress: Smart floss can measure blood sugar and inflammation markers
  • The Young Dentist: Smart Floss tests gum fluid for metabolic markers
Community Note

This is an early prototype tested on 12 people. It is not an approved medical device, and the team says it needs larger studies before anyone should rely on it.

Medical Xpress largely reproduces the university’s release; the peer-reviewed paper in Science Advances is the primary source for the numbers above.

Sources: Science Advances; HKUST; Medical Xpress; The Young Dentist

California’s attorney general subpoenas Nvidia over whether its AI chips or secrets reached China, and whether investors were misled

Rob Bonta served the investigative subpoena on Thursday, citing reports that former CEO Jensen Huang had an affair with a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy. The state wants to know whether advanced chips or confidential information went to China, and whether Nvidia broke California securities law by misleading investors about that risk (California Attorney General, AP, Politico).

“Companies cannot mislead their investors,” Bonta said. Huang resigned this month. House Democrats have also opened a probe into whether Nvidia tried to influence a chip-smuggling investigation, and Nvidia has halted shipments to China under a new Commerce Department rule (California Attorney General, CNBC, Detroit Free Press).

Why it matters: Nvidia makes most of the chips that power today’s AI, so a legal fight over its China dealings touches everything from export policy to the stock market’s AI selloff.

OpenAI says it cut attempts to copy its models’ hidden reasoning to a 0.2% success rate, as House panels urge a ban on Chinese AI in federal systems

“Distillation” is training one AI model on another’s answers. OpenAI says copying campaigns grew sharply over the summer, with weekly enforcement against thousands to tens of thousands of accounts, and that new safeguards in GPT-6.2 Astra cut the success of attacks trying to pull out its step-by-step reasoning from 54% to 0.2% in its own red-team tests (OpenAI, ANI).

The same day, two House committees released a joint report warning that U.S. companies are increasingly running Chinese open-weight models that may contain capabilities extracted from American systems, and recommended keeping them out of federal systems and critical infrastructure (House Select Committee on the CCP, Bloomberg Law).

Why it matters: Copying frontier models cheaply could hand their abilities, including cybersecurity skills, to developers without the same safety limits, and Washington is now moving from warnings toward rules.

Sources (4)
  • OpenAI: Combatting adversarial distillation
  • The Hindu BusinessLine (ANI): OpenAI strengthens GPT-6.2 Astra safeguards against unauthorised AI reasoning extraction
  • House Select Committee on the CCP: Joint investigation report on Chinese AI models
  • Bloomberg Law: House panels call to ban Chinese AI from US government systems
Community Note

The 0.2% figure comes from OpenAI’s own internal testing and hasn’t been independently checked.

The House report is a set of recommendations, not a law. Distillation itself is a normal technique; the dispute is about doing it without permission.

Sources: OpenAI; ANI; House Select Committee on the CCP; Bloomberg Law

FKids from Bangladesh’s hill country strum and sing in five languages, and their guitar school’s Facebook videos win over millions

Seen on: Facebook: the school’s own page, as reported by The Daily Star.

Bawm Ram Tiam Amlai started the Basic Guitar Learning School in Bandarban in 2018 with four or five students in a small upstairs room. It now teaches about 50 children from several Indigenous and Bengali communities, some travelling 5 to 10 km for lessons, and they sing in Chakma, Marma, Tripura, Bengali and English (The Daily Star, The Business Standard, Prothom Alo).

Amlai says he only posted the videos to his Facebook page as keepsakes for the kids. Their covers of Bangladeshi rock songs spread so widely that the students have since performed with the band Warfaze and sung the national anthem at the National Parliament Complex, where other kids lined up for their autographs (The Daily Star).

Why it matters: It’s a joyful example of a small teacher-led project going big online, and of kids keeping their own languages alive through music.
