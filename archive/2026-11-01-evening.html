Alberta

A sports court lets Russia back into world hockey, and its federation says its next stop is the world juniors in Edmonton and Red Deer

The Court of Arbitration for Sport ruled on Friday that the International Ice Hockey Federation must reinstate Russia, overturning the ban imposed after the 2022 invasion of Ukraine. The IIHF says the ruling touches five 2027 tournaments, starting with the world junior men’s championship that opens Dec. 26 in Edmonton and Red Deer. Russia’s federation named the Alberta tournament as its next major event (The Canadian Press via Sportsnet, CTV News, Daily Faceoff).

Foreign Affairs Minister Anita Anand and Secretary of State for Sport Adam van Koeverden said Canada strongly opposes the decision and will keep restricting Russian athletes at federally funded events. Hockey Canada says the implications are not yet clear. Premier Danielle Smith said Alberta would comply, while the Ukrainian Canadian Congress asked Ottawa to refuse visas to Russian teams (The Canadian Press via Sportsnet, CTV News, Daily Faceoff).

Why it matters: Alberta is home to more than 400,000 people of Ukrainian descent, and the tournament starts in less than two months, so whether Russia plays here is now a live question for Ottawa, Hockey Canada and the province.

Sources (3)
  • Sportsnet (The Canadian Press) — Canadian government ‘strongly opposes’ Russia returning to IIHF competitions
  • CTV News — Hockey Canada, Ukrainian groups react to overturning of ban against Russia
  • Daily Faceoff — Hockey Canada, others react to overturning of ban against Russia
Community Note

Nothing is settled yet. The IIHF says adding a team weeks before a 10-country tournament raises scheduling, travel and security problems, and Hockey Canada says Ottawa has not put money into the 2027 world juniors, so it is unclear whether the federal restriction on funded events applies. The province gave $11 million in 2024 toward hosting.

Sources: The Canadian Press; CTV News; Daily Faceoff.

Canada

Canada–U.S. trade talks are set to restart after a Carney–Trump call, and Trump says Keystone XL is part of the deal

Prime Minister Mark Carney said on X on Sunday that he had a “good conversation” with President Donald Trump, building on recent talks about the outline of a trade deal, and that both teams would work “in the coming weeks” to deliver it. A source told The Globe and Mail the call happened Saturday and that formal negotiations, stalled since Canada walked away in August, should resume within days (The Globe and Mail, Reuters, Politico).

Speaking in Dubuque, Iowa, Trump said the two sides have “the concept of a deal” and that it would include the Keystone XL pipeline: “They’re going to build the Keystone Pipeline. They’re going to pay for it.” Carney’s office did not immediately respond to that claim (Reuters, Politico, The Globe and Mail).

Why it matters: Tariffs on steel, autos and trucks have hit Canadian jobs for months; a restart of formal talks is the first concrete sign of movement since August, and a revived Keystone XL would carry Alberta oil south.

Sources (3)
  • The Globe and Mail — Canada, U.S. expected to resume formal trade talks, source says
  • Reuters (via StreetInsider) — Trump floats ‘concept’ of US-Canada deal that includes pipeline
  • Politico — Mark Carney says U.S., Canada teams will work on trade deal in ‘coming weeks’
Community Note

No deal text exists yet. The pipeline terms come only from Trump; Canada has not confirmed them. Carney left talks in August saying Washington “asked too much and offered too little,” including on trucks and French-language streaming rules, and those issues are not known to be resolved.

Sources: The Globe and Mail; Reuters; Politico.

Poilievre’s budget wish list: no federal diesel tax until Canada Day, and cancel the Toronto–Quebec City fast train

In a letter to Carney ahead of Thursday’s budget, Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre asked Ottawa to drop all federal taxes on diesel until Canada Day, suspend the GST on fuel until summer and permanently scrap what he calls the fuel standard tax (Global News, The Canadian Press via Toronto Star, The Canada Brief).

He also called for cuts to the bureaucracy and foreign aid, cancelling the ALTO high-speed rail project between Toronto and Quebec City, and sticking to the promised debt and deficit targets. The Parliamentary Budget Officer projects a $63.3-billion deficit this year (Global News, The Canadian Press via Toronto Star).

Why it matters: The Liberals lead a minority, so the budget needs support from at least one other party to pass, and these are the Conservatives’ opening terms.

Sources (3)

  • Toronto Star (The Canadian Press) — Poilievre lays out demands ahead of budget
  • The Canada Brief — Poilievre asks Carney to scrap diesel taxes before the Nov. 5 budget
Community Note

These are opposition demands, not budget measures. The “fuel standard tax” is the Conservatives’ name for the federal Clean Fuel Regulations, which are not a tax collected by Ottawa; the government says they are a standard fuel suppliers must meet.

Bulgaria elects its first woman president, Iliana Yotova, who wants dialogue with Russia

Acting President Iliana Yotova won Sunday’s runoff with about 58 per cent of the vote, according to early results and pollsters, against roughly 38 to 39 per cent for former interim prime minister Andrey Gyurov, who conceded. Turnout was low, around 29 per cent by exit-poll estimates (DW, AP, Euronews).

Yotova, 62, an ally of Prime Minister Rumen Radev, opposes military aid to Ukraine and favours talks with Moscow and easing sanctions, while saying Bulgaria stays “a strong state within a strong Europe.” Gyurov, backed by pro-Western parties, had signed a security deal with Kyiv. EU Commission chief Ursula von der Leyen congratulated her (DW, AP, Euronews).

Why it matters: Bulgaria is in NATO and the EU and borders the Black Sea, where drone attacks on ships are rising; its president commands the armed forces and can veto laws and appoint caretaker governments.

Germany’s “Gen Z” protests turn violent in Frankfurt: 17 shops damaged and more than 100 police hurt

Tens of thousands of mostly young people rallied in dozens of German cities on Saturday under the banner “Gen Z regelt” (Gen Z takes charge), protesting school cuts, the cost of living and military conscription. Most marches were peaceful, but after police broke up the Frankfurt march in the evening, rioters smashed windows, looted five stores including a gun shop, set fires and threw petrol bombs, stones and bottles (Reuters, Tagesschau, ZDF).

Hesse’s interior minister said 1,300 officers were deployed, more than 100 were injured and 54 people aged 13 to 29 were arrested. Federal Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt promised severe penalties. Organizers distanced themselves from the violence (Reuters, Tagesschau, Euronews).

Why it matters: It follows weeks of similar youth protests in France and shows anger over living costs spreading across Europe’s biggest economies.

Hundreds of Philippine families spend All Saints’ Day in shelters after Mayon volcano erupts

Mayon, the Philippines’ most active volcano, had a “moderately explosive eruption” on Friday, sending up an ash plume. The volcanology agency Phivolcs raised the alert to Level 3, meaning a new phase of magma eruption, and police sealed off the six-kilometre danger zone (Inquirer, ABS-CBN, GMA News).

About 1,500 residents of Tabaco, Ligao and Camalig in Albay province remain in evacuation centres, and about 420 families spent Sunday’s All Saints’ Day holiday there. Phivolcs logged 15 volcanic earthquakes and 30 rockfalls in a day, and officials can’t yet say when people can go home (Inquirer, Daily Tribune, ABS-CBN).

Why it matters: Families usually spend All Saints’ Day at relatives’ graves, so this year many in Albay could not; Level 3 can last weeks.

Oil giant Aramco closes a US$2.6-billion deal for 49% of Saudi Arabia’s national AI company

Saudi Aramco has completed its purchase of a 49 per cent stake in HUMAIN, the AI company launched by the Public Investment Fund in May 2025; the PIF keeps 51 per cent. Aramco is putting in about 9.9 billion riyals (US$2.6 billion) in cash this year plus its own AI, computing and digital assets (Arab News, Argaam, Arabian Business, Aramco).

HUMAIN builds data centres, cloud services and AI models, including the Arabic language model ALLaM, and plans 3 gigawatts of data-centre capacity by 2030. Aramco plans to run AI across its oil and gas operations on HUMAIN’s infrastructure (Arab News, Arabian Business).

Why it matters: It shows oil money moving fast into AI computing, the same race that is bringing proposed data-centre campuses to the Edmonton region.

The FBI now blames North Korea for the record US$6.3-billion Binance heist

What changed since the last edition: Last time we covered this hack (Oct. 30), North Korea was only suspected by private experts. On Saturday the FBI formally tied it to the North Korean group TraderTraitor, and Binance said it can cover about 90% of the losses.

The FBI said Thursday’s theft from Binance, the world’s largest crypto exchange, was the work of TraderTraitor, the North Korean group also behind last year’s US$1.5-billion Bybit hack. It says the hackers are converting the stolen coins mainly into ether and will likely try to launder them through DeFi and instant-swap services, and it asked exchanges to block linked addresses (AP via PBS, Al Jazeera, Euronews).

Binance says it holds enough of the same coins to cover about 90 per cent of what was taken and has promised customers full reimbursement. President Trump said Saturday, “We don’t know if it’s North Korea” (AP via PBS, Al Jazeera, Reuters via Investing.com).

Why it matters: It is the largest crypto theft on record, and the money is expected to help fund North Korea’s weapons programs; anyone in crypto should watch for frozen or flagged transfers.

Science corner

Science corner: a 4,500-year-old Egyptian paint, rebuilt as glowing flakes that light up the living brain

Egyptian Blue, one of the first human-made pigments, coated ancient statues and tomb walls. It has a hidden talent: under near-infrared light it glows in a band that cameras can see through skin and bone. Swiss researchers have now turned it into ultra-thin flakes that glow far brighter (Empa via Nanowerk, News-Medical, Tech Times).

The team, from Empa, ETH Zurich, the University of Zurich and Balgrist University Hospital, calls the material “Zurich Blue.” Instead of grinding copper ore like ancient painters, they build the pigment in a flame from nanoparticles, then uses a laser to make the particles assemble into bright two-dimensional sheets, a process they say can scale up to industrial levels. In mice, the flakes let them image blood flow through the skull and follow single immune cells that had swallowed the pigment, in real time. The work appears in Advanced Materials (Nanowerk, News-Medical, Tech Times).

Why it matters: Brighter, safer contrast agents could let doctors watch inflammation and disease inside the body at a much finer scale, and the team hopes to attach drugs to the same flakes.

Sources (3)
  • Nanowerk (Empa) — Ancient Egyptian blue pigment transformed into glowing nanosheets for brain imaging
  • News-Medical — Ancient Egyptian pigment inspires new bright brain imaging tool
  • Tech Times — Egyptian Blue nanosheets track living brain immune cells in real time
Community Note

This is lab and mouse work only. The team says clinical testing is the next step, and safety in people has not been studied yet.

Sources: Empa via Nanowerk; News-Medical; Tech Times; Advanced Materials.

AI news and advances

DeepSeek open-sources an always-on coding agent that waits in a GitHub repo and answers with pull requests

China’s DeepSeek has released dsh-superagent, a free, MIT-licensed research prototype from its Harness team. It watches one GitHub repository and, optionally, a Feishu chat; when an approved person asks for something, it opens a separate work session for that job and replies, often with a pull request (GitHub, MarkTechPost, AI Affairs).

The design splits the work: a lightweight “router” keeps listening while isolated “workers” do the jobs in parallel. In DeepSeek’s own simulation with about 300 messages, the slowest 5% of tasks finished in 15.7 minutes versus 29.5 for a single-session agent. By default it only records what it would have done unprompted (GitHub, MarkTechPost).

Why it matters: It is a free, open challenger to paid coding agents such as Claude Code and Codex, and its idea is that human attention, not AI ability, is now the bottleneck.

Sources (3)
  • GitHub — deepseek-ai/dsh-superagent-prototype
  • MarkTechPost — DeepSeek open-sources dsh-superagent, a router-worker coding agent
  • AI Affairs — DeepSeek Harness team releases coding agent prototype for GitHub
Community Note

DeepSeek calls it a prototype that is not production-ready. Its speed figures come from simulated messages, not real coding work, and the workers run with full access to the computer’s user account, so it should be run in an isolated account.

Sources: GitHub; MarkTechPost; AI Affairs.

OA student kept sitting in the dirt at a bus stop, so the couple who lived there built her a bench, then a little library

Seen on: TikTok, where Maren Douglass (@teacherandtraveler) posted the doorbell-camera clips, as reported by People, Good News Network and The Times of India.

Florida teachers Maren and David Douglass noticed on their security camera that a student assigned to a bus stop at the end of their driveway sat on the ground next to a busy road every morning. They were in hospital at the time for David’s cancer treatment, so they ordered a bench online and set it up as soon as they got home (People via Yahoo, Good News Network, The Times of India).

The camera caught her jumping for joy when she found it, and she left a note: “Now I don’t have to worry about dirt and bugs.” The couple then built a small free library, which other neighbourhood kids now use, and added an umbrella and snacks. The video passed 14 million views (People via Yahoo, Good News Network).

Why it matters: A cheap, simple gesture turned into a neighbourhood book swap, and millions of people shared it as a reminder of what kindness to a stranger’s kid can look like.
