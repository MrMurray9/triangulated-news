Alberta

Etihad lands Calgary’s first non-stop flight from the Persian Gulf, with Abu Dhabi service four times a week

Etihad Airways flight EY29 from Abu Dhabi landed at Calgary International Airport on Sunday, Oct. 25, and was greeted with a water-cannon salute. It is the first direct air link between Calgary and any Gulf city. Tourism Minister Joseph Schow and Calgary Airports CEO Chris Dinsdale took part in the ribbon-cutting (Calgary Herald, Alberta government).

The route uses a Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner and runs four times a week, on Tuesdays, Thursdays, Saturdays and Sundays. The return flight, EY30, leaves Calgary at 1 p.m. and reaches Abu Dhabi at 12:55 p.m. the next day, about 14 hours in the air. Calgary is Etihad’s second Canadian destination, after Toronto (Gulf News, Aviation A2Z).

Why it matters: Albertans get a one-stop route to India, South Asia and the Middle East without connecting through Toronto, Vancouver or Europe. Gulf hubs also connect Alberta’s energy industry to Middle East customers and investors.

Edmonton International does not get this route; Edmontonians would need to fly to Calgary or Toronto to connect. The schedule and aircraft listed are Etihad’s published plan, and seasonal changes are possible.

Misericordia and Grey Nuns emergency rooms add walk-through weapons scanners, joining the Royal Alex and University of Alberta

Covenant Health has installed walk-through weapons detection systems at the emergency departments of Edmonton’s Misericordia and Grey Nuns Community hospitals. Screening is voluntary. Patients who decline it but need care will still be treated, with Protective Services keeping watch. Lockers are available for items people can’t bring in, and a one-bag policy is on the way (Covenant Health, CityNews Edmonton).

The Royal Alexandra Hospital added screening in May after a stabbing, and the University of Alberta Hospital also screens visitors. A United Nurses of Alberta survey found about four in 10 nurses had experienced physical violence at work (CityNews Edmonton, Ai Channel).

Why it matters: Covenant’s two south and west Edmonton emergency rooms see thousands of patients a week. With this change, screening now covers most of the city’s major ERs.

Sources (3)
  • Covenant Health — Misericordia and Grey Nuns install weapons detection systems
  • CityNews Edmonton — Covenant Health adds weapons screening to Misericordia and Grey Nuns ERs
  • Ai Channel — Weapons detection at Edmonton hospitals
Community Note

Covenant Health is the operator and a primary source; the details were independently reported by CityNews and Ai Channel. Walk-through scanners like these detect metal objects; they are not airport-style X-ray screening, and the hospitals have not said how many weapons the scanners find.

Sources: Covenant Health; CityNews Edmonton; Ai Channel.

Canada

Ontario picks mayors and councils Monday: Olivia Chow and Brad Bradford are neck and neck in the country’s biggest city race

About 11.8 million Ontarians can vote in municipal elections on Monday, Oct. 26, with 6,663 candidates running for about 2,800 positions. In Toronto, polls are open from 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern (8 a.m. to 6 p.m. in Edmonton), and 222,550 people, about 10.4 per cent of electors, voted in advance (Global News, City of Toronto).

The latest polls disagree. Nanos for CTV has Mayor Olivia Chow ahead 49.8 to 46.6 per cent, and Pollara has her up 55 to 44. Liaison Strategies and Mainstreet Research both show Coun. Brad Bradford narrowly ahead, 49 to 47 and 48.9 to 47.5 (NOW Toronto, Global News, Nanos).

Why it matters: The Toronto race is a test of Chow’s progressive agenda on housing, transit and taxes against Bradford’s pitch to cut costs and speed up construction. Mayors elected Monday will be working with Ontario’s strong-mayor powers.

Joly gives Stelco’s U.S. owner until early November to reverse Hamilton job cuts or face a forced sale

Industry Minister Mélanie Joly has given Cleveland-Cliffs 15 days from Oct. 22 to reverse planned job cuts at the former Stelco steel mill in Hamilton, Ont. In a letter released to the media, she says the plan breaks the employment commitments the U.S. steelmaker made under the Investment Canada Act when it bought Stelco (Reuters via Mining Weekly, The Globe and Mail).

If the company doesn’t comply, Joly says Ottawa could go to court to seek a forced sale of the plant and fines of up to C$10,000 a day. Cleveland-Cliffs denies breaking its commitments. The United Steelworkers say 1,766 union and 603 salaried jobs are covered by those commitments (The Deep Dive, United Steelworkers).

Why it matters: This is a rare test of whether Ottawa will enforce a foreign takeover’s job promises at a time when U.S. tariffs are already squeezing Canadian steel.

The 15-day clock means the deadline falls around Nov. 6. A forced sale would need a court order; it is not automatic. The job numbers come from the union, and Cleveland-Cliffs has not published its own figures.

Saudi defences shoot down a Houthi missile and drone barrage, with debris falling near Mecca and Jeddah

The Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted 10 ballistic missiles fired from Yemen at southern Saudi Arabia, plus a missile and drone over Riyadh and a cruise missile aimed at Khamis Mushait. Shrapnel fell in Jeddah, Taif, Rabigh and Mecca province, and two people were hurt by debris (Al Jazeera, Anadolu Agency, CNBC).

Gulf Co-operation Council states called the attack a “grave violation of the sanctity of the Holy Sites.” The Houthis deny aiming at holy sites, and their spokesperson Yahya Saree warned civilians to stay away from Riyadh’s airport. The airport later resumed international flights from Terminal 2 (Al Jazeera, CNBC).

Why it matters: The attack is a major widening of the renewed Yemen war onto Saudi cities and the Hajj region. It puts Gulf air travel and oil infrastructure at risk, which can quickly push up fuel prices.

Court keeps Brazil’s polls open two extra hours; early count has Lula slightly ahead of Flávio Bolsonaro, with no winner yet

What changed since the last edition: Voting has ended and counting has begun. Polls were supposed to close at 2 p.m. Edmonton time, but a court order kept many open two extra hours. With about 2 per cent of ballot boxes counted at 4:17 p.m. MDT, Lula was narrowly ahead. No winner has been declared.

Supreme Court Justice Cristiano Zanin extended voting by two hours, to 7 p.m. Brasília time (4 p.m. in Edmonton). He acted at the request of Lula’s campaign, which complained that voters in about 10 states faced restrictions on public transit to polling stations (The Guardian, O Globo).

The count began as polls closed. At 7:17 p.m. Brasília time, with 2.11 per cent of ballot boxes counted, President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva had 51.72 per cent of valid votes and Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro had 48.28 per cent. That margin is too early to call because results arrive unevenly from different regions (TSE data via CGN, Band).

Why it matters: Last week’s polls were virtually tied, so the extension and the order in which regions report will be scrutinized. Both camps have said they will watch the count closely.

Workers strike at Canada’s largest copper mine as Teck’s Highland Valley talks break down

About 1,200 members of United Steelworkers Local 7619 walked off the job at Teck Resources’ Highland Valley Copper mine near Logan Lake, B.C., on Saturday, Oct. 24, at 8 a.m., and picket lines went up at 11 a.m. Their contract expired Sept. 30, and 100 per cent of the 944 members who voted backed a strike (CFJC Today, Reuters via Mining.com.au).

The union says wages, pensions and keeping workers are the main issues. Teck says the mine is operating at reduced capacity during the strike. The company recently got approval to extend the mine’s life to 2046 (Teck, CFJC Today).

Why it matters: Highland Valley is Canada’s biggest open-pit copper mine. A long strike would reduce supply of a metal used in power grids and electric vehicles, and hit B.C.’s Interior economy.

Science corner

A ceramic cell that grows its own nanoparticles doubles the hydrogen made from steam using the same electricity

Researchers at the University of Groningen in the Netherlands, led by Dr. Vassilis Kyriakou, built a protonic ceramic electrolyser cell that splits steam at 400–650°C. Its perovskite electrode “self-assembles,” sprouting tiny praseodymium oxide nanoparticles on its surface. Combined with a mild acid wash, this doubled hydrogen output for the same electrical input (University of Groningen, Interesting Engineering).

The cell ran for more than 500 hours with current efficiency above 95 per cent. The work is published in Energy & Environmental Science (TechXplore, EuropaWire).

Why it matters: Making hydrogen from water cheaply is the main barrier to using it for steel, fertilizer and heavy trucks. A cell that can use waste heat from industry could cut costs, which matters for Alberta’s hydrogen plans.

Sources (4)
  • University of Groningen — Doubling hydrogen production through a synergy of materials
  • Interesting Engineering — Ceramic cell delivers double clean hydrogen
  • TechXplore — Self-assembling electrode doubles hydrogen output
  • EuropaWire — University of Groningen hydrogen research
Community Note

TechXplore and EuropaWire largely republish the university’s release; Interesting Engineering is the only fully independent write-up. This is a lab-scale cell, and 500 hours is short compared with the tens of thousands of hours industrial electrolysers must run.

Sources: University of Groningen; Interesting Engineering; TechXplore; EuropaWire.

AI news and advances

Jack Dorsey’s Block unveils Dessa, a voice AI that listens and talks at the same time and runs on a laptop

Block, the company behind Square and Cash App, announced Dessa on Oct. 23. It is a “full-duplex” speech-to-speech model, meaning it can listen and talk at the same time and handle interruptions like a person. Block says it was trained only on human speech, using a single rack of NVIDIA GPUs (TechCrunch, The Verge).

Block says Dessa runs locally on a MacBook with an M4 Max chip or better. For now it is a research preview with no public access, and Block is inviting safety researchers to test it first (TokenPost, Block).

Why it matters: An AI voice that sounds human and runs offline on a laptop would make AI assistants more private. It would also make voice-cloning scams harder to spot.

F“Banana hero”: a woman in a banana costume calls police after an SUV ploughs through an Orlando patio

Seen on: Facebook — The Moderne Bar’s post asking the public to help find her

Around 4 a.m. Saturday, Stephanie Oropeza was heading home from a Halloween party in a full banana costume when she saw an SUV veer across the road and smash through the outdoor patio of The Moderne Bar on Colonial Drive in Orlando. She stopped and called police. Nobody was hurt (News 6/ClickOrlando, Orlando Weekly).

The bar posted security video of the costumed caller to find and thank her, and it spread fast. Owner Michael Nguyen said people asked, “Is this AI?” The bar tracked her down and treated her to a free meal. Oropeza says her superhero name would be the “Banana Bandit” (News 6, WFTV).

Why it matters: It’s a funny, true reminder that stopping to call for help matters, whatever you happen to be wearing.
