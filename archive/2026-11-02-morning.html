Alberta

Alberta’s first workday on permanent “Alberta Time”: darker school mornings, a one-hour gap to Toronto, and some phones still showing the wrong hour

Clocks did not fall back on Sunday. Alberta, British Columbia, Manitoba, northwestern Ontario and the Northwest Territories stayed on daylight time, so Alberta now sits at UTC−6 all year, a setting the province calls Alberta Time (ABT). Today is the first school and work day under the new clock (The Canadian Press via National Observer, Calgary Herald, Government of Alberta).

Some people reported Sunday that their phones showed the wrong time. Telus said the problem comes from how some phones update their time-zone data, not from its network, and suggested turning on “set time zone automatically” and restarting. Google had already told Android users in Canada to install pending updates and restart, or pick a matching time zone by hand. An Edmonton day-home operator said children won’t have naps thrown off, while a Calgary hairstylist worried about clients missing appointments this week (The Canadian Press, The Verge).

Why it matters: Mornings stay dark longer: around the winter solstice the sun won’t rise in Edmonton until close to 9:50 a.m. Toronto is now just one hour ahead instead of two, and U.S. stock markets open at 8:30 a.m. our time until March.

Justice minister refuses to delay Alberta’s 2027 election, as the disputed riding-map report heads to the legislature

What changed since the last edition: On Oct. 31 we reported that McClure asked MLAs either to drop the new riding map or to push back the vote. On Sunday the government turned down both options, and the boundaries report itself is due in the legislature this week.

In a letter posted Sunday, Justice Minister Mickey Amery told Chief Electoral Officer Gordon McClure the government has “no intention of changing” the election date, which the law sets for on or before Oct. 18, 2027. He said MLAs will settle the final boundaries before the fall sitting ends, which would leave Elections Alberta about 320 days, roughly 11 months, to get ready, and noted that provincial law does not promise the agency “lengthy advance notice” (Global News, The Canadian Press via 980 CJME, KaCheng).

McClure had warned that a provincial vote normally takes up to 18 months to prepare and that his office is stretched. The advisory panel’s report on new ridings was set to be tabled in the legislature Monday. Opposition Leader Naheed Nenshi, who was briefed on it, says it gerrymanders Calgary and Lethbridge, and two panel members said the proposed map separates rural and urban voters (Global News, The Canadian Press).

Why it matters: The riding map decides how many seats Calgary, Edmonton, Lethbridge and rural Alberta each get in 2027, and the province’s own election officer says the timeline is tight.

Six federal byelections set for Dec. 7, from Saskatchewan’s Yorkton–Melville to three Quebec seats

Prime Minister Mark Carney announced Sunday that voters will fill six empty House of Commons seats on Dec. 7: Brantford—Brant South and Scarborough North in Ontario; Laurier—Sainte-Marie, Rosemont—La Petite-Patrie and Saint-Hyacinthe—Bagot—Acton in Quebec; and Yorkton—Melville in Saskatchewan (Prime Minister’s Office, Global News, CTV News, paNOW).

The seats opened up for different reasons. Conservative Larry Brock went back to the Crown prosecutor’s office, Liberal Shaun Chen left for health reasons, NDP MP Alexandre Boulerice and the Bloc’s Simon-Pierre Savard-Tremblay won seats in Quebec’s provincial election, Liberal Steven Guilbeault resigned in August, and Conservative Cathay Wagantall stepped down Aug. 31. The Liberals are running former Desjardins CEO Guy Cormier in Laurier—Sainte-Marie and Yorkton Mayor Aaron Kienle in Yorkton—Melville, where the Conservatives picked Nelson Pohl. In Brantford, pastor Dave Carroll, whom Brock had endorsed, plans to run as an independent against Conservative nominee Kevin Davis (Global News).

Why it matters: These are the first federal votes after this Thursday’s budget, and the only Prairie race is in Saskatchewan, a test of Conservative strength in a seat they have held since 2015.

More than 300,000 Yemenis forced from their homes in two months as fighting flares in the west, UN migration agency says

The International Organization for Migration said Sunday that more than 300,000 people have fled fighting in western Yemen, almost all of them in September and October. Families face soaring prices, too little shelter and checkpoints that slow aid (IOM, Arab News, Khaleej Times, Free Malaysia Today).

More than 43,000 people have left one district alone, Al-Madaribah Wa Al-Aarah in Lahj province, since early October. Many families from Taiz province are heading south to Aden, which now hosts more than 56,000 displaced people, and clashes around the city of Taiz have pushed some food prices up as much as fivefold. The IOM is asking for US$45 million over six months and has five mobile medical teams in Taiz, Marib, Lahj and Aden (IOM, Arab News).

Why it matters: Yemen was already one of the world’s worst humanitarian emergencies; a new wave of displacement next to the Red Sea shipping lane comes as aid budgets are shrinking.

Kherson braces for shelling again as the six-day Oleshky truce expires, after Ukraine moves 167 civilians to safety

The local ceasefire around Russian-held Oleshky in southern Ukraine expired late Sunday, and Anadolu reports neither side recorded a violation during it. Why it matters: The Dnipro riverbank around Kherson is one of the deadliest stretches of the front for civilians, and a local truce that held for six days is rare in this war.

Marvell agrees to buy laser maker Lumentum in a US$115-billion all-stock bet on AI data-centre links

Chipmaker Marvell Technology will pay 4.5 of its own shares for each Lumentum share, about US$1,132 apiece, a 23.9 per cent premium to the Oct. 28 prices before deal talk leaked. Marvell shareholders would own about 67 per cent of the combined company and Lumentum’s about 33 per cent (Marvell, Reuters via Investing.com, CNBC).

Lumentum makes the lasers, optical transceivers and optical switches that carry data between AI chips over light, while Marvell designs networking and custom chips. The companies promise more than US$1 billion a year in cost savings and expect to close in the first half of 2028. Marvell CEO Matt Murphy stays in charge, and Lumentum CEO Michael Hurlston joins Marvell’s board. Lumentum stock was halted before Monday’s open (Investing.com, Reuters, Marvell).

Why it matters: Moving data between chips, not just the chips themselves, has become a choke point for AI data centres, and this would put one company in charge of both the electronics and the lasers.

India’s Affle will take Israeli ad-tech firm Perion private for US$420 million in cash

Affle agreed to pay US$11.70 a share for Perion Network, about 35 per cent above Friday’s close of US$8.70. Perion shares jumped about 31 per cent in premarket trading Monday (MarketWatch, ExchangeWire, InsideArbitrage).

Perion, based in Tel Aviv with most of its business in North America, sells advertising across connected TV, retail media and search; Affle focuses on mobile ads in emerging markets. Perion’s US$228 million in cash covers more than half the price, Citibank is lending the rest, and Perion has 40 days to look for a better offer. The deal should close in early 2027, after which Perion leaves Nasdaq and the Tel Aviv exchange (MarketWatch, ExchangeWire).

Why it matters: It’s another sign that small online-ad companies are being bought up cheaply as AI changes how people search and how ads are sold.

Science corner

Science corner: a paper-thin electronic skin that draws a picture of every touch in light

Most touch sensors turn pressure into numbers that software has to draw on a screen. A team at the National University of Singapore built one that draws the picture itself. Called eLuminator, it is about 70 micrometres thick, thinner than a sheet of office paper, and shines light in the exact shape and strength of each press (NUS via EurekAlert, Nature Communications, SmartWearGuide).

The trick is a layer of tiny domes. When a finger or object squeezes them, the electric field across the film gets stronger, and copper-doped zinc sulfide particles light up in that spot. The result is a live glowing map of the touch, sharp to about 30 micrometres, roughly 847 dots per inch and enough to show fingerprint ridges. The same film also gives an electrical pressure reading up to 180 kilopascals. The work is published in Nature Communications (NUS, Nature Communications, SmartWearGuide).

Why it matters: A skin that shows touch with no camera or computer could make pressure-mapping simpler for robot grippers, prosthetics and wearables.

AI news and advances

SenseTime gives away a 9-billion-parameter model that runs its own layers twice to think deeper

China’s SenseTime has released SenseNova-Loop-9B-Preview as an open model under the Apache 2.0 licence, with weights on Hugging Face and code on GitHub. It reads text and images and writes text, and is aimed at document reading, coding and tasks that call outside tools (TechNode, Hugging Face, GitHub, MindStudio).

Instead of stacking more layers, the model passes data through a shared block of 24 decoder layers twice, giving it a deeper chain of computation without adding weights. SenseTime says it was trained on sequences up to 262,144 tokens long and published inference and training instructions alongside it (TechNode, Hugging Face).

Why it matters: Looping layers is one way to get more out of a small model that fits on cheaper hardware, and it adds to the stream of free Chinese models competing with paid U.S. ones.

Sources (4)
  • TechNode: SenseTime open-sources 9-billion-parameter AI model that reuses layers
  • Hugging Face: sensenova/SenseNova-Loop-9B-Preview
  • GitHub: OpenSenseNova/SenseNova-Loop
  • MindStudio: What a looped decoder means for agentic AI
Community Note

It is labelled a preview, and its benchmark numbers are SenseTime’s own. Reusing layers saves memory but not computing time, since each loop still has to run, and some speed-up features are still in development.

Sources: TechNode; Hugging Face; GitHub; MindStudio.

OAn orphan in China with almost no family invited strangers to his wedding, and thousands drove to his mountain village to celebrate

Seen on: Douyin (China’s version of TikTok), where Xu Chenghong posted his invitation video, as reported by AsiaOne, Malay Mail and Global Times.

Xu Chenghong, 24, from Zhaotong in Yunnan province, lost his father at three and was raised by his grandmother, who also died while he was a child. He saved more than 160,000 yuan to marry his girlfriend, Luo Chui, but worried there wouldn’t be enough guests to fill even a few banquet tables, so he invited the public on Douyin (AsiaOne, Malay Mail, Global Times).

Thousands came to the Oct. 31 wedding. Guests brought appliances, food and even livestock, volunteer chefs cooked the banquet, and one well-wisher lent a Maybach for the couple. The village road was too narrow for the crowd, so the party moved to a larger square while local officials handled traffic, crowds and food safety (AsiaOne, Sina, Malay Mail).

Why it matters: A young man who feared an empty wedding got a whole country’s goodwill instead, and millions shared it as a reminder of what strangers can do.
