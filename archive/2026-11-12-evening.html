Alberta

Alberta puts $14 million into AI software that speeds up MRI scans, after a pilot on 10 machines added almost 12,000 exams

The province and Acute Care Alberta announced Thursday that they will spend about $14 million to install artificial-intelligence software directly on hospital MRI scanners across Alberta. The software, approved by Health Canada, sharpens images so that scans can be done in less time. A pilot on 10 scanners that began in April 2025 led to almost 12,000 more MRIs (The Canadian Press via Global News and CTV News, CityNews Edmonton).

The government expects the rollout to be finished by 2028 and says it will raise MRI capacity by about 20%, or more than 41,000 extra scans a year. Hospitals Minister Adriana LaGrange said Alberta did a record number of MRIs last year. The province says the software runs on each scanner and doesn’t send patient records or images to outside networks (Global News, CityNews, CTV News).

Why it matters: MRI waits are among the longest in Alberta’s health system, and getting more scans out of machines the province already owns is cheaper and faster than buying new ones.

Canada

In Dublin, Carney is again the only non-European leader at Europe’s big security summit, and he presses Greece and Cyprus on the Canada-EU trade deal

Prime Minister Mark Carney attended the European Political Community summit in Dublin on Thursday, the second time he’s been the only leader from outside Europe at the table. Leaders discussed economic resilience, security and Ukraine, and Carney joined a meeting with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on peace efforts and Europe’s security arrangements (Prime Minister’s Office, Global News, The Globe and Mail).

On the sidelines he met the leaders of Greece and Cyprus, two of the EU countries that still haven’t ratified CETA, the Canada-EU trade deal that has been provisionally in force since 2017. Cypriot media reported that Cyprus said it remains committed to ratifying it. Carney also met the leaders of Denmark, France, Ireland, Latvia and Sweden before leaving for the Philippines, Cambodia and China (Politis, Prime Minister’s Office, The Irish Times).

Why it matters: Europe is Canada’s second-biggest trading partner, and Ottawa is leaning on it harder for trade and defence while relations with Washington stay rocky.

World

America’s record-breaking 1950s ocean liner is sunk on purpose off Florida to become the world’s largest artificial reef

The SS United States, a 990-foot liner longer than the Titanic, slowly went down on Wednesday about 22 nautical miles southwest of Destin–Fort Walton Beach. Crews flooded it without explosives, and it took hours to disappear; NPR reports it went under about 10 p.m. Eastern time and settled upright roughly 180 feet down. Okaloosa County bought the ship in 2024 after the conservancy that owned it couldn’t turn it into a hotel or museum (AP, NPR, Pensacola News Journal).

On its maiden voyage in July 1952 the ship won the Blue Riband for the fastest Atlantic crossing, and it went on to cross the ocean more than 800 times. The county moved the sinking up a day to dodge high winds. Divers from Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution and local teams are surveying the wreck first, local charters begin this weekend, and the conservancy plans a museum on land (Pensacola News Journal, NPR, AP).

Why it matters: A famous piece of 20th-century history gets a second life as fish habitat and a tourist draw, instead of being cut up for scrap.

Democrat Scott Colom unseats Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith by about 5,600 votes, becoming Mississippi’s first Black U.S. senator since Reconstruction

The Associated Press called the race for Colom on Wednesday, eight days after the Nov. 3 midterm vote, once updated counts from Jackson County put him ahead for good. The latest tally has him at about 474,800 votes to Hyde-Smith’s 469,200, or 49.2% to 48.7% (AP, NPR, The Guardian).

He is the first Black senator from Mississippi since the Reconstruction era after the Civil War, and the first Black person elected to the seat by popular vote. A Democrat hasn’t won a Senate race in the deeply Republican state in decades, and the result widens the new Democratic Senate majority (AP, The Washington Post, ABC News).

Why it matters: It’s one of the biggest upsets of the U.S. midterms and adds to the Democratic majority that will now control both houses of Congress.

AI cloud company Nebius cuts its 2026 sales forecast because data-centre space it rents is running late, and its stock drops about 17%

Amsterdam-based Nebius, which rents out Nvidia-powered computing to AI companies, said Thursday that some third-party data-centre capacity due this year, including a site in New Jersey, has slipped into the first half of 2027. It trimmed its 2026 revenue forecast to US$3 billion to $3.2 billion, from up to $3.4 billion, even though third-quarter revenue of $981 million beat estimates (Reuters via CNA, CNBC, The Next Web).

It also raised this year’s spending plan to about $27 billion and expects $40 billion to $45 billion in 2027, saying most of that will be covered by customer prepayments and asset-backed financing tied to contracts with Microsoft and Meta. “None of that capacity is cancelled,” CEO Arkady Volozh said. The shares closed down about 16.8%, in a rough day for AI stocks (Reuters via CNA, CNBC, Investing.com).

Why it matters: Delays at “neocloud” companies like Nebius are an early test of whether the AI data-centre boom can be built and paid for as fast as promised.

Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia’s CEO; finance chief Colette Kress takes over for now and the stock closes down about 4%

What changed since the last edition: This morning the board had met overnight with no decision. It has now accepted Huang’s resignation, named Colette Kress interim CEO and Harvey Jones chair, and the stock has finished its first trading day without him.

Nvidia said Thursday that its board accepted Huang’s resignation as president, chief executive and director on Wednesday night, effective immediately. Chief financial officer Colette Kress, 59, becomes interim CEO while keeping her finance job, and longtime director Harvey Jones, a former Synopsys CEO, becomes the company’s first board chair. The board will consider inside and outside candidates for a permanent CEO (Nvidia, SEC filing, TechCrunch, Reuters).

The shares plunged about 14% at the open, then recovered to close down about 4.3% at US$166.19. Huang, the co-founder who built the company over 33 years, keeps about a 3.5% stake. Kress told staff it had been “an extraordinarily difficult period” and set an all-hands meeting for Nov. 18, the day after Nvidia’s quarterly results (Reuters, The Guardian, TechCrunch).

Why it matters: Nvidia makes most of the chips behind the AI boom, and is one of the biggest holdings in many index and pension funds, so who runs it matters well beyond Silicon Valley.

Science corner

Science corner: Penn engineers make soft artificial muscles that feel their own position, so a robot finger can play piano keys and robot eyes can hold a gaze

Soft “muscles” made from liquid crystal elastomers shrink when they’re heated, but they’re hard to stop halfway: they keep contracting or relax as they cool. A team led by Shu Yang and Cynthia Sung at the University of Pennsylvania threaded an ultrathin copper coil through a fibre of elastomer mixed with graphene. The coil does two jobs at once. It heats the fibre to make it contract, and its electrical resistance changes as the fibre moves, so it doubles as a built-in sensor (Penn Engineering, Advanced Functional Materials).

By watching that signal and adjusting the heat in real time, the fibres, called CoilLCE, can hold an in-between position for a long time, which the team says is the first feedback control shown on this kind of actuator. They used them in opposing pairs, like real muscles, to press piano keys with a robotic finger and to steer a pair of artificial eyes with four pairs of muscles. Adding graphene makes the fibres tougher, so they survive pulling against each other (Penn Engineering, Newzino).

Why it matters: Small, quiet muscles that know where they are could one day give robot hands a gentler touch and let humanoid faces make natural expressions, without bulky motors.

Sources (3)
  • Penn Engineering: Artificial muscles give robots precise control
  • Advanced Functional Materials: Multistage, self-sensing artificial muscles with coordination from liquid crystal elastomer–graphene composite fibers
  • Newzino: Penn engineers build artificial muscles that sense their own position
Community Note

This is lab-stage research. The muscles work by heating, so they are slower than motors and need time to cool, and the team says linking many of them into one system is the next challenge.

Coverage is still thin: the main sources are the peer-reviewed paper and Penn’s own release, with Newzino summarising them, so we treat it as a light science item rather than a news story.

AI news and advances

OpenAI launches AI agents that try to hack a company’s own apps and then write the fixes, and says firms will be only as secure as the AI they defend with

OpenAI unveiled two products in its Daybreak security line on Thursday. Daybreak Red Team, in early access, uses a fleet of AI agents to probe a company’s web apps for weaknesses, check bug reports from scanners and bug bounties, and test whether patches actually work. Daybreak Defense Factory scans codebases for flaws and drafts patches, using OpenAI’s GPT-6 Cyber model, and engineers review and approve each fix before it ships (The Register, OpenAI, The Deep View).

“We’re now at a point where companies will be secure only to the extent of the frontier model that they use to defend themselves,” OpenAI president Greg Brockman said in a keynote, while adding that security basics still matter. The same day, a group of nonprofits including Public Citizen sued OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and SpaceX’s AI business in San Francisco, asking a court to stop them building systems more capable than their current best (The Register, The Deep View).

Why it matters: AI models are getting good enough to find software holes quickly, for attackers and defenders alike, so tools like these could change how quickly bugs get fixed.
