Alberta

Advance voting day two is open across Alberta; special ballots must arrive by 5 p.m. Friday

Albertans cast ballots for a second day of advance voting Wednesday in the Oct. 19 referendum. Elections Alberta says electors may use any advance station in their own electoral division through Saturday, Oct. 17; on Election Day they must use their assigned location. Day-one tech delays that pushed 17 of 311 stations late opening Tuesday have been resolved, the agency said, with affected sites given extended Tuesday hours so voters still received a full shift.

Special (mail) ballots must reach Elections Alberta in Edmonton by 5 p.m. Friday, Oct. 16. As of Monday evening the agency had reported roughly 391,000 of about 658,000 requested special ballots returned. Unofficial results are due within 48 hours of polls closing Monday; Question 10 (Canada vs. a path to a binding separation referendum) is counted first.

Why it matters: Four advance days remain after today — and Friday’s special-ballot deadline is the last chance for mail voters.

What changed since the last edition: Tuesday evening covered late openings and extended hours. New this morning: day-two voting continues provincewide with those tech issues marked resolved, plus a hard reminder of Friday’s 5 p.m. special-ballot cutoff.

Science corner

Science corner: MIT lipid nanoparticles reprogram NK cells inside the body to hunt cancerous and autoreactive T cells

MIT engineers report in Nature Biotechnology a way to create CAR natural-killer (NK) cells without removing them from the patient. Antibody-coated lipid nanoparticles deliver mRNA that encodes a chimeric antigen receptor straight to NK cells in vivo. In mice, about 80% of targeted NK cells were reprogrammed; treated animals showed remissions in melanoma models, tumour clearance in a humanized T-cell lymphoma model after repeated doses, and healing of autoimmune hepatitis after a single injection.

Senior author Daniel Anderson says skipping ex-vivo manufacturing could cut cost and complexity versus conventional CAR therapies. The work was funded in part by Sanofi and the Marble Center for Cancer Nanomedicine; there are still no FDA-approved CAR NK drugs.

Why it matters: If an off-the-shelf nanoparticle infusion can teach a patient’s own NK cells to kill bad T cells, CAR therapy becomes far easier to scale.

Sources (3)
  • MIT News — Reprogrammed NK immune cells target cancer and autoimmune disorders
  • Nature Biotechnology — In vivo engineering of CAR natural killer cells
  • Phys.org — Nanoparticle injection reprograms NK immune cells
Community Note

These are mouse and humanized-mouse results, not an approved human therapy. Safety, dosing and off-target risks still need clinical trials.

Sources: MIT News; Nature Biotechnology; Phys.org.
