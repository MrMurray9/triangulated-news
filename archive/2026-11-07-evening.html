Triage doctors are now working in six Alberta emergency rooms, the premier says, nearly a year after the Grey Nuns death

Premier Danielle Smith said on her Saturday call-in radio show that physician triage teams were in place as of Saturday in six emergency departments. “It took longer than we hoped, but we now have six facilities where, as of today, they’ve got triage docs in their emergency room,” she said. The idea is to treat and release patients who don’t need admission, or move them elsewhere, so they aren’t stuck for hours in the waiting room (The Canadian Press via CityNews Edmonton and CTV News).

The province first promised the triage liaison physician role in January, after 44-year-old Prashant Sreekumar died on Dec. 22, 2025, having waited nearly eight hours at Edmonton’s Grey Nuns Community Hospital with chest pain. The planned Feb. 1 start slipped while the government and the Alberta Medical Association worked out contracts. In October, the AMA’s emergency-medicine section president, Dr. Paul Parks, warned that without enough nurses behind them, the doctors out front would be “a Band-Aid” (Edmonton Journal, CTV News Edmonton).

Why it matters: If it works, the sickest patients should be seen sooner and the mildest sent home faster at Alberta’s busiest ERs, just as flu season starts.

A public inquiry into Sreekumar’s death resumes in December.

Canada

A stamp will cost $2 in 2027, up from $1.44, and then rise about 7% a year under the budget’s Canada Post plan

The federal budget says the price of a single domestic stamp will jump from $1.44 to $2 sometime in 2027, about a 40% increase, and then climb roughly 7% every year after that. Ottawa estimates the change will cost a typical consumer about $6 a year and a small business about $9, and says it brings Canada Post closer to prices in other OECD countries (Global News, National Post).

The budget also proposes $1.4 billion over two years to keep Canada Post running and $1.2 billion over seven years to move urban door-to-door delivery to community mailboxes. Letter volumes fell from 5.5 billion in 2006 to about 2 billion in 2025, and the Crown corporation reported a $1.57-billion loss last year (CTV News via BNN Bloomberg, National Post). The last stamp increase, in 2025, was 25%.

Why it matters: Mailing a card or a bill will cost noticeably more, and Ottawa is signalling that Canada Post has to lean on higher prices and community mailboxes rather than more bailouts.

The exact 2027 start date isn’t in the budget. The $1.44-to-$2 change and the 7% yearly increases are budget proposals, and they still need regulatory steps before they take effect. Global’s comparison put a U.K. stamp at about $3.39 Canadian and a U.S. stamp at about $1.15 Canadian.

Ottawa asks MPs for another $22.1 billion, led by icebreakers, Arctic satellites and child care

Treasury Board President Shafqat Ali tabled Supplementary Estimates (B) for 2026–27 on Friday, asking Parliament to approve $22.1 billion in new voted spending. With $2.3 billion more in statutory spending, planned federal budgetary spending for the year rises to $538.3 billion (Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat, NetNewsLedger, The Hill Times).

National Defence wants about $5.1 billion, including $1.924 billion for the icebreaker program and $1.563 billion for Arctic military satellite communications, plus $300 million in military aid to Ukraine. Employment and Social Development is seeking $2.4 billion to fill short-term gaps in $10-a-day child care, and Health Canada $821.6 million for the dental care plan. Indigenous programs, VIA Rail ($930.4 million) and $841.2 million in regional tariff relief are also on the list. A supply bill to approve the money is expected in December (NetNewsLedger, The Hill Times).

Why it matters: These estimates show where Ottawa’s money goes in the next few months: the North, defence and keeping child care and dental care funded while the trade fight drags on.

A gunman kills six people in Kraków’s Main Market Square, and police find three more bodies at his home

A Polish man drove into Kraków’s busy Main Market Square at about 3 p.m. Saturday and opened fire with an automatic weapon at several stops. He killed six people, four women and two men, one of them a British citizen, and wounded about 15 others before police shot and arrested him. Poland’s defence minister called it an “act of terror” (AP, The Guardian, Onet).

Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said the suspect acted alone and was fascinated by far-right ideology, holding “ultra-nationalistic” views. Police later found three bodies at his home. Polish outlets say he used a car registered to his father, a licensed gun owner. Prosecutors say the man is in hospital, and the defence minister said he has never been a soldier. He also fired at a passing tram, but no passengers were hurt (Onet, Fakt, The Guardian).

Why it matters: It is one of the deadliest mass shootings in Poland’s modern history, in the heart of a major European tourist city, and officials are treating it as far-right terrorism.

Why it matters: It’s a sign that travel spending is holding up despite war and high prices, and that consumer apps are racing to build their own AI agents before Google and OpenAI become the place where people book trips.

Science corner

Science corner: a smartwatch that flags signs of high blood pressure without ever using a cuff

Google Research scientists report in Nature that an AI model can spot signs of hypertension using only a consumer smartwatch’s optical pulse sensor and its motion sensor, plus the wearer’s height, without the cuff calibration that earlier wearables needed. It looks at many readings over time and gives a probability that the wearer has high blood pressure (Nature, Medical Xpress).

The model learned from nearly 500,000 hours of watch data from 2,109 people. The team then tested it on 196 people against 24-hour ambulatory blood-pressure monitoring, the clinical gold standard. The watch caught 65.8% of hypertension cases, compared with 55.3% for standard office cuff readings, and both had 90% specificity. The authors say the difference was not statistically significant (Nature, Medical Xpress, Nature Asia press release).

Why it matters: More than half of adults with high blood pressure don’t know they have it. A watch that quietly nudges them toward a proper cuff check could catch it years earlier.

Sources (4)
  • Nature: Opportunistically Detecting Signs of Hypertension on a Consumer Smartwatch
  • Medical Xpress: AI-powered smartwatch detects high blood pressure with sensitivity similar to office screening
  • Nature Asia: Smartwatches that can clock high blood pressure (press release)
  • AI Scoop News: Google’s smartwatch hypertension study
Community Note

This is a research result, not a feature on anyone’s watch today, and it doesn’t measure blood pressure itself. The authors say it can’t replace cuff testing. It’s a screening nudge, and a positive result should lead to a proper clinical check. Much of the coverage draws on Nature’s own press materials, and the study was run by Google, which makes the Pixel Watch.

Sources: Nature; Medical Xpress; Nature Asia

AI news and advances

An AI research system cracks an 18-year-old quantum-computing puzzle, the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture, with a proof a computer has checked line by line

Physical Superintelligence (PSI), a research lab, says its in-house AI system found a proof of the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture on the third day of a run. The problem has been open since 2008 and asks whether quantum computers can be exponentially faster than ordinary ones on unstructured inputs. The Lean proof assistant, software that checks every logical step, accepted the proof, and the system then strengthened the result twice (PSI, Unite.AI, Silicon Report).

PSI says the system ran a non-public Anthropic model on PSI’s own machines. Anthropic did not comment on which model was used (Business Insider). The full Lean 4 formalization is public on GitHub, with no gaps or added axioms, and two independent Lean checkers replayed it. Stanford’s Guy Blanc, Jordan Docter, Carmen Strassle and Li-Yang Tan, who proved a special case in August, called it “a huge result” in an assessment PSI published (PSI, Unite.AI).

Why it matters: If it holds up, AI has helped settle a real open question about the limits of quantum computing, and the proof can be checked by machine instead of resting on trust.

Sources (5)
  • PSI: A proof of the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture
  • Business Insider: Unreleased Anthropic model helps solve major quantum-computing problem
  • Unite.AI: PSI reports machine-checked proof of Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture
  • Silicon Report: Physical Superintelligence says AI proved Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture
  • GitHub: physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis (Lean 4 formalization)
Community Note

Lean confirms the proof is internally correct. Experts still need to confirm that the formal statement matches the original conjecture, and the readable paper hasn’t been peer reviewed yet. The Stanford praise comes from an assessment PSI published, not a separate paper. PSI researcher David Turturean ran the system and takes responsibility for the paper’s correctness.

Sources: PSI; Business Insider; Unite.AI; Silicon Report

FA drone drops bananas to chimps stranded by Florida floodwater, and Kaleb, 38, digs right in

Seen on: Facebook — Save the Chimps’ own page (credited by Hindustan Times), picked up by AP and the New York Post.

About 19 inches of rain left much of the 150-acre Save the Chimps sanctuary in Fort Pierce, Fla., underwater. With an overflowing canal blocking access, caregivers flew a drone to drop a paper bag of bananas and blankets to chimps on their islands. Footage shared by the sanctuary shows 38-year-old Kaleb pulling a banana from the bag (AP, New York Post, Hindustan Times).

The sanctuary says all of its 200-plus chimps, rescued from labs, entertainment and the pet trade, are safe on high ground. Facilities director Mark Yohannan told WPTV it’s the worst flooding in 25 years, but “they haven’t missed a beat” on food, medicine or enrichment. About 15 golf carts have broken down, and the sanctuary is asking to borrow tall four-wheel-drive vehicles (Hindustan Times, WPTV via Hindustan Times, AP).

Why it matters: It’s a cute clip, and it also shows animal caregivers improvising to keep 200 chimps fed through a disaster.
