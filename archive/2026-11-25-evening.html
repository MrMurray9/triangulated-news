Alberta

A Law Society appeal panel clears former justice minister Kaycee Madu over his call to Edmonton’s police chief after a traffic ticket

In 2021, then-justice minister Kaycee Madu got a $300 distracted-driving ticket in Edmonton and immediately phoned police chief Dale McFee. A Law Society of Alberta hearing committee later found him guilty of conduct deserving of sanction, reprimanded him and ordered him to pay nearly $39,000 in costs. A seven-member appeal panel set all of that aside in a decision released Tuesday (Calgary Herald, Global News).

“The ticket was undoubtedly mentioned during the call,” the panel wrote, but nothing in McFee’s account showed “that Mr. Madu used his position during the call to seek any benefit, dispensation or intervention in respect of the ticket.” It also ruled the original committee erred in law by admitting retired judge Adèle Kent’s 2022 report, calling it “untested opinion, from an extra-statutory political process” (Calgary Herald, BetaVoices).

Madu has said he called to raise concerns about racial profiling. In a statement he said he felt “a profound sense of relief and vindication,” but that the damage to his reputation and family “cannot simply be undone” (Calgary Herald).

Why it matters: Kent’s 2022 report, which found Madu had tried to interfere with the administration of justice, led to his removal as justice minister. The Law Society, which licenses Alberta’s lawyers, has now ruled it can’t be used as evidence, and that its own case wasn’t proven.

The panel didn’t say the call was a good idea. It agreed there were “other, preferable ways” Madu could have raised his concerns, but said the question was only whether the Law Society had proven the conduct alleged in its citation.

This ruling is about Madu’s licence as a lawyer. It doesn’t change the separate 2022 government-ordered report, and Madu is no longer an MLA.

Too warm to make snow: Snow Valley pushes back its opening and hopes to welcome skiers in early December

Edmonton’s Snow Valley ski hill usually opens in mid-November, but it doesn’t have enough snow yet and hasn’t set a new date. The hill relies on man-made snow, and its snowmaking equipment needs a long stretch of cold weather (Taproot Edmonton, CTV News Edmonton).

Assistant general manager Paul Robinson said the hill is hoping for early December. Temperatures are expected to stay above freezing for much of this week, and what little snow falls isn’t likely to stick (Taproot Edmonton). The hill’s own conditions page still lists it as closed.

Why it matters: Snow Valley is the in-city ski hill where many Edmonton kids learn to ski, so every week of delay is a week cut from a short season.

Sources (3)

Natural snow isn’t the main issue. Even a small hill needs several cold nights in a row to build a base with snow guns, and Edmonton’s forecast highs stay at or above freezing through Friday.

With no embassy in Kabul, Canada names Qatar its “protecting power” to help Canadians in trouble in Afghanistan

Canada and Qatar signed an investment protection agreement in Ottawa on Wednesday, and also agreed that Qatar will act for Canada on consular cases in Afghanistan. Canada closed its Kabul embassy when the Taliban took power in August 2021, and it lists the Taliban as a terrorist organization (The Canadian Press, Global Affairs Canada).

A protecting power offers consular help where a country has no mission of its own. Canada already relies on Italy in Iran and Sweden in North Korea. Former deputy ambassador Ben Rowswell said a Qatari diplomat might now be able to visit a detained Canadian, help aid workers in trouble with local authorities, or assist with former Afghan interpreters Ottawa is trying to resettle (The Canadian Press).

“In the absence of being on the ground, you need someone else to help protect your interests,” said Arif Lalani, a former Canadian ambassador to Afghanistan. Global Affairs didn’t immediately say when the arrangement takes effect or what it will cost (The Canadian Press).

Why it matters: Canada still advises against all travel to Afghanistan, but some Canadians live and work there. Until now, if one was arrested there was no diplomat who could visit them.

Sources (4)

Community Note

This doesn’t reopen Canada’s embassy or recognize the Taliban government. Qatar acts as a go-between, and countries usually pay each other back for this kind of service.

Qatar is an unusual choice. Freedom House rates it as restricting civil liberties, and Canada’s other protecting-power deals are mostly with democracies. Lalani said it still makes sense because Qatar has an embassy in Kabul and a working relationship with the Taliban that Canada doesn’t.

Colombian special forces storm a Cartagena supermarket after a 17-hour siege and free all the hostages

Masked gunmen took over an Éxito supermarket in Cartagena around 8 a.m. Tuesday, tying up customers and staff. Police later put the number of hostages at about 70, and AFP reported 76. Ten were let go during the day. Special forces broke in at about 1:30 a.m. Wednesday, set off stun grenades and freed everyone left inside (The Guardian, AFP, AP).

Four suspects were arrested after some swapped their black clothes for street clothes and tried to blend in with the hostages. President Abelardo de la Espriella said the group had tried to rob an armoured cash truck and fled into the store when security guards spotted them (The Guardian, AP).

One man was killed in the raid. Police described him as a hostage-taker who resisted. Local media identified him as 51-year-old Haider Muñetón Zapata, whose wife had told radio he went in to buy buñuelos, and reports say he had no criminal record (The Guardian, El Tiempo).

Why it matters: Cartagena is Colombia’s top tourist city. The siege came the same week as a car bomb in Villavicencio, as the new president’s crackdown on armed groups meets violent pushback.

Why it matters: Under Orbán, Hungary was the EU member most often blocking help for Ukraine. Hungary has since cleared the way for Ukraine’s EU membership bid, and this trip turns that thaw into practical deals.

Expedia will acquire Czech flight-booking site Kiwi.com, adding a fourth travel company to its recent shopping list

Expedia Group announced Wednesday it will buy Kiwi.com, a Brno-based online travel agency known for stitching flights from airlines that don’t partner into one itinerary. Neither company disclosed the price. The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027 (Expedia Group, Skift, PhocusWire).

Kiwi had €227 million in revenue in 2025, down about 20% from its 2023 peak, though its operating profit rose after deep cost cuts. Majority owner General Atlantic, which invested in 2019, says it is selling its entire stake (Skift, General Atlantic).

CEO Ariane Gorin said Kiwi’s technology will strengthen Brand Expedia and Expedia’s business-to-business arm “in air.” Skift counts Kiwi as Expedia’s fourth recent purchase, after Tiqets, CarTrawler and Layla (Skift).

Why it matters: Kiwi’s mix-and-match fares are often the cheapest way to fly across Europe. Under Expedia, that technology could appear on Expedia’s own sites and the many airline and bank travel portals it powers.

Dubai Islamic Bank agrees to buy rival Emirates Islamic in an all-share deal, creating the world’s second-largest listed Islamic bank

Dubai Islamic Bank (DIB) will buy all of Emirates Islamic from its parent, Emirates NBD, paying entirely in new DIB shares. The combined bank would hold about AED 585 billion (roughly US$159 billion) in assets, based on Sept. 30 figures (Dubai Islamic Bank, Reuters, The National).

Emirates NBD, Dubai’s biggest bank, will become a major strategic shareholder in DIB. The banks say the merger makes DIB the UAE’s largest Islamic banking group and the world’s second-largest listed one by assets. It still needs shareholder approval and sign-off from regulators including the UAE Central Bank (Gulf News, WAM).

Why it matters: Islamic banks follow Sharia rules that bar charging interest. Combining Dubai’s two biggest Islamic lenders creates a much larger player in a fast-growing part of global finance.

Science corner: Caltech’s “BrainBots” use a flash of infrared light to push cancer drugs across the blood–brain barrier

The blood–brain barrier keeps most medicines out of the brain, which makes brain tumours very hard to treat. Wei Gao’s lab at Caltech built nanorobots about 250 nanometres across, shaped like lopsided balloons. Each one carries a drug and a pocket of gas-making fuel sealed behind a heat-sensitive gate (Caltech, Science Robotics).

Doctors inject them into the blood, then shine near-infrared light through the skull. The light melts the gate, the fuel releases gas, and the bots get a push toward the cells lining the barrier. Little DNA “hooks” called aptamers grab those cells, which pull the bots inside, and the bots break down to release their medicine (Caltech).

In mice with glioblastoma, light-activated BrainBots got about five times more of the chemotherapy drug doxorubicin into the brain than unactivated ones. Tumours grew more slowly, the mice lived longer, and the barrier wasn’t damaged (Caltech, Medscape, Chosun Biz).

Why it matters: Nearly every brain disease, from cancer to Parkinson’s, is harder to treat because drugs can’t get in. A light switch that lets drugs through only where it’s aimed could make lower, safer doses work.

Sources (4)
  • Caltech: Tiny robots deliver treatments to the brain
  • Science Robotics: Photothermally gated nanorobots for active blood–brain barrier transcytosis and brain delivery
  • Medscape: ‘BrainBots’ could help deliver chemotherapy to the brain
  • Chosun Biz: Korean-U.S. team uses nanorobots to treat brain tumours in mice
Community Note

This was tested only in mice. Gao says the team still has to work on delivering the light and on long-term safety before any human study.

Only some of the bots reach the brain. The rest are expected to break down in the liver and spleen, as most nanoparticle drug carriers do.

Sources: Caltech; Science Robotics; Medscape

Tidal will delete music made entirely by AI, after finding 43% of recent uploads were fully machine-made

Starting Dec. 9, the streaming service Tidal will remove every track it determines is 100% AI-generated and reject new ones. Songs made with help from AI tools are still allowed (Tidal, The Guardian, TechCrunch).

Tidal says 43% of tracks delivered to it in the past 90 days were fully AI-made, and on Nov. 6 alone the figure hit 60%. About 27 million songs, 6.3% of its catalogue, are tagged as fully AI, and 96% of them got zero streams in the last three months. It has also taken down 65,000 tracks flagged as impersonating real artists (The Guardian).

Tidal said fully AI music “diverts streams and royalties away from the artists, and confuses fans” (The Guardian, Rolling Stone).

Why it matters: It’s the strictest stance yet from a big streaming service. Spotify and Apple Music label AI tracks but still carry them, so other services will be watched to see if they follow.

Sources (4)

  • The Guardian: Tidal bans 100% AI-generated music with plans to delete more than 27m tracks
  • TechCrunch: Tidal to ban and remove millions of AI-generated tracks
  • Rolling Stone: Tidal bans music made entirely with artificial intelligence
Community Note

The figures come from Tidal. It hasn’t explained in detail how it decides a song is 100% AI, though the Guardian reports metadata showing a track was made only with AI tools is one trigger.

Bandcamp banned music made “wholly or in substantial part” by AI in January. Spotify said in October it had deleted 75 million “spammy” tracks, without banning AI music outright.

Pinterest lets parents turn down how much AI-generated content shows up in their teen’s feed

Pinterest has added a setting to its parental controls that lets parents set how much AI-generated content appears in a teen’s recommendations. TechCrunch says that makes it one of the first big social platforms to offer such a control (Pinterest, TechCrunch, The Verge).

The update also lets parents turn off video autoplay and adjust push, email and in-app notifications. A parental passcode locks the settings, so teens need permission to loosen them (Pinterest, TechRepublic).

Why it matters: AI images and videos are filling social feeds, and parents have had few ways to filter them. Most AI controls for teens so far block chatbots, not AI-made posts in the feed.
