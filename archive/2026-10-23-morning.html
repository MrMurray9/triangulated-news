Alberta

Grande Prairie now has its own police force: the city’s new service took over from the RCMP this week, two years ahead of schedule

The Grande Prairie Police Service (GPPS) took over responsibility for policing the city on Oct. 21, replacing the RCMP, which had policed Grande Prairie for decades. The handover came two years earlier than first planned, the service and the RCMP said. Chief Dwayne Lakusta’s officers now answer all police calls in the city, with dispatch handled through the city’s Public Safety Communications Centre (Grande Prairie Police Service, RCMP Alberta, CTV News).

Nothing changes for residents dialing for help: 911 still works for emergencies and the non-emergency line stays 780-830-5700. The RCMP keeps policing the surrounding County of Grande Prairie from its Clairmont detachment and still has an office in the city. GPPS has worked alongside the RCMP since 2024 while hiring and training officers, and it pairs sworn police with the city’s enforcement services and a mobile outreach team.

Why it matters: Grande Prairie is the province’s biggest test yet of replacing the RCMP with a city-run force — something other Alberta municipalities, and the province itself, have been weighing.

Sources (3)
  • Grande Prairie Police Service — GPPS assumes responsibility for policing
  • RCMP Alberta — Transition of policing in Grande Prairie
  • CTV News Edmonton — Municipal police force takes over RCMP role in Grande Prairie
Community Note

This affects only the City of Grande Prairie, not the surrounding county. Cost and staffing figures have been reported differently in different places; the two agencies’ own statements confirm the date, the call numbers and who polices where, but not a final price tag.

Sources: Grande Prairie Police Service; RCMP Alberta; CTV News Edmonton.

Smith to separatists in her own party: accept the referendum or join “a different party” — and one riding president says he isn’t leaving

In a video to party members on Wednesday, Premier Danielle Smith said the United Conservative Party “is not an independence party” and invited members who want to keep pushing separation “to join a different party.” Anyone running for the UCP or holding an official party role must back staying in Canada, she said (The Canadian Press via Winnipeg Free Press, The Globe and Mail, Edmonton Journal).

Mitch Sylvestre, president of the Bonnyville-Cold Lake-St. Paul constituency association and a leading separation activist, said he will keep talking about leaving Canada: “She does not run the party; she runs the government.” He added that separatists won’t leave to start a new party, “not at this stage.” Asked whether she would expel holdouts, Smith said she’d wait to see if it becomes necessary. She has also swapped her “sovereign Alberta” slogan for “stronger Alberta within a united Canada.”

Why it matters: With the next election set for Oct. 18, 2027, Smith is betting that drawing a line on separation wins more votes in Calgary and Edmonton than it costs her in the rural base — and a split on the right could help the NDP.

  • The Globe and Mail — Smith tells separatists in UCP to accept result
  • Edmonton Journal — Smith on separatism views in the UCP
Community Note

Smith did not say any member would be expelled, and the UCP’s annual general meeting next month — where some separatists want a leadership review — is the next real test. The referendum’s remain-side share of about 75 per cent is the provincial total; the split between rural and urban ridings was much closer in some places.

Sources: The Canadian Press; The Globe and Mail; Edmonton Journal.

Canada Post opens a postal-code lookup for its switch to community mailboxes — and says it is still losing money even as parcels grow

At its annual public meeting on Thursday, Canada Post launched an online tool that lets people enter a postal code to see when their door-to-door delivery will switch to a community mailbox. About four million addresses are to convert over roughly five years, about 700,000 of them by the end of 2027; the first conversions are in Winnipeg and Sept-Îles, Que. (iPolitics, BNN Bloomberg, Canada Post).

The Crown corporation reported a pre-tax loss of $482 million for the first half of 2026, though its second-quarter loss narrowed to $277 million from $407 million a year earlier and parcel revenue rose 20.7 per cent. Weekend parcel delivery has started in Ottawa and Montreal, with Toronto due in late November. Outgoing CEO Doug Ettinger, who steps down in March, said the urban post-office network is “overbuilt” (The Globe and Mail/The Canadian Press, BNN Bloomberg).

Why it matters: If you get mail at your door, this tells you when that ends. It is also the clearest sign yet of how Ottawa’s overhaul of a money-losing service will reach households.

Sources (3)
  • iPolitics — Canada Post begins transition to community mailboxes
  • BNN Bloomberg — Canada Post CEO touts hints of recovery
  • The Globe and Mail (The Canadian Press) — Canada Post sees signs of financial recovery
Community Note

The loss and revenue figures are Canada Post’s own unaudited quarterly numbers. Conversion dates from the lookup tool are plans and can change, so check your own postal code.

Sources: iPolitics; BNN Bloomberg; The Globe and Mail/The Canadian Press.

Singapore’s Boroo agrees to buy Yukon’s collapsed Eagle Gold mine for up to $140 million — and Yukon would get its cleanup money back first

The court-appointed receiver for Victoria Gold has agreed to sell the Eagle Gold mine near Mayo, Yukon, to Boroo Pte. Ltd. of Singapore for up to $140 million: a $20-million deposit after court approval, $20 million at closing, $50 million once restart permits are issued and $50 million at commercial production. The receiver plans to seek approval from an Ontario court in early December, aiming to close around Dec. 31 (Yukon government, Boroo, PwC as receiver).

The Yukon government has advanced $185 million to the receiver for remediation since the mine’s heap-leach pad failed in June 2024, and it is first in line to be repaid from the sale. Boroo says it plans about $800 million from 2027 to 2029 to stabilize the failed pad, treat cyanide-affected water and restart the mine, plus more than $600 million later — over $1.5 billion in all. It would also post $104 million in new reclamation security.

Why it matters: It decides who pays to fix one of the worst mining failures in recent Canadian history — and whether Yukon taxpayers get back the money they have already spent.

Sources (3)
  • Government of Yukon — Agreement reached for sale of Eagle Gold Mine
  • Boroo — Agreement to acquire Victoria Gold Corp. (release)
  • PwC (receiver) — Victoria Gold sale process
Community Note

Only $40 million is certain; the other $100 million depends on permits and production, and if permits don’t come within four years the receiver may owe Boroo an exit payment of up to $55 million. The $1.5-billion figure is Boroo’s plan, not a binding commitment. Talks with the First Nation of Na-Cho Nyäk Dun, on whose territory the mine sits, are still ongoing, Boroo says.

Sources: Government of Yukon; Boroo; PwC.

Europe’s top human-rights prize goes to the International Criminal Court — days after new U.S. sanctions on its judges

European Parliament President Roberta Metsola announced on Thursday that the 2026 Sakharov Prize for Freedom of Thought goes to the International Criminal Court and its officials. The award came days after the Trump administration imposed fresh sanctions on the court and its judges and prosecutors (European Parliament, AP, Euronews).

Lawmakers framed the choice as a defence of international justice under pressure; the court, in a statement, said it was facing “unprecedented challenges” and thanked Parliament. The prize, named after Soviet dissident Andrei Sakharov, comes with €50,000 and will be handed over at a ceremony in Strasbourg on Dec. 16.

Why it matters: It is a pointed show of European support for a court that Washington is trying to squeeze. Canada is a founding member of the ICC.

Italian truckers’ fuel-price blockades empty supermarket shelves in the south and leave two airports without jet fuel

Truck drivers protesting high diesel prices blocked roads across southern Italy this week, leaving gaps on supermarket shelves and cutting fuel deliveries; airports in Crotone and Reggio Calabria ran out of jet fuel. Retailers warned of food shortages, and Industry Minister Adolfo Urso warned blockaders they could face criminal charges. Police said they had identified 1,339 people, reported 71 to prosecutors and issued 242 fines (Euronews, Reuters via Internazionale, ANSA).

The government has pointed to more than €1 billion in support for the sector, including about €480 million to offset fuel costs, and talks with trucking groups are set for Oct. 27. By Friday morning the last roadblocks in Campania had been cleared, ANSA reported, though supply chains are expected to take several days to recover.

Why it matters: Fuel costs are hitting truckers across Europe; Italy shows how quickly a road protest turns into empty shelves — the same pressure point Canada saw with the 2022 convoys.

Western Digital’s sales jump 55% on AI data-centre demand, but shares slip as memory costs and spending climb

Hard-drive maker Western Digital reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of US$4.38 billion on Thursday, up 55 per cent from a year earlier, with adjusted earnings of $4.64 a share and a 58.1 per cent gross margin. It forecast about $5.1 billion in revenue and roughly $5.80 a share for the current quarter and raised its dividend (Western Digital, Bloomberg, Investing.com).

Shares still fell in after-hours trading. Analysts pointed to rising costs for the DRAM and NAND memory that goes into its drives and to capital spending running slightly above the company’s target of 4 to 6 per cent of revenue (Investing.com, Bloomberg).

Why it matters: Cloud companies are buying huge amounts of storage to hold AI data — but even strong results don’t guarantee a rising stock when costs climb too.

Calgary’s Strathcona Resources wants to ship 250,000 barrels a day of oil by rail to Prince Rupert — which would need an exemption from the B.C. tanker ban

Strathcona Resources, the Calgary oil producer chaired by Adam Waterous, is pitching a plan to carry up to 250,000 barrels a day of crude on CN’s existing rail line to a new export terminal at Prince Rupert, B.C., aimed at Asian buyers. It says the project could be running within two years (Calgary Herald, The Logic, World Oil).

The catch is the 2019 Oil Tanker Moratorium Act, which bans large oil tankers from loading on B.C.’s north coast. Strathcona hasn’t filed a formal application and plans to approach Ottawa as early as next week to ask for an exemption. The B.C. government and coastal First Nations have opposed loosening the ban.

Why it matters: It is a rail-based test of whether Ottawa will reopen the northern B.C. coast to oil exports — a big question for Alberta producers looking to sell beyond the U.S.

Sources (3)

  • The Logic — Adam Waterous on crude exports to Asia
  • World Oil — Strathcona pitches 250,000-bpd Prince Rupert crude terminal
Community Note

This is a proposal, not an approved project. The timeline and capacity are the company’s own claims, and the tanker law would have to change or be waived first.

Sources: Calgary Herald; The Logic; World Oil.

Science corner

A personalized mRNA cancer vaccine cut melanoma’s return by 42% in a large phase 3 trial

Merck and Moderna’s intismeran autogene is built for each patient: doctors sequence the tumour, and an mRNA shot is made to teach the immune system to spot up to 34 of that cancer’s unique mutations. In the phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial of 1,137 people whose high-risk melanoma had been surgically removed, adding it to the immunotherapy drug Keytruda cut the risk of the cancer returning or death by 42 per cent compared with Keytruda alone (AP, CancerNetwork, AJMC).

After two years, 82.2 per cent of patients on the combination were alive without recurrence, compared with 70.0 per cent on Keytruda alone, and the risk of cancer spreading to distant organs fell 38 per cent. Serious side-effects were similar in both groups (14.2 vs. 13.4 per cent). Full results are being presented Saturday at the ESMO cancer congress, and the study has been accepted by the New England Journal of Medicine.

Why it matters: It would be the first personalized mRNA cancer vaccine to succeed in a phase 3 trial — the same technology behind COVID shots, aimed instead at each person’s own tumour.

Sources (3)
  • AP — Moderna cancer vaccine cuts melanoma recurrence
  • CancerNetwork — Intismeran plus pembrolizumab reduces recurrence risk
  • AJMC — Merck/Moderna cancer vaccine cuts melanoma recurrence risk by 42%
Community Note

The vaccine is still investigational and not approved in Canada or anywhere else. The trial was funded by the two companies, and it is too early to know whether it helps people live longer overall. It is a treatment after surgery for people who already have melanoma, not a vaccine to prevent cancer.

Sources: AP; CancerNetwork; AJMC.

AI news and advances

Hugging Face releases Carbon-X, a free, open AI that lets researchers “chat with” DNA

Hugging Face has released Carbon-X, an open AI model that reads both DNA sequences and plain English, so researchers can paste in a stretch of genetic code and ask questions about it. The 14.8-billion-parameter model is built on Alibaba’s Qwen3-14B, was trained on about 302 billion tokens, and reads DNA in six-letter chunks (Wired, Business Insider, The Next Web).

It is released under the permissive Apache 2.0 licence and is small enough to run on a single 80-gigabyte GPU, so university labs can use it without big cloud bills. Hugging Face says it averaged 66.7 per cent across five DNA benchmarks — tests the company chose itself.

Why it matters: Most powerful biology AI models are closed and costly; a free one lowers the barrier for researchers working on things like gene therapies.

Sources (3)
  • Wired — Hugging Face’s new AI biology model lets you chat with DNA
  • Business Insider — Hugging Face’s Carbon-X model for DNA sequences
  • The Next Web — Hugging Face Carbon-X DNA AI model
Community Note

Carbon-X is a research tool, not a medical one: it has not been clinically validated and shouldn’t be used to interpret anyone’s personal genetic test. Its benchmark scores come from Hugging Face and haven’t been independently reproduced yet.

Sources: Wired; Business Insider; The Next Web.

OA TV reporter covering floods north of Brisbane ends up diving in to pull a driver from his sinking car — on camera

Seen on: Other — video shot by the ABC’s own camera crew and shared online, as reported by ABC Australia, AP, Euronews and Mediaweek

ABC Australia reporter Will Murray was covering flash flooding on Buchanan Road in Morayfield, north of Brisbane, when a four-wheel drive was swept off the road and began to sink. Murray and bystander Daniel Hainey jumped into the water and pulled the driver out through a rear door while camera operator Liz Pickering kept filming (ABC Australia, AP, Euronews).

“Initially I could see him, but he disappeared from my sight within seconds so I made the call to jump in,” Murray said. Afterwards, “all three of us gave each other a big hug.” The footage made headlines around the world (ABC Australia, Mediaweek).

Why it matters: Everyone got out — and the clip is a vivid reminder of why emergency crews say never drive into floodwater.
