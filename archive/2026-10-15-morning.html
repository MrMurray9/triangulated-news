Alberta

AMA survey: 55% of responding Alberta doctors say they would leave if the province separates

The Alberta Medical Association’s Pulse survey of 1,649 practising physicians found that 55 per cent would move to another province or territory and stop practising in Alberta if the province separated from Canada. Another 8 per cent said they would retire or leave medicine earlier than planned; 15 per cent said they would stay and keep practising; 18 per cent were uncertain.

AMA president Dr. Heidi Fell stressed the association is not telling Albertans how to vote. In an Oct. 13 members’ letter she framed the results as stated intentions — not a forecast — and asked how Alberta would replace about $7 billion in Canada Health Transfer funding and how coverage rules tied to other ballot questions would work when minutes matter at the bedside. A health-ministry spokesperson said the government is reviewing the AMA’s letter and would not speculate before Albertans vote.

Why it matters: Access to care already tops kitchen-table worries — and this is the clearest public read yet on how many physicians say they would walk if separation moved from theory to process.

What changed since the last edition: Evening covered day-one advance turnout and Smith’s remain vote. New this morning: the AMA Pulse results and Fell’s health-system questions, reported by Postmedia, CP and the Globe.

Science corner

Science corner: Cornell team reports a silicon-doped oxide semiconductor with a bandgap above 7 eV — and builds a working transistor

Researchers led from Cornell University report in Nature that silicon-doped α-(AlₓGa₁₋ₓ)₂O₃ films grown by suboxide molecular-beam epitaxy can reach bandgaps exceeding 7.0 eV — into a range usually treated as insulating — while remaining conductive enough for devices. They demonstrate a Schottky diode and a field-effect transistor they call the AlphaFET on abundant sapphire substrates.

A companion Nature News & Views piece frames the work as pushing semiconductors into “insulator territory,” with conductivity in the colossal-bandgap regime reported orders of magnitude above prior films. Wider bandgaps matter for high-power, high-efficiency electronics; doing it on sapphire aims at lower cost and larger wafers than diamond or cubic boron nitride.

Why it matters: If ultrawide-bandgap parts get cheaper, electric cars, grids and data centres waste less power as heat — a materials story with real electricity bills attached.

Sources (3)
  • Nature — A 7-eV bandgap semiconductor based on silicon-doped α-(AlxGa1−x)2O3 (7 Oct. 2026)
  • Nature News & Views — Semiconductors pushed into insulator territory
  • Cornell University — research affiliation / materials science
Community Note

This is a peer-reviewed materials advance, not a commercial product on store shelves. Device metrics in the paper (on/off ratios, thresholds) are lab demonstrations; manufacturing scale-up is the next hard problem. Bandgap figures depend on composition x and measurement method.

Sources: Nature; Nature News & Views; Cornell.
