Environment Canada flags the season’s first accumulating snow for Alberta this Thanksgiving weekend, possibly including the Edmonton–Calgary corridor

Environment and Climate Change Canada issued a special weather statement at 4:43 a.m. MDT Thursday covering the City of Edmonton, St. Albert and Sherwood Park, among other regions. It says rain will spread across northern Alberta today, change to snow on Friday in west-central regions and over much of the province by Saturday, then taper off from north to south on Sunday.

Many areas are expected to see accumulating snow on Saturday, “possibly including the Calgary-Edmonton corridor.” The heaviest amounts are expected in the west, including Grande Cache, Hinton and Jasper National Park, where totals could exceed 20 cm; amounts elsewhere remain uncertain. Edmonton’s forecast calls for 5 to 10 mm of rain tonight, rain Friday with temperatures steady near 7°C, rain or snow Saturday with a high of 2°C, then a sunny Thanksgiving Monday with a high of 8°C.

Why it matters: It’s a long weekend with heavy highway travel; the agency warns of “quickly changing and deteriorating travel conditions,” especially on routes west toward Hinton and Jasper.

Edmonton Public Schools trustees order a review of enrolment policies that can keep children without immigration status out of class

Toronto-born poet Anne Carson wins the 2026 Nobel Prize in Literature

The Swedish Academy awarded the prize Thursday to Anne Carson, 76, “for her bold and inventive oeuvre that, in playful dialogue with the classical tradition, has created new forms for contemporary literature.” The prize is worth 12 million Swedish kronor, about $1.7 million. Permanent secretary Ingrid Carlberg said Carson took the call in Iceland, where she was “listening to the wind.”

A poet, essayist, translator and classics scholar, Carson is known for books that mix poetry, essay and fiction, including Autobiography of Red, Nox and Eros the Bittersweet, and for translations of Sappho, Sophocles and Euripides. She earned three degrees at the University of Toronto, whose president, Melanie Woodin, called it “a very proud moment.” The Globe and Mail counts her as the third Canadian to win the literature Nobel.

Why it matters: It’s the first literature Nobel for a Canadian since Alice Munro in 2013, and a spotlight on Canadian writing heading into Thanksgiving weekend.

Margaret Hamilton, who led the software team that helped land Apollo 11 on the moon, has died at 90

MIT said Margaret Hamilton died Sept. 30. Hired in 1965 as the first programmer on the Apollo project at MIT’s Instrumentation Lab, she went on to lead the team writing the on-board flight software for the crewed missions; by 1968 more than 400 people were working on Apollo’s software.

During Apollo 11’s descent in 1969, computer alarms warned that the guidance computer was overloaded. Because the software was designed to drop lower-priority tasks and keep the most important ones running, the landing could continue rather than be aborted, CBS Boston and MIT report. NASA credits Hamilton with popularizing the concept of “software engineering,” and she received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016.

Why it matters: The error-handling ideas her team built for Apollo underpin the safety-critical software used today in aircraft, medical devices and spacecraft.

Community Note

Apollo’s software was a team effort involving hundreds of people at MIT and NASA; Hamilton led the on-board flight software group. MIT says a memorial service is planned for the spring in Cambridge, Mass.

That “messy” new North American time-zone map is accurate: Alberta won’t fall back on Nov. 1

A map showing how time zones will look after Nov. 1 has been shared widely, with many calling it chaotic. It’s accurate for Alberta: the province has adopted permanent Alberta Time (ABT), the former Mountain Daylight Time, at UTC−6, so clocks will not go back an hour this fall.

That means that this winter Alberta will be an hour ahead of Montana and two hours ahead of California. A University of Calgary psychology professor told CityNews that later sunrises will make an 8 a.m. class “feel like a 7:00 a.m. course.”

Verdict: Confirmed for Alberta, per the Alberta government and timeanddate.com.

Why it matters: Calendars, flight times and cross-border calls with Montana, B.C. and the U.S. will shift relative to Alberta this winter.

Science corner

Peking University team cools chips at a record 1,570 watts per square centimetre with a non-conductive liquid

Researchers at Peking University reported in Nature Electronics on Wednesday a “horizontal-up” microchannel cooler etched into the chip itself that boils a dielectric (electrically non-conductive) fluid, HFE-7100, to carry heat away. It reached a critical heat flux of up to 1,570 W/cm², compared with under 1,000 W/cm² for earlier dielectric-coolant designs, even with pre-cooling and pressurization.

The trick is the plumbing: horizontal inlets keep fresh liquid flowing to hot spots while vertical outlets vent vapour quickly, delaying the “dry-out” that limits boiling coolers. Water-based embedded coolers can handle about 3,000 W/cm², but water conducts electricity, so a leak can short a chip. The device is made with standard MEMS techniques, so it could be built into existing semiconductor processes.

Why it matters: Heat is a key limit on AI chips and data centres; safer, more effective cooling could let chips run harder while using less energy for cooling.

Sources (3)
  • Nature Electronics — research paper
  • Scienmag
  • We Are The Frontier
Community Note

This is a lab demonstration. The paper does not claim a commercial product or timeline, and reliability, cost and performance at production scale still have to be shown.

Sources: Nature Electronics; Scienmag.

Common Sense Media rates ChatGPT for Teens an “unacceptable risk,” saying parent alerts often failed; OpenAI disputes the testing

Common Sense Media’s Youth AI Safety Institute said Wednesday it tested more than 4,000 prompts on accounts registered to 13- to 17-year-olds and found some protections worked, such as refusing sexual role-play, but alerts meant to notify parents about suicide, self-harm or eating-disorder risks often didn’t trigger. “A teen can spend an hour talking about self-harm without their parent getting a single alert,” executive director Tom Siegel said. The group urged OpenAI to restrict ChatGPT to adults until the gaps are fixed.

OpenAI told AP it is “deeply committed to teen safety” and said “the bulk of their testing may have begun and concluded before activation of parental controls was complete, making their findings inaccurate.”

Why it matters: Many Alberta students use ChatGPT; parents relying on its teen settings should know they may not work as advertised and keep talking with their kids about it.
