Alberta

Alberta offers up to $5 million for Highway 2A intersections in a Foothills County road swap

Transportation Minister Devin Dreeshen said negotiations are underway for Foothills County to take over about 10.7 km of Highway 2A between the High River border and Aldersyde, while the province takes 498th Avenue to extend Highway 543. The county would get up to $5 million for rebuilding two busy intersections that see about 6,000–8,000 vehicles a day (Fort Macleod Gazette / AlbertaChat, Foothills County notice, Penticton Herald reprint).

Dreeshen told the legislature a deal is in place for the intersections: engineering this winter, construction tenders in early 2027. Livingstone-Macleod MLA Chelsae Petrovic said residents want clarity on the safety upgrades. The county is also surveying a special infrastructure levy of about $89,940 per hectare in the Highway 2A industrial area structure plan, with an open house Nov. 10 (Fort Macleod Gazette, Foothills County).

Why it matters: A growing industrial corridor south of Calgary gets clearer local traffic control — and a price tag for who pays for the roads.

Primary Care Alberta launches a menopause “HER Strategy” with Calgary partners, aiming for February release

Primary Care Alberta, the Calgary Health Foundation and Calgary-area primary-care networks kicked off the HER (Health. Empowerment. Resilience.) Strategy at a summit this week to improve perimenopause and menopause care. Calgary Health Foundation is putting about $120,000 toward the work. CEO Kimberley Simmonds said the plan will strengthen primary-care capacity, improve coordination and help women find reliable information without waiting months for specialty referrals (Primary Care Alberta, Calgary Herald, Medicine Hat News).

Simmonds pointed to a “25-year deficit” in menopause teaching after the early-2000s Women’s Health Initiative scare on hormone therapy, and to a 2022 Menopause Foundation of Canada survey that found most women had little awareness of common symptoms. The completed strategy is expected in February 2027 and is being reviewed with researchers and Indigenous community members for inclusivity (Calgary Herald, CTV News Calgary, Primary Care Alberta).

Why it matters: Alberta is trying to pull menopause care back into everyday family medicine and cut through social-media misinformation.

Canada

Ottawa lengthens low-wage TFW permits to two years and lets provinces opt into a 15% hiring cap

Employment and Social Development Canada said that as of Nov. 6, Labour Market Impact Assessments in the Temporary Foreign Worker Program’s low-wage stream can run up to two years instead of one. Early childhood educators join health care, construction and food processing as exceptions that can still be processed in census metro areas with unemployment of 6% or higher. Budget 2026 also earmarks $151.5 million over five years for service delivery (Government of Canada, The Globe and Mail).

Separately, provinces and territories that opt in can let eligible employers in communities they name staff up to 15% of their workforce with low-wage TFWs — up from today’s 10% — while priority sectors keep the 20% ceiling. Those regional flexibilities are slated for March 1, 2027, after talks with provincial ministers. Ottawa also points to $82 million over four years for the Migrant Worker Support Program (Canada.ca news release, Globe and Mail).

Why it matters: Employers get longer permits and a path to hire more temporary foreign workers where provinces agree, while high-unemployment cities stay mostly closed to new low-wage LMIAs.

Budget 2026 floats an “RRSP for life,” ending the forced RRIF conversion at 71

Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne’s budget proposes folding the RRSP and RRIF into a single “RRSP-for-life” account so Canadians would no longer have to convert savings by the end of the year they turn 71. Minimum withdrawals would still start then. Ottawa is also looking at letting a surviving spouse become a “successor holder” of an RRSP — as RRIFs already allow — and at simpler tax-deferred transfers to other qualifying survivors (The Globe and Mail, Financial Post, IG Wealth Management budget brief).

Planners say the change is mostly administrative and could take years of federal and provincial law tweaks; a public consultation runs to Jan. 8, 2027. Open questions include whether contributions after 71 would be allowed, how withholding tax would work, and whether pension-income splitting and the pension credit would apply to the new withdrawals (Globe and Mail, Financial Post).

Why it matters: Retirees and heirs could face less paperwork, but nothing changes until legislation and rules land — and that may be a Gen-X/millennial reform more than a boomer one.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard says it has lifted its self-imposed 2,000-km missile-range limit

IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebbi said Saturday that Iran has removed the roughly 2,000-kilometre (1,200-mile) ceiling it had publicly observed on ballistic-missile range, and that the expanded capability would be shown “in practice.” He did not name a new maximum range or a specific missile type (Associated Press, Tasnim News Agency, Iran International).

The statement lands amid heightened Middle East tension and separate IRGC remarks about readiness to adjust nuclear doctrine if ordered — wording that regional outlets flagged as signalling, not a confirmed doctrine change. Independent verification of any new flight tests was not available in Saturday morning coverage (AP, Iran International, Al Jazeera live blog).

Why it matters: Dropping the old range ceiling, even as a political signal, widens the map of cities and bases that neighbours and distant powers have to treat as potentially in reach.

SpaceX targets Nov. 13 to catch both Super Heavy and Starship with tower “chopsticks”

Elon Musk said Friday that SpaceX’s next Starship flight — widely tracked as Flight 15 around Nov. 13 — will attempt to catch both the Super Heavy booster and the Starship upper stage with the launch tower’s mechanical arms. “If both are successful, it will mark the first time that all parts of an orbital rocket return to Earth intact,” he wrote on X (Teslarati, Tesla North, India Today).

Earlier flights have caught boosters or soft-splashed stages, but never recovered the full stack intact after an orbital attempt. Musk has previously put the odds of a dual catch as better than even while stressing failure is still possible and that more flights are queued (The Verge archive on the dual-catch goal, Business Today, Teslarati).

Why it matters: A working dual catch is the core of SpaceX’s rapid-reuse plan for Starship — and a make-or-break demo for investors watching SPCX proxies.

Science corner

Science corner: MIT’s CheMeleon predicts how molecules behave when lab data are scarce

MIT chemical engineers describe CheMeleon, an open-source foundation model that first learns about a million PubChem molecules via roughly 1,600 classical descriptors, then fine-tunes on small task-specific sets to predict properties such as potency, toxicity and solubility. Across 58 benchmarks it beat other foundation models and classical descriptor baselines; nine of the top 20 entries in an OpenADMET contest used it (MIT News, ChemProp documentation, SciBeat).

The team — including Akshat Shirish Zalte, Jackson Burns and Prof. William Green, with BASF collaborators — built CheMeleon on ChemProp so labs can download weights and fine-tune locally. Green says the point is shrinking the number of molecules that must be synthesized and tested before a drug or materials screen gets useful (MIT News, ChemProp docs, SciBeat).

Why it matters: When only a handful of assay results exist for a new target, a general chemistry model can still rank which compounds are worth making next.

Sources (3)
  • MIT News: How to predict the behavior of molecules from scant data
  • ChemProp docs: CheMeleon foundation fine-tuning
  • SciBeat: MIT’s CheMeleon AI model
Community Note

Benchmark wins are not clinical proof. CheMeleon ranks candidates; wet-lab confirmation is still required before any drug or material claim.

Sources: MIT News; ChemProp; SciBeat
