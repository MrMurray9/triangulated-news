Edmonton asked to switch eight speed-on-green cameras back on; Alberta says yes to one

The provincial government has approved just one of the eight intersections where Edmonton wanted to restore speed-on-green photo enforcement, a north-end crossing where crashes have increased. Four requests were rejected and three are still under review (Taproot Edmonton, CTV News Edmonton, Global News).

Mayor Andrew Knack said one approval is better than none, but accused the province of not using the city’s collision data. Transportation Minister Devin Dreeshen said the rejected sites did not meet the province’s exemption criteria under its photo-radar crackdown (CTV News Edmonton, Taproot Edmonton).

Why it matters: Speed-on-green cameras catch drivers speeding through a green light at busy intersections. Where they go, and how many, is now decided in the legislature rather than at city hall.

Canada

Parks Canada scraps its 55-year-old search-and-rescue dog program in Banff, and thousands sign a petition to save it

Parks Canada has eliminated its only dog-handler position, in Banff National Park, ending a program that has found avalanche victims, lost hikers and poachers since the early 1970s. The decision came days after the handler finished training a new dog to replace Leroy, who retired in August, and amid about $145 million in agency-wide cuts and roughly 500 job losses (Rocky Mountain Outlook, CTV News Calgary, Calgary Herald).

Leroy alone tracked 156 people and found 57 lost in the wilderness in seven years. Yellowhead MP William Stevenson’s petition to restore the program drew about 5,500 signatures in two days. Parks Canada says search and rescue will continue through trained staff and outside partners (Rocky Mountain Outlook, CTV News Calgary).

Why it matters: Avalanche season is weeks away, and dogs can search snow and backcountry far faster than people, so critics say the savings are small and the risk is real.

Summit ends with an “Alliance for the Future”: Carney sets June 2027 to make Canada the EU’s first associate member

What changed: Thursday morning’s edition covered the summit opening and the EU dropping the “associate member” label from the draft. Since then the leaders launched a named alliance with more than 20 measures, and Carney put a June 2027 deadline on the membership talks.

“They killed us”: Novaya Gazeta, the Russian paper of Anna Politkovskaya and a Nobel-winning editor, will close Nov. 9

Novaya Gazeta, Russia’s best-known independent newspaper, said it will formally stop operating on Nov. 9, a day after authorities began evicting its last staff from their Moscow offices. “They killed us,” its editors wrote on Telegram, in a post that by law had to carry a “foreign agent” warning (RFE/RL, AP, NBC News).

Founded in the early 1990s with help from Mikhail Gorbachev, the paper lost Anna Politkovskaya and at least five other journalists and contributors to killings or suspicious deaths. Editor Dmitry Muratov shared the 2021 Nobel Peace Prize. UN human rights chief Volker Türk called the closure a “devastating blow.” A European edition run by former staff from Paris continues (RFE/RL, AP).

Why it matters: One of the last independent newsrooms inside Russia is going dark, leaving Russians with even fewer homegrown sources not controlled by the Kremlin.

Pope Leo mails in his ballot, the first pope known to vote in a U.S. general election

The Vatican confirmed that Pope Leo XIV, born Robert Prevost in Chicago, voted by mail in Illinois ahead of Tuesday’s U.S. midterm elections. It is the first known ballot cast by a pope in a U.S. general election (Vatican News, AP, USA Today).

Why it matters: It is a quiet example from the world’s most famous American abroad, days before a vote that decides control of Congress.

Intel stock crashes 29 per cent, its worst day since the 1970s, on a weak forecast and a deadly plant explosion

Intel shares fell about 29 per cent on Friday after the chipmaker forecast fourth-quarter revenue of US$15 billion to US$16 billion and adjusted earnings of 25 cents a share, well below the roughly US$17.1 billion and 43 cents analysts expected (CNBC, Yahoo Finance, NDTV Profit).

Intel blamed a PC market squeezed by soaring memory and component costs, plus an explosion earlier this month at a fab in Kiryat Gat that killed two workers and will cost about US$600 million in revenue over two quarters. The drop erased about US$12 billion from the value of the U.S. government’s 10 per cent stake, and pulled other chip stocks down as the AI sell-off deepened (CNBC, Calcalist, Yahoo Finance).

Why it matters: Intel is America’s only maker of advanced logic chips and a big bet for Washington, so a stumble this large ripples through tech funds and pension portfolios.

Canada’s economy stalls for a second straight month, and an early read says it shrank in September

Statistics Canada says real GDP was essentially unchanged in August, the second flat month in a row. Retail rebounded 1.2 per cent and rail jumped 3.6 per cent on a record wheat crop, but wholesale trade fell 1.4 per cent and oil and gas extraction slipped for a third month as some oil-sands mines underwent maintenance (Statistics Canada, BNN Bloomberg, Financial Post).

An advance estimate shows GDP falling 0.1 per cent in September. Taken together, the economy grew about 0.3 per cent in the third quarter, roughly 1.3 per cent annualized, far slower than the U.S. pace (Statistics Canada, Reuters via Investing.com, BNN Bloomberg).

Why it matters: A stalled economy makes the Bank of Canada’s fight with 3 per cent inflation harder, and Alberta’s oil-sands slowdown shows up directly in the numbers.

Science corner

Science corner: chemists build a carbon ribbon less than a nanometre wide that conducts like a metal

Researchers at the University of Science and Technology of China made the first precise nanoribbons built only from five- and seven-sided rings of carbon atoms, about 0.9 nanometres across. Theorists predicted in 1996 that such a carbon pattern could behave like a metal; nobody had been able to make it until now (Nature, Phys.org, USTC News).

The trick was to start with a stable molecule holding an eight-atom carbon ring on a silver surface, then heat it so each ring splits into two pentagons and neighbouring chains zip together into heptagons. Lifting a ribbon with a microscope tip and passing current through it showed one version is a true metal with near-ideal conductance, while a second is a semiconductor (Phys.org, Nature Research Briefing).

Why it matters: As chips shrink, copper wires heat up and lose efficiency. Carbon wires a few atoms wide could one day carry signals inside chips with far less waste.

Sources (4)
  • Nature — Narrow carbon pentaheptite nanoribbon as a covalent metal
  • Nature Research Briefing — Ribbon made of carbon pentagons and heptagons has metallic properties
  • Phys.org — Ultrathin carbon ribbons conduct electricity like metals in first successful synthesis
  • USTC News — New one-dimensional carbon-based covalent metal
Community Note

This is lab-scale work on a silver surface under a microscope. The authors say much more work is needed before the ribbons can be built into real devices.

Sources: Nature (peer-reviewed); Nature Research Briefing; Phys.org; USTC News.

Britain opens a competition probe into Facebook’s owner over terms that could let it feed publishers’ content to its AI

The U.K. Competition and Markets Authority has opened a formal investigation into whether Meta abused a dominant position with new commercial terms for publishers and advertisers. The terms could let it crawl businesses’ websites and use that content, including for AI products, without a meaningful way to opt out (CMA, Reuters via StreetInsider, Euronews).

Meta has paused the rollout while it consults the industry. The CMA says it is gathering evidence and may consider interim measures; U.K. magazine publishers welcomed the probe amid wider global pushback against the terms (Global Competition Review, PPA, Euronews).

Why it matters: The case is an early test of whether regulators will stop big platforms from taking news and other content to train or power AI without paying or asking.

Reddit will start requiring logins to shield its posts from AI scrapers, as AI search cuts its Google traffic

Reddit says it is moving to a mainly “logged-in experience,” with tests in some markets outside the U.S. this quarter and no date yet for everyone. It says the change protects its content from AI scraping, so it can license it to AI companies instead (TechCrunch, The Verge, Ars Technica).

Search referrals fell by more than a million a week on average in October as AI answers replace clicks, and U.S. daily users slipped to 50.7 million. Front pages and links shared from search will stay open, and an anonymous incognito mode is being rebuilt (TechCrunch, Axios, Reuters via Investing.com).

Why it matters: One of the web’s biggest open archives of human advice is closing its doors to anyone not signed in, a sign of how AI is reshaping the open internet.
