Alberta

Edmonton raises electricity franchise fee to 19.4% and maps four-year budget process

City council voted 8–5 on Tuesday to raise Edmonton’s electricity franchise fee to 19.4% of distribution charges — matching Calgary’s rate — aiming to ease pressure on property-tax increases. Draft four-year budgets are due on the city’s website Nov. 2, with a public hearing starting Nov. 23. Mayor Andrew Knack said the next four-year budget must be more disciplined than 2023–26.

Why it matters: Higher franchise fees show up on power bills (especially for large users) while council tries to hold down property-tax growth ahead of the Nov.–Dec. budget fight that sets Edmonton’s capital and operating plans.

What changed: Tuesday’s 8–5 vote locked in 19.4% (effective with the city’s 2027 franchise-fee adjustment process) and published the Nov. 2 / Nov. 23 budget calendar Edmontonians will use to weigh in.

Sources (3+)
  • Edmonton Journal
  • CTV News Edmonton
  • City of Edmonton — Notice of Application (franchise fee)
  • Alberta Utilities Commission — Disposition 30507-D01-2026
Community Note

The 19.4% figure is a franchise fee on distribution charges, not a wholesale power-price freeze. Residential bill impacts are described as modest in reporting; large commercial and institutional users can see much larger dollar increases. AUC filings and city notices govern the utility process — council’s vote sets municipal policy within that framework.

Sources: Edmonton Journal; CTV News Edmonton; City of Edmonton notice; AUC disposition.

Oct. 19 referendum: special ballots, advance voting, and safeguards

Albertans vote Oct. 19 on 10 referendum questions, including one on a separation process. Elections Alberta reports more than 652,000 special ballots approved; packages must be returned by 5 p.m. Oct. 16. Advance voting runs Oct. 13–17. The agency has outlined measures intended to safeguard the vote.

Why it matters: Special-ballot volume and the advance-voting window set the practical timeline for how Albertans can cast ballots on the 10 referendum questions before Oct. 19 — now less than two weeks away after Quebec’s PQ minority win refreshed national-unity coverage.

Sources (3+)
  • Elections Alberta
  • CTV News Edmonton / Canadian Press
  • Calgary Herald
Community Note

The ~652,000 figure is approved special ballots sent, not votes already cast. Completed packages must arrive by 5 p.m. Oct. 16; electors whose packages are not received can still vote in advance or on Oct. 19. Safeguards described by Elections Alberta include manual review of applications against the voter register and ID with returned packages — they do not by themselves prove outcomes.

Sources: Elections Alberta; CTV/CP; Calgary Herald.

Canada

Carney calls Quebec moment “crucial,” urges economic focus after PQ minority win

A day after the Parti Québécois won a minority government (59 of 127 seats), Prime Minister Mark Carney said Tuesday he spoke with premier-designate Paul St-Pierre Plamondon and urged focus on housing, infrastructure, electricity and tariff impacts rather than a referendum years away. The PQ has said any sovereignty vote would wait until after January 2029. The Coalition Avenir Québec won zero seats. AP and Canadian outlets noted Alberta’s Oct. 19 separation-process referendum is less than two weeks away.

Why it matters: Quebec’s first PQ government since 2014 is a minority needing opposition support — and Carney’s Tuesday framing of “cooperative federalism” collides with Alberta’s approaching Oct. 19 vote in national-unity coverage.

What changed: Overnight seat math (PQ 59; CAQ shutout) is now joined by Carney’s Tuesday call with Plamondon, his “crucial time / boldness” remarks, and federal ministers defending projects such as Alto high-speed rail that the PQ has opposed.

Sources (3+)
  • The Globe and Mail / Canadian Press
  • National Post
  • Seattle Times / Associated Press
  • Al Jazeera (AP/Reuters)
  • Global News / Canadian Press
Community Note

This is a minority, not a majority: 59 seats vs. 64 needed. Popular vote for the PQ was about 28%. A sovereignty referendum would still need National Assembly support and, per PQ campaign statements, would wait until after January 2029. Winning government is not the same as majority public support for secession. Alberta’s Oct. 19 ballot is a separate provincial process — not a Quebec independence vote.

Sources: Globe/CP; National Post; AP; Al Jazeera; Global/CP.

Six Nations Haldimand Tract trial opens in Toronto

A civil trial over the Six Nations’ Haldimand Tract land claim opened Monday in Toronto. The case concerns historical and ongoing claims related to lands along the Grand River. Six Nations seeks financial compensation, not the return of privately held land; Canada and Ontario dispute liability.

Why it matters: Phase 1 of the trial decides Crown liability only on a major Grand River land claim; Six Nations seeks financial compensation, while Canada and Ontario dispute liability.

Sources (3+)
  • The Hamilton Spectator
  • Two Row Times
  • Mississaugas of the Credit First Nation
Community Note

Phase 1 decides Crown liability only; damages would come later. Compensation figures cited in reporting are claim ranges, not court awards. Ottawa has stated settlements of this kind are financial and do not seize third-party property. Mississaugas of the Credit is an intervener that supports accountability but disputes that the Haldimand Proclamation is a treaty.

Sources: Hamilton Spectator; Two Row Times; MCFN.

Science corner

Brain implant decodes speech and gestures at once for a mind-controlled avatar

UCSF researchers led by Samantha Brosler and neurosurgeon Edward Chang showed that a single high-density electrocorticography (ECoG) implant — about iPhone-sized, with 253 electrodes on the surface of the sensorimotor cortex — can decode attempted speech and upper-body gestures simultaneously in people with severe paralysis. Parallel AI decoders drove a personalized full-body virtual avatar in real time (onscreen text plus gesture animation). The peer-reviewed study appeared in Nature Neuroscience (14 Sept. 2026); outlets including Nature’s news desk, New Scientist and Smithsonian covered it into early October.

Why it matters: most experimental brain–computer interfaces restore either speech or movement. Doing both at once is closer to natural conversation — a “maybe” with a nod is not the same as a “maybe” with a head shake. Participants included someone paralyzed after stroke and someone with ALS; conversation-task accuracy for speech and gesture was high in the reported sessions, though vocabularies and gesture sets remain limited.

Sources (3+)
  • Nature Neuroscience (study)
  • Nature news
  • New Scientist
  • Smithsonian Magazine
Community Note

Early-stage clinical research, not a commercial product. Full simultaneous avatar tests focused on a small number of participants; speech vocabularies and gesture inventories are still far from everyday conversation. Outside experts note speech-decoding rates can lag some prior single-function BCIs, and continuous free-form talk-plus-movement remains future work. Surface ECoG covers a wide cortex area without penetrating tissue — a design trade-off, not a cure.

Sources: Nature Neuroscience; Nature news; New Scientist; Smithsonian.
