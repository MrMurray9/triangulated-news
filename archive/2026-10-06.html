Alberta

Smith says she has no knowledge of reported separatist–U.S. strategist meetings

Premier Danielle Smith’s office said Monday she has no knowledge of reported meetings between Alberta separatists and U.S. strategists, and that she opposes separation or Alberta becoming a U.S. state. The comments followed a Globe and Mail investigation. Separatist figures Jeffrey Sylvestre and Jeffrey Rath denied offering oil, water, or royalties.

Sources (3+)

  • The Globe and Mail
  • CityNews Edmonton
Community Note

The underlying Globe and Mail investigation describes alleged contacts; Sylvestre and Rath dispute characterizations that they offered resources. Whether meetings occurred and what was discussed remains contested between the investigation and the individuals named.

Oct. 19 referendum: special ballots, advance voting, and safeguards

Albertans vote Oct. 19 on 10 referendum questions, including one on a separation process. Elections Alberta reports more than 652,000 special ballots approved; packages must be returned by 5 p.m. Oct. 16. Advance voting runs Oct. 13–17. The agency has outlined measures intended to safeguard the vote.

Sources (3+)
  • Elections Alberta
  • CTV News Edmonton / Canadian Press
  • Calgary Herald
Community Note

The ~652,000 figure is approved special ballots sent, not votes already cast. Completed packages must arrive by 5 p.m. Oct. 16; electors whose packages are not received can still vote in advance or on Oct. 19. Safeguards described by Elections Alberta include manual review of applications against the voter register and ID with returned packages — they do not by themselves prove outcomes.

Sources: Elections Alberta; CTV/CP; Calgary Herald.

Canada

Parti Québécois wins minority government — first return since 2014

The Parti Québécois won a minority government overnight, taking 59 of 127 seats — its first return to power since 2014, but short of a majority. The Coalition Avenir Québec was shut out of the legislature. Fréchette lost Trois-Rivières. The PQ has indicated a sovereignty referendum would come only after 2029. Prime Minister Mark Carney congratulated the winner.

Sources (3+)
  • The Globe and Mail
  • Global News / Canadian Press
  • National Post
  • CTV News Montreal
  • Al Jazeera (AP/Reuters)
Community Note

This is a minority, not a majority: 59 seats vs. 64 needed. Popular vote for the PQ was about 28%. A sovereignty referendum would still need National Assembly support and, per PQ statements during the campaign, would wait until after January 2029. Support for independence in Quebec polling remains well below levels of the 1995 referendum era; winning government is not the same as majority public support for secession.

Sources: Global/CP; National Post; Globe and Mail; Al Jazeera/AP.

Six Nations Haldimand Tract trial opens in Toronto

A civil trial over the Six Nations’ Haldimand Tract land claim opened Monday in Toronto. The case concerns historical and ongoing claims related to lands along the Grand River. Six Nations seeks financial compensation, not the return of privately held land; Canada and Ontario dispute liability.

Sources (3+)
  • The Hamilton Spectator
  • Two Row Times
  • Mississaugas of the Credit First Nation
Community Note

Phase 1 decides Crown liability only; damages would come later. Compensation figures cited in reporting (from roughly $14 billion up to multi-trillion estimates in federal documents) are claim ranges, not court awards. Ottawa has stated settlements of this kind are financial and do not seize third-party property. Mississaugas of the Credit is an intervener that supports accountability but disputes that the Haldimand Proclamation is a treaty.

Sources: Hamilton Spectator; Two Row Times; MCFN.

Canada
False

Claim: Anyone outside Alberta can get a mail-in referendum ballot with no residency check

“Mail-in voting is open to anybody… No verification of Alberta residents and they will mail the ballot anywhere.”

Verdict: False. The online form may accept a submitted application with an out-of-province address, but Elections Alberta manually reviews applications against the voter register and declines invalid ones. ID showing Alberta residence is required with the returned package. Canadian Press verified a widely shared Vancouver-address application was declined the next day.

Sources
  • Global News / Canadian Press
  • The Canadian Press Fact File
  • Elections Alberta
Community Note

A “submitted” confirmation is not an approved ballot. Screenshots of the first application screen omit the later human review step that Elections Alberta says blocks out-of-province addresses from being mailed a package.

Sources: CP/Global; Elections Alberta.

U.S.
False

Claim: Brazil’s same-day vote count proves U.S. elections are “corrupt”

On Truth Social (Oct. 4), President Trump pointed to Brazil’s first-round presidential results arriving the same day and argued that slower counts in Detroit, Philadelphia, California and elsewhere mean U.S. voting is “CORRUPT.”

Verdict: False. PolitiFact rated the claim Pants on Fire. Brazil uses a nationally standardized in-person electronic system with no mail ballots; U.S. states set their own rules, and expansive mail voting takes longer to process. Speed of counting is not evidence of rigging.

Sources
  • PolitiFact
  • PBS NewsHour (Brazil runoff context)
Community Note

Comparing one country’s election-night projections to another’s multi-week certification deadlines mixes different legal systems. No U.S. state completes an official certified count on election night.

Sources: PolitiFact; PBS NewsHour.
