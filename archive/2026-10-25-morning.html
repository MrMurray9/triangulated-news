Alberta

Edmonton police in riot gear at MacEwan University after security detains a student with a megaphone at a pro-Palestinian protest

A pro-Palestinian demonstration inside MacEwan University’s library on Thursday, Oct. 22, ended with campus security holding a student who had been using a megaphone. Edmonton police officers wearing helmets and carrying shields arrived around 4 p.m. The student was released about an hour later, and police said no one was arrested or hurt (Edmonton Journal, CityNews Edmonton, The Griff).

The Griff, MacEwan’s student paper, reported that the student was given a seven-day campus ban. The Students’ Association of MacEwan University issued a statement condemning what it called “institutional violence against students.” The university said the protest broke its rules for demonstrations and that security asked for police help.

Why it matters: It is the most forceful response to a campus protest in Edmonton this fall, and it tests where universities draw the line between free expression and their own conduct rules.

Alberta readies 194 temporary hospital spaces and puts doctors in ER waiting rooms before flu season peaks

Health Minister Adriana LaGrange said Friday that Alberta now has 9,003 acute-care spaces, the most it has ever had, plus 194 temporary spaces that can be opened if respiratory viruses swamp hospitals. A new provincial operations centre will track bed use across the system in real time (Calgary Herald, CochraneNow).

The province is also rolling out its Triage Liaison Physician program in busy Edmonton and Calgary emergency departments: a doctor or nurse practitioner works in the waiting room to assess people sooner, order tests and start treatment. Emergency doctors told the Edmonton Journal the program arrives months later than promised and that crowding is “the worst it’s ever been.” Last flu season had a record number of influenza deaths in Alberta (Calgary Herald, TDot News).

Why it matters: If you end up in an Edmonton ER this winter, you may now see a physician in the waiting room — and the province is betting extra beds keep hallway care down.

Sources (4)
  • Calgary Herald — Province increases acute care capacity ahead of flu season
  • CochraneNow — Alberta prepares 194 temporary hospital spaces for respiratory virus season
  • TDot News — Alberta launches Triage Liaison Physician program in emergency department waiting rooms
  • Edmonton Journal — “The worst it’s ever been”: Alberta implements triage liaison physicians
Community Note

“Spaces” is not the same as staffed beds: the temporary spaces only help if there are nurses and doctors to run them, and the province did not say how many staff are lined up. The Calgary Herald and Edmonton Journal are both Postmedia papers, so we count them as one source alongside CochraneNow and TDot News. Flu and COVID shots are free at pharmacies for Albertans six months and older.

Sources: Calgary Herald; Edmonton Journal; CochraneNow; TDot News.

Canada

Crime Stoppers breach exposed more than 1.5 million Canadian tips — Toronto, Ottawa, Winnipeg and others stop taking online reports

Hackers broke into P3, a tip platform run by U.S. company Navigate360 that many Canadian Crime Stoppers programs use, in March. A news investigation this week found the stolen files hold more than 1.5 million tips from Canada going back about 25 years. In Toronto alone, 176,859 tips from January 2022 to November 2025 were exposed, including 14,973 that named the tipster (Global News, CTV News).

Toronto Crime Stoppers told people “please do not send us tips” online for now. Police in Toronto, Ottawa, Hamilton, Halton, London, Winnipeg and Brandon have paused or suspended their part in the program, and the London Police Service says it is reviewing what was exposed. Navigate360 says it has “no evidence of misuse” of the data.

Why it matters: Anonymous tips only work if people trust they stay anonymous; tipsters named in the files, and the people they reported, may now be at risk.

Sources (3)
  • Global News — Toronto Crime Stoppers cyber breach
  • CTV News Toronto — “Please do not send us tips,” Toronto Crime Stoppers says following data breach
  • London Police Service — LPS suspends participation in Crime Stoppers program
Community Note

The breach happened in March but became public only this week. We could not confirm whether Edmonton or Alberta tips are in the stolen files; Edmonton Crime Stoppers had not announced a pause in the reports we checked. If something is happening now, call 911; for non-emergencies in Edmonton, the police line is 780-423-4567.

Sources: Global News; CTV News; London Police Service.

Carney, premiers and First Nations chiefs meet Monday in Ottawa — the first meeting of its kind — after a day of ceremony today

Prime Minister Mark Carney and the premiers sit down with First Nations leaders in Ottawa on Monday, Oct. 26, for the first First Nations–First Ministers’ Meeting. The Assembly of First Nations asked communities to hold ceremonies across the country today to open it. Only the opening prayer and remarks will be livestreamed, at 7 a.m. Edmonton time; the main table has 28 seats (APTN News, AFN).

National Chief Cindy Woodhouse Nepinak says the meeting must not be “one and done” and wants regular meetings. Expected topics include the Building Canada Strong Act and fast-tracked major projects, pipelines, and a proposed First Nations sovereign wealth fund, which Ottawa has not committed to. Some Alberta Treaty 6 nations, including Onion Lake Cree Nation, sent the AFN a cease-and-desist letter, saying it cannot speak for them on treaty matters (The Globe and Mail, APTN News).

Why it matters: Major Alberta projects — pipelines included — need First Nations partners, and what Ottawa offers Monday could shape how fast they move.

Results in: B.C. Conservatives win a big majority — 64 of 93 seats — and David Eby steps down as NDP leader

What changed since the last edition: Our Oct. 24 morning edition covered voting day, when the last poll had the Conservatives about five points ahead. Now the votes are counted: a large Conservative majority, the NDP down about 20 seats, and Eby out as leader.

Lorne Doerkson’s Conservatives were elected or leading in 64 ridings on Saturday night, with the NDP at 27 and the Greens at two, based on 99.6 per cent of ballot boxes. The Conservatives took about 48 per cent of the vote to the NDP’s 37 and the Greens’ 11. It is the first Conservative government in B.C. since 1928 (Elections BC, Vancouver Sun, The Globe and Mail).

“B.C. is definitely blue,” Doerkson told supporters. Eby held his own seat in Vancouver-Point Grey but resigned as party leader. Green Leader Emily Lowan won her riding. Preliminary turnout was about 48 per cent. Some seats are close — one NDP lead in Burnaby is 66 votes — and the final count runs Nov. 6 to 10 (Global News, Elections BC).

Why it matters: Alberta gets a like-minded government next door, which could change the politics of pipelines to the West Coast and the province’s fights with Ottawa.

Paris, Marseille and Bordeaux see clashes as nationwide cost-of-living marches end with 1,314 arrests

Hundreds of thousands of people marched across France on Saturday over prices and cuts to public services — about 410,000 by the Interior Ministry’s count and close to 800,000 according to the CGT union. Most marches were peaceful, but clashes broke out late in the day in Paris, Marseille, Bordeaux, Toulouse and Tours, and police fired tear gas (RFI, AP, CBS News).

The ministry said Sunday that 1,314 people were arrested and 53 officers hurt. About 70,000 police and gendarmes were on duty, 20 per cent more than the weekend before, when 450 Paris shops were damaged and 228 looted. Paris police blamed this time’s trouble on about 500 rioters at the back of the march near Porte de Vincennes; one shop was robbed (RFI).

Why it matters: Living costs are driving protests in several European countries, and France’s unrest is a test for its government ahead of next year’s presidential race.

Brazil votes today in a coin-flip runoff: Lula against Flávio Bolsonaro, with polls closing at 2 p.m. Edmonton time

Brazilians choose between President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, 80, who is seeking a fourth term, and Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro, the eldest son of former president Jair Bolsonaro. The final polls are within the margin of error: a CNT/MDA poll had Bolsonaro ahead 46.3 to 45.0 per cent, and Datafolha had him up 48 to 47 (CNBC, Al Jazeera, The Rio Times).

Voting ends at 5 p.m. Brasília time, which is 2 p.m. in Edmonton. Brazil votes electronically, so a winner is usually clear within a few hours. The candidates spent Saturday rallying — Lula in Rio and São Paulo, Bolsonaro in the battleground state of Minas Gerais. Jair Bolsonaro is serving a sentence of more than 27 years for plotting a coup after he lost in 2022 (CNBC).

Why it matters: Brazil is the biggest economy in Latin America and a major competitor to Canadian farm and mining exports; the result will also show whether the Bolsonaro movement can win without its jailed founder on the ballot.

Business

Audi will build its first U.S. factory in Georgia — a $4-billion SUV plant with 4,500 jobs — as tariffs push carmakers south of the border

Audi confirmed Saturday that it will build a plant at the Middle Georgia megasite near Fort Valley, south of Macon. It plans to invest about US$4 billion and hire about 4,500 people, with room to build up to 200,000 SUVs a year. Site work starts in 2027, construction in 2028, and full production is planned for 2031 (Audi, Office of Gov. Brian Kemp).

President Donald Trump announced the plant at a rally in Georgia and credited his tariffs. Audi, owned by Volkswagen, has no factory in the U.S. today and imports every car it sells there, mostly from Mexico and Europe; it said tariffs cost it €1.2 billion last year (AP, CNBC).

Why it matters: Each big auto plant that lands in the U.S. instead of Canada or Mexico shows how tariffs are pulling car investment south — a live issue as Canada negotiates its own auto deal.

Sources (4)

  • Office of Gov. Brian Kemp — Audi AG selects Georgia to invest $4 billion
  • AP — Audi to build Georgia plant, Trump announces
  • CNBC — Trump announces Audi will build $4 billion U.S. factory in Georgia
Community Note

Production is about five years away, and plans that long can change. Which models will be built has not been officially confirmed; a U.S. official told Reuters it would be the Q7, Q8 and Q9 SUVs. The Audi and Georgia releases are primary sources; AP and CNBC are the independent reports.

Sources: Audi; Office of the Governor of Georgia; AP; CNBC.

Science corner

A touchscreen you can throw in the wash: Singapore engineers weave a working colour display into a shirt sleeve

Researchers at the National University of Singapore wove 54 thin “pixel fibres” into fabric to make a sleeve that is also a screen. Each fibre carries 96 tiny LEDs plus a touch-sensing layer, giving a 96-by-54-pixel colour display that runs at 30 frames per second and responds to taps and swipes (NUS News, Nature).

The sleeve worked almost the same after 50 machine washes and 1,000 bends. It runs wirelessly and includes sensors for temperature, motion, direction, sound and light, so it could show maps, messages or health readings on your arm. The work was published in Nature on Oct. 21.

Why it matters: Electronics that survive the laundry are the big hurdle for smart clothing; this is one of the first full displays to clear it.

Sources (3)
  • NUS News — World’s first touchscreen in a garment
  • Nature — research paper
  • Nature News — Fabric user interface can be worn on your sleeve — and machine-washed
Community Note

This is a lab prototype, not a product; the team has not given a price or a date. The resolution is low — closer to a scoreboard than a phone. Sourcing note: NUS is the research institution and the paper is the study itself; Nature’s news piece is written by its separate news team. We did not find a third fully independent outlet, so treat this as well documented but early.

Sources: NUS News; Nature; Nature News.

AI news and advances

Microsoft: Chinese state hackers used AI agents to hit more than 2,250 Microsoft 365 organizations after breaching ConnectWise

Microsoft says a China-aligned group it calls Storm-0940 used ARTEX, an open-source framework for autonomous AI agents, to automate an attack after it compromised ConnectWise’s Cloud Backup service. The agents tried to sign in to more than 2,250 Microsoft 365 tenants, peaking at about 2,900 attempts a minute (Microsoft Security Blog, CSO Online, The Record).

Most attempts failed, but in some cases the attackers reached privileged access in under three hours. Microsoft and ConnectWise advise customers to disable the affected backup apps and check service-principal sign-ins and directory changes back to Oct. 15.

Why it matters: It is one of the clearest documented cases of AI agents speeding up a real nation-state hack — which means defenders now have hours, not days, to react.

Sources (3)
  • Microsoft Security Blog — Storm-0940 uses ARTEX agentic AI in ConnectWise supply-chain compromise
  • CSO Online — China-linked hackers used agentic AI in attack on 2,250 Microsoft 365 tenants
  • The Record — Microsoft blames ConnectWise attacks on Chinese state hacker using ARTEX AI agents
Community Note

The attribution to China comes from Microsoft; China routinely denies such accusations. Microsoft sells security products, so its blog is a primary source; CSO Online and The Record reported independently. Home users of personal Microsoft accounts were not the target — this hit business tenants linked to the ConnectWise backup app.

Sources: Microsoft; CSO Online; The Record.

Confirmed

OBukangi the shark is free: after a month stuck in a Busan canal and 1.6 million visitors, she has been spotted healthy out at sea

Seen on: Other — South Korean news livestreams and video clips; most of the attention was in person at the canal

A 3.5-metre female dusky shark nicknamed Bukangi got stuck in an artificial canal at Busan’s North Port waterfront park on Sept. 18. Crowds came to see her — more than 1.6 million visitors, by the Korea Herald’s count — while caretakers worried as the water cooled and she stopped eating (Korea Herald, AP).

After two failed attempts, rescuers guided her out on Oct. 21 in an eight-hour operation. She briefly swam back in through a gap, so the barrier net was removed, and she left again. On Oct. 24 she was seen about 2 km outside the port, swimming normally (AP, Asia Business Daily).

Why it matters: A happy ending to a story South Korea followed day by day — and a lesson in patience from the rescuers.

Sources (3)

  • AP — Busan’s stranded shark Bukangi
  • Asia Business Daily — Bukangi spotted outside Busan port
Community Note

True and well documented. Dusky sharks are not considered a danger to people at the park, and officials kept visitors out of the water. Some spellings use “Bukang-i.”

Sources: Korea Herald; AP; Asia Business Daily.
