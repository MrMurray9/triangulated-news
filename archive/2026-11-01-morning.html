Alberta

Pflag Canada says Alberta’s new school flag rules will effectively ban Pride flags from January

Under Alberta’s new Flag Display Regulation, school boards must fly only the Canadian and Alberta flags outside their buildings during the school year starting Jan. 4, 2027. Any flag that would “constitute a political, social or ideological statement” by the board is not allowed, and boards that want to show a flag not on the approved list, such as a Pride flag, have to apply to the education ministry in writing (Edmonton Journal, The Globe and Mail, Government of Alberta).

Pflag Canada, which supports LGBTQ people and their families, said on Friday that in practice the rules will remove Pride flags from Alberta schools. Education Minister Demetrios Nicolaides says requests will be judged case by case, and that the rules keep the focus “on student learning and success.” The rules do not cover students’ own clothing or pins (The Globe and Mail, Edmonton Journal, Culture Alberta).

Why it matters: Every Edmonton public and Catholic school will have to follow the same list, so any school that has flown a Pride flag in June will need the minister’s sign-off to do it again.

Sources (4)
  • The Globe and Mail — Pride flag effectively banned from being displayed at Alberta schools, LGBTQ non-profit says
  • Edmonton Journal — New regulations outline which flags can be displayed in Alberta schools
  • Culture Alberta — Alberta schools must fly Canadian and Alberta flags under new rules
  • Government of Alberta — Flag Display Regulation
Community Note

The regulation does not name the Pride flag; it bans flags that state a board’s position and lists which ones are allowed. “Effectively banned” is Pflag Canada’s reading. The province says boards can still apply for exceptions, and has not said how many it expects to approve.

Sources: The Globe and Mail; Edmonton Journal; Culture Alberta; Government of Alberta.

Enoch Cree Nation breaks ground on 166 family homes just west of Edmonton, the first step toward 500

Enoch Cree Nation held a groundbreaking on Friday for a neighbourhood of about 166 homes, to be leased at affordable rates only to its members. The nation says about 350 families are on its housing wait list, and this is the first of five phases meant to deliver more than 500 homes (Edmonton Journal, Canada Infrastructure Bank, CFWE Northern Alberta).

The Canada Infrastructure Bank is lending $30 million for roads, water and sewer lines, utilities, site work and solar panels, its biggest investment yet in an Indigenous housing project, and the First Nations Finance Authority is funding the wider build. Builder Akash Homes says the first houses, with four or five bedrooms and finished basements, should be ready around March (Edmonton Journal, Canada Infrastructure Bank).

Why it matters: Enoch borders Edmonton’s west end, and Chief Cody Thomas pitches the new homes as a way for members now living in the city to come home.

Sources (3)

  • Canada Infrastructure Bank — CIB to loan Enoch Cree Nation $30 million for housing-enabling infrastructure
  • CFWE Northern Alberta — Enoch Cree Nation advances development for 166 new homes
Community Note

The $30-million loan pays for servicing the land, not the houses themselves. The March move-in date is the builder’s estimate, and later phases toward 500 homes have not been funded yet.

Sources: Edmonton Journal; Canada Infrastructure Bank; CFWE Northern Alberta.

Canada

Ottawa’s deficit for the first five months of the year is less than half last year’s, at $5.36 billion

The Finance Department’s monthly Fiscal Monitor shows a $5.36-billion deficit for April to August 2026, down from $11.07 billion in the same months of 2025. Revenue rose about 7 per cent to $215.54 billion, helped by personal income tax and GST, while customs duties and energy taxes brought in less (Finance Canada, The Canadian Press via CityNews, Reuters via Investing.com).

Program spending climbed to $195.95 billion, and interest on the national debt rose to $25.10 billion from $23.01 billion. The update comes days before the federal budget on Thursday, Nov. 5 (Finance Canada, The Canadian Press via CityNews, Reuters via Investing.com).

Why it matters: Interest on the debt alone now costs about $5 billion a month, and Thursday’s budget will show whether the improvement holds for the full year.

Sources (3)
  • Finance Canada — The Fiscal Monitor, August 2026
  • CityNews (The Canadian Press) — Ottawa posts $5.36B deficit for first five months of 2026-27 fiscal year
  • Investing.com (Reuters) — Canada’s budget deficit over first five months almost halves to C$5.36 billion
Community Note

Monthly Fiscal Monitor figures are early and unaudited, and the deficit usually grows later in the year. The full-year forecast, which the budget officer has put above $63 billion a year, is a different measure from this five-month total.

Sources: Finance Canada; The Canadian Press; Reuters.

World

Iran says it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days, if Washington meets its terms

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told Al Jazeera that talks with Qatari mediators had produced a plan to reopen the strait in two days, down from the five days Qatar had suggested. Iran’s conditions include lifting sanctions, fewer U.S. forces in the region, restored oil waivers and the release of its frozen funds (Dawn, Middle East Eye, Times of India, AP).

Iran says it is not meeting U.S. officials directly. At the same time, Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei warned that any U.S. or Israeli attack would get an immediate response with no “geographic limits,” and Saudi and Egyptian leaders urged calm (AP, Times of India, Dawn).

Why it matters: About a fifth of the world’s oil and LNG used to pass through Hormuz; it has been effectively shut since February, a big reason pump prices in Alberta and everywhere else are so high.

More than 50 million Americans have already voted ahead of Tuesday’s midterms, a record

More than 50.5 million early ballots had been cast by Saturday morning, according to a University of Florida analysis, already about 5 million more than all early votes in the 2022 midterms. Texas alone passed 7 million (The Guardian, Reuters via CNA, Aju Press).

Among states that track party registration, 10.7 million registered Democrats had voted versus 7.8 million Republicans. Early voting is up 83 per cent in South Carolina and 34 per cent in Kansas, both states with unusually close Senate races. Republicans are defending a 53–47 Senate majority (The Guardian, AP, Reuters via CNA).

Why it matters: Tuesday’s result decides whether President Trump keeps control of Congress for his last two years, which matters for tariffs, trade talks with Canada and the war with Iran.

About 150,000 people march through Taipei for Taiwan Pride, calling for an end to stigma seven years after marriage equality

Organizers estimated 150,000 or more people from Taiwan and abroad joined the 24th Taiwan Pride parade on Saturday, looping out from Taipei City Hall Plaza under the theme “Our Voices, Our Journey,” with more than 180 groups taking part (Focus Taiwan, AP, Taiwan News).

Marchers said same-sex marriage, legal since 2019, has not ended discrimination. Many cited an Oct. 3 police search of a gay bar in Taipei’s Wanhua district, for which the city has apologized; prosecutors are investigating. Visitors from Hong Kong and Japan said they came for freedoms they lack at home (Focus Taiwan, AP, Taiwan News).

Why it matters: Taiwan was the first place in Asia to legalize same-sex marriage, and its Pride parade now draws LGBTQ people from across the region, including places where such events are shut down.

Business

OPEC+ holds oil output flat for December, with Brent near US$118 and Hormuz still closed

Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman met online on Sunday and agreed to keep December production targets at September’s level, about 31 million barrels a day combined. Their statement announced no new increases and set the next meeting for Nov. 29 (OPEC, The National, Reuters via CNBC).

Most of the seven are pumping below their targets anyway because of the Iran war. Brent crude rose 2.4 per cent on Friday to US$117.64 a barrel, a six-month high and its fourth monthly gain in a row (The National, Reuters via CNBC, Business Standard).

Why it matters: High oil prices help Alberta’s budget and energy jobs but keep gas and diesel expensive for everyone else; OPEC+ is choosing not to add supply for now.

Sources (4)

  • Business Standard — OPEC+ agrees in principle to keep oil output targets steady in December
Community Note

These are targets, not actual output. With Gulf shipping disrupted, several members cannot reach their quotas, so holding targets steady does not by itself change how much oil reaches the market.

Sources: OPEC; The National; Reuters via CNBC; Business Standard.

Science corner

Science corner: a USB-stick-sized implant that burns off extra blood sugar with a dash of electricity

Swiss researchers at ETH Zurich have built an implant called ECHO that tackles high blood sugar without any drug. Inside is a graphite pad covered in tiny gold flakes dusted with platinum. When a small current runs through it, it turns glucose into gluconate, which the body either burns or sends out in urine (Science, ETH Zurich, Scientific American).

A standard arm-worn glucose sensor tells a phone when sugar climbs after a meal, the phone switches the implant on wirelessly, and it switches itself off once levels are back down. In obese diabetic mice it brought glucose under control within an hour and kept it there for six weeks, with weight loss and no scarring. A 5-litre flask test, about the blood volume of an adult, worked too (Scientific American, ETH Zurich, Medindia).

Why it matters: Type 2 diabetes affects millions of Canadians; a device that removes excess sugar instead of pushing the body to store it could one day mean fewer daily pills or shots.

Sources (4)
  • Science — Wireless electrocatalytic closed-loop system for drug-free management of diabesity (Maity et al.)
  • ETH Zurich — Tackling diabetes with an electronic implant
  • Scientific American — This implant burns up excess blood sugar with electricity
  • Medindia — Scientists develop USB-sized electronic implant to lower blood sugar
Community Note

So far ECHO has only been tested in mice and in a flask, not in people. An independent chemist quoted by Scientific American said long-term safety and working at human scale are the key next tests.

Sources: Science; ETH Zurich; Scientific American; Medindia.

AI news and advances

Anthropic is negotiating to lease an 800-megawatt AI data-centre campus in Sturgeon County, sources say

The Globe and Mail, citing two unnamed sources, reports that Anthropic, which makes the Claude chatbot, is in talks to be the tenant of Calgary-based Beacon Data Centers’ planned Heartland campus, about seven kilometres east of Gibbons, northeast of Edmonton. Neither company would comment, and no lease has been announced (The Globe and Mail, Reuters, Culture Alberta).

Beacon plans four data halls drawing about 800 megawatts. The Alberta Utilities Commission approved a 920-megawatt gas-fired power plant for the site on Oct. 23, with conditions, and Beacon is aiming for first operations in fall 2027. That would be about twice the size of the Nova Scotia campus Anthropic signed a lease for last month (The Globe and Mail, Culture Alberta, CTOL Digital Solutions).

Why it matters: It would be one of the biggest AI projects in the Edmonton region, after Meta’s planned $13-billion campus nearby, bringing construction jobs and new demand on the province’s power grid.

Sources (4)

  • Reuters (via TradingView) — Anthropic in talks to lease hyperscale data centre campus near Edmonton
  • Culture Alberta — Anthropic eyes Sturgeon County data centre near Edmonton
  • CTOL Digital Solutions — Alberta clears a 920 MW gas plant for the proposed campus

Only the Globe has its own sources on the Anthropic talks; Reuters and the other outlets are repeating its report. What has been confirmed is the regulator’s approval of the power plant, which does not cover the data centre itself or its grid connection. The Samson Cree Nation opposed the project at the regulator.

Apple Intelligence finally reaches China, with Alibaba’s Qwen built into Siri, in a beta starting Wednesday

Apple says its AI features will reach China in iOS 27.3. A developer beta arrives on Wednesday, Nov. 4, and the public release follows in December. Alibaba’s Qwen models power parts of Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence, much as ChatGPT does elsewhere (The Verge, The Next Web, China Daily, Xinhua).

The first Chinese release brings earlier features such as notification summaries, live translation, proofreading and photo clean-up, but not the newer Siri announced at WWDC 2026. Chinese regulators approved Apple Intelligence in July (The Verge, The Next Web, China Daily).

Why it matters: China is one of Apple’s biggest markets, and until now iPhones sold there have gone without the AI features Canadians have had for more than a year.

Sources (4)

  • The Next Web — Apple confirms its AI features reach China in December, with Qwen
  • China Daily — Qwen-powered Apple AI to launch in China in December
  • Xinhua — Alibaba’s Qwen AI model to integrate into Apple Intelligence
Community Note

China Daily and Xinhua are Chinese state media. The features apply only to devices in mainland China; nothing changes for Apple users in Canada.

Sources: The Verge; The Next Web; China Daily; Xinhua.

FA chocolate Lab named Tank becomes the chocolate in a s’more costume, and his glare wins 14 million views

Seen on: Facebook, where his owner’s page “Tank the Brown Lab” posted the clip, and TikTok, as reported by TAG24 and PetHelpful.

Austin, Texas, dog owner Ally Floth sandwiched Tank between two cardboard graham crackers and puffs of pillow-stuffing “marshmallow,” turning the chocolate Labrador into the chocolate in a s’more. Her caption calls Halloween “his least favorite holiday,” and the video has topped 14 million views (TAG24, PetHelpful via Yahoo).

Tank just stares at the camera, clearly waiting for it to be over. “Where did the dog go? I can only see a s’more,” one commenter wrote, while others insisted he was an ice-cream sandwich. One noted “the audacity of him being the chocolate when he can’t even eat it” (TAG24, PetHelpful via Yahoo).

Why it matters: It’s a simple, funny Halloween moment, and a reminder that some dogs are happier with a festive bandana than a full costume.
