Science corner: Google “cultivates” near-perfect magic states on its Willow quantum chip, cutting errors about 40-fold

Google Quantum AI reported in the journal Science on Thursday that “magic state cultivation” works on real hardware. Magic states are special quantum states needed for the “T gate,” the operation that lets a quantum computer do things an ordinary computer can’t; making clean ones is so costly that some estimates say up to 95 per cent of a machine could be tied up producing them. Cultivation starts a rough magic state on a few qubits, repeatedly checks it, throws away failures and grows the survivors across more qubits.

Using 54 of the Willow chip’s 105 qubits, the method cut errors about 40-fold and reached 99.99 per cent logical fidelity for these “T states.” The team also “grafted” a cultivated state from one error-correcting code (a colour code) into the surface code Google’s machines use, showing it can survive the hand-off.

Why it matters: Cheaper magic states could shrink the size — and cost — of the quantum computer needed for useful work such as simulating molecules for new drugs and materials.

Sources (4)
  • Science — Google Quantum AI research paper (doi:10.1126/science.aee7961)
  • Google — Magic state cultivation on Willow (Google blog)
  • Scientific American — Google’s quantum Willow chip may be able to grow ‘magic states’
  • Investing.com via Yahoo Finance — Google demonstrates quantum computing breakthrough, cuts errors 40-fold
Community Note

The method discards about 92 per cent of attempts, Duke University’s Kenneth Brown, who wasn’t involved, told Scientific American. Today’s error rate (about one in 10,000) is still far from the one in a million to one in 100 million needed for molecule simulation, and much further from what breaking modern encryption would take. The work is peer-reviewed but comes from Google’s own team.

Sources: Science; Google Quantum AI; Scientific American; Investing.com.

China proposes export controls on 23 AI technologies — including foundation models, AI agents and computing clusters

China’s commerce ministry has proposed adding 40 technologies in artificial intelligence, biomedicine and solar power to the country’s list of technologies banned or restricted from export. The AI portion covers 23 items spanning foundation models, intelligent agents, computing clusters and information processing. Fourteen technologies would be banned outright, including AI that can generate and mutate malicious code and perovskite and tandem solar-cell manufacturing.

Why it matters: China is adopting the export-control playbook the U.S. has used on chips, which could limit how Chinese AI models and know-how spread abroad as the rivalry deepens.

“Fanum tax” — when a friend “taxes” your food by stealing a bite

Fanum tax is a joking claim to part of someone’s food (or anything else). It comes from streamer Fanum, whose habit of “taxing” friends’ food began in 2022 and went viral in 2023. Example: “You got fries? Fanum tax.” Recency: Merriam-Webster slang entry, still widely used.

“It’s giving” — the quick way to name a vibe

It’s giving describes the energy or look something gives off, with or without anything after it: “That outfit? It’s giving.” or “It’s giving main character in a rom-com.” It comes from ballroom culture and is now everyday online slang. Recency: Dictionary.com entry dated June 5, 2026.

“Main character energy” — confidence (or a bit too much of it)

Main character energy means acting self-assured, like the star of your own movie — usually a compliment, sometimes a dig at someone who treats everyone else like extras. Example: “She walked into the gym with full main character energy.” Recency: Merriam-Webster entry updated May 11, 2026.
