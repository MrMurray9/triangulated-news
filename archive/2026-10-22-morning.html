BioWare, the Edmonton studio behind Mass Effect and Dragon Age, is closing after more than 30 years — and the next Mass Effect is cancelled

Electronic Arts told employees Wednesday it is shutting the studio and its Edmonton workspace after deciding not to continue early development on a new Mass Effect game. “BioWare is a legendary studio that helped define the role-playing genre, and we do not take this decision lightly,” EA executives Cam Weber and David Tinson wrote in a memo published by Variety. The move is part of a restructuring that cuts about 400 jobs, roughly 3 per cent of EA’s staff; Bloomberg reported about 50 of them are at BioWare (Variety, Reuters, AP).

BioWare was founded in Edmonton in 1995 by doctors Ray Muzyka and Greg Zeschuk and Augustine Yip, and its games include Baldur’s Gate, Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, Dragon Age and Anthem. “After more than three decades of shared adventures, BioWare is closing,” the studio wrote on its blog. EA, taken private in August in a US$55-billion deal led by Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund, says it will focus on fewer, bigger action games such as Marvel’s Iron Man and the next Star Wars Jedi (Reuters, BioWare, Variety).

Why it matters: BioWare was the anchor of Edmonton’s video-game industry and a training ground for many of the city’s local studios; its closure is a blow to the city’s tech sector and to a generation of players.

Science corner

Science corner: Wisconsin engineers turn ordinary walls into “virtual cameras” that can see around two corners from one spot

Seeing around a single corner with laser light has been possible in labs for years. A University of Wisconsin–Madison team led by Talha Sultan and Andreas Velten has now imaged a scene hidden behind two corners without moving its equipment. It fires short laser pulses at a visible wall; the light bounces into hidden spaces and some returns. A grid of 256 single-photon detectors (a 16-by-16 SPAD array built at Politecnico di Milano) records exactly when each photon comes back, and algorithms work backward to rebuild what’s out of sight (UW–Madison, Nature Communications).

Each wall patch acts like a “virtual camera” that can be computationally focused. By aiming light and detector at a spot in the first hidden room, the team placed a second virtual camera there and looked around the next corner. The second image was blurrier and noisier, but the researchers say larger detector arrays and better algorithms could push the method past two corners, with uses in caves, search and rescue, and seeing through fog or water (UW–Madison, UW Computational Optics Group).

Why it matters: Being able to look into spaces that are dangerous or impossible to reach — a collapsed building, a cave system — without sending anyone in could make rescues and exploration safer.

Sources (3)

  • UW–Madison Computational Optics Group — Publications (Nature Communications paper)
  • arXiv — Cascaded non-line-of-sight imaging (related two-corner method)

AI news and advances

AI-assisted researchers uncover “PHP2Shell,” critical flaws in the code behind most websites — and governments from Finland to Ottawa take sites offline until it’s patched

The PHP project shipped emergency fixes on Wednesday — versions 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35 — for two critical vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619) that can let an attacker run code on a web server without logging in. PHP powers roughly 70 per cent of websites, including WordPress, Drupal and Wikipedia’s software. Wiz Research says every PHP version from the past decade is affected by at least one of the flaws, and the U.S. cyber agency CISA urged immediate patching, saying it hadn’t seen exploitation yet (PHP Foundation, Wiz, CISA, Tenable).

Finland’s government IT centre Valtori took public sites offline as a precaution, the Netherlands pulled sites including DigiD and the House of Representatives, and federal AI minister Evan Solomon said some Government of Canada websites were temporarily taken down too; the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security told anyone who can’t patch to take affected sites off the public internet (Yle, Reuters, NOS, Canadian Centre for Cyber Security). Wiz says frontier AI models turned a flaw that had been present for years into a working exploit, and security firm Calif reported a related remote-code-execution bug on Aug. 11 during an audit. The PHP Foundation admits the underlying memory problem was first spotted in 2024, but its fix stalled for months (Wiz, PHP Foundation).

Why it matters: It’s one of the clearest signs yet that AI is now finding serious, long-hidden security holes — which helps defenders, but means attackers can do the same, so the time to patch is shrinking.
